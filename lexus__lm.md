#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation, timeframe & codes:
  - This is the second‑generation Lexus LM (launched 2023; sold as MY24 onward in many markets). Lexus markets it as the LM II — the model family traces back to the first generation launched in 2019/2020. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexus_LM) and Lexus press materials note the two‑generation lineage.
- Where it sits in the Lexus lineup:
  - The LM is Lexus’s top‑end chauffeur/MPV offering — positioned above the NX/RX crossover family and alongside flagship limo/sedan territory (the LM targets buyers who might otherwise consider an S‑Class or an S‑Class‑sized people‑mover for rear‑seat luxury). It plays the “business‑class on wheels” role within Lexus’ catalog; think flagship comfort rather than sporting dynamics. See Lexus global newsroom on positioning and launch details: [Lexus Newsroom](https://newsroom.lexus.eu/all-new-lexus-lm-dpl-press-kit/).
- Shared platform & parts availability:
  - The LM II is built on Lexus/Toyota’s GA‑K platform (the same mid‑to‑large passenger‑car architecture used across many Toyota/Lexus models), which helps parts availability for things like suspension links, brakes and electrical components. Lexus confirms the GA‑K underpinnings in the press kit: [Lexus Newsroom](https://newsroom.lexus.eu/all-new-lexus-lm-dpl-press-kit/).
  - The LM is closely related to the Toyota Alphard/Vellfire family (they share body engineering and many mechanicals), so replacement minor trim items and some mechanical spares are more widely available through Toyota dealer channels in Asia and select markets — helpful for long‑term serviceability ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexus_LM)).
- Market‑specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drive configurations (what you will see in Australia and export markets):
  - LM 350h — 2.5‑litre self‑charging hybrid (reported displacement ~2,487 cc) with a combined system output in the ~247–250 PS range. Hybrid drive uses a planetary‑type e‑CVT in many LM 350h variants; available as FWD or AWD (E‑Four style) in select markets. Manufacturer specs: [Lexus technical pack PDF](https://media.lexus.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/pdf/240206-LM-Tech-Specs.pdf) and Lexus UK model specs confirm the 2.5‑litre hybrid options ([Lexus UK](https://www.lexus.co.uk/new-cars/lm/specifications)).
  - LM 500h (market dependent) — higher‑output hybrid variant with Direct4 torque vectoring and a Direct‑Shift/automatic layout; aggregated spec listings show combined system figures up to ~371 hp for flagship Direct4 variants ([Auto‑Data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/lexus-lm-ii-500h-371hp-hybrid-direct4-direct-shift-4-seat-50209)).
  - Gearboxes reported: e‑CVT on LM 350h; Direct‑Shift 6‑speed or multi‑mode arrangements for the higher‑output LM 500h variants in some markets ([Autocar India](https://www.autocarindia.com/cars/lexus/lm/specifications), [Auto‑Data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/lexus-lm-ii-500h-371hp-hybrid-direct4-direct-shift-4-seat-50209)).
- Rivals in the Australian market (the real shopping list buyers will compare):
  - Mercedes‑Benz V‑Class (and V‑Class VIP conversions), Toyota Alphard/Vellfire (including the Toyota Vellfire Hybrid), high‑end people‑movers and executive SUVs such as imported S‑Class long‑wheelbase saloons or large luxury SUVs (buyers cross‑shop Mercedes‑V, Alphard, and executive sedans). Comparison pages and competitor lists show the LM is scoped against V‑Class and Alphard territory ([V3Cars competitors list](https://www.v3cars.com/lexus-cars/lm/competitors), [DriveDuel comparison](https://uk.driveduel.de/compare/mercedes-benz/v-class/vs/lexus/lm)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Lexus LM?
- Exterior
  - Panel alignment & build quality:
    - Lexus/Kyushu plant quality control is famously strict — takumi specialists measure panel gaps — so factory panel alignment is generally excellent. That said, buyer reports from owners’ forums show small fitment quirks are sometimes spotted after dealer prep or transport handling; visually inspect panel gaps and door shut feel during inspection. See ClubLexus discussion on Takumi gap checks: [ClubLexus](https://www.clublexus.com/forums/es-7th-gen-2019-2025/1034482-at-toyota-lexus-kyushu-factory-measuring-panel-gaps-and-alignment-are-critical-for-tak.html).
  - Paint and trim durability:
    - There are scattered reports across Lexus models of paint adhesion/peeling on certain colours (e.g., pearl whites) and sun‑exposed trim peeling on older cars. While LM is newish and typically gets premium paint processes, check for any bubbling, edge peeling on bumpers and cosmetic trims — warranty may cover factory paint faults in many markets. See owner report summaries: [KevinsAutos paint problem notes](https://kevinsautos.com/faq/what-is-the-paint-problem-with-lexus.html) and trim peeling threads ([ClubLexus trim peel](https://www.clublexus.com/forums/ls-4th-gen-2007-2017/1039983-peeling-trim.html)).
  - ADAS and camera/parking system faults:
    - There has been a factory recall/service campaign for a Parking Assist control‑unit software fault that can briefly freeze the Panoramic View Monitor when reversing if reverse is selected shortly after start; Lexus/Toyota recall lookups should be checked by VIN and dealers will have service campaigns if applicable. Coverage of the campaign is reported in recall roundups: [Car‑Recalls.eu record](https://car-recalls.eu/recall/lexus-lm-2024-2025-electronic-injury/) and news summaries ([CarWale](https://www.carwale.com/news/lexus-lm-lx-nx-and-rx-recalled-over-parking-assist-software-issue/), [TorqueNews](https://www.torquenews.com/17992/another-recall-toyota-and-lexus-models-due-faults-parking-assist-control-unit-could-cause)).
  - Practical check on test drive:
    - Verify surround cameras, parking sensors and the panoramic monitor operate reliably through several start/stop cycles — freezing behaviour is the exact symptom reported in the campaigns above.

- Interior
  - Durability under heavy use:
    - The LM is built for VIP/chauffeur duty, but that doesn’t make it indestructible. Leather, contrast stitching, power‑seats, and delicate interior finishes are expensive to repair. If a demo or ex‑fleet LM has been used for rideshare/airport transfers, expect scuffs on seat bolsters, door sills and the lower trim — check for replaced panels and service invoices.
    - Owners and reviewers call the cabin high‑tech and feature‑dense; the complexity increases the odds of software or switch failures over time ([Top Gear interior notes](https://www.topgear.com/car-reviews/lexus/lm/interior), [MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/2024-lexus-lm-first-drive-review)).
  - Entry/egress and ergonomics:
    - Door sill heights and wide sliding/rear doors (market‑dependent seating layouts) make entry/exit generous for adults. Check door check angles and sliding door runners for smoothness and any binding — lubrication and rail service are cheap; rail replacement is not.
  - Electrical behaviours and 12‑V battery drain:
    - With extensive rear‑seat electronics (screens, climate, massage seats, fridges), 12‑V battery drain is a real risk where vehicles sit unused. Soft warnings from Lexus forum experience: ensure cabin batteries have been maintained and check for aftermarket accessories wired into the permanent circuit.
  - Infotainment & connectivity:
    - Lexus infotainment on modern models has occasional freezes, Bluetooth dropouts and wireless charger complaints — common across Lexus lines. Soft resets can clear many frozen screens (for example holding the volume knob 10–20 seconds), but persistent faults need dealer software updates or part replacement. Troubleshooting and reset guides: [OEM GPS infotainment common problems](https://oemgpsnavigation.com/blogs/news/common-lexus-infotainment-system-problems-and-repair-solutions), [WheelsGreed reset how‑to](https://wheelsgreed.com/lexus-infotainment-system-reset/), and navigation fixes guide ([Gearfixes](https://gearfixes.com/lexus-navigation-not-working/)).
  - Screen sizes & hardware versions:
    - Trim‑dependent: rear luxury seats may have separate screens and entertainment modules — check that all rear‑seat systems, Bluetooth pairing, USB‑A/C ports and wireless chargers are present and functioning in the specific car you inspect.

- Mechanically
  - Lexus engineering reputation:
    - Lexus/Toyota mechanicals are conservative and engineered for longevity. Hybrid systems and accessory components benefit from Toyota’s volume and dealer network — this helps parts and service coverage. For long‑term ownership, the brand reputation is a plus ([CarDepreciation.org Lexus summary](https://cardepreciation.org/lexus/)).
  - Security vulnerabilities (Toyota/Lexus family CVE/CAN injection):
    - Modern Toyotas/Lexuses — including models sharing wiring architecture with the LM — were flagged in technical vulnerability research where attackers could physically access wiring (headlight connectors) and inject CAN messages to bypass immobilisers (referenced in CVE‑2023‑29389 and write‑ups). This is not LM‑specific but affects Toyota/Lexus perimeter‑architecture vehicles. Practical mitigation: fit an aftermarket intrusion detection system (immobiliser with CAN bus monitoring), secure headlight wiring, and park in secure locations. Technical analysis: [NVD CVE‑2023‑29389](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2023-29389), industry write‑ups on headlight/CAN injection risks ([Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how_thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/)).
  - Aftermarket anti‑theft (pragmatic):
    - Physical: steering column lock, wheel clamp for long‑term storage.
    - Electronic: intrusion detection with CAN anomaly detection; GPS tracker with immobilisation; hardwired OBD‑port lock; and for extra paranoia, headlight harness shielding/locking.
  - High‑priority workshop checks when buying:
    - Full hybrid system scan (battery state, module codes).
    - ADAS recalibration history (has the car had bodywork or camera recalibration?).
    - Rear‑seat electronics test (all motors, heaters/coolers/heated seats and massage functions).
    - Service history: confirm dealer servicing at recommended hybrid intervals; check for open recalls with Lexus VIN lookup ([support.lexus.com recall tool](https://support.lexus.com/s/topic/0TOWP000000H3Hd4AK/check-for-recalls)).

- Engines
  - Note: below we discuss every single engine/variant that appears in the available research. Where specific internal codes aren’t published in the search snippets, we use the published variant names and drivetrain descriptions.
  - *LM 350h — 2.5‑litre self‑charging hybrid (approx. 2,487 cc, combined ~247–250 PS; e‑CVT or hybrid planetary gearset)*
    - Known failure points & owner notes:
      - Hybrid battery degradation is rare in Lexus, but expensive to repair if out of warranty. Check state‑of‑health and that battery cooling vents are clear.
      - High‑pitched whine under load is a documented user complaint on some Toyota/Lexus hybrid e‑CVTs — often benign but worth checking during accelerate‑from‑low‑speed tests ([ASR Gearbox writeup](https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/8-speed-hybrid-e-cvt-toyota-lexus/)).
      - Shop for any history of coolant bypass valve or thermostat issues in companion petrol engine circuits — these are standard wear items across modern Toyota engines; the tech pack confirms the 2.5‑litre architecture ([Lexus technical pack PDF](https://media.lexus.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/pdf/240206-LM-Tech-Specs.pdf)).
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Service interval: follow Lexus dealer schedule — hybrids typically have 10,000 km or annual service cycles in many markets; check local Lexus Australia servicing schedule and invoices. Manufacturer literature uses standard service intervals (consult Lexus dealer).
      - Hybrid battery and inverter checks at 100,000–150,000 km as a health baseline; watch for unusual exhaust/engine behaviour when battery assistance is active.
  - *LM 500h — higher‑output hybrid with Direct4 torque vectoring and Direct Shift automatic (where offered; combined outputs reported up to ~371 hp)*
    - Known failure points & owner notes:
      - More complex power electronics and Direct4 systems increase component count — more things to diagnose later (more nodes, sensors and actuators).
      - If the LM variant uses a Direct‑Shift gearbox rather than pure e‑CVT, check for typical torque‑converter/shift‑mechatronics wear items under heavy city use.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Dealer servicing at prescribed intervals, with particular attention to transmission fluid condition for Direct‑Shift units and inverter cooling fluid/service where fitted.
  - *First‑generation LM petrol hybrids and naked petrol variants (historical note)*
    - First‑gen LM had non‑hybrid petrol (LM 350) in some markets. The second generation is largely hybrid‑only. If you inspect older first‑gen cars, apply generic Toyota engine checks: timing chain/drive, cam chain tensioner noises and spark/coil condition — confirm engine variant with VIN and invoice. (Background: Wikipedia LM generation notes: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexus_LM)).

- Transmission
  - e‑CVT behaviour (LM 350h):
    - Expected character: ultra‑smooth, seamless power delivery, good fuel economy in town because the hybrid system masks gearbox shifts. Some owners report an unusual engine drone or high‑pitched whine under load (e‑CVT characteristic), which is often normal but should be distinct from grinding/metallic noises. For guidance on symptoms and maintenance, see GearFixes and ASR articles: [GearFixes e‑CVT advice](https://gearfixes.com/lexus-cvt-transmission-problems/), [ASR gearbox write‑up](https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/8-speed-hybrid-e-cvt-toyota-lexus/).
    - Failure modes: fluid contamination, inverter issues or electronic control faults can lead to loss of drive or jerky behaviour; rare but expensive.
  - Direct‑Shift / torque converter automatics (LM 500h or Direct‑Shift variants):
    - Behaviour: sharper acceleration feel, stepped shifts compared to e‑CVT. Failure modes include torque converter shudder, mechatronic faults and fluid leaks — symptoms include slipping, shuddering and unusually high revs under load ([TransmissionTroubleHub summary](https://www.transmissiontroublehub.com/lexus-transmission-problems-facts-and-recalls/)).
  - Practical check:
    - On test drive: no jerks, consistent shift points, no whining that’s clearly gearbox‑derived. Verify service history lists transmission fluid changes if called for by Lexus; inspect for leaks.

- Depreciation metrics & marketplace context:
  - Lexus brand tends to retain value better than many European luxury marques per aggregated studies (CarDepreciation.org cites Lexus retaining roughly ~55% of its value at 5 years on their dataset), but the LM is a niche, low‑volume luxury MPV — its depreciation curve can be atypical because demand is limited and model availability is tight. For brand context: [CarDepreciation.org Lexus overview](https://cardepreciation.org/lexus/).
  - Baseline 3‑year drop: expect standard Lexus retention benefits, but for LM specifically expect steeper early depreciation if the market’s appetite for high‑end MPVs in Australia is muted — alternatives like a used Mercedes‑V or high‑spec Alphard may depreciate differently. Hard numbers for LM are thin due to tiny volumes; use the NSW listing as your market anchor (AUD $159,932 for near‑new 2024, ~2,800 km).
- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy — how to think about it for an LM:
  - Fuel economy baseline:
    - LM 500h (Direct4 higher‑output variants) WLTC combined example: ~7.4 L/100 km (reported for LM 500h Direct4 by spec aggregators) — good for a vehicle of this size given hybrid tech ([Auto‑Data WLTC figures](https://www.auto-data.net/en/lexus-lm-ii-500h-371hp-hybrid-direct4-direct-shift-4-seat-50209)). Manufacturer WLTP figures for LM 350h are available in Lexus brochures: [Lexus Europe LM brochure](https://www.lexus.eu/content/dam/lexus/tme/car-models/my26/lm/Final-LM-MB-MY26-Master.pdf).
  - Calculating fuel costs (worked example):
    - Method: annual km × (L/100 km) × fuel price/L = annual fuel spend.
    - Example scenario (illustrative only): 20,000 km/year × 7.4 L/100 km = 1,480 L/year. At AU$1.80/L fuel => AU$2,664/year. Swap the fuel price to your local pump price to compare.
    - If you’re considering a non‑hybrid variant (older LM 350 petrol or comparable non‑hybrid MPV), expect fuel consumption notably higher in city driving — the hybrid will likely save fuel in urban chauffeur duty.
  - Maintenance & servicing:
    - Lexus dealer servicing is pricier than mainstream brands but cheaper (generally) than German S‑class level servicing. Hybrid frenetic parts (inverter, battery pack) are expensive if out of warranty — confirm transferable warranty or extended cover.
    - Luxury rear‑seat tech (massagers, refrigerated compartments) are expensive to repair — factor repairs into a 5‑year TCO scenario.
  - Insurance, registration & luxury taxes:
    - Factor higher comprehensive insurance premiums (luxury fitouts attract premium claims costs) and possible import/registration fees if the example was an import.
  - Residual value vs paying premium for hybrid:
    - For the LM, hybrid is essentially standard on the second generation. The hybrid premium (if comparing to older petrol LMs or Alphards) will usually be offset by fuel savings in stop/start city/airport runs — especially for high urban mileage. Use an AFDC/energy or KBB style calculator to plug your numbers — referenced tools: [AFDC vehicle cost calculator](https://afdc.energy.gov/calc/), KBB hybrid break‑even article ([KBB](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/)).
- Buying recommendation — should you buy a used Lexus LM in Australia?
  - Short answer:
    - If you need a chauffeur‑grade people‑mover with Lexus build quality, are prepared to pay near‑new money, and you value rear‑seat luxury features more than boot‑space efficiency or SUV road presence — yes, but only with strict caveats. The NSW market shows near‑new units only (AUD $159,932 for a 2024 with ~2,800 km), so you’re buying almost a new car at used prices.
  - Who should buy:
    - Corporate fleets, chauffeur services, and families who want maximum rear‑seat comfort and are comfortable with Lexus dealer servicing. If you run lots of urban kilometres and make use of the hybrid economy and rear‑seat tech, it makes sense.
  - Who should walk away:
    - Buyers who want maximum resale flexibility or off‑road/country capability — consider large SUVs instead. Also avoid if you can’t access Lexus dealer service (electronics and hybrid systems are best looked after by brand techs).
  - Best model year/trim to target:
    - The optimal sweet spot for value will appear when early demonstrator/demo/demo fleet cars hit the market post‑warranty plateau (2–4 years, 30–80k km) — but that market only appears occasionally because of low volumes. If you must buy now and can afford near‑new, the 2024 LM 350h Takumi/4‑seat configuration (highest rear comfort) represents the best experience-to-price balance; if you hunt for an LM 500h Direct4 you pay a significant premium for performance and extra tech that is only worth it if you use and value the Direct4 dynamics.
  - Final practical checklist before you sign:
    - VIN recall/market campaign check with Lexus (support Lexus recall tool): [Lexus recall lookup](https://support.lexus.com/s/topic/0TOWP000000H3Hd4AK/check-for-recalls).
    - Full dealer service history and proof of completion of any Parking Assist/PVM software campaign (recall material cited: [Car‑Recalls.eu](https://car-recalls.eu/recall/lexus-lm-2024-2025-electronic-injury/)).
    - Hybrid battery health test and inverter scan.
    - Comprehensive test of rear‑seat electronics (motors, massage, screens, fridges).
    - Verify security hardening if you park in areas with elevated theft risk (CAN injection awareness: [NVD CVE‑2023‑29389](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2023-29389)).
    - Negotiate based on the scarcity: with only one NSW listing at ~AUD $160k, there’s not much market pressure — use independent mechanical inspection leverage and demand documented software‑update records and warranty transfer.
