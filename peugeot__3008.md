#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: Second‑generation Peugeot 3008 (commonly called Mk2), launched 2016 with refreshes through 2023; the line moved to a new third generation (2023+) built on Stellantis’ STLA Medium platform. Source: [Wikipedia — Peugeot 3008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_3008) and Stellantis model materials for new 3008 generation.
- Internal architecture: The 2016–2023 3008 sits on PSA’s EMP2 modular chassis (Efficient Modular Platform), a transverse‑engine, front‑wheel biased architecture that also accommodates hybrid variants. EMP2 underpins many PSA/Stellantis compact/mid cars, which helps parts availability and independent‑shop knowledge. See [EMP2 platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMP2_platform).
- Product hierarchy (what sits above / below):
  - Above: Peugeot 5008 (7‑seat cousin, larger footprint) and brand premium models in some markets.
  - Below: 2008/4008 small‑crossover entries. The 3008 is Peugeot’s core compact family SUV — the mainstream contender against Japanese/Korean and European mid‑sizers. See comparison context at [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/peugeot/3008/vs).
- Platform‑mates (examples): other EMP2 cars include many Peugeot/Citroën/Opel compact models and earlier versions of the 308 family — helpful when chasing used mechanical parts or cross‑brand spares. (EMP2 reference above.)
- Market‑specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drive:
  - Petrol:
    - **1.2‑litre PureTech turbo (three‑cylinder)** — common small petrol across the range; available with 6‑speed or 8‑speed automatics in various markets.
    - **1.6‑litre THP / petrol turbo (four‑cylinder)** — used in earlier Mk1/Mk2 variants in some markets; known to be punchy but with service caveats (see engines section).
  - Diesel:
    - **1.6 BlueHDi / 2.0 BlueHDi** — popular in Europe; Australian diesel supply is mixed depending on year/trim.
  - Electrified:
    - Mild‑hybrid variants (e‑HDi / mHEV) and plug‑in hybrids were offered later in the Mk2 life; third‑generation 3008 (2023+) introduced full‑electric and refreshed hybrid line‑up on STLA Medium. See Peugeot official pages and specs notes for electrified options: [Peugeot 3008 official (2024/25)](https://qtr.peugeot.com/en/our-models/peugeot-3008/all-new.html).
  - Drivetrains: Mostly FWD for petrol/diesel; AWD only on specific hybrid or earlier HYbrid4 variants.
  - Transmissions: 6‑speed automatics and Aisin‑built EAT6/EAT8 torque‑converter automatics were common; some later mild‑hybrid models use dual‑clutch hardware in other Stellantis cars but the 3008’s main automatics are torque‑converter units. See gearbox notes at [ProPeugeotSpares](https://www.propeugeotspares.co.za/blog/peugeot-eat6-eat8-transmission-guide/).
- Marketplace rivals: Toyota RAV4, Mazda CX‑5, Kia Sportage, Hyundai Tucson, Nissan X‑Trail/Qashqai and a host of European rivals (Audi Q3 in premium comparisons). Comparative context: [Auto Express — rivals](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/best/peugeot-3008-alternatives) and [CarsGuide comparisons](https://www.carsguide.com.au/peugeot/3008/vs).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Peugeot 3008?

- Exterior:
  - Panel fit and paint: Generally good — Peugeot pitched the 3008 as a premium‑styled compact SUV — but owners report intermittent trim and paint vulnerabilities:
    - Pearlescent white paint blistering appears repeatedly in owner threads; some dealers have provided warranty resprays in affected cases. If buying a light‑coloured car, check for tiny blistering patches, and inspect wheel‑arch trims for UV fading. See owner reports: [Peugeot Forums — paint blistering thread](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/3008-pearlescent-white-paint-blistering.358963/).
  - Underbody/subframe recall risk: Recent model recall notices (e.g., 2024 bodywork/subframe corrosion on certain builds) show Peugeot has issued safety campaigns — always check VIN for open recalls before purchase. Example recall listing: [car‑recalls.eu — 2024 bodywork recall](https://car-recalls.eu/recall/peugeot-3008-2024-bodywork-accident/). Use Peugeot’s official recall tools in market for VIN checking.
  - Trim vulnerabilities: Gloss‑black plastics and piano‑black bezels on the dash are prone to visible scratching and fingerprints; expect cleaning/detailing to keep the “premium” illusion.
  - ADAS and sensor issues:
    - Adaptive cruise control and radar/camera faults can be intermittent — owners report "Adaptive cruise control fault" messages appearing and sometimes returning after dealer interventions. Often caused by sensor obstruction, calibration drift after repairs, or software quirks. Community troubleshooting: [Peugeot Forums — ACC fault thread](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/adaptive-cruise-control-fault-with-amber-spanner.384938/).
    - Recall/TSB history shows ADAS recalibration and radar alignment are occasionally needed after panel repairs. If the car has been in a crash, insist on proof of professional radar/camera recalibration. General ADAS troubleshooting guidance: [ADASLine — Distance control deactivated](https://adasline.co.uk/peugeot/distance-control-deactivated).

- Interior:
  - Build and wear: The 3008’s interior is widely praised for “wow” factor and good materials — but cheap elements exist in specific trims (gloss plastics, some hard trim around door pockets). Parkers and Carbuyer note high‑quality soft‑touch surfaces balanced with a few ergonomic compromises: [Parkers — interior](https://www.parkers.co.uk/peugeot/3008/review/interior/) and [Carbuyer interior praise](https://www.carbuyer.co.uk/peugeot/3008/interior).
  - Durability under heavy use (rideshare/taxi): Leatherette and cloth wear reasonably, but expect accelerated scuffing on door sills, seat bolsters (particularly on city cars with lots of ingress/egress) and gloss trim scratching. Check rear seat ISOFIX covers and boot scuffs if the car has been used commercially.
  - Entry/exit: Compact SUV stance — door sills are higher than a hatch but lower than a large SUV. Older owners' threads praise passenger access but note the central console height can feel intrusive for larger adults.
  - Electrical behaviour:
    - Infotainment hardware: Persistent reports of screen failures, frozen displays and the so‑called “ghost touch” on the newer curved 21‑inch display variants (2024–2026). Many instances are software‑fixable (reboot or update), but some require hardware replacement. See detailed write‑ups: [GearFixes — ghost touch](https://gearfixes.com/peugeot-3008-screen-ghost-touch-problem/) and forum reports on frozen screens: [Peugeot Forums — touchscreen TSB threads](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/touchscreen-failure-frozen-unresponsive-tsb.347307/).
    - MicroSD/navigation unit: Some infotainment black‑screen faults trace to a faulty microSD in the navigation module — harmless to the car mechanically but a dealer diagnosis can be tedious. See [CarNews — infotainment faults](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-peugeot/peugeot-3008/infotainment-faults-peugeot-3008-easily-resolved/).
    - 12V battery drain: As with many modern cars, aftermarket accessories, alarm systems or failing modules can cause parasitic drain. No unique epidemic, but check the 12V health during inspection and ask for history of any repeated battery replacements.
  - Connectivity: Apple CarPlay / Android Auto present on most mid‑to‑upper trims, but owners report occasional disconnects or reboots; verify phone pairing and test both platforms during inspection.

- Mechanically:
  - Brand reputation: Mixed reliability across the 3008 life — well‑engineered in parts but with recurring electronic and timing‑train headaches in some engines. Dealer networks in Australia (Peugeot Australia) exist but are thin compared to Japanese brands, so independent specialists knowledgeable in PSA/Peugeot cars are valuable. Autocar and reliability writeups discuss a mixed record: [Autocar — reliability summary](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-review/peugeot/3008-2016-2024/reliability).
  - Security vulnerabilities:
    - Keyless theft and sophisticated attacks: The 3008 has been a theft target in high‑theft‑rate markets (e.g., France) — not because the car is inherently “weak” mechanically but because it's popular and has keyless/start electronics thieves can exploit (relay attacks, mouse‑jamming). Reports on the 3008’s theft rates and electronic methods are covered by Invoxia and broader CAN‑injection research: [Invoxia — why 3008 stolen often](https://www.invoxia.com/blog/gpstrackers/peugeot-3008-one-of-the-most-stolen-vehicle/) and CAN injection technical background: [Ken Tindell CAN Injection](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/).
    - Counters: Fit a physical steering‑wheel lock for peace of mind, upgrade to a dealer‑approved immobiliser if available, fit a proven aftermarket tracker with remote cut‑off and ensure keys are stored in RF‑shielded pouches at home. Consider a garage‑mounted camera or alarm with shock sensors — the cheapest theft risk mitigation is layered: physical + tracking + good key‑habits.
  - Recommended anti‑theft checklist:
    - Keep keys in a Faraday pouch at home.
    - Fit a visible mechanical deterrent (Steering lock).
    - Fit an insurer‑approved GPS tracker (linked to an immobiliser if possible).
    - Ask seller about any history of key replacements or repeated unlocking faults.

- Engines:
  - The following *italicized* subsections cover engine variants highlighted by owners and technical sources. For each, I list known weak points and suggested preventive maintenance with conservative Australian servicing cadences (recommendation: service every 10,000 km or 12 months if you do a lot of short trips; otherwise every 15,000 km at minimum).

  - *1.2‑litre PureTech (three‑cylinder turbo)*  
    - Failure points: Early PureTech variants had timing‑train/timing belt problems (wet timing belt shedding fibres), which can contaminate oil and damage the oil pump; owners have reported premature timing wear and oil consumption. There are documented cases and technical write‑ups mapping these faults. Sources: [MyCarFault — 1.2 PureTech timing issues](https://mycarfault.com/engines/peugeot-1-2-puretech-problems) and [carnews.nl — PureTech belt fibres issue](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-peugeot/peugeot-3008/).
    - Preventive maintenance:
      - Oil change and filter: every 10,000 km (use manufacturer‑specified oils); check oil for belt fibres or metallic debris.
      - Replace timing components at the first major scheduled interval recommended for the specific VIN — treat any unusual timing rattle seriously and do not delay diagnosis.
      - PCV and oil breather inspections if the engine shows smoke or oil consumption.
    - Other notes: If buying a 1.2 PureTech car, ask to see timing‑train invoices and check whether PSA recall/service action items were completed.

  - *1.6 THP (Prince family turbo petrol)*  
    - Failure points: Historical reports of timing‑chain tensioner failures, high‑pressure fuel pump (HPFP) problems and carbon build‑up on direct‑injection intake valves. These can lead to rough idling, start‑up rattle and in worst cases catastrophic engine damage if chain tension is lost. See [CarChecker — 1.6 THP reliability](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/peugeot_3008_mk1_1.6_thp.html) and general owner guides in the aggregated problems pages.
    - Preventive maintenance:
      - Oil and filter at 10,000 km intervals; use correct grade and do not extend oil changes beyond recommended life.
      - Listen for timing rattles at cold start and insist on compression/diagnostic checks if noisy.
      - Intake cleaning/carbon advice: consider walnut‑blasting at higher mileages if misfire/rough running develops.

  - *1.6 BlueHDi / 2.0 BlueHDi (diesel)*  
    - Failure points: Diesel‑specific items such as DPF regens failing, AdBlue system faults and injector problems show up on high‑mileage or motorway‑poor usage cars. Diesel injectors can be costly; DPF replacement or cleaning can be $$$. Source summary: [WhoCanFixMyCar / FixMyCar — diesel issues](https://www.whocanfixmycar.com/advice/peugeot-3008-common-problems).
    - Preventive maintenance:
      - Regular longer runs to allow DPF regeneration; service AdBlue system as per schedule.
      - Injector checks and regular fuel filter replacements (every 20,000–40,000 km depending on fuel quality).

  - *Hybrid / PHEV powerplants (where fitted in later Mk2 and 3rd generation)*  
    - Failure points: Battery degradation, charging circuit faults and high‑voltage inverter/electronics — less common but expensive. PHEV economics depend heavily on consistent home charging. See manufacturer technical pages and range claim summaries: [Auto‑Data PHEV spec](https://www.auto-data.net/en/peugeot-3008-iii-phase-i-2023-1.6-195hp-plug-in-hybrid-e-dcs-52873) and driving/ownership impact at [DrivingElectric](https://www.drivingelectric.com/peugeot/3008/range).
    - Preventive maintenance:
      - Follow high‑voltage system service intervals at authorised workshops; battery health checks and retain charging records.

- Transmission:
  - EAT6 / EAT8 torque‑converter automatics:
    - Mechanical behaviour: generally robust Aisin designs but sensitive to ATF condition and software calibration. Owners report shuddering, slipping, harsh up/down shifts and limp mode events — common root causes are aged/contaminated ATF, clogged valve bodies or failing solenoids. Sources: [ASR Gearbox Repairs — common gearbox problems](https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/peugeot-3008-common-gearbox-problems/) and [CarNews.nl on autos](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-peugeot/peugeot-3008/solving-automatic-transmission-problems-peugeot-3008/).
    - Failure modes:
      - ATF contamination → clutch slipping and poor pressure regulation.
      - Solenoid/valve body failure → harsh shifts and fault codes.
      - Software/TCU calibration sometimes fixed by dealer reflashes.
    - Preventive maintenance:
      - ATF service every 60,000 km is a conservative starting point for torque‑converter units; do not blindly accept “lifetime fluid” claims. A fluid & filter change (and software check) before the 80,000 km mark can prevent many problems.
      - Test‑drive to detect shudder, slipping or abrupt engagements; ask for transmission service history.

  - Dual‑clutch / other boxes:
    - Not the main transmission in most 3008s, but if present in specific trims/markets verify slipping/clutch wear behaviour and get clutch pack adaption history from a dealer.

International used pricing sites corroborate that older 3008s depreciate aggressively compared to mainstream Japanese rivals. See used‑price aggregators and depreciation tools: [CarPriceTracker](https://www.carpricetracker.net/peugeot/3008), [TheMoneyCalculator depreciation tool](https://www.themoneycalculator.com/vehicle-finance/calculators/car-depreciation-by-make-and-model/PEUGEOT/3008/), and depreciation studies noting steep losses: [Zutobi depreciation report](https://zutobi.com/us/driver-guides/motoring-depreciation-uk-and-us-report).
- Depreciation metrics:
  - Expect a larger-than-segment average three‑year drop: historically Peugeot compact SUVs have lost value faster than reliable Japanese rivals (a combination of brand perception, dealer network density and reliability reputation contributes). Use the depreciation calculators above to model specific scenarios for your purchase price.
- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy:
  - TCO elements to include:
    - Depreciation (biggest single cost for a used car in first 3–5 years).
    - Fuel: For a 1.2 PureTech petrol expect real‑world consumption ~6.0–7.0 L/100 km on mixed driving (Motoreu suggests combined WLTP ~5.1 L/100 km but real world higher ~6.6 L/100 km). See [Motoreu — 1.2 PureTech data](https://motoreu.com/peugeot-3008-1.2-puretech-data-202841).
    - Servicing & repairs: Expect higher electric/infotainment fault diagnosis costs and the potential for timing‑train or transmission work if service records are spotty. Authorised dealer servicing is typically more expensive than competent independents for PSA brands.
    - Insurance & registration: Peugeot parts/repairs can attract higher insurance premiums versus mass‑market Japanese rivals.
    - Fuel savings case for hybrids (PHEV): PHEV variants show tiny official L/100 km figures (Auto‑Data lists 0.9 L/100 km for a 1.6 PHEV in test cycles), but real savings require regular charging and short‑trip urban driving to exploit electricity — otherwise the PHEV can be heavier and cost more to repair/replace battery components in the long run. See PHEV specs/range: [Auto‑Data PHEV](https://www.auto-data.net/en/peugeot-3008-iii-phase-i-2023-1.6-195hp-plug-in-hybrid-e-dcs-52873) and practical EV/plug‑in analysis: [DrivingElectric](https://www.drivingelectric.com/peugeot/3008/range).
  - Practical TCO maths (simple worked example):
    - Ownership horizon: 5 years; annual km: 15,000 km.
    - Petrol 1.2 (real‑world 7.0 L/100 km): Fuel cost @ AUD $1.80/L → 15,000 km pa uses 1,050 L → $1,890 pa.
    - PHEV (mixed, if you charge daily): Electricity cost small but assume 40% electric driving and 60% petrol; you’ll need to factor in home‑charger installation and a higher purchase price. If you do mostly highway runs or forget to charge, the fuel advantage evaporates.
  - Conclusion on paying premium for hybrid/PHEV:
    - If you have reliable daily home charging, short urban commutes and a high fuel price environment, paying a premium for a PHEV or mild‑hybrid can be justified. If you do long highway runs or can’t charge routinely, a well‑serviced petrol or diesel will be cheaper and less risky.
- Final buying recommendation — clear verdict:
  - If you want style, a premium cabin and a comfy compact SUV and you can tolerate potentially higher running‑and‑repair costs and a thinner dealer network — a used 3008 is an appealing choice.
  - However: be pragmatic and pick your variant carefully:
    - Avoid early, unproven examples of the **1.2 PureTech** unless you can verify timing‑train work and oil‑change history. Ask for invoices proving timing components or recall/STC campaigns were completed.
    - If you value long‑term low maintenance and resale premiums, consider Japanese rivals (Mazda CX‑5, Toyota RAV4) over a 3008 — they typically depreciate less and have larger dealer/service networks.
  - Best value sweet spot (Australia): A 2018‑model Mk2 with full service history, ideally the mid‑range Allure / GT Line trim (practical kit without paying top‑dollar for the GT premium), with an automatic and a well‑documented transmission and timing service history. The NSW listing you provided (2018, ~89k km, $19,850) is in the ballpark — but negotiate on known service items (timing, ATF) and insist on a pre‑purchase inspection by a Peugeot‑competent workshop.
- Practical inspection checklist before you buy:
  - Full service history and invoices for timing‑train / belt or chain work.
  - Transmission ATF service history (or plan for a fluid/service post‑purchase).
  - Evidence of recall campaigns completed (use VIN with Peugeot recall tool).
  - Test for infotainment glitches and ADAS warnings; verify AV/ACC functionality on the road.
  - Check for paint blistering, underbody corrosion and any evidence of crash repairs (and make sure radar/camera recal is professionally done).
  - Ask seller about key security history — any extra keys made? Have keys ever been cloned or stolen?
  - Scan for stored fault codes (OBD check), particularly on engines and gearbox modules.
  - If buying a diesel, get a DPF/AdBlue history and evidence of motorway usage.
