#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

The fifth-generation Ram 1500, internally designated the **DT** platform, hit showroom floors globally in January 2018 and remains the backbone of Stellantis North America’s half-ton lineup today [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_pickup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_pickup). Inside the Canadian-Australian-American product matrix, the Big Horn trim slots neatly above the base-spec Express and Tradesman utilitarian variants, while sitting beneath the trail-ready Rebel, the opulent Tungsten, and the track-bred TRX. It’s the pragmatic choice: stripped of some plastic trim but loaded with functional towing hardware, upgraded cooling, and proper interior soft-touch materials.

Structurally, the DT rides on a proprietary high-strength steel frame architecture shared across several Stellantis NA platforms. This cross-pollination means bulkhead components, suspension bushings, brake master cylinders, and electrical harnesses often share part numbers with the **Jeep Grand Cherokee WK2**, **Dodge Durango**, and **Chrysler Pacifica**. For grey import buyers in Australia, that parts-commonality reality is a lifeline when OEM Mopar stock runs dry down under.

Stellantis does not officially distribute Ram 1500s through its Australian franchise network, which means every Big Horn SWB on our roads arrives as a parallel import, freight-forwarded, and locally registered. Consequently, Australian specifications vary wildly depending on the original North American window sticker. Enthusiasts chasing power have three primary mill choices: the naturally aspirated **3.6L Pentastar V6**, the iron-block **5.7L HEMI® V8**, or the newer twin-scroll **3.0L Hurricane® Twin-Turbo I6** [https://www.ramtrucks.com/ram-1500.html](https://www.ramtrucks.com/ram-1500.html). Every variant pairs exclusively with an automatic transmission (typically a ZF 8HP-derived torque converter box). Drivetrain layout is fundamentally RWD-based with a full-time 4x4 system offered as a factory option; front lockers are exclusive to the Rebel and TRX trims.

Marketplace rivals in Australia are a completely different breed. You’re pitting a 2.5-tonne American full-size cruiser against compact and midsize local favourites like the **Ford Ranger**, **Toyota Hilux**, **Volkswagen Amarok**, and **Isuzu D-Max**. The Ram doesn’t play by ute sales rules. Where Japanese and Korean trucks optimise for urban manoeuvrability and diesel efficiency, the Big Horn prioritises cabin volume, payload tolerance, long-haul highway composure, and flat-floor towing stability. If your daily involves navigating double-lane parking garages or fitting through narrow Sydney terrace driveways, you’ll curse its footprint before you ever start digging. But for towing a 3.5-tonne caravan across the Nullarbor or hauling timber between Brisbane job sites, it simply doesn’t sweat.

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used RAM Big Horn SWB?

**Exterior**
Panel gap consistency on early DT builds (2019–2020) frequently shows asymmetry along the hood edges and tailgate seams, particularly on units that endured overseas ocean freight without pre-delivery inspection (PDI) adjustment. Gloss black accent trim scratches at arm-rest proximity distances, turning into visible swirl patterns within six months of regular washing. Factory paint durability relies heavily on clear-coat thickness; sun bleaching accelerates rapidly if ceramic coating wasn’t applied post-import. ADAS implementation uses a forward-facing camera and radar stack that frequently triggers false positive braking warnings under heavy Australian dust storms, thick fog, or washed-out road signage. Later firmware patches (mid-2020 onwards) improved algorithm sensitivity, but owners should verify that the latest radio software is flashed before purchase.

**Interior**
Cabin durability tracks remarkably well even under strenuous taxi or tradesperson duty. Rear door sill heights sit approximately 420mm off the ground, demanding a deliberate two-step entry unless you’ve got knee mobility to spare. Door check cables are rated for heavy-duty swing angles, meaning those thick glass doors close with a satisfying thud rather than a feeble sigh. Electrical behaviour tells a more nuanced story: the 12V auxiliary battery commonly drains overnight if the Uconnect head unit fails to enter sleep mode properly. Infotainment hardware scales by trim—you’ll find either a **10.1-inch** or **12.0-inch** vertical touchscreen depending on the model year and region [https://www.ramtrucks.com/ram-1500.html](https://www.ramtrucks.com/ram-1500.html). Wireless Apple CarPlay/Android Auto connectivity holds stable, but the USB-C hub cluster behind the centre console develops intermittent contact faults once the odometer ticks past 60,000 km, manifesting as charging drops or media sync errors.

**Mechanically**
Engineering philosophy leans toward overbuilt strength with moderate complexity. The brand’s core reputation rests on cast-iron block longevity and robust axle designs, but the adaptive air suspension (if equipped on higher regional specs) introduces compressible wear points in the strut bellows and compressor relay boxes. Security vulnerabilities are notable: the passive keyless entry system lacks rolling-code encryption hardening against CAN bus injection exploits. Relays intercept signal loops near the driver’s B-pillar, tricking the vehicle into unlocking and starting without a physical fob nearby. Mitigation requires aftermarket counter-measures: install a physical steering column boot lock (e.g., Abloy FK or Disk Lock), fit a hidden kill-switch integrated into the starter control circuit, and deploy a discreet GPS tracker with geofencing alarms. Digital CAN bus inhibitors alone aren’t enough; layer physical denial-of-entry protocols.

**Engines**
*_3.6L Pentastar V6_*
The workhorse variant. Cast-aluminium block with DOHC architecture. Primary failure vectors include cracked coolant bypass housing threads leading to slow pressure loss, and PCV valve diaphragm rupture causing excessive crankcase pressure. Carbon accumulation on intake valves is minimal thanks to port injection heritage, though direct-injection modifications introduced by third-party tuners accelerate deposits. Recommended Australian service interval: synthetic engine oil and filter every 10,000 km or 12 months (whichever arrives first). Coolant flush at 100,000 km. Inspect serpentine belt tensioner pulley bearing noise above 80,000 km.

*_5.7L HEMI® V8_*
Iron-block, OHV design renowned for high-torque resilience. High-pressure fuel pump (HPFP) cam followers exhibit accelerated wear if diesel fuel or ethanol-blended biofuels are intermittently pumped. The Multi-Displacement System (MDS) lift solenoids fail silently, triggering MIL codes P2646/P2647 and knocking under light-load cruising. Cooling vent filter blockages in the lower radiator cowling trap leaf litter and road grit, restricting airflow and pushing operating temperatures into the red during sustained hill climbs. Mandatory maintenance cadence: oil change every 10,000 km, MOPAR HT-4 synthetic specification only. Timing chain tensioners require ultrasonic clearance measurement at 120,000 km. Verify secondary air injection pump operation if Euro-compliance was retrofitted.

*_3.0L Hurricane® Twin-Turbo I6_*
Stamped-aluminium, double-overhead-cam layout with sequential twin-scroll turbos. Built for high-RPM breathing and torque delivery without V8 vibration penalties. Early production batches exhibited turbocharger wastegate actuator stiction under extreme ambient heat, causing temporary boost faltering. Intercooler piping hose clamps suffer thermal cycling fatigue, resulting in micro-leaks that trigger rich-run enrichment and sooty exhaust tips. Oil cooler gaskets occasionally leak onto the accessory drive, creating burnt rubber odours during initial startup. Strict service discipline required: 10,000 km synthetic change using Pennzoil Platinum Full Synthetic (OEM equivalent), thermostat housing bolt torque verification at 50,000 km, and coolant system pressure testing annually before summer.

**Transmission**
The DT generation pairs exclusively with a hydraulically controlled eight-speed torque converter automatic. Unlike dual-clutch layouts found in European hot hatches, this gearbox uses a traditional wet multi-plate clutch pack and planetary gearset arrangement, prioritising smoothness and towing capacity over shift speed. Under normal conditions, gear progression is seamless. Known failure modes surface between 60,000 and 110,000 km: mechatronic unit valve body sticking causes hunting in stop-start traffic, while delayed engagement occurs when friction plates glaze from aggressive launch techniques or fluid neglect. Shift shock on downshifts (often during lane changes) indicates worn accumulator springs inside the case. Fluid degradation is the silent killer; ignoring the factory-recommended 60,000–80,000 km drain-and-fill cycle guarantees premature governor band wear. Do not mistake this for a continuously variable transmission; it shifts, it locks, and it rewards meticulous maintenance with century-plus kilometre life.

#### 3. Should you buy a used RAM Big Horn SWB?

Live NSW dealer data currently shows one active listing priced at exactly **AUD $130**, median valuation **$130**, year range **2023–2023**, median odometer reading **2,144 km**, and an automatic-only transmission mix. Either you’ve stumbled upon a typographical glitch in the scraper database, a corporate fleet administrative write-off mislabelled as inventory, or a deeply discounted trade-in pipeline leak. Treat that figure as an anomaly and benchmark against realistic grey import valuations, which typically anchor between **AUD $85,000 and $115,000** for late-model Big Horn SWBs, heavily dependent on engine choice, compliance plate status, and service history completeness.

Depreciation curves for the Ram 1500 behave inversely to mainstream Japanese compacts. Because official distribution is absent, the Australian buyer pool self-selects for dedicated enthusiasts, tradies, and towing families. This niche filtering flattens the initial ownership drop: expect roughly **35–40%** cumulative depreciation over the first three years post-import, followed by a longer, slower decline compared to core segment rivals like the Hilux or Ranger, which bleed value faster initially but retain tighter resale liquidity. The Big Horn’s depreciation floor stabilises around year five, provided maintenance records are intact.

Total cost of ownership calculations demand realism. Average blended fuel consumption under Australian driving patterns lands between **14.5 L/100km** (cruising interstate) and **16.2 L/100km** (mixed suburban/hill duty). With premium 95 RON unleaded mandatory for the Hurricane and HEMI variants, weekly fuel costs regularly exceed $250 for average usage. Alternative powertrain premiums—hybrids or EV conversions—fail the mathematical test for this application. Until battery replacement liabilities offset fuel savings, and given that hybrid systems add mass to a already heavy chassis, the conventional ICE Big Horn delivers superior payload retention, simpler repair pathways, and predictable servicing costs. EV swaps introduce thermal management bottlenecks under sustained towing loads and require grid infrastructure upgrades most Australians cannot justify.

Final buying directive: target a **2022–2023 model year Big Horn SWB** equipped with either the **5.7L HEMI V8** or **3.0L Hurricane I6**. These years benefit from resolved Uconnect software mappings, updated transmission calibration files, and revised cooling airflow ducting. Verify compliance plates meet Australian Design Rule (ADR) requirements for emissions, lighting, and impact protection. Demand a pre-purchase inspection focusing on HPFP solenoid resistance readings, ZF fluid condition (colour should be amber, not burnt brown), and underbody frame rail corrosion (common on coastal imports). If the paperwork aligns, maintenance logs track strictly to 10,000 km intervals, and you actually need a truck rather than a lifestyle prop, the Big Horn SWB remains one of the most competent, comfortable, and rewarding workhorses you can legally park in a Australian driveway. Just keep the tow hitch grease gun handy and never trust a gloss-black bumper in a car wash.
