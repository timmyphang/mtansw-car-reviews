#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: Seventh‑generation Lexus ES (commonly sold as the 2018–2025 model run; replaced by the eighth generation for 2026). See the model lineage summary on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexus_ES) and official new‑generation notes on [Lexus.com](https://www.lexus.com/models/ES).
- Internal codes: the ES line on recent models sits on Lexus/Toyota’s global passenger car platform (the ES moved onto the global Toyota platform family used across Camry/Avalon‑class cars), with the 2026 update explicitly described as a “multi‑pathway” platform able to carry ICE, hybrid and BEV drivetrains ([Lexus 2026 ES page](https://www.lexus.com/models/ES); Lexus press release for 2026 lineup [here](https://pressroom.lexus.com/2026-lexus-es-350h-hybrid-joins-battery-electric-es-350e-es-500ein-eighth-generation-es-lineup/)).
- Product ladder: In Lexus’s range the ES sits above the compact IS and well below the flagship LS — it’s the comfortable, quieter executive sedan choice that sits in the mainstream luxury mid‑size slot (compare rivals and segment position at [TheCarConnection](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/lexus_es_choices)).
- Platform sharing / parts availability: the ES benefits from Toyota/Lexus parts commonality across global mid‑size platforms — this helps with parts availability and servicing compared with low‑volume European rivals. The official Lexus marketing for 2026 emphasises the same multi‑pathway platform being used across electrified and ICE variants ([Lexus.com ES overview](https://www.lexus.com/models/ES)).
- Market engines, outputs, transmissions and drive layouts in Australia (what you will encounter in the used market):
  - ES 250 / 2.5‑litre four‑cylinder (≈203 hp / ~151 kW in overseas specs) — offered with AWD on many recent model years and an 8‑speed automatic on some markets. Sources: [Edmunds 2025 ES specs](https://www.edmunds.com/lexus/es/2025/st-402038796/features-specs/), [KBB 2024/2025 specs](https://www.kbb.com/lexus/es/2024/specs/).
  - ES 350 / 3.5‑litre V6 — ≈302 hp, normally FWD and paired with Lexus’s Direct‑Shift 8‑speed automatic on petrol‑only models (see [Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/lexus/es/2025/st-402038796/features-specs/) and [ForCar.org](https://forcar.org/brand/lexus/es/2024/specs/)).
  - ES 300h / ES 350h — 2.5‑litre hybrid system (net combined outputs ~215 hp on older hybrid tuning); the hybrid uses an electronically‑controlled CVT (eCVT) layout and has very different fuel economy and mechanical behaviour compared with petrol models ([Edmunds 2025 specs](https://www.edmunds.com/lexus/es/2025/st-402038796/features-specs/), [FuelEconomy.gov data for ES 300h](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=48184)).
- Primary marketplace rivals: Acura TLX, Volvo S60, Cadillac CT5, Genesis G80 (and for many buyers, BMW 3/5 Series or Mercedes‑Benz C/E depending on spec). See competitor comparisons at [The Car Connection](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/lexus_es_choices) and dealer comparison pages.

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Lexus ES?

Exterior
- Panel gaps & perceived QC: Several owners report widening or inconsistent panel gaps on 2019–2025 ES examples — not a structural failure but a hit to perceived build quality compared with older Lexus models ([ClubLexus discussion](https://www.clublexus.com/forums/es-7th-gen-2019-2025/971128-expanding-panel-gaps.html)).
- Paint and clearcoat: Older ES examples (and many lexus/Toyota models of earlier vintages) show occasional clearcoat peeling or paint top‑edge wear; check door tops, bonnet and roof edges on older cars ([owner reports on paint peeling](https://us.lexusownersclub.com/forums/topic/90797-paint-peeling-from-door-tops/), [ClubLexus clearcoat thread](https://www.clublexus.com/forums/es-1st-to-6th-gen-1990-2018/230515-my-clearcoat-is-peeling.html)).
- Trim vulnerabilities: The usual suspects — gloss black panels and brightwork on the F SPORT trims scratch easily, and painted lower bumper inserts stone‑chip quickly in Australian conditions. Inspect carefully at wheel‑arch and bonnet‑leading edges.
- Recalls & safety campaigns: Always VIN‑check for outstanding recalls via official Toyota/Lexus recall lookup; US/NHTSA resources are available as an example pathway to search recall history — use the manufacturer VIN tool to confirm outstanding campaigns ([Toyota Owners recall lookup](https://www.toyota.com/recall/), NHTSA general recall search [NHTSA recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)). Aggregated recall histories for ES are summarised at [RecallExplained](https://recallexplained.com/lexus/es/).
- ADAS quirks and false activations:
  - Front radar / PCS warnings, code P2583: common on 2019–2024 ES models — a dirty or obstructed emblem/grille can disable AEB / adaptive cruise, and calibration or replacement is sometimes required after front repairs ([Go‑Parts P2583 guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p2583-lexus-es-2019-2024)).
  - Sudden automatic braking & reverse AEB false activations: multiple owner complaints exist about the ES slamming on brakes when reversing or in low‑speed manoeuvres; some cases reported to authorities as incidents ([ClubLexus threads on unexpected braking](https://www.clublexus.com/forums/es-7th-gen-2019-2025/1041354-why-did-my-car-slam-on-it-s-brakes.html), [CarSafetyDB ADAS complaints](https://carsafetydb.com/lexus/es/2023/driver-assist)). These are often software/sensor fusion or calibration problems — verify dealer fixes and ask for ADAS‑calibration history when buying.

Interior
- Infotainment instability: The head unit on many 2023–2024 ESs has had software instability and freezing/reboot complaints. Lexus issued a factory head‑unit software update (Panasonic) covering 2023–2024 models; if buying a 2019–2024 ES get proof the TSB/software update has been applied ([NHTSA TSB for Panasonic head unit update PDF](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2024/MC-10251079-9999.pdf), ClubLexus threads on infotainment freezes).
- Wireless charging & USB: Owners report the wireless pad will intermittently stop charging unless phone is perfectly positioned; sensitivity to cases and heat means some owners prefer wired charging ([ClubLexus wireless charger issues](https://www.clublexus.com/forums/es-7th-gen-2019-2025/1043460-wireless-charger-issues-2.html)).
- Dashboard materials under heat: There is a TSB concerning sticky or cracked dashboards/door panels with prolonged heat/humidity exposure; check for tacky plastics or surface cracking on older cars in hot climates ([NHTSA TSB about sticky dashboards](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2018/MC-10145760-9999.pdf)).
- Practical cabin notes: The ES is designed as an easy‑entry luxury mid‑sized saloon — good rear legroom, conventional door sills and benign door check angles make ingress/egress simple for elderly passengers. However, rideshare/taxi duty will show wear quickly on leather or NuLuxe surfaces and on seat bolsters.

Mechanically
- Brand engineering reputation: Lexus built on Toyota mechanicals historically yields above‑average longevity and parts availability; that does not make the ES immune to electronics and transmission failures as it ages — the biggest complaints by cost and frequency across recent model years are engine/transmission and electrical/hybrid system repairs ([Consumer Reports 2025 reliability summary](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/lexus/es/2025/reliability/), aggregated owner problems at [CrewChief](https://crewchief.cc/wiki/vehicle-problems/lexus-es)).
- Theft/security vulnerabilities: The industry‑wide headlight/CAN‑injection theft technique affects Toyotas and Lexuses; attackers access front‑end wiring, inject CAN frames and bypass immobilisers. This is not a Lexus‑specific quirk but a modern automotive security crisis — review the technical writeups and take precautions ([NVD entry CVE‑2023‑29389 NIST](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2023-29389), Canis Automotive Labs writeup on CAN injection, and public reporting such as [Autoblog on headlight CAN bus theft](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update)).
  - Practical countermeasures: mechanical steering‑wheel lock, wheel clamps, aftermarket immobiliser/relay blockers, interior cabin‑wiring hardening, and parking in secured garages. If you’re in a high‑theft area ask a dealer about any factory security updates and consider an OBD‑port lock and alarm upgrade.

Engines
- Format note: every engine variant below is given its own italicised subsection followed by targeted faults, diagnostics and recommended service cadence.

  *2.5‑litre four‑cylinder (ES 250 / base petrol variants)*
  - Common wear points:
    - Emissions sensors (oxygen sensors) and VVT solenoids can throw faults as kilometres stack up — expect mid‑range diagnostic costs.
    - Fuel/ignition components wear like any modern NA four; no endemic catastrophic failures reported in recent small‑block 2.5s but knock/limitations on high‑mileage cars are possible.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Oil and filter: every 10,000 km (Lexus/Toyota recommended servicing in Australia typically follows a 10,000 km / 12‑month cycle — follow the stamped service schedule).
    - Spark plugs at 100,000 km intervals (inspect earlier if misfires appear).
    - VVT and intake inspection at major services (every 30–60k km).
  - Buyer checklist:
    - Service book with regular 10,000 km oil changes.
    - No persistent misfire codes, stable fuel trims in a basic OBD2 scan.

  *3.5‑litre V6 — bolded engine family **2GR‑FKS** (ES 350)*
  - Known failure points:
    - Timing‑chain‑cover oil leaks (owner threads and technical notes highlight timing cover gasket leakage on **2GR** family variants) — can result in slow oil loss and oil contamination on front accessories ([ClubLexus timing chain cover oil leak thread](https://www.clublexus.com/forums/es-7th-gen-2019-2025/1012410-timing-chain-cover-oil-leak.html), engine family summary at [MotorReviewer](https://www.motorreviewer.com/engine.php?engine_id=129)).
    - General V6 long‑term items: valve cover gaskets, camshaft oil seals and occasional cooling system hoses — expect higher potential repair bills than the hybrid eCVT package.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Oil & filter: 10,000 km service intervals are pragmatic.
    - Watch for external oil leaks from the timing cover area from ~80–150k km onwards.
    - Cooling system inspection at every service (rubber hoses and water pump lifespan vary with climate).
  - Buyer checklist:
    - Request evidence of regular oil changes and any timing cover repairs.
    - Compression and leak‑down check for high‑km examples.

  *2.5‑litre Hybrid system (ES 300h / ES 350h variants)*
  - Hybrid subsystem vulnerabilities:
    - Inverter / power‑converter issues can trigger “Hybrid System Malfunction”; common starter diagnostics often point to 12V battery problems first, but inverter coolant pump failures or corroded connectors are a known cause of expensive repairs ([Go‑Parts hybrid inverter/power converter guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/power-converter-toyota-rav4-lexus-es300h-lexus-nx300h-2012-2025), [EngineCrux ES300h problems overview](https://enginecrux.com/lexus-es300h-problems-facts-and-owner-feedback/)).
    - Hybrid battery pack degradation is possible but Toyota/Lexus chemistry and thermal strategies tend to be conservative; check battery state via dealer diagnostic or independent hybrid specialist.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - 12V battery health: check and replace proactively (especially if car has lived in cold/hot extremes); weak 12V cells commonly mask deeper hybrid faults.
    - Inverter coolant: inspect and service per Lexus schedule (ask the dealer for coolant change points — many Toyota hybrids require inverter coolant inspection and occasional replacement).
    - Hybrid system health scan at purchase — get a hybrid specialist report if vendor can’t show recent hybrid‑system diagnostics.
  - Fuel economy (practical): EPA combined rating for ES 300h ≈ 44 US mpg which converts to roughly 5.3 L/100 km — real‑world Fuelly owner logs track low‑to‑mid 40s mpg in mixed driving ([FuelEconomy.gov ES 300h](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=48184), Fuelly owner data [Fuelly ES300h](https://www.fuelly.com/car/lexus/es300h)).
  - Buyer checklist:
    - Proof of inverter coolant service, recent 12V replacement if older than 4–6 years, and hybrid diagnostic printouts.

Transmission
- Petrol automatics (Direct‑Shift 8‑speed — UA80 family in Toyota/Lexus nomenclature): known symptoms in owner reports and expert writeups include harsh 1–2 shifts, delayed downshifts and torque‑converter shudder in some cars. Often these can be mitigated by software updates or fluid service, but the UA80 family has drawn class‑action attention and at least civil complaints in 2026 ([CarComplaints UA80 transmission article](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2026/toyota-ua80-transmission-problems-lawsuit.shtml), mechanics notes at [Gearfixes](https://gearfixes.com/lexus-transmission-problems/)).
  - What to check:
    - Smoothness during low‑speed crawling and kick‑down; no slipping under acceleration.
    - Service history showing regular transmission fluid changes where recommended.
    - Any transmission TSBs applied — ask for scan/repair records.
- Hybrid eCVT (electro‑mechanical planetary gear set used across Toyota/Lexus hybrids): widely regarded as very reliable in owner forums and tech writeups — many owners call it “nearly bulletproof” vs belt‑type CVTs from other brands ([ClubLexus eCVT thread](https://www.clublexus.com/forums/es-7th-gen-2019-2025/916335-cvt-transmission-reliability.html)). That said, inverter and 12V failures can mimic transmission faults and must be ruled out early.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Lexus ES?

- Year range in market: 2018 – 2025
- Median odometer: 40,844 km

Price context and depreciation
- Expect strong residuals versus European rivals. Published depreciation studies show Lexus ES tends to hold around 55% of MSRP at five years (≈35–45% depreciation in the first five years depending on spec), better than many German marques ([Cardepreciation.org Lexus ES page](https://cardepreciation.org/lexus/es/), [CarEdge depreciation notes](https://caredge.com/lexus/es-350/depreciation)).
- Practical used‑price bands in NSW: low‑end AUD $33k examples will be older 2018–2019 high‑km ES cars or stripped spec units; the median AUD $57.5k aligns with late‑model mid‑spec cars (2020–2023) with circa 40k km.

Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy — how to run the numbers yourself
- Fuel: real world hybrid ES 300h ≈ 44 US mpg → ~5.3 L/100 km (FuelEconomy.gov data) while ES 350 petrol sits around 25 US mpg → ~9.4 L/100 km. See the EPA figures for numerical conversions ([ES 300h EPA data](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=48184), [ES 350 EPA data](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=48181)).
- How to calculate: multiply your annual km by the L/100 km figure to get annual litres, multiply by your local fuel price to get annual fuel spend, add scheduled servicing and anticipated repairs (use Edmunds True Cost to Own as a reference template — see [Edmunds TCO for ES](https://www.edmunds.com/lexus/es/2025/cost-to-own/)).
- Hybrids vs petrol: use KBB’s hybrid break‑even guidance — at typical Australian private‑use annual kms (say 10,000–20,000 km), the ES hybrid’s superior fuel use can pay back a modest premium over 3–5 years, particularly if you keep the car long‑term and avoid high‑cost petrol consumption. KBB hybrid break‑even logic is useful here ([KBB hybrid vs gas article](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/)).
- Servicing & warranty: Lexus dealer network in Australia is broad, but independent specialist workshops with Toyota/Lexus experience will be significantly cheaper for routine checks after warranty. Keep up with 10,000 km oil services and hybrid inverter checks.

Value‑for‑money buying recommendation (clear conclusion)
- If you want quiet, comfortable, low‑stress ownership and fuel economy: buy an ES 300h (hybrid) from 2020–2022 with <80,000 km if you can find one near the NSW median price. Hybrids give best daily cost and the eCVT/inverter is reliable if well maintained; expect real‑world fuel around ~5–6 L/100 km in mixed suburban/hwy driving ([Fuelly owner data](https://www.fuelly.com/car/lexus/es300h), [FuelEconomy.gov](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=48184)).
- If you want V6 smoothness and stronger overtaking performance: a well‑serviced ES 350 is fine but factor in higher long‑term fuel and likely higher repair bills for engine seal leaks later in life. Ask about timing cover history on **2GR‑FKS** engines ([MotorReviewer on 2GR issues](https://www.motorreviewer.com/engine.php?engine_id=129)).
- Avoid / be cautious with:
  - Early 2019–2020 cars that lack documented head‑unit/ADAS software updates (ask for TSB or dealer logs for the head‑unit update — see the [NHTSA TSB PDF for the head unit](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2024/MC-10251079-9999.pdf)).
  - Cheap, high‑km ES350s with patchy service history — transmission and V6 repairs escalate quickly.
- Best financial sweet spot: 2020–2022 ES 300h mid‑spec (Luxury or F Sport Design if you want sharper looks) with dealer‑stamped services, recent 12V replacement (or clear hybrid diagnostics), and evidence that the infotainment/ADAS software TSBs have been applied. Those will sit near the NSW median price and give the best blend of low running costs, retained value, and low‑risk ownership.

Final buying checklist — before you sign
- VIN recall check via Toyota/Lexus owner recall tool ([Toyota Owners recall lookup](https://www.toyota.com/recall/)).
- Proof of regular 10,000 km oil services and stamped dealer/independent service history.
- If hybrid: inverter coolant inspection history and hybrid system scan (no pending DTCs).
- Ensure head‑unit software update (TSB) applied for 2023–2024 units (ask for dealer printout) — see the [NHTSA TSB](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2024/MC-10251079-9999.pdf).
- ADAS check: drive on open road and test AEB/adaptive cruise behaviour; check for dash radar‑fault warnings and ask if radar camera recalibration has been done after any front‑end work ([Go‑Parts radar fault guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p2583-lexus-es-2019-2024)).
- Security hardening: if parked on street/garage in a high‑risk suburb, plan for an aftermarket immobiliser / wheel clamp or OBD lock given ongoing CAN‑attack reports.

If you want Lexus comfort with the lowest long‑term running cost and the least drama, the ES 300h from the 2020–2022 window is the practical, Australian‑market sweet spot. You won’t get the sporting badge thrills of European rivals, but you will get a serene cabin, dependable Toyota‑family engineering, and a better‑than‑average resale curve — and that’s why plenty of buyers keep choosing the ES.
