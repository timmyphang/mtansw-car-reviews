#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: second‑generation Subaru BRZ (informally "BRZ II"), produced 2022–present (current MY 2025/2026 updates continue the same generation). This is the small, rear‑wheel‑drive coupe revival of the BRZ recipe: light, low centre‑of‑gravity, driver‑focused.
  - For background specs and trim lists see Subaru’s official BRZ pages: [Subaru BRZ 2025 overview](https://www.subaru.com/vehicles/brz/2025.html) and [2025 specs & trims](https://www.subaru.com/vehicles/brz/2025/specs-trim.html).
- Product hierarchy: the BRZ sits below Subaru’s performance halo models (think WRX/STI historically) — it’s the affordable two‑door sports car in the Subaru stable, optimised for handling rather than outright power.
- Shared platform architecture and parts commonality:
  - Co‑developed with Toyota — platform and much of the drivetrain shared with the Toyota GR86 (the obvious parts donor for suspension, body panels and many mechanical bits). See comparative commentary on platform twins: [SlashGear alternatives (BRZ/GR86 relationship)](https://www.slashgear.com/1841056/subaru-brz-alternatives/) and platform specs: [subaru-specs.com BRZ 2022–present drivetrain](https://subaru-specs.com/brz/2022-present/drivetrain/specs/).
  - That commonality improves parts availability and aftermarket support — you’re not hunting special‑order sheetmetal for years.
- Market‑specific powertrains, outputs and drive layouts:
  - Engine: **2.4‑litre SUBARU BOXER (FA24)**; official US spec lists 228 hp and 184 lb‑ft (≈ 170 kW / 249 Nm) for modern BRZs — fits the 2022+ BRZ line-up with manual or automatic options. See Edmunds for the FACTS: [2025 BRZ specs (Edmunds)](https://www.edmunds.com/subaru/brz/2025/features-specs/).
  - Transmissions: 6‑speed manual (driver favourite) or 6‑speed torque‑converter automatic (available on some trims). Subaru confirms the automatic is conventional (not a CVT) in model documentation: [Subaru consumer PDF & model page](https://www.subaru.com/content/dam/subaru/downloads/pdf/consumer-reports/2025/2025_BRZ_Consumer_Reports_v2.pdf).
  - Drive: Rear‑wheel drive with limited‑slip differential standard on modern trims.
- Explicit marketplace rivals (what Aussies compare it to):
  - Toyota GR86 (platform twin) — primary rival.
  - Mazda MX‑5 (lightweight roadster alternative).
  - Nissan Z (step up in power but still in sports coupe shopping lists).
  - For a wider shopping list see [The Car Connection BRZ competitors](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/subaru_brz_choices) and SlashGear’s alternatives list above.

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Subaru BRZ?
- Exterior:
  - Panel alignment & fit:
    - Some owners reported interior/exterior trim gap issues (dealer TSBs address specific fit problems) — there is an official bulletin that corrected excessive gaps around the centre lower panel for earlier 2022 models: [TSB — lower centre panel redesign (dot.report)](https://dot.report/bulletins/11000815).
  - Paint & clearcoat:
    - Clearcoat peeling is a recurring owner complaint going back years across FT‑86 family cars (BRZ/86/FR‑S). Forum threads and DIY videos document clearcoat lifting and peeling with owners repairing or repainting affected panels: community reporting and DIY coverage here: [FT86Club clearcoat thread](https://www.ft86club.com/forums/showthread.php?t=45020) and an owner video guide: [HandyGinger clearcoat fix](https://www.handyginger.com/videos/subaru-brz-clear-coat-peeling-heres-an-easy-diy-fix).
    - Piano‑black trim around the shifter/console is easily scratched — the usual cosmetic wear and tear on glossy plastics is common and replacement pieces exist: [shifter trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-subaru-brz-toyota-gr86-2022-2025).
  - ADAS & safety systems on later cars:
    - Recent model years (2022–2025) that include Subaru’s EyeSight driver‑assist suite are the subject of owner complaints and a May 2026 class action alleging phantom emergency braking, missed hazard detection and errant lane‑keeping behaviour — these are software/ calibration claims rather than mechanical faults. Coverage of the filing: [CarComplaints EyeSight lawsuit](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2026/subaru-eyesight-lawsuit.shtml) and summary reporting [Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/news/subaru-eyesight-lawsuit-says-cars-can-slam-on-the-brakes-for-no-reason/).
    - NHTSA listing for the 2025 BRZ showed zero recalls/complaints at the time of the listing, but the class action indicates ongoing concern; always VIN‑check for service actions: Subaru vehicle recalls page [Subaru recalls](https://www.subaru.com/recalls.html) and NHTSA [BRZ vehicle page](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2025/Subaru/BRZ/).
- Interior:
  - Durability under hard use (rideshare/taxi duty):
    - The BRZ is built as a weekend sports car; thin door sills, low roofline and sporty low seating make it a poor candidate for constant ingress/egress or heavy cargo duty. Expect scuffed door sills, worn bolsters and scratched glossy trim in high‑use cars.
    - B‑pillar trim and other interior clips have been reported to rattle in 2022–2025 cars — common fix is clip replacement or re‑positioning: [B‑pillar trim rattles (Go‑Parts)](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/body-b-pillar-trim-panel-toyota-gr86-subaru-brz-2022-2025).
  - Infotainment & electronics:
    - The 8‑inch Denso CP1 head unit used in 2022–2025 BRZs has documented freezing/black screens and reboot faults. Subaru issued a reprogramming TSB (15‑297‑22R) to address firmware bugs; dealers can reflash the unit but owners still report intermittent problems and occasional unit replacement: [Infotainment freezing guide (Go‑Parts)](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-subaru-brz-toyota-gr86-2022-2025), plus the TSB doc reference: [TSB 15‑297‑22R listing](https://www.tsbsearch.com/Subaru/15-297-22R).
    - Starlink system failures (earlier Subaru infotainment litigation & class action) show this problem isn’t unique and may affect warranty servicing: reporting on class action settlement references: [Starlink lawsuit settlement overview](https://thenationaltriallawyers.org/article/6-25m-settlement-reached-in-subaru-starlink-lawsuit/).
    - Wireless charging pads and phone connectivity have the usual quirks — check functionality; troubleshooting guides exist: [Wireless charging fixes (Auto Insider)](https://theautoinsiderblog.com/subaru-wireless-charger-not-working/).
  - Electrical drains:
    - No large‑scale, model‑unique 12V parasitic‑drain epidemic documented in the public datasets we searched, but the infotainment/head unit bugs can cause battery drain if stuck in boot loops. If you see a dead 12V battery on a low‑mileage BRZ, suspect the head unit or accessory modules and insist on a full diagnostic before purchase.
- Mechanically:
  - Brand engineering reputation:
    - Subaru builds durable mechanicals but the BRZ’s boxer engine layout and low oil‑sump geometry places unusual demands on oil control in high‑G driving. Community and media coverage has flagged specific engine reliability concerns (see engines below).
  - Security vulnerabilities:
    - Modern BRZ/GR86 models share CAN bus architectures with plenty of exposed signals. Industry reports on “CAN injection” thefts (where criminals access headlight/taillight wiring and inject messages to spoof the key‑fob ECU) apply to many modern cars and are relevant to the FT‑86 platform. Recommendations:
      - Physical: fit a steering‑wheel lock and consider mechanical wheel/gearbox locks if you park streetside.
      - Digital/hardware: have a dealership or trusted workshop fit a verified aftermarket immobiliser/secondary ECU lock, and consider blocking access to headlight wiring with anti‑tamper clips or alarm systems that monitor CAN‑bus anomalies.
    - Read up on the technique and real examples here: [Dr Ken Tindell CAN injection explainer](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/) and mainstream coverage: [The Register — CAN bus hacks](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how_thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/).
- Engines:
  - Note: every engine subsection below is italicised and focused on failure points and recommended servicing.
  - *FA24 — 2.4L SUBARU BOXER (2022+ BRZ, primary modern engine)*
    - Known failure points:
      - Oil‑pressure / oil‑starvation concerns have been raised in multiple owner reports and media write‑ups. Symptoms include dropping oil pressure under sustained cornering or high–RPM use, and in extreme cases catastrophic bearing damage. Community and industry coverage: [Hagerty FA24 failures overview](https://www.hagerty.com/media/news/fa24-engine-failures-dont-daunt-the-brz-and-gr86-faithful/) and [The Drive reporting on class actions](https://www.thedrive.com/news/theres-now-a-class-action-lawsuit-over-toyota-gr86-subaru-brz-engine-failures).
      - Factory RTV sealant over‑application alleged to clog oil pickup leading to oil starvation is part of litigation reporting: [Law firm summary on RTV sealant claims](https://sauderschelkopf.com/automotive/subaru-excessive-rtv-sealant-and-engine-failure-class-action-investigation/). The community has responded with inspection and sump modifications.
    - Preventative maintenance & owner actions (pragmatic checklist you can do in Australia):
      - Oil: use high‑quality synthetic oil, change at least every 10,000 km or 6–12 months (lean to the shorter side if you’re doing spirited driving). Community experts recommend more frequent oil changes (5,000–7,500 km) if track use or hard cornering is in the plan — see community reliability guides: [SubiMods FA24 reliability guide](https://subimods.com/blogs/subimodsblog/ultimate-fa24-reliability-guide-subaru-owners).
      - Inspection: drop the oil pan and inspect pickup screen for RTV/contaminant build‑up if you buy a low‑mileage used car; many tuners recommend oil‑sump baffling or upgraded oil pickup solutions for hard‑use cars — see Crawford Performance mitigation notes: [BRZ oil‑starvation article](https://crawfordperformance.com/blogs/news/subaru-brz-oil-starvation-causes-prevention).
      - Watch‑list: unexplained oil‑pressure drops, engine knocking, or odd metal particles in oil changes — these are red flags.
  - *FA20 — 2.0L BOXER (older first‑generation BRZ / FR‑S / 86 engines)*
    - Known failure points:
      - Community history includes connecting‑rod/rod‑bearing weaknesses and valve spring issues when engines are highly modified. Thorough search of owner forums shows FA20 owners reporting catastrophic failures in abused cars: [FT86Club threads on FA20 failure](https://www.ft86club.com/forums/showthread.php?t=154054).
      - Direct‑injection carbon build‑up is a known risk in FB/FA family engines in some duty cycles.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Oil service every 10,000 km at minimum, consider 7,500 km intervals for spirited driving; if the car has been modded/tuned, shorter intervals (5,000–7,500 km) are prudent.
      - Engine health checks (oil analysis, borescope where warranted) on cars with unknown history.
- Transmission:
  - Automatic (6‑speed torque converter):
    - Common failure modes and symptoms: slipping, harsh or delayed shifts, shuddering and torque‑converter lock‑up issues. Subaru issued a torque‑converter-related TSB for affected BRZ years (TSB 16‑136‑22R), acknowledging symptoms and offering countermeasures: [TSB listing (AboutAutomobile)](https://www.aboutautomobile.com/Technical-Service-Bulletin/2022/Subaru/BRZ/Torque-Converter).
    - Community reports and diagnostic threads show many issues trace back to hydraulic circuits, solenoids or the torque converter lock‑up clutch. Diagnostic scanning for transmission codes (P0700 family etc.) and a pressure‑test at a specialist is recommended before purchase: see forum/diagnostic writeups: [P0700 guide (Go‑Parts)](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p0700-subaru-brz-2013-2020-fa20-2-0l) and [transmission troubleshooting summaries](https://www.transmissiontroublehub.com/subaru-brz-automatic-transmission-problems-and-solutions/).
  - Manual (6‑speed):
    - Generally robust and the preferred option for most BRZ buyers. Clutch wear is normal — inspect clutch slip and pedal feel — but no widespread catastrophic gearbox failures reported in manuals for service‑life cars.
  - Maintenance tips:
    - For automatics, insist on a transmission service history; if no record, budget for a fluid and filter change and full scan. A specialist pressure test will reveal early internal issues.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Subaru BRZ?

  - That listing looks like a near‑new dealer car (38 km). Use this as your local market anchor for NSW buyers.
- Current used marketplace ranges and depreciation context:
  - National used market data shows wide pricing spread (older BRZs commonly sell from under $10k for high‑mileage project cars up to $40k+ for recent low‑km examples in the US market references). Websites consolidating listings show average used BRZ prices around the mid‑$20k mark in North American aggregations; Australian pricing will skew differently depending on import, condition and trim: see KBB, CarGurus and TrueCar used listings for context: [CarGurus used average](https://www.cargurus.com/Cars/l-Used-Subaru-BRZ-d2134) and [KBB used BRZ listings](https://www.kbb.com/cars-for-sale/used/subaru/brz).
  - Depreciation metrics:
    - iSeeCars analysis suggests the BRZ historically depreciates faster than the Toyota GR86 (example 5‑year loss: BRZ ~30.3% vs GR86 ~24.9% in their dataset), which means the Toyota twin often retains value better: [iSeeCars BRZ vs GR86 comparison](https://www.iseecars.com/compare/subaru-brz-vs-toyota-gr86).
- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy:
  - Use official cost calculators as a starting point: KBB’s cost‑to‑own for recent models shows a 5‑year total ownership figure in the tens of thousands (KBB TCO snapshots for 2025/2026 BRZ): [2025 KBB cost to own summary](https://www.kbb.com/subaru/brz/2025/cost-to-own/) and [2026 pricing summary from Subaru showing MSRP start](https://www.subaru.com/vehicles/brz/2026.html).
  - Fuels and running costs:
    - Real‑world owner fuel data is best judged via owner logs — Fuelly aggregates user data across BRZ owners: [Fuelly BRZ real‑world MPG data](https://www.fuelly.com/car/subaru/brz/all). Translate reported MPG into L/100km for Australian use when calculating weekly fuel spend (a rough expectation for modern 2.4L BRZs in mixed driving is mid‑to‑high single‑digit L/100km to low double‑digit under heavy throttle — verify with local test drives and Fuelly averages).
  - Maintenance & repair risk:
    - Add a risk premium in your TCO if buying a 2022–2025 BRZ, especially one with the FA24, to cover the possibility of higher‑cost engine repairs or gearbox TSB repairs. KBB/Edmunds TCOs include average repair cost assumptions; treat them as baseline and add 10–15% to be conservative if the car lacks full service history.
  - Hybrid/EV alternative verdict:
    - Not relevant within the BRZ segment — BRZ is an ICE, rear‑drive sports coupe; paying more for a hybrid or EV within a different segment (e.g., getting a hot hatch hybrid) changes the ownership math completely. For short, low‑km urban commuters a hybrid might save fuel dollars; for drivers who value steering feedback, balance and engagement, the BRZ’s ownership is about enjoyment, not lowest cost‑per‑km. See KBB’s hybrid vs gas break‑even guidance for methodology: [KBB hybrid vs gas break‑even](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/).
- Financial conclusion and recommendation:
  - Given the NSW listing at AUD $49,990 for a 2025 low‑km automatic, be very picky. That price is essentially new‑car territory in Australia for higher trims (it aligns with a well‑equipped Limited or special tS example). Cross‑check local dealer ARP and the Subaru MSRP in Australia — the US MSRP starting point is shown here for context: [Subaru 2026 BRZ pricing page](https://www.subaru.com/vehicles/brz/2026.html).
  - If you want the purest value:
    - Buy a good‑condition manual from the 2022–2023 run if you can find one under AUD $35k–$40k in Australia. That’s where you likely get best value per dollar — manual boxes hold fewer expensive repair precedents and are preferred by purists.
    - If you must have a 2025 dealer unit, and the $49,990 listing is your local price, negotiate hard. That price is fair if it’s a Limited or tS with dealer options — but not if the car is a base Premium with an automatic.
  - Risk management checklist before purchase:
    - VIN‑check for recalls and TSBs: [Subaru recall lookup](https://www.subaru.com/recalls.html) and NHTSA BRZ page [NHTSA 2025 BRZ](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2025/Subaru/BRZ/).
    - Insist on full service history (10,000 km service intervals locally is common for spirited cars — if the vendor can’t show regular oil/engine checks, price accordingly).
    - For FA24 cars: budget for an inspection that checks for oil‑pickup/RTV contamination if the vehicle has a history of hard driving; if buying new/near‑new from dealer, confirm dealer has applied any goodwill or fix programs that may exist in your market.
    - For automatics: scan the TCM for stored transmission codes and ask about any torque‑converter countermeasures performed (reference torque converter TSB): [TSB torque converter (AboutAutomobile)](https://www.aboutautomobile.com/Technical-Service-Bulletin/2022/Subaru/BRZ/Torque-Converter).
- Bottom line buy/no‑buy:
  - Buy if:
    - You crave an affordable, well‑balanced rear‑wheel‑drive sports coupe, drive it sensibly (or are prepared for the extra maintenance if you track it), and you can find a low‑km manual or well‑documented automatic at a sensible price.
  - Hesitate if:
    - The asking price sits near new‑car money for a 2025 automatic (that NSW listing at AUD $49,990). If warranty coverage, dealer history and a clear record of software/TSB fixes (infotainment reflash, torque‑converter countermeasure if required) aren’t provided, walk.
  - Best value sweet spot:
    - A well‑serviced 2022–2023 manual BRZ with 20k–60k km will likely be the best combination of price, joy and lower exposure to the FA24/TSB noise — you get the chassis and the simplicity of a manual gearbox without paying the new‑car premium. Use national listing aggregators and local dealers (and the NSW MTA snapshot) to set realistic offer targets: check KBB/CarGurus/Cargurus for ranges ([CarGurus](https://www.cargurus.com/Cars/l-Used-Subaru-BRZ-d2134), [KBB used BRZ](https://www.kbb.com/cars-for-sale/used/subaru/brz)).

Final quick advice for the driveway
- If you want sideways grins and a car that’s cheap to own if treated like a weekend toy, a BRZ is a genuine joy. Buy one with full service history, manual if you can, and don’t get suckered into paying new‑car money for an automatic unless the spec justifies it.
- If you’re a daily hyper‑commuter or need a maintenance‑forgiving, high‑mileage workhorse — get a different car. The BRZ is brilliant at what it is, and expensive if you treat it like a wrench and track toy without doing the preventative checks outlined above.
