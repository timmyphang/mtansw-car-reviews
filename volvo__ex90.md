#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: First-generation Volvo EX90 (SPA2 debut platform), production: launched May 2024 — current model years in Australia are 2024–2025 (global launch late‑2022; production ramp mid‑2024). See Volvo’s EX90 product pages and media specs for timelines and data: [Volvo Cars EX90 specs](https://www.volvocars.com/us/cars/ex90-electric/specifications/) and Wikipedia’s platform note on SPA2 ([SPA2 platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalable_Product_Architecture_platform)).
- Internal codes / architecture: Built on Volvo’s EV‑dedicated Scalable Product Architecture 2 (SPA2) — the same skateboard EV architecture introduced on the EX90 and shared with Polestar 3. SPA2 underpins the skateboard battery layout and dual‑motor configurations ([SPA2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalable_Product_Architecture_platform)).
- Product hierarchy: The EX90 is Volvo’s flagship full‑size BEV SUV — it sits above the XC90 (now being phased out in ICE form) in terms of size, tech and price. It’s Volvo’s three‑row luxury EV offering; smaller BEV siblings include the EX30/EX40 in the future line‑up.
- Platform siblings / parts flow: The EX90 shares its EV underpinnings with the Polestar 3 (helpful for parts availability and technical commonality) and follows the same EV skateboard approach used across Volvo/Polestar EVs ([Volvo EX90 specs](https://www.volvocars.com/us/cars/ex90-electric/specifications/), [SPA2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalable_Product_Architecture_platform)).
- Market-specific powertrains and drivetrains (Australia):
  - Twin‑motor AWD (standard on Australian market EX90s listed in global spec pages): dual electric motors (front + rear), single‑speed reduction gears per axle. Battery: ~111 kWh gross (~107 kWh usable on many trims) — check local spec confirmation when buying ([Volvo EX90 specs](https://www.volvocars.com/us/cars/ex90-electric/specifications/)).
  - Output examples: Twin‑motor variants quoted internationally around ~330–449 kW/hp equivalent and torque figures ~670 Nm depending on tune and trim (see aggregated technical specs) ([Auto‑Data EX90 106 kWh 449hp](https://www.auto-data.net/en/volvo-ex90-106-kwh-449hp-twin-motor-awd-55419), [Edmunds trims](https://www.edmunds.com/volvo/ex90/2025/features-specs/)).
  - Range / energy: Volvo publishes the battery and charging architecture — real world range depends heavily on trim, wheel size and driving conditions (Volvo specs list the battery and charging figures; US specs are useful baseline) ([Volvo EX90 specs](https://www.volvocars.com/us/cars/ex90-electric/specifications/)).
- Explicit marketplace rivals (what buyers in Australia will cross‑shop):
  - Mercedes‑Benz EQS SUV, BMW iX, Audi Q8 e‑tron, Rivian R1S, Tesla Model X, Lucid Gravity (where available), and three‑row EVs like the Kia EV9 and VW ID.Buzz for family buyers who value space over outright luxury ([The Car Connection comparison](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/volvo_ex90_choices), [Edmunds three‑row comparison](https://www.edmunds.com/car-news/electric-suv-3-row-comparison-volvo-ex90-volkswagen-idbuzz-kia-ev9.html)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Volvo EX90?

Exterior:
- Panel alignment & fitment: Early owner threads and forums report trim rattles, inconsistent panel gaps and occasional fit‑and‑finish niggles typical of a complex first‑run flagship (owner forum reports and complaint aggregators). Expect to inspect door gaps, bonnet alignment and tailgate operation closely on any used example ([Volvo EX90 Forum](https://volvoex90forum.com/), [CarComplaints body/paint](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Volvo/EX90/body_paint/index.shtml)).
- Paint & trim vulnerabilities: Several owners have flagged paint chips and scratching on gloss black trim. Large black plastic trims and gloss black grille/touchpoints are prone to visible scratching — typical for low‑clearance entry points and door sills.
- Power tailgate & recall history:
  - There’s an active recall campaign for the EX90 POT (power‑operated tailgate) where a spindle nut in the drive unit can detach and cause the tailgate to separate or slam unexpectedly; this has been documented in a formal NHTSA recall report for certain 2025 EX90s ([NHTSA recall PDF RCLRPT-25V654-9211](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2025/RCLRPT-25V654-9211.pdf)) and summarised in automotive press.
  - Check the VIN for open recalls on delivery — Volvo’s recall portal is the official route ([Volvo recall portal](https://www.volvocars.com/us/l/recall/)).
- ADAS hardware sensitivity and LiDAR controversy:
  - Early EX90s use a roof‑mounted LiDAR module and a complex sensor suite. Reports show the LiDAR required frequent recalibration, could disable driver‑assist features when faulty, and in some viral clips was implicated in camera sensor concerns. Volvo has publicly reworked its LiDAR approach and future EX90s are expected to drop the roof bump due to supplier and implementation challenges ([au7o.io known issues](https://au7o.io/known-issues/volvo-ex90), [CarBuzz on dropping LiDAR](https://carbuzz.com/volvo-cuts-lidar-ex90), [Carscoops LiDAR issue summary](https://www.carscoops.com/2025/10/the-volvo-ex90s-latest-problem-might-literally-drop-on-you/)).
  - Practical check: verify ADAS calibration status, ask for dealer service records showing any ADAS recalibration, and test all lane‑keeping / adaptive cruise functions on public roads at low speeds during a test drive.

Interior:
- Build & materials: The EX90 interior is airy and scandi‑minimal, but critics call out the prevalence of touchscreen controls and some artificial leather or trim areas that feel less luxurious than the price suggests. The 14.5‑inch central touchscreen dominates controls and is the single human–machine interface for most functions — a one‑point failure risk if it misbehaves ([Parkers EX90 interior notes](https://www.parkers.co.uk/volvo/ex90/review/interior/)).
- Infotainment reliability: Central computer problems are the headline risk for used buyers.
  - Reports indicate pervasive Android Automotive software bugs, freezes, reboots and in at least one high‑profile account Volvo elected to replace the EX90’s central computer across many cars, suggesting fixes beyond simple OTA patches ([InsideEVs central computer replacement coverage](https://insideevs.com/news/773202/volvo-ex90-software-issues/), [go‑parts infotainment module notes](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/radio-audio-volvo-ex90-polestar-3-2025-2026)).
  - Owner forum and Recharged pieces list infrequent but annoying interruptions: wireless phone‑charger hiccups (support doc), screen freezes and dependency on the central unit for climate and seat controls ([Volvo wireless charger troubleshooting](https://www.volvocars.com/en-ca/support/topic/af247a8070a8c26ec0a8015106a8fe01/), [Recharged common problems](https://recharged.com/articles/volvo-ex90-common-problems-and-fixes/)).
- Electrical / 12V battery behaviors:
  - Owners reported parasitic 12V drains and odd wake/reboot sequences tied to the infotainment/central computer; because the central unit controls many subsystems, its instability can amplify 12V drain symptoms. Check battery health and that the original central‑computer software update/delivery actions have been applied ([Delivery Action D10294 TSB](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2024/MC-11011746-0001.pdf), [Recharged reliability roundup](https://recharged.com/articles/2025-volvo-ex90-problems/)).
- Practical checks for used buyers:
  - Boot/tailgate open/close cycles (listen for odd noises).
  - Reboot the center screen; verify climate, heated seats, camera views, and voice commands all work.
  - Ask for service log showing software updates and VIN recall clearances.

Mechanically:
- General engineering reputation: Volvo’s hardware pedigree and electrification partners are solid — the main issues on EX90 so far are software and supplier‑level components (tailgate drive, LiDAR module), not fundamental drivetrain failures; Recharged and owner forums characterise most teething problems as software heavy ([Recharged long‑term review](https://recharged.com/articles/volvo-ex90-long-term-review-2026/)).
- High‑priority security vulnerabilities:
  - The broader industry has seen ‘CAN injection’ thefts using exposed wiring around lights/headlamps to spoof key presence. While not unique to Volvo, the EX90’s complexity means you should lock down physical access points and adopt layered protection ([CAN injection background](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/), [Autoblog summary of headlight CAN thefts](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update)).
  - Volvo’s official anti‑theft guidance covers immobiliser and alarm systems — still, mechanical deterrents are recommended ([Volvo EX90 Anti‑theft support](https://www.volvocars.com/us/support/car/ex90/article/47d2c97fd33effd3c0a8cc3718c999b7-aceccee3d527682bc0a8ac330d5efc54-8664b2fa77a7e089c0a8296870d1a409/)).
- Recommended aftermarket counter‑measures:
  - Wheel lock or club, steering‑wheel lock for street parking, wheel‑bay camera or motion sensor, and use of a Faraday pouch for the keyfob at home to prevent relay attacks.
  - For garages or long‑term parking, consider an ECU/charging port lock or aftermarket immobiliser if available.

Engines (powertrain variants) — every single variant discussed in the research (EV motors/battery):
- *Twin Motor AWD — ~111 kWh battery / dual motors (front + rear)*:
  - Typical spec: ~106–111 kWh gross battery capacity (many spec pages quote 111 kWh gross / ~107 kWh usable) and offers Twin‑Motor AWD layouts across main trims; published power varies by trim (Edmunds, Auto‑Data) ([Volvo EX90 specs](https://www.volvocars.com/us/cars/ex90-electric/specifications/), [Edmunds specs](https://www.edmunds.com/volvo/ex90/2025/features-specs/), [Auto‑Data 106 kWh 449hp](https://www.auto-data.net/en/volvo-ex90-106-kwh-449hp-twin-motor-awd-55419)).
  - Failure points reported or plausible:
    - Battery charge/discharge anomalies — early owner reports of charging irregularities and BMS quirks; software updates often targeted charging‑related behaviours ([Recharged common problems](https://recharged.com/articles/volvo-ex90-common-problems-and-fixes/)).
    - Thermal management / HVAC loop: watch for heater‑core / coolant loop warnings in dash logs — EVs with large batteries rely on robust cooling; verify service history and that thermal system recalls/updates have been applied.
    - High‑voltage connectors and software interplay — many complaints are software driven rather than raw hardware failure, but a faulty HV connector would be expensive and slow to repair.
  - Preventative maintenance & intervals:
    - EVs have fewer moving parts; follow Volvo dealer service schedule for HV system checks and 12V battery inspection. For Australian buyers, perform a full dealer pre‑purchase check and insist on proof of software updates. As a practical rule, book an EV health check every 10,000 km (battery condition, coolant, 12V system) — not because Volvo mandates 10k specifically here, but because early software/hardware issues have been fixed at dealer service points and frequent checks catch recall items sooner.
    - Keep charging habits sensible: avoid repeated fast‑charging to 100% unless needed, and store at 20–80% for daily driving where appropriate (manufacturer charging guidance in owner’s manual).
  - Warranty: many early faults are covered under Volvo’s new‑car warranties and recall campaigns; always verify transferability and outstanding recall status.

Transmission:
- Mechanical behaviour: EV single‑speed reduction gear per axle (single‑speed gearbox / reduction drive). No multi‑ratio gearbox; the “transmission” is an electric motor + reduction gear set. This gives instant torque delivery, strong low‑speed response and minimal mechanical wear compared with multi‑gear ICE automatics ([Volvo EX90 specs](https://www.volvocars.com/us/cars/ex90-electric/specifications/), [Auto‑Data technical listing](https://www.auto-data.net/en/volvo-ex90-generation-9104)).
- Known failure modes:
  - There are no widespread reports of gearbox mechanical failure; most reported faults have been electronics/software or component supplier issues (tailgate drive, LiDAR, infotainment). Monitor for abnormal noises from reduction gears under load — any whining that worsens with speed or torque should be inspected.
  - Regenerative braking safety bulletin: check for any related software recalls that affect regen behaviour; NHTSA issued a warning in a broader Volvo recall campaign related to braking/regeneration that owners should be aware of ([NHTSA urgent brake recall press release](https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/volvo-recall-urgent-brake-failure-warning-select-vehicles)).

Used‑market pricing context:
- These NSW dealer prices put the EX90 squarely in the AUD $100k+ luxury EV bracket in Australia — that’s what you pay for the Volvo badge, three‑row space and premium safety/tech suite.
- Internationally, used price listings have already softened in some markets (CarGurus and Edmunds show used EX90 listings and price shifts; CarEdge models depreciation curves) — CarGurus notes a year‑over‑year fall in average listing prices and CarEdge anticipates heavy depreciation over 5 years for early units ([CarGurus used EX90 listings](https://www.cargurus.com/Cars/l-Used-Volvo-EX90-d3372), [CarEdge depreciation](https://caredge.com/volvo/ex90/depreciation)).

Depreciation metrics:
- Early forecasts and market evidence point to steep initial depreciation common to luxury EVs and first‑gen flagship models:
  - CarEdge projects roughly ~51% depreciation after 5 years under their baseline assumptions. Use that as a warning: early flagship EVs often lose value faster than established ICE models because of rapid tech cycles and early reliability perception ([CarEdge depreciation](https://caredge.com/volvo/ex90/depreciation)).
  - In the short term (1–3 years) expect 20–35% depreciation in many markets depending on demand, local incentives, and supply. Recent listing data (CarGurus / Edmunds) show falling average values in the used EX90 pool — but your NSW dealer median of AUD 109,990 implies Volvo is still holding price tight locally, partly because supply of used EX90s in Australia is tiny right now.

Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy — how to think about it in Australia:
- Key TCO line items to model for the EX90 in Australia:
  - Depreciation (largest single cost for new luxury EVs).
  - Electricity cost: kWh/100km * price per kWh. Official EV energy consumption figures come from regulatory agencies — use realistic averages from long‑term reviews. Recharged and Edmunds offer long‑term energy/performance insights; official Volvo specs list battery capacity and official energy use ([Volvo specs](https://www.volvocars.com/us/cars/ex90-electric/specifications/), [fueleconomy.gov EX90 data](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/PowerSearch.do?action=noform&baseModel=EX90&make=Volvo&pageno=1&srchtyp=ymm&year1=2025&year2=2025)).
  - Charging infrastructure & installation: home charger install, public fast charging rates (per kWh), idle fees.
  - Maintenance & repairs: EVs generally have lower scheduled maintenance (no engine oil, fewer moving parts) but potential for costly software/central‑computer or battery repair if out of warranty. Edmunds and CarEdge TCO pages are useful references for estimating multi‑year costs ([Edmunds TCO EX90](https://www.edmunds.com/volvo/ex90/2025/cost-to-own/), [CarEdge costs](https://caredge.com/volvo/ex90/costs)).
- Is the premium for an EV EX90 worth it over a petrol XC90 or similar?
  - If you mostly do urban and suburban driving and can charge at home cheaply, the EX90’s running energy cost per year is usually lower than a large petrol V6/XC90 equivalent — but the purchase premium and faster depreciation often swamp running savings for private buyers who buy used.
  - Use this strategy: compute your break‑even by adding the EX90 premium (difference in purchase price) to projected depreciation and insurance differences, then subtract expected annual fuel savings (electricity vs petrol) and maintenance delta. If you do >20–25,000 km/year with a home charger and cheap off‑peak power, the EX90 gets more attractive; lower mileage and short ownership horizons favour the cheaper ICE alternative.
  - For fleet or long‑term owners who want lower energy costs and lower scheduled maintenance, EVs often make sense — but for a used buyer seeking a 3‑year ownership window, the depreciation risk is the real killer.

Buying recommendation — clear verdict and sweet spot:
- If you want all the EX90’s three‑row space, the Volvo safety package and that soft Scandinavian interior experience — and you can tolerate teething software risk — a used EX90 from late 2024/2025 can be a competitive choice if priced sensibly.
- Practical buying checklist and sweet spot:
  - Best financial sweet spot: a low‑kilometre 2024–2025 EX90 with full dealer service records, VIN‑cleared recall status, and documented application of major software/central‑computer updates (look for TSB/Delivery Action entries such as D10294). NSW dealer median at AUD 109,990 is a live market reference; hunt for examples under AUD 100k if depreciation is a core concern (your current NSW listings show AUD 99,990 as the low end).
  - Do not buy any EX90 with unresolved central‑computer issues, multiple ADAS fault codes, or an open tailgate recall. Insist that the selling dealer performs recall/VIN checks and provides documentation of software updates and any central‑computer replacement or ADAS recalibration ([Delivery Action D10294](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2024/MC-11011746-0001.pdf), [Recharged reliability guide](https://recharged.com/articles/2025-volvo-ex90-problems/)).
  - If you want lower risk: wait for a second‑hand market with a larger supply, more dealer‑repaired units and firmware‑stabilised software — typically 12–24 months after launch is when early hardware and software issues get ironed‑out.
- Final take: Buy if you prioritise safety‑first design, a premium three‑row BEV with Volvo’s ergonomics and you can accept early‑model software wrinkles and watch for recalls. Don’t buy if you’re buying solely for investment or uncertain about managing OTA updates, dealer downtime and potential for larger repairs outside warranty. If your ownership horizon is short (≤3 years) and depreciation sensitivity is high, you’re probably better waiting for the model to mature and used prices to settle.
