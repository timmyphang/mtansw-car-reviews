#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation and codes: the modern Maserati GranTurismo you see in Australian showrooms is the second-generation reintroduction (internal code often referenced as **M189** in global spec lists) that returned in 2023–2024. The original first-generation GranTurismo (2007–2019) was the **M145** line — those older V8 cars are still widely traded on the used market. (Overview: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_GranTurismo), [UltimateSpecs](https://www.ultimatespecs.com/car-specs/Maserati-models/Maserati-GranTurismo))
- Product ladder: GranTurismo sits at the top of Maserati’s grand‑tourer product stack — above the mid‑sized Ghibli/Grecale territory and parallel to halo models like the MC20 (sport) and the flagship Quattroporte in different packaging. It’s Maserati’s two‑door statement car: luxury GT, not a track weapon (unless you tick the most athletic options).
- Platform & parts commonality: the latest GranTurismo is built on Maserati’s modern architecture shared with recent Maserati models (Grecale/GranCabrio family elements) and borrows drivetrain/elec‑arch hardware that increases parts availability relative to the old bespoke V8 era. This helps with routine parts (sensors, brakes, electrical harness sections) — important when you’re buying into an Italian marque with limited local supply chains. (Maserati official model page: [Maserati Global](https://www.maserati.com/global/en/models/granturismo))
- Engines / transmissions / drivetrains on sale in Australia (market‑level summary):
  - Petrol: twin‑turbo 3.0‑litre V6 (Nettuno‑derived/twin‑turbo) with outputs advertised up to ~483 hp in high‑spec trims, paired to an 8‑speed automatic; AWD is offered on higher‑spec or performance trims. ([Maserati US model page](https://www.maserati.com/us/en/models/granturismo/granturismo-trim), [Car & Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/maserati/granturismo/specs/2024/maserati_granturismo_maserati-granturismo_2024))
  - Electric: GranTurismo Folgore (BEV) with 800‑volt architecture and high‑power outputs in Folgore guise (Maserati claims race‑derived efficiency tech). Energy figures and usable battery: see EV Database. ([EV Database](https://ev-database.org/car/1803/Maserati-GranTurismo-Folgore), [Maserati Folgore page](https://www.maserati.com/global/en/models/granturismo/granturismo-folgore))
  - Legacy (used‑market common): first‑generation V8s — **4.2‑litre V8** (early M145) and **4.7‑litre F136 V8** used in later S/MC derivatives, with 6‑speed ZF automatics or the Graziano MC‑Shift units in some cars. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_GranTurismo), [Auto‑Data / UltimateSpecs](https://www.ultimatespecs.com/car-specs/Maserati-models/Maserati-GranTurismo))
- Explicit rivals in the Australian market: Aston Martin DB11/DB12, Ferrari Roma/Portofino (depending on spec), Bentley Continental GT, Porsche 911/911‑derived GTs and the wider luxury GT competitors. Reviews place it squarely against Aston Martin and Ferrari for emotional appeal, and Porsche/Bentley when buyers cross‑shop for long‑term ownership value. (Comparisons: [Autocar](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/aston-martin-db12-vs-ferrari-roma-vs-maserati-granturismo-trofeo), [Evo](https://www.evo.co.uk/maserati/granturismo/208546/new-maserati-granturismo-revealed-gt-car-updated-to-face-aston-martin))

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Maserati GranTurismo?
This is the bit buyers skip at their peril. Maserati makes beautiful, charismatic cars — and like any exotic, they have recurring problem themes. I’ll break them down so you can go to inspection armed.

- Exterior:
  - Panel fit and trim: older GranTurismo (M145) owners report trim‑clip failures, brittle mounting tabs and panel alignment issues after repairs; aftermarket or repaint work can make colour matching tricky because of faded clearcoats or bespoke paint codes. Trim mounting tab integrity matters more than a dinged bumper for safety at speed. (Trim/clip notes: [Go‑Parts exterior guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-maserati-granturismo-maserati-grancabrio-2007-2019))
  - Paint delamination & clearcoat issues: there are documented instances of paint peeling and clearcoat delamination on GranTurismo examples (forums and aggregated complaints). If the car has been in strong sun (think Aussie summers), check door shuts, bonnet underside and boot lips for bubbling or lifted clear. ([MaseratiLife forum](https://www.maseratilife.com/threads/paint-peeling.127315/), [CarComplaints](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Maserati/GranTurismo/body_paint/index.shtml))
  - Carbon‑fibre and high‑gloss bits: special trims fade or become tacky over time; replacement is expensive and sometimes bespoke. (Maseratinet note: exterior trim degradation — [MaseratiNet](https://maseratinet.com/news/maserati-granturismo-47-v8-most-common-problems-and-what-fails))
  - ADAS / camera issues: recent model years were subject to radio/infotainment software faults that could block the rear‑view camera image (NHTSA campaign RCRIT‑24V892‑2995 / Recall 25V098). Remedy has been software updates either OTA or at dealers, but pre‑buy check: verify all ADAS camera feeds and lane/AEB systems work and that software recalls have been completed. ([NHTSA recall pdf](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2024/RCRIT-24V892-2995.pdf), recall summary: [25V098](https://oemdtc.com/recall/25V098000/))
  - ADAS calibration sensitivity: the forward camera is the sensory hub for ACC/AEB/LKA — any windscreen replacement or collision repair will need professional static/dynamic ADAS calibration. Don’t accept a “looks OK” — get the calibration report. (Calibration notes: [Go‑Parts ADAS camera guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/video-camera-maserati-grecale-maserati-grancabrio-maserati-granturismo-2023-2026), [Bang AutoGlass commentary](https://bangautoglass.com/blog/maserati-granturismo-adas-calibration-can-cost-questions-ask-first))
- Interior:
  - Infotainment stability: Maserati’s Uconnect‑based systems (modern cars) and legacy head units have been reported to freeze or fail to boot. Common fixes include soft resets and dealer software updates; some older units required drive/hard‑disk cloning or head‑unit replacement. There was a formal recall action around infotainment software preventing rearview image display — check service history for an OTA/dealer update. (Infotainment troubles & recall: [Go‑Parts infotainment guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-maserati-granturismo-maserati-grancabrio-maserati-quattroporte-2008-2019), [autoevolution recall story](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/maserati-recalls-entire-lineup-over-infotainment-software-problem-247406.html))
  - USB/Wireless charging: the USB/wireless charging module used on recent models is a known service item and replacement part on Grecale/GranTurismo shared hardware; easy enough to replace but can be costly if multiple ports fail. (Parts reference: [Go‑Parts USB/Wireless module](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/usb-port-maserati-grecale-maserati-granturismo-maserati-grancabrio-2023-2025))
  - Cabin wear & rideshare duty: Maserati leather/trim will age quickly under hard use (rideshare/taxi is a bad idea). Door sill heights and seat bolsters are grand‑tourer friendly for adults, but repeated ingress/egress will wear seat leather and door checks — check for splitting on the inner bolster edges. Manuals are very clear: high luxury cars like this don’t love hard commercial duty. (Owner manual for feature references: [GranTurismo Owner’s Manual 2024](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/3025614/Maserati-Granturismo-2024.html))
  - 12V battery drains / deep sleep: modern Maserati electronics are sophisticated and can be prone to parasitic drains if modules or software misbehave. If a parked GranTurismo needs a jump, insist on a healthy battery test and ask for module sleep behaviour to be checked. (Infotainment freeze and battery interaction: [Fast Lane European](https://fastlaneeuropean.com/blog/factors-leading-to-maseratis-touchscreen-freezing-problems/), [Vmsom troubleshooting](https://vmsom.com/maserati-granturismo-screen-is-not-working-p3943/))
- Mechanically:
  - Brand engineering reputation: Maserati builds naturally aspirated and turbocharged engines with Ferrari‑aided heritage (older V8s) and now an upscale Nettuno‑derived V6. The engineering is high‑performance but maintenance‑intensive compared with mainstream German rivals. Expect dealer labour rates and OEM part premiums. (Historic engine development: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_GranTurismo))
  - Security vulnerabilities: modern vehicles with CAN bus architectures are susceptible to physical attack vectors like CAN injection attacks that have been demonstrated in the wild (headlight connector access being a common method). While no specific Maserati model has a public widespread exploit tied to an organized campaign, the general vulnerability means you should treat high‑end exotics like theft magnets. Consider physical wheel locks, visible immobiliser systems, and secure parking. (CAN injection research/coverage: [CVE/NVD overview](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2023-29389), [Can‑CIA write up](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/), mainstream reporting: [Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update))
  - Aftermarket anti‑theft: fit an engine immobiliser with discrete kill‑switch, high‑security wheel clamp when parked, and consider accredited tracking (LKa/LoJack style). Also ask the dealer whether the car’s software and modules have the latest security patches.
- Engines:
  - Italicized engine subsections follow for each variant commonly encountered on the used marketplace. I’ve pulled failure patterns from owner reports, specialist technical pages and parts guides — these are the recurring items to inspect.

  *4.2‑litre V8 (early M145 models)*  
  - Common failure points: oil leaks (valve covers, timing cover), ageing ancillary brackets and coolant hose degradation. The older 4.2 V8s were robust for their era but now are maintenance‑heavy if neglected. (Reliability summary: [CarChecker / 4.2 V8 report](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/maserati_granturismo_m145_4.2_v8.html))  
  - Preventative maintenance: synthetic oil changes every 10,000 km if you want to be conservative in Australia’s hot conditions; inspect cooling hoses, clamps and radiator yearly; full coolant flush and thermostat checks at 60,000 km.

  *4.7‑litre F136 V8 (S / MC variants)* — bold major component: **F136 V8**  
  - Common failure points: cam phaser (variator) rattle on cold starts, persistent oil leaks (timing cover/valve cover), cooling system failures leading to overheating, and in some reports piston/oil consumption symptoms. These are well‑documented on Maserati owner networks and specialist sites. (Failure list: [MaseratiNet](https://maseratinet.com/news/maserati-granturismo-47-v8-most-common-problems-and-what-fails), timing cover leaks: [Go‑Parts timing cover guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/engine-timing-cover-maserati-quattroporte-maserati-ghibli-maserati-levante-2008-2024))
  - Preventative maintenance: oil service every 10,000 km; inspect and replace cam phaser/variator oil feeds and engine mounts at 60–100k km if noisy; coolant system refresh every 5 years / 60,000 km; check crank/snout and bearings on high‑mileage units.

  *3.0‑litre twin‑turbo V6 (Nettuno‑inspired modern unit)* — bold major component: **Nettuno V6 / twin‑turbo 3.0**  
  - Common failure points: modern twin‑turbo engines tend to show issues around high‑pressure fuel system components, turbo plumbing, intercooler pipes and coolant hoses rather than piston failures. Public reviews and factory specs show this engine in the 2024+ GranTurismo produces ~483 hp in Modena/Trofeo trims. Newer engines benefit from modern R&D but still carry expensive service items and specialist dealer tooling. (Specs reference: [Maserati US](https://www.maserati.com/us/en/models/granturismo/granturismo-trim), [Car & Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/maserati/granturismo/specs/2024/maserati_granturismo_maserati-granturismo_2024))
  - Preventative maintenance: conservative oil and filter changes at 10,000 km in Australia; turbo inspection and intercooler pipe checks at every major service (30,000 km). Always use dealer‑recommended oil and have dealer calibrations recorded.

  *Folgore BEV (battery electric)*  
  - Failure points: battery/pack health, high‑voltage inverter cooling and software updates. The Folgore uses an 800‑volt architecture and has an 83 kWh usable pack (EV Database), with real‑world consumption near 198 Wh/km (WLTP ~420 km). EV ownership trades fuel concerns for charging and battery health considerations — but don’t expect cheap repairs if HV components fail. (EV specs: [EV Database](https://ev-database.org/car/1803/Maserati-GranTurismo-Folgore), Maserati marketing: [Folgore page](https://www.maserati.com/global/en/models/granturismo/granturismo-folgore))
  - Preventative maintenance: battery state‑of‑health checks; keep software updated; thermal management checks especially if regularly fast‑charging in hot climates. Many EV warranty packages cover the battery but check specifics with Maserati Australia.

- Transmission:
  - Legacy transmissions: older GranTurismo cars used the ZF 6HP series torque‑converter automatics and in some trims the Graziano MC‑Shift (robotised manual). The ZF units are generally strong but torque‑converter shudder, harsh or delayed shifts, and valve body contamination have been recurring complaints. (Technical writeups: [Wikipedia transmission notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_GranTurismo), [Go‑Parts torque converter guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/automatic-transmission-torque-converter-maserati-quattroporte-maserati-ghibli-maserati-grancabrio-2007-2020))
  - Modern transmissions: the new 8‑speed automatic in the latest GranTurismo has revised calibrations; in reviews it works well but remains an expensive component if damaged. (Car & Driver and MotorTrend specs reference: [Car & Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/maserati/granturismo/specs/2024/maserati_granturismo_maserati-granturismo_2024), [MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/cars/maserati/granturismo))
  - Known failure modes: torque‑converter shudder, clutch wear in robotised MC‑Shift units, valve body contamination requiring overhaul. If the test‑drive shows harsh downshifts, delayed engagement from standstill or shudder under light throttle, walk away or budget a specialist transmission inspection.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Maserati GranTurismo?
Short answer: yes, if you understand what you’re buying — a romantic, depreciated Italian GT — and can afford the maintenance and a sensible contingency fund. No, if you’re chasing low running costs or worry about dealer service access in regional NSW.

- NSW market pricing (live snapshot): there are 2 listings priced AUD $369,880–$379,880 (median $374,880), years 2024–2026, median odometer ~20 km, both automatic. That’s the local near‑new market reality — you’re paying close to new‑car money for a low‑km demonstrator/late demo car. Use that as your negotiation anchor and demand full dealer front‑end support and warranty transfer. (NSW dealer data: user supplied)
- Depreciation metrics:
  - Maserati GranTurismo historically depreciates quickly relative to Porsche/Bentley peers. CarEdge estimates typical GranTurismo owners face ~65% depreciation after 5 years (example projection used by CarEdge), and iSeeCars data shows GranTurismo losing ~41.9% of value over 5 years compared to a Porsche 911’s ~17% — the headline is clear: Maserati loses value fast. If you plan to flip in 3–5 years, expect significant loss relative to German rivals. ([CarEdge depreciation](https://caredge.com/maserati/granturismo/depreciation/), [iSeeCars comparison](https://iseecars.com/compare/maserati-granturismo-vs-porsche-911/))
- TCO calculation strategy (how to work out whether this is affordable for you):
  - Primary cost drivers: depreciation and insurance dominate TCO. CarEdge’s model estimates 5‑year ownership costs that are massive compared to mainstream cars; depreciation alone can be the lion’s share. ([CarEdge costs](https://caredge.com/maserati/granturismo/costs))
  - Fuel vs EV decision (Folgore vs petrol): the BEV Folgore promises lower energy cost/km and fewer mechanical service items (no oil, no high‑pressure fuel pump, no turbos). EV Database lists real‑world consumption ~198 Wh/km and WLTP range ~420 km — good for a GT. FuelEconomy.gov estimates savings vs average new cars in the US context for the Folgore as well. Whether the electric variant mathematically pays off depends on:
    - Kilometres per year: if you do high annual kms (25–40k km), the petrol fuel spend adds up and the EV’s lower energy-cost per km (even after accounting for home/supercharger prices) begins to look attractive.
    - Purchase premium & resale: EVs currently command a higher new price but may also have patchy resale until the market matures. Use the run numbers: for an EV consuming 0.198 kWh/km, at a conservative Australian household tariff of ~AUD $0.30/kWh (assumption for illustration), energy cost ≈ AUD $0.059/km — compare to petrol at 12 L/100 km and AUD $2.00/L giving ≈ AUD $0.24/km. Over 40,000 km/year that’s a saving of ~AUD $6,440/year in fuel vs petrol — but you must weigh in higher insurance and potential higher depreciation on the BEV. (EV energy spec: [EV Database](https://ev-database.org/car/1803/Maserati-GranTurismo-Folgore), US MPG comparisons: [FuelEconomy.gov](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=48716))
  - Practical TCO advice: build a 5‑year model where you put depreciation first (use market averages from CarEdge/iSeeCars), add insurance quotes from your broker (these cars are expensive to insure), and factor dealer service intervals and premium parts. If you drive low kms (<8k–10k km/year) and value ownership experience, a near‑new GranTurismo (dealer‑backed) can be justified as an emotional purchase. If you use the car daily and want rational TCO, a Porsche 911 or even a Bentley that retains value better will likely be cheaper per km in the long run.
- Final buying recommendation (straight talk):
  - If you want the new‑car smell and the Maserati experience without drama: buy a near‑new dealer car with remaining factory warranty (2024–2026 models in NSW fall in the AUD $370k–$380k band). Insist on a full dealer‑performed pre‑delivery, completed software/ADAS recalls, and a warranty/servicing package included in the deal. For peace of mind, the local dealer also means Maserati‑calibrated ADAS and software updates will be accessible.
  - If you’re value‑hunting: older M145 V8s (2008–2017) can be had for a fraction of the new price (often sub‑$100k for decent examples) and give you Italian V8 character for less cash — but expect higher maintenance, potential oil/cooling leaks and the transmission/infotainment idiosyncrasies noted above. If you’re not a home‑wrencher with cash in the kitty, avoid high‑mileage V8s without full service history.
  - Best financial sweet spot (my pick): buy a late‑model petrol 3.0 V6 (2024–2026) from a Maserati dealer with a certified pre‑owned program if you want modern reliability, electronics, warranty and lower running complexity than the old V8s — but only if you accept the NSW market price band (AUD ~$370k+). If you want drama and value, buy a well‑documented mid‑life 4.7 V8 with full service history and a pre‑purchase inspection by a Maserati specialist — you’ll get the sound, but keep AUD $20k–$40k in a maintenance reserve.
- Practical pre‑purchase checklist (quick):
  - Verify recall and software remedy history (ADAS/camera and infotainment recalls). See NHTSA recall references for the rearview camera issue. ([NHTSA recall pdf](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2024/RCRIT-24V892-2995.pdf), [25V098 summary](https://oemdtc.com/recall/25V098000/))
  - Full service history from an authorised Maserati dealer or recognised specialist.
  - Test for infotainment freezes, full ADAS operation, and all camera feeds.
  - Transmission behaviour on a long test drive: watch for shudder, harsh or delayed shifts.
  - Engine health: oil leaks, cold‑start noises (cam variator on older V8s), coolant system service.
  - If electric: battery health report, recent software updates and charging history.

Bottom line, Aussie way: if you want to be looked at on a Sunday drive and can stomach the bit of pain that comes with Italian exotica — plus the bills — the GranTurismo is a ridiculously pretty, balletic GT. If you want to be economically rational, comparison‑shop hard with Porsche/Bentley options and model the five‑year numbers before signing. And whatever you do, get the software and ADAS recall work completed and lock in a dealer‑backed warranty transfer before you pay $370k+ in NSW.
