#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation & codes: This review covers the Subaru Levorg (first-generation onwards into early second‑generation models). The Levorg debuted in 2014 and the first generation ran through the late‑2010s; the later Levorg II moves to the Subaru Global Platform (SGP). [Wikipedia – Levorg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru_Levorg) and [Subaru Global Platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru_Global_Platform).
- Product hierarchy: The Levorg sits between the compact Impreza wagon and the larger Legacy/Outback in Subaru’s passenger-car line-up — effectively the sporty estate option in Subaru’s range (you want more luxury/space and you step up to Legacy/Outback; less, and you’re in an Impreza/Crosstrek).
- Platform & parts sharing:
  - First‑generation Levorg shares architecture and many components with Impreza/WRX and Legacy derivatives. [Wikipedia – Levorg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru_Levorg)
  - Second generation is on the Subaru Global Platform (SGP), shared with modern Impreza, Forester and WRX derivatives — useful for parts availability and suspension/structural components. [Subaru Global Platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subaru_Global_Platform)
- Market engine / drivetrain mix (Australian‑relevant summary):
  - 1.6‑litre turbocharged boxer — commonly listed as **FB16DIT** in first‑generation Levorgs sold globally (about 125 kW / 250 Nm in many markets). [Carsales / Drive range notes](https://www.carsales.com.au/research/subaru/levorg/2020/2-0-sti-sport/)
  - 2.0‑litre turbocharged boxer — **FA20** family in early sporty/GT‑S and STI Sport variants (higher output ~197 kW / 350 Nm in performance spec). [Carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/research/subaru/levorg/2020/2-0-sti-sport/)
  - Later Levorg II adopted the **CB18** 1.8 turbo (and, in other markets/years, hybrid variants), paired with Subaru’s Lineartronic CVT and standard AWD on most models. [List of Subaru engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Subaru_engines) and [auto‑data Levorg II specs](https://www.auto-data.net/en/subaru-levorg-model-2088)
  - Transmission: Subaru Lineartronic CVT (often the only automatic offered in many markets), all‑wheel drive (AWD) standard on Levorgs except rare market‑specific exceptions. [HonestJohn / RAC overviews refer to CVT use](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/subaru/levorg/)
- Main rivals in Australia:
  - Sporty/compact wagons and premium small estates: Volkswagen Golf/Golf Variant, Skoda Octavia wagon, Subaru WRX wagon (in performance territory), and premium rivals like Audi A4 Avant / BMW 3 Series Touring when buyers move upmarket. (See comparison lists and segment grouping at CarsGuide and Parkers). [CarsGuide comparisons](https://www.carsguide.com.au/subaru/levorg/vs) • [Parkers – premium estates context](https://www.parkers.co.uk/car-group-tests/volvo-v60-v-bmw-3-series-touring-v-audi-a4-avant/)

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Subaru Levorg?

Exterior:
- Panel fit & trim:
  - Generally well built for a Japanese wagon; owners report occasional trim rattles (A‑pillar trims and minor interior/exterior trim pop/rattle are the most common) — see owner threads on Levorg owner forums. [Levorg Club forum](https://levorgclub.com/)
  - Gloss black and piano‑black trims show scuffs easily; door mirrors and rocker mouldings can chip if the previous owner wasn’t gentle.
- Paint & stone‑chip:
  - No Levorg‑specific large‑scale paint recall listed in the searches, but owners report normal Subaru paint stone‑chip vulnerability on bonnet/leading edges (forums and aggregated owner reports). [EngineCrux paint problems overview](https://enginecrux.com/understanding-subaru-paint-problems-and-solutions/)
- Safety system (ADAS / EyeSight):
  - EyeSight (Subaru’s stereo‑camera suite) is effective but sensitive. Owners report camera fouling (dirt/stone damage to the camera housing) and occasional false/phantom braking events. Public reports and legal actions have risen in recent years; Subaru and dealers issued bulletins and warranty extensions for some EyeSight components. See the Subaru EyeSight settlement material and a Subaru TSB on warranty/repair coverage for EyeSight & Reverse Automatic Braking. [EyeSight settlement overview](https://www.eyesightsettlement.com/) • [Subaru service bulletin (NHTSA-hosted PDF)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2025/MC-11021266-0001.pdf)
  - Practical buying check: inspect windshield/forward fascia for camera housing damage, ask dealer for EyeSight software/calibration history, and insist on a test drive to provoke any ADAS misbehaviour.
  - Community advice: clean the EyeSight camera windows regularly and ensure calibration after any windscreen/bumper work. [Autodoc EyeSight issues summary](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-subaru-levorg)

Interior:
- Durability & practical wear:
  - Interior quality is generally praised (Autocar called it “Subaru’s best interior to date” for Levorg long‑term tests), but hard plastics in lower trim levels will show scuffs with heavy commercial or rideshare use. [Autocar long‑term test](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/subaru-levorg-long-term-test-final-report)
  - Door sills and entry ergonomics are good for a wagon — lowish sill height helps repeated entry/exit; door checks are long‑throw but some owners report creaks on older examples.
- Electrical / infotainment:
  - Subaru Starlink/infotainment series has a track record of freezing, slow response, and intermittent CarPlay/Android Auto issues across Subaru models (2019–2023 era complaints have even prompted class‑action attention in some markets). Expect occasional reboots or software updates needed. [ClassAction.org on Subaru Starlink infotainment](https://www.classaction.org/subaru-touch-screen-infotainment-issues-lawsuits) • [TheAutoInsider overview](https://theautoinsiderblog.com/subaru-infotainment-system-problems/)
  - Screen sizes vary by trim and model year (higher trims get larger screens and additional cameras). Confirm the fitted unit on the specific car and test both wired and wireless phone connectivity.
  - 12V battery drain: owner communities report parasitic drains related to aftermarket installs or misbehaving modules; check battery health and ask for last battery replacement date. (Forum reports: [Levorg Club](https://levorgclub.com/))
  - Wireless charging pads can be fiddly — often user‑error and phone case-related but check operation with your phone. [Subaru wireless charger guide / troubleshooting](https://techinfo.subaru.com/stis/doc/accInstallGuide/H1010FN200%20Wireless%20Charger%20-%20Push%20Start%20-%20IxI.pdf)
- Rideshare/taxi duty caution: If you’re buying a high‑odometer Levorg from rideshare use expect wear to seats, trim and likely hard‑use service lapses. Insist on a comprehensive service history.

Mechanically:
- Subaru engineering reputation: generally solid, especially the AWD hardware and chassis. However, the Levorg line inherits Subaru’s long‑term CVT and turbo boxer wrinkles which you must treat like a ticking maintenance obligation rather than a set‑and‑forget.
- High‑priority security vulnerabilities:
  - Like many modern vehicles, the Levorg’s CAN bus and connected systems have documented attack surfaces. CAN‑injection attacks that access wiring (e.g., behind headlamps) exist in the wild; connected services had vulnerabilities that were patched historically. This is a general modern vehicle risk, not Levorg‑only. [CAN injection explanation (Canis Automotive Labs)](https://can-cia.org/fileadmin/cia/documents/publications/cnlm/june_2023/cnlm_23-2_p20_the_can_injection_attack_ken_tindel_canis_automotive_labs.pdf) • [Security Magazine – Subaru STARLINK vulnerability report](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/101343-vulnerability-discovered-in-subarus-connected-vehicle-service) • [AboutAutomobile TSBs for Levorg ignition/anti‑theft](https://www.aboutautomobile.com/Technical-Service-Bulletin/2022/Subaru/Levorg/Ignition-Anti-Theft)
  - Aftermarket countermeasures to recommend:
    - Faraday pouch/box for your key fob to avoid relay theft.
    - Robust visible physical deterrents (wheel clamp/steering lock) to increase time and attention needed for a thief.
    - Professional alarm/immobiliser system that adds a secondary authenticated immobiliser or tamper‑protected ECU relay rather than hackable convenience features.
    - VIN etching and secure parking / tracking subscription where appropriate.

Engines:
- Overview: Levorg engines to look for (from the provided sources) are the **FB16DIT 1.6 turbo**, **FA20 2.0 turbo**, and later **CB18 1.8 turbo** in Levorg II. Each has distinct failure notes pulled from owner reports and aggregated reliability write‑ups. [List of Subaru engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Subaru_engines) • [MotorReviewer FB16DIT details](https://www.motorreviewer.com/engine.php?engine_id=210) • [Autodoc Levorg common faults](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-subaru-levorg)
- Preventative service rhythm (practical Australian expectation):
  - Engine oil & filter: every 10,000 km or 12 months (Subaru dealers commonly operate 10,000 km service intervals — check the car’s stamped book). If the seller used 15,000 km services, ask for details and oil spec used.
  - Spark plugs and major services: follow stamp book but expect plug/service attention around 40–60k km intervals.
  - Turbo systems: inspect hoses, intercooler and turbo oil feed lines at each major service.
  - Timing chain – the boxers use chains, but noisy chains/tensioner wear have been reported; watch for rattles at cold start and have a mechanic inspect if present.

- *FB16DIT (1.6 turbo)*:
  - Known notes: Timing‑chain related noise and wear has been reported in the 1.6 turbo family with symptoms sometimes appearing early (community reports range widely: ~15,000–80,000 km). Engine knocking or rattling under acceleration and a rough idle are red flags. [MotorReviewer – FB16DIT overview](https://www.motorreviewer.com/engine.php?engine_id=210) • [myCarly/Levorg community lists](https://www.mycarly.com/community/brands/subaru/levorg/)
  - Specific failure points:
    - Timing chain tensioner/guide wear → early rattle and potential catastrophic wear if ignored.
    - Intercooler/hose oil contamination and boost control valve wear on heavily driven cars.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Engine oil every 10,000 km with high‑quality synthetic and log the work.
    - Early inspection of timing chain noise; if noisy, replace chain/tensioners promptly (service intervals for replacement are not a fixed factory item; proactive inspection is key).
- *FA20 (2.0 turbo)*:
  - Known notes: Some owners and aggregated guides report elevated oil consumption and occasional smoke under hard use (oils and piston ring/cylinder sealing issues have been a Subaru turbo theme on some lines). [Autodoc – Levorg faults list (oil consumption on 2.0)](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-subaru-levorg)
  - Specific failure points:
    - Oil consumption (watch for bluish smoke and 0.5–1L/5,000 km scale symptoms).
    - Turbocharger wear if oil changes neglected.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Oil changes at 10,000 km with correct spec; inspect for oil leaks and top‑up records.
    - Watch for PCV/breather blockages — these can accelerate oil consumption and carbon build‑up.
- *CB18 (1.8 turbo – Levorg II)*:
  - Known notes: A newer compact/concentrated boxer with design aim at efficiency; early adopters show better fuel numbers but long‑term failure modes are still being evaluated in owner communities. [CB engine note on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Subaru_engines) • [Auto‑Data Levorg II specs](https://www.auto-data.net/en/subaru-levorg-model-2088)
  - Specific failure points:
    - As a later engine, fewer widespread failure reports exist; routine oil and turbo care remain the baseline.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Service as per Subaru schedule — oil at 10,000 km for Aussie service habits; turbo intercooler and breather inspections regularly.

Transmission:
- The Levorg is commonly fitted with Subaru’s **Lineartronic CVT**. Known behaviours and failure modes across Subaru CVT examples:
  - Symptoms to watch for on test drive:
    - Jerking / shudder when pulling away from low speeds or passing (often appears from 20k–30k km in some reports).
    - Overheating under heavy use (long hill climbs, towing or spirited driving) and limp modes at higher mileages. [Autodoc Levorg CVT commentary](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-subaru-levorg) • [HonestJohn on Levorg CVT](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/subaru/levorg/)
  - Maintenance notes:
    - CVT fluid formulation and change interval are critical. Subaru updated CVT fluid specs and formulations over time — using the wrong fluid or skipping changes increases failure risk. [Gears Magazine – Lineartronic failures and fluid importance](https://gearsmagazine.com/magazine/worth-another-look-subaru-lineartronic-failures-faults-and-fixes/)
    - Practical owner advice is to confirm CVT fluid change history and, if absent, budget for a fluid and filter service (owner forums and independent techs often suggest fluid service every 30–60k km depending on driving style and heat exposure). [Automoblog 2026 CVT guide](https://www.automoblog.com/reviews/maintenance/subaru-cvt-problems/)
  - If you encounter shuddering or delayed engagements on a test drive — walk away or haggle hard. CVT repairs are expensive.

Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy
- What to include: depreciation, fuel, servicing (including CVT fluid), tyres, insurance, and likely outlays for ADAS checks/repairs and potential engine or CVT service. For a Levorg you must pay special attention to:
  - CVT maintenance premium (fluid changes / possible cooler upgrades).
  - Turbo engine care (synthetic oil, more frequent oil changes if driven hard).
  - ADAS/Camera recalibrations after windscreen or bumper repairs.
- Practical TCO example (methodology you can replicate):
  - Choose annual km (e.g., 15,000 km), fuel price assumption (example: AUD $1.80/L — update to local/current price), and combined fuel consumption for the petrol Levorg (approx 7.3–7.4 L/100 km for later petrol 1.8/1.6 variants — from auto‑data owner/official combined figures). [Auto‑Data Levorg II fuel](https://www.auto-data.net/en/subaru-levorg-ii-generation-8034)
  - Do the maths: 15,000 km / 100 × 7.4 L × $1.80 = annual fuel bill ≈ $1,999. Hybrid variants (where available) claim much better economy — e.g., TopGear/TopGear Philippines and Carscoops report e‑Boxer / S:HEV hybrid figures around 19 km/L (≈ 5.3 L/100 km) for some hybrid Levorg variants — in that same example: 15,000 / 100 × 5.3 × $1.80 ≈ $1,432 annually (saving ≈ $567/year at those assumed numbers). [TopGear Philippines on hybrid economy ~19 km/L](https://www.topgear.com.ph/news/car-news/subaru-levorg-layback-s-hev-japan-a6938-20260616) • [Carscoops hybrid announcement](https://www.carscoops.com/2026/06/subaru-levorg-layback-hybrid/)
  - Break‑even: if a hybrid costs a $5,000 premium, annual fuel saving of $567 means ~8.8 years to recoup just on fuel — you must also consider lower servicing (no heavy turbo maintenance) and resale benefits to fully assess the premium. Use the OwnershipBeat break‑even method as a template. [OwnershipBeat hybrid break‑even methodology](https://ownershipbeat.com/fuel-cost-reality/do-hybrid-suvs-still-save-you-money-in-2026-heres-the-break-even-math)
- Dealer service expectations in Australia: Subaru dealer schedules commonly operate at ~10,000 km intervals; if the car shows 15,000 km service stamps instead, ask why — both intervals are used by different brands, but Subaru tends to 10k by local dealers; confirm with the seller and the stamped service book.

Final verdict & buying recommendation
- Is a used Levorg worth buying in Australia at a glance?
  - Yes — if you want a practical, sporty AWD wagon with Subaru character, decent kit in higher trims, and you accept a little ongoing maintenance attention (CVT fluid care, turbo maintenance, EyeSight vigilance).
  - No — if you want the lowest running costs, minimal technical surprises, or a fuss‑free CVT experience. There are cheaper, simpler wagons and small SUVs that will cost less to run.
- On the NSW listing at $20,990 for a 2018 with 114,447 km:
  - That’s a plausible price if the car is a mid‑range GT with a full service history and no CVT or EyeSight faults. If the car is a 2.0 turbo with heavy use, negotiate hard. If it’s a 1.6 with evidence of early timing‑chain work or CVT service gaps — walk or demand a reduced price to cover imminent servicing.
  - Must‑ask items before you put money down:
    - Full stamped service history (oil frequency at 10k km intervals? CVT fluid change records?).
    - Any EyeSight calibrations, windscreen replacements, or TSB/recall work completed (check VIN recall status via Subaru site/recall databases). [Subaru recall checker](https://www.subaru.com/recalls.html)
    - Evidence the CVT hasn’t been overheating (no limp mode events, no delayed/jerking shift behaviour on test drive).
    - Ask about recent tyres, brakes, and whether turbo oil consumption has been monitored.
- Sweet spot pick:
  - If you want the best balance of equipment vs risk, aim for a later first‑generation or early Levorg II example (2019–2020) in the GT Premium trim with documented dealer service, CVT fluid servicing, and a clean EyeSight history. These carry the kit you want (adaptive cruise, Apple CarPlay/Android Auto in higher trims) without paying the STI Sport premium.
  - If you’re buying solely for performance and don’t mind higher running costs, a well‑maintained 2.0 GT‑S / STI Sport is the pick — but budget for turbo upkeep and potential higher insurance.

Bottom line (short and to the point)
- The Levorg is a lovely, niche Subaru: useful, characterful and unique in Australia if you need AWD wagon utility with a sporty flavour. But treat every used Levorg like a precision German watch — it will run for miles if serviced properly, and it will bite you in the wallet if ignored (CVT and turbo care, EyeSight quirks). For the NSW $20,990, 2018/114k example: inspect service history, confirm CVT fluid changes and EyeSight fixes, and be prepared to walk away if the car shows CVT shudder, ADAS oddities, or unproven timing‑chain work. If the paperwork is clean, that price is workable for a buyer who values Subaru’s wagon package.
