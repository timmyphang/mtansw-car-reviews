#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation, production timeframe and internal codes  
  The car you’re asking about is the second‑generation Range Rover Evoque (launched 2018 — current generation from 2018→ ), the compact premium crossover from Jaguar Land Rover. The Evoque lineage goes back to 2011 (first generation L538) but the post‑2018 model is the cleaner, more tech‑heavy II generation sold in Australia and globally. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_Rover_Evoque) and Land Rover’s own technical pages cover the generational split and model history.

- Product hierarchy (what sits above / below)  
  The Evoque sits at the small/luxury end of the Range Rover family: below the mid‑sized Range Rover Velar and well below the full‑size Range Rover Sport and flagship Range Rover — it’s the badge‑conscious city SUV that trades bulk for style and tech.

- Shared platform architecture and other models on that foundation  
  The modern Evoque uses Land Rover’s compact SUV architecture shared across a number of JLR products (the 2018+ Evoque shares architecture and powertrain families with models like the Discovery Sport and other Jaguar Land Rover compact models). Land Rover’s own technical/spec pages list the global model/span of powertrains and platform commonality — useful when hunting parts or used components. See official model/spec pages for the Evoque family [RangeRover.com models & specifications](https://www.rangerover.com/en-xi/range-rover-evoque/models-and-specifications.html).

- Market‑specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drive configs (Australia)  
  The Evoque sold in Australia since 2018 has been offered with a mix of turbo petrol and diesel Ingenium engines, 48‑volt mild‑hybrid variants, and (in later years) a plug‑in hybrid P300e. Transmission choices are primarily automatic boxes — the ZF 9‑speed automatic being common in many years — and drivetrains usually include front‑biased AWD on most higher specs. Manufacturer technical inserts and the 2025 spec PDF show petrol, diesel and PHEV options across markets: [Land Rover technical insert (2025)](https://www.landrover.com/content/dam/lrdx/pdfs/xi/wltp/Land-Rover-Range-Rover-Evoque-TD-Insert-1L5512650000XIEN01P.pdf) and the tech hub [rangerover.com tech specs](https://www.rangerover.com/en-gb/range-rover-evoque/tech-specs.html).

- Explicit marketplace rivals (Australia)  
  Expect to be checking the Audi Q3, BMW X1/X2, Mercedes‑Benz GLA, Volvo XC40 and Lexus UX for alternatives. Reviewers group the Evoque with these small premium SUVs — many buyers choose Evoque for looks and interior niceties rather than outright space or mechanical simplicity. See typical competitor roundups: [The Car Connection](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/land-rover_range-rover-evoque_choices) and [Carwow competitor list](https://www.carwow.co.uk/blog/range-rover-evoque-alternatives-0444).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Land Rover Range Rover Evoque?

Exterior
- Panel fit, trim and paint
  - Recurrent reports of loose exterior trim and rattles (tailgate finisher, pillar mouldings and other plastic retainers) caused by brittle/cheap retaining clips. These bits rattle, then fall off if not fixed — common across 2016–2024 examples. See a guide to exterior trim issues and replacements: [Go‑Parts trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-land-rover-range-rover-evoque-2016-2024-2).
  - Owner threads and forums show paint clearcoat peeling/chipping around black trims and lower valances — not universal but reported enough to check closely on any used car for bubbling or flaking paint: [Evoque Owners Club paint thread](https://www.evoqueownersclub.co.uk/threads/paint-coating-peeling-off.25974/page-2).
  - Panel‑gap inconsistency has been noted by owners (rear bumper/tail light junctions in particular). These aren’t always structural but look sloppy and may indicate poor build tolerance on some early production runs: community posts discuss this at length (example: [Evoque forum panel gap thread](https://www.evoqueforums.net/threads/panel-gap-issues-on-rear-all-affected.546/)).

- Recalls & safety system problems
  - There’s an ongoing recall history for multiple items spanning camera/radar modules, DC‑DC/gear‑shift modules and touchscreen failures. Recent formal recall filings (NHTSA Part 573) cover many 2021–2024 built Evoques — always check the VIN before purchase: [NHTSA recall report RCLRPT‑25V454 (2025)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2025/RCLRPT-25V454-3682.pdf) and aggregated recall listings: [Cars.com recalls page](https://www.cars.com/research/land_rover-range_rover_evoque/recalls/).
  - ADAS sensor vulnerability: forward cameras and surround‑view sensors are a common failure source that can disable lane assist, emergency brake assist and other systems — these often need recalibration or module replacement which is not cheap. See the ADAS camera failure outlines and TSB/recall data: [Go‑Parts ADAS camera guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/video-camera-land-rover-range-rover-evoque-land-rover-discovery-sport-land-rover-range-rover-sport-2009-2022) and NHTSA/RepairSurge complaint aggregation: [RepairSurge Evoque complaints & recalls](https://www.repairsurge.com/nhtsa-data-explorer/Land%20Rover/Range%20Rover%20Evoque).

Interior
- Cabin durability and daily use
  - The Evoque’s cabin finishes are premium, but some materials and switches show wear under hard urban duty (rideshare/taxi-style use) — leather bolsters, glossy black trims and touch switches are prone to scratches and fingerprints. Hard plastic in lower doors and cupholder areas is tougher but watch for worn seat bolsters on high‑kilometre examples.
  - Entry/egress: low door sills and relatively narrow door apertures make the Evoque user‑friendly in city parking but tight for taller people hauling stuff in and out. Door check angles are typical of a compact SUV — not a problem unless hinges are damaged.

- Electronics and infotainment
  - The Evoque has a recurring history of infotainment and USB/charging issues. Centre screens (InControl / Pivi Pro depending on year) can freeze, go blank at start or reboot randomly — a software timing fault prompted a recall affecting over 31,000 vehicles in a recent campaign (recall N972) and dealers issued software remedies: see the reporting on the touchscreen recall and dealer firmware fixes: [CarComplaints — touchscreen recall](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2025/land-rover-touchscreen-failures-recall.shtml).
  - USB ports and wireless chargers can stop charging or fail to maintain CarPlay/Android Auto connections — many problems are due to the USB repeater/module and are fixable without replacing the entire head unit: [Go‑Parts USB/charger diagnostic guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/usb-port-land-rover-range-rover-evoque-land-rover-range-rover-velar-land-rover-range-rover-2017-2024) and technical module discussions: [Go‑Parts USB Repeater module](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/usb-repeater-socket-control-module-land-rover-range-rover-evoque-jaguar-f-type-jaguar-f-pace-2012-2026).
  - Beware of blank screens at start or intermittent failures — dealers often push software updates first; insist on seeing proof of completed TSB/recall work.

Mechanically
- Brand engineering reputation & service culture
  - Land Rover builds desirable cars but historically trades higher refinement for higher ownership complexity and cost. Expect higher-than‑average servicing bills and occasional electronic gremlins. Aggregated reliability discussions and owner complaints across model years back this up: [CarParts reliability summary](https://www.carparts.com/blog/range-rover-evoque-reliability-and-common-problems/).

- Security vulnerabilities
  - Range Rover models — Evoque included — are a target for modern theft techniques. CAN‑bus injection and relay/relay‑box/ headlight wiring exploits have been used to unlock/start vehicles by impersonating key messages on internal networks. Independent cybersecurity and tracker specialists warn owners to use layered aftermarket protection: see writeups on CAN‑injection and mitigation strategies: [The Register — CAN bus theft explanation](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how-thieves-steal-cars-using-vehicle-can-bus/), [Vicone blog CAN‑injection overview](https://vicone.com/blog/how-to-get-away-with-car-theft-unveiling-the-dark-side-of-the-can-bus/) and practical tracker/immobiliser advice [LockandTrack guide](https://lockandtrack.co.uk/blog/how-to-protect-your-range-rover-from-theft-the-ultimate-2026-security-guide/).
  - Physical/digital aftermarket mitigations I’d recommend in Australia: a professionally installed Thatcham‑equivalent Category S5 tracker (or Meta Trak S5 level device) with a hard‑wired immobiliser and, crucially, an independently switched clutch or fuel‑cut immobiliser for when you park in urban risk areas. Make sure the installer has experience with JLR vehicles.

Engines
- Overall: the Evoque uses variants of the JLR **Ingenium** family and earlier Ford‑derived 2.0 engines in older cars. Diesel variants carry DPF/AdBlue complexity; petrol turbo variants are sensitive to oil and servicing practices. Multiple sources show timing‑chain, turbo and oil dilution issues on some Ingenium diesels, especially where DPF regenerations are incomplete. See the technical guides and failure notes: [2.0 Ingenium failure guide (LRDirect)](https://www.lrdirect.com/technical-guides/2-0-ingenium-engine-failure) and timing chain writeups: [JLR Engines timing chain article](https://jlrengines.co.uk/blog/range-rover-evoque-timing-chain-problem/).

For readability I’ll list every engine variant the research mentions and what to watch for:

- *2.0‑litre Diesel Ingenium (commonly badged as D165 or similar)*  
  - Failure points: timing‑chain wear leading to rattles and potentially catastrophic failure, turbocharger failures, oil dilution from incomplete DPF regenerations, EGR clogging and DPF blockages. Early diesels (pre‑improved software) are the most at risk. See timing chain and diesel failure diagnostics: [LRDirect 2.0D guide](https://www.lrdirect.com/technical-guides/2-0-ingenium-engine-failure) and [JLR Engines timing chain problem](https://jlrengines.co.uk/blog/range-rover-evoque-timing-chain-problem/).  
  - Preventative maintenance: insist on oil changes at least every 10,000 km (or annually whichever comes first) with OEM‑grade oil; run regular longer drives to allow DPF regenerations; have the DPF/EGR inspected at 80–100k km. If the car is used mostly for short urban hops, avoid older diesels — the DPF will suffer. Consider fuel‑additive and frequent service scans to catch regenerations.

- *2.0‑litre Petrol Ingenium (P250 / P200 – turbo variants)*  
  - Failure points: turbocharger wear, oil leaks from cam cover/gaskets, occasional high‑pressure fuel pump or injector faults on direct injection engines leading to rough running. Electronics controlling timing and cam phasers can give limp‑home modes. Petrol Ingeniums are broadly better behaved than diesels but still sensitive to oil and maintenance. Manufacturer pages and spec listings confirm the petrol turbo family usage: [Range Rover brochure / spec](https://cab.jaguarlandrover.com.edgesuite.net/L551/New-Range-Rover-Evoque-Brochure-1L5512010000BGBEN01P.pdf).
  - Preventative maintenance: oil and filter at 10,000–12,000 km; inspect turbo hoses and PCV systems at service; use high‑quality fuel and avoid extended idling.

- *2.2 Diesel (DW12C) — older model references*  
  - Failure points: DPF clogging, EGR valve clogging, turbocharger failures and oil leaks. This older tech shows similar diesel issues and aged components can be expensive to rectify: see community guides on 2.2 problems: [rangerovergarage 2.2 guide](https://rangerovergarage.co.uk/common-range-rover-evoque-2-2-engine-problems-and-their-fixes).
  - Preventative maintenance: DPF monitoring and EGR cleaning at the first signs of chopping/regeneration failures; oil services every 10,000 km.

- *1.5 P300e Plug‑In Hybrid (PHEV)*  
  - Failure points: PHEV systems bring high voltage complexity, DC‑DC converters and battery cooling/management concerns. The P300e’s electric range and low on‑paper L/100 km are attractive, but real‑world PHEV economics depend heavily on charging frequency. Manufacturer and third‑party spec pages give WLTP/paper figures but warn that on‑road results vary: [Auto‑Data P300e specs](https://www.auto-data.net/en/land-rover-range-rover-evoque-ii-1.5-p300e-309hp-plug-in-hybrid-awd-automatic-40728) and [DrivingElectric PHEV running costs](https://www.drivingelectric.com/land-rover/range-rover-evoque/running-costs).  
  - Preventative maintenance: hybrid battery health checks, inverter/DC‑DC checks at service intervals (follow Land Rover’s prescribed HV system checks in the official service schedule); for best TCO, charge daily if your commute is short.

Transmission
- Common boxes and known failure modes
  - **ZF 9HP 9‑speed automatic** — used in many petrol variants — is known across the industry to be sensitive to fluid condition and software calibration. Symptoms reported: harsh/jerky shifts, delayed gear engagement, and ‘Transmission Fault’ limp modes. Software updates and valve‑body overhauls often fix behavioural issues, but internal wear on clutches or mechatronics is expensive. Guides and owner reports summarise common gearbox faults: [CarTipsDaily gearbox fault overview](https://cartipsdaily.com/range-rover-gearbox-fault), [RCV gearbox faults & fixes](https://www.rcv.co.uk/range-rover-evoque-gearbox-faults-causes-fixes/).
  - AWD/Haldex modules and electronic torque‑vectoring systems can fail or go out of calibration — watch for AWD warning lights and abnormal tyre wear patterns. RepairSurge/TIS data show related complaints: [RepairSurge Evoque complaints](https://www.repairsurge.com/nhtsa-data-explorer/Land%20Rover/Range%20Rover%20Evoque).
  - Recommended checks: get a road‑test to feel for smooth upshifts and downshifts through all ratios; insist on a transmission fluid condition report where possible and ask whether software updates have been applied.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Land Rover Range Rover Evoque?

- NSW dealer data snapshot (live)

Use that as a reality check — at ~85k km for a 2021 Evoque asking $38,990, you’re looking at a mid‑five‑figure price for a compact luxury SUV with some years of warranty elapsed.

- Current used marketplace entry to ceiling price (Australia)  
  With the NSW listing at $38,990 for a 2021 car and broader national markets varying: expect prices for later‑model (2019–2021) Evoques in Australia to commonly sit in the mid‑$30k to mid‑$50k bracket depending on kilometres, spec and PHEV vs petrol/diesel. For global context, valuation tools flag variable resale — CarGurus and CarPriceTracker list average used Evoque prices lower than new MSRPs ([CarGurus used price trends](https://www.cargurus.com/research/price-trends/Land-Rover-Range-Rover-Evoque-d2121)). For precise NSW market values, lean on local dealer comps and that MTA NSW snapshot.

- Depreciation metrics  
  - Expect heavy depreciation compared with mainstream rivals: various sources suggest ~50–56% depreciation over 5 years for Evoque examples (CarEdge estimates ≈52% depreciation after 5 years; AutoPadre/CarPadre give similar curves). See CarEdge depreciation summary: [CarEdge Evoque depreciation](https://caredge.com/land-rover/range-rover-evoque/depreciation) and broader analyses showing steep luxury brand dropoffs: [iSeeCars resale analysis](https://www.iseecars.com/resale-value-brands/land-rover).
  - Practical takeaway: pay market price, not sticker‑shock; the Evoque loses value — that can be a buyer advantage if you accept the TCO.

- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy & PHEV math
  - Build your TCO model around three big levers: depreciation, servicing/repairs, and fuel/energy. Use Edmunds TCO examples as a methodology for lifecycle calculation — they include depreciation, fuel, insurance and repair estimates: [Edmunds True Cost to Own (2023 Evoque)](https://www.edmunds.com/land-rover/range-rover-evoque/2023/cost-to-own/).
  - Fuel & PHEV: the P300e PHEV posts very low official L/100 km — on paper 1.4–1.7 L/100 km WLTP and quoted electric range ~60–66 km — but reality depends on charging discipline. If you can charge daily and your typical commute is <50 km round trip, the PHEV reduces fuel cost markedly and can justify higher purchase premiums; if you rarely charge, the PHEV’s extra weight and complexity will hurt economy and servicing. See driving‑real world caveats: [Auto‑Data P300e specs](https://www.auto-data.net/en/land-rover-range-rover-evoque-ii-1.5-p300e-309hp-plug-in-hybrid-awd-automatic-40728) and running costs commentary: [DrivingElectric PHEV page](https://www.drivingelectric.com/land-rover/range-rover-evoque/running-costs).
  - Servicing & repairs: expect higher annual services than a mainstream compact SUV. Labour rates and parts at Land Rover dealers in Australia will be premium; factor in occasional electronics and ADAS recalibration costs. Use the Edmunds TCO framework and inflate repair line items by 20–30% relative to a VW/Skoda class car to be conservative.

- Buying recommendation (clear final guidance)
  - If you want the Evoque because you want the look, badge and a compact luxurious city cruiser, go in with eyes open: check VIN for recalls, insist on proof of software/recall compliance (touchscreen/infotainment recall N972 and camera/ADAS recalls), and pay for a full pre‑purchase inspection (PPI) that includes a scan for historic codes and ADAS/calibration status. Use the official recall lookup for VIN checks: [Land Rover recall VIN checker](https://www.landroverusa.com/ownership/vin-recall.html) and consult NHTSA/recall PDFs where applicable.
  - Which year/trim is the sweet spot? From an Aussie used market value vs risk perspective: a 2020–2021 petrol/low‑kilometre Evoque (non‑diesel) with a full service history and completed software/recall work is the practical sweet spot. The NSW live listing you provided — a 2021 with 85,085 km asking $38,990 — could be fair if:
    - It has complete service history with Land Rover or reputable specialist stamps,
    - All dealer recalls/TSBs are marked complete (infotainment/camera/DC‑DC where applicable),
    - The gearbox shifts smoothly across all ratios and the PPI confirms no major engine/transmission codes.
  - Avoid older diesel Evoques used principally for short urban runs unless you can prove DPF/EGR health and recent long‑drive regeneration history. Avoid cars with unresolved infotainment or ADAS errors even if they’re cheap — the fix can be surprisingly expensive.
  - If you plan to keep the car >5 years and do a lot of high‑kilometre rural driving, consider a well‑spec’d petrol or the PHEV if you can reliably charge — otherwise the cost of ownership might compound.

Final blunt take: you buy an Evoque for the badge and cabin experience. That comes at the cost of higher servicing, a history of infotainment and electronics niggles, and potential diesel DPF/timing chain risk in older diesel Ingeniums. If you want sharp value from depreciation and you’re mechanically wary, buy a well‑maintained petrol‑engined 2019–2021 Evoque with documented recall work done and negotiate the $38k‑$45k band depending on kilometres. If you’re a charging‑disciplined commuter and can stretch to a certified PHEV with complete service records, it’s the best long‑term running‑cost bet — otherwise be ready to spend on repairs and lock down security measures (tracker + immobiliser).
