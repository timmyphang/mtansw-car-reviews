#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: Fifth‑generation Discovery (internal code: L462). Production timeframe: 2017–present (this is the body style and architecture you’ll see on most “modern” Discoveries in Australian classifieds). (Wikipedia: [Land Rover Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Rover_Discovery))
- Product hierarchy: Discovery sits above the compact Discovery Sport and below the Range Rover family — it’s Land Rover’s full‑size, three‑row, family/adventure SUV offering. The Discovery is positioned as a more practical, less couture alternative to Range Rover but more capable and up‑market than the Discovery Sport. (Land Rover models pages: [landrover.com — Discovery models & specs](https://www.landrover.com/discovery/discovery/models-and-specifications.html))
- Platform and parts commonality: the L462 moved the Discovery to a modern unibody architecture used across contemporary Land Rover products and shares many powertrains, electronics and suspension sub‑assemblies with other Jaguar Land Rover (JLR) family vehicles — that helps parts availability and servicing (dealers and specialist breakers stock shared components). See Land Rover’s model/spec pages and technical sheets for the engine/transmission family overlaps. ([landrover Models and Specs PDF](https://www.landrover.com/discovery/discovery/models-and-specifications.html); technical spec PDF: [Land Rover Discovery TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION 2025 (PDF)](https://www.landrover.com/content/dam/lrdx/pdfs/xi/wltp/Land-Rover-Discovery-TD-Insert-1L4622600000XIEN01P.pdf))
- Market‑specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drive configurations (what you’ll find in Australia and in global spec pages):
  - Petrol MHEV: **P300** — ~2.0‑litre I4 MHEV (approx. 300 PS in some markets); **P360 / P360‑I6** — 3.0‑litre inline‑6 mild‑hybrid in higher trims (around 360 PS). Both use automatic transmissions and AWD. (Land Rover tech/spec pages; Edmunds summary: [2025 Land Rover Discovery specs](https://www.edmunds.com/land-rover/discovery/2025/suv/features-specs/))
  - Diesel MHEV: **D250 / D350** — 3.0‑litre diesel I6 mild‑hybrids in some markets (outputs depend on tune). Auto + AWD. (Land Rover spec PDFs)
  - Older market & secondhand stock you'll meet in Australia: earlier V6 diesel/petrol derivatives (2.7 TDV6, various V8s in older derivatives), and the various Ingenium 2.0 I4 engines that entered the line‑up since 2017. (What‑Breaks buyer guide: [L462 engines & faults](https://what-breaks.com/land-rover/discovery-l462/); Wikipedia)
- Chief rivals in the Australian market: Lexus GX, Toyota Land Cruiser/LandCruiser Prado (depending on size), Jeep Grand Cherokee/Wagoneer, Volvo XC90, Audi Q7, BMW X5/X7 and other premium seven‑seat SUVs. (The Car Connection & WhatCar comparisons: [CarConnection rival list](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/land-rover_discovery_choices); [What Car? best seven‑seat SUVs](https://www.whatcar.com/best/7-seat-cars/n2344))

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Land Rover Discovery?
This section is the nuts‑and‑bolts body‑blow diagnosis — what owners actually fix, how often and at what cost. If you’re buying one, read this like a checklist you intend to tick off at inspection time.

Exterior
- Panel fit & trim:
  - Expect occasional panel alignment inconsistencies and trim fallout — owners report A‑pillar trim issues and loose roof mouldings on the 2017–2024 models. Check join lines around the A‑pillars, roof rails and roof moulds for movement or mismatched paint/clips. ([Go‑Parts: exterior trim issues 2017–2024](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-land-rover-discovery-2017-2024-2))
  - Small gloss/black plastic bits scratch easy — cosmetic repairs common but inexpensive compared with mechanical issues. Check lower bumpers for chips in high‑use cars.
- Paint durability:
  - No mass‑recall on paint, but look for stone chips and underside scratches if the vehicle has been used off‑road — Discovery owners like getting dirty.
- Body recalls & safety items:
  - Historical recall for Autonomous Emergency Braking (AEB) being unavailable affected some 2018 Discoveries — check for open campaigns against the VIN. Don’t assume the car is done; ask the seller for recall history. (NHTSA recall bulletin: [AEB not available — recall N227 (18V‑625)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2018/RCMN-18V625-1892.pdf); CarComplaints summary: [Recall N227](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Land_Rover/Discovery/2018/recalls/autonomous-emergency-braking-system-failure-18v625000.shtml))
- ADAS quirks:
  - Owners report AEB, lane‑keep and adaptive cruise systems sometimes disable without clear cause; symptoms can be linked to sensor calibration issues, software bugs or unrelated electrical gremlins. Some firmware/service‑bulletin fixes exist but outcomes vary by vehicle and service history — make sure ADAS warnings are absent during a test drive and request history of software/ECU updates. (Complaints & forum threads: [dot.report — 2025 Discovery complaints](https://dot.report/vehicle/land-rover/discovery/2025/complaints); Discovery forum threads: [Disco5 technical faults](https://www.disco5forums.co.uk/viewforum.php?f=11))

Interior
- Infotainment & screens:
  - The JLR Pivi Pro / touchscreens have known failure modes: blank screens, ghost touches, and random blackouts. There was a recall/field action in later model years for touchscreen failures (blanking at start). If the screen disappears, it can disable ADAS warnings and sometimes essential vehicle menus. Ask to see the infotainment working cold (start‑up) and on‑road. ([CarComplaints — touchscreen failures recall 2025](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2025/land-rover-touchscreen-failures-recall.shtml); Go‑Parts diagnostic guide for Pivi Pro failures: [Infotainment display guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-land-rover-range-rover-sport-land-rover-range-rover-jaguar-e-pace-2015-2026))
  - USB ports and wireless chargers are finicky in real use — frequent reports of intermittent Apple CarPlay/Android Auto dropout and charging issues. Test every USB port and wireless pad with the seller’s phone. ([Go‑Parts USB/charging guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/usb-port-land-rover-range-rover-evoque-land-rover-range-rover-velar-land-rover-range-rover-2017-2024))
- Durability under heavy use:
  - As a rideshare/taxi prospect, the Discovery is a poor choice unless you want constant retrofit work. Leather, powered seats and electronics are costly to replace; carpets/door sills can show rapid wear. If the car’s been used for commercial duty, the electrics (powered tailgate, seats) often show accelerated wear.
- Entry/egress:
  - Tall door sills and a higher seating position are strengths for family use — but check door check arms and hinges for creak, especially if the previous owner had kids or heavy usage.

Mechanically
- General reputation:
  - The Discovery trades refinement for complexity — it’s a very capable machine, but complexity brings higher maintenance frequency and cost compared with mainstream rivals. Aggregated reliability trackers flag above‑average rates of electrical and suspension complaints. (OwnerKeep reliability summary: [OwnerKeep — Discovery reliability](https://ownerkeep.com/models/land-rover-discovery))
- Theft & security vulnerabilities:
  - JLR has acknowledged the rise in keyless theft methods and has pushed BCM (Body Control Module) security updates and Ultra‑Wide Band (UWB) protections for many models. Ask the seller if the vehicle received the security update (JLR has offered complimentary BCM updates for vehicles from 2016 onward in some markets). If not done, the Discovery is a higher‑risk target for relay/CAN attacks; an aftermarket hard immobiliser is recommended. ([JLR security release](https://media.landrover.com/en-gb/news/2023/11/jlr-invests-vehicle-security-help-reduce-thefts); AM‑Online security rollout: [JLR fast‑tracking security updates](https://www.am-online.com/news/jlr-accelerates-security-rollout-to-battle-keyless-car-theft-of-older-models); aftermarket option: [Autowatch Ghost immobiliser advert](https://autowatch-ghost.co.uk/product/autowatch-ghost-2-immobiliser-land-rover-defender-range-rover-discovery-evoque-velar/))
- Recommended anti‑theft measures:
  - Confirm BCM/UWB updates.
  - Fit a stand‑alone CAN‑bus‑domain immobiliser (e.g., Autowatch Ghost) or steering‑column lock as an additional barrier.
  - Keep software updates logged in service records.

Engines
Below are the engine variants flagged by owners and technical write‑ups; each engine gets an italicised subsection with its failure patterns and suggested preventative practice. Where mileage risks are quoted, they are those reported by owners and technical blogs — use them as red flags, not absolutes.

- *2.0‑litre Ingenium I4 petrol (e.g., P300)*
  - Typical issues: turbocharger actuator faults and occasional oil consumption reports; electrical sensors/pumps on the timing chain side can cause warning lights. Pivi infotainment integration can affect engine diagnostics (symptom masking) if the vehicle has outstanding software faults.
  - Preventative maintenance: oil and filter every 10,000 km for hard use (rideshare, towing) or max 15,000 km for normal private use; inspect turbo and boost hoses ~80,000 km; software/ECU updates at dealer intervals.
  - Notes: fuel consumption in mixed driving will usually be in the high teens L/100km for heavy suburban loads — check manufacturer WLTP/EPA numbers for your variant. (Official specs: [Land Rover technical PDF](https://www.landrover.com/content/dam/lrdx/pdfs/xi/wltp/Land-Rover-Discovery-TD-Insert-1L4622600000XIEN01P.pdf))

- *3.0‑litre inline‑6 petrol mild‑hybrid (P360 / I6 MHEV)*
  - Typical issues: complexity of electrified ancillaries; higher repair bills for turbo/intercooler or exhaust aftertreatment systems; software dependency for MHEV systems (needs current firmware).
  - Preventative maintenance: oil & filter 10,000–15,000 km, careful attention to cooling system service (water pump/thermostat) at 100,000 km; dealer software checks to ensure MHEV system interlocks are healthy.
  - Notes: the I6 MHEV is smoother and stronger but replacement costs for major incidents are higher. (Land Rover spec & outputs: [Land Rover technical PDF 2025](https://www.landrover.com/content/dam/lrdx/pdfs/xi/wltp/Land-Rover-Discovery-TD-Insert-1L4622600000XIEN01P.pdf))

- *3.0‑litre diesel I6 (D250 / D350 MHEV)*
  - Typical issues: turbo and EGR clogging on long‑life diesels; DPF regeneration problems if the car has been used for mainly short trips. Diesel turbocharger and soot‑related issues can be expensive.
  - Preventative maintenance: service every 10,000–15,000 km, ensure regular high‑speed runs for DPF regeneration or force regeneration service as required; check turbo and EGR condition around 120,000 km.
  - Notes: diesels hold torque and towing ability, but expect higher repair bills for turbo/EGR/DPF work vs petrol I4s. (Technical and owner reports: [Autodoc common problems](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-land-rover-discovery); What‑Breaks L462 list)

- *2.7 TDV6 (older V6 diesel used on earlier Discovery generations)*
  - Typical issues: known for fragile plastic coolant components, oil‑pump casing concerns and turbo/EGR trouble. Some TDV6 engines have had examples of oil pump or head gasket failure leading to catastrophic damage.
  - Preventative maintenance: pre‑purchase check for coolant leaks, oil pressure history; oil change to manufacturer standard every 10,000 km or sooner if heavy use. If buying an older TDV6, insist on a compression and leak‑down test.
  - Sources: technical guides and historical problem lists: [RangerRoverGarage TDV6 faults](https://rangerovergarage.co.uk/land-rover-discovery-3-2-7-tdv6-engine-problems/) and aggregated owner posts.

- *TD5 (older turbo‑diesel series)*
  - Typical issues: the TD5 oil pump and early‑life failures around 80,000–120,000 miles in many reports; ECU water ingress and seized turbo wastegates also appear frequently.
  - Preventative maintenance: if you encounter a TD5 in the marketplace, ensure documented early engine overhauls or component replacements; avoid high‑mileage TD5s without full history. (TD5 problems summary: [MotorHungry TD5 problems](https://motorhungry.com/land-rover-td5-engine-problems/))

Transmission
- Behaviour & known failure modes:
  - Automatic torque‑converter gearboxes in the Discovery can show shudder between gear changes, slipping, erratic shift or limp‑home mode. Common root causes are low/dirty ATF, valve body wear, and torque converter issues. Owners report that if a shudder persists after a proper ATF change, the torque converter or valve body may require replacement — pricey work. ([YouCanic — Land Rover automatic transmission problems](https://www.youcanic.com/land-rover-automatic-transmission-problems/); forum threads on torque converter replacement)
- Preventative steps:
  - Inspect the transmission fluid colour and level (service records should show ATF change intervals). For high‑use vehicles, a fluid and filter service every 60,000–80,000 km is prudent; for uncertain previous care, assume a gearbox service is due.
  - Road test under load: look for delayed up‑shifts, harsh downshifts, shudder around 1,800–2,800 rpm and consistent limp‑home behaviour. If present, budget for a transmission MRI (diagnostic + fluid/op filter + possible valve body work).

  - Price range: AUD $31,950 – $31,950
  - Median price: AUD $31,950
  - Year range: 2017 – 2017
  - Median odometer: 2,017 km
  - Transmission mix: Sports Automatic: 1
  - Commentary on that snapshot: one 2017 example at $31,950 with only 2,017 km is very likely a demonstrator trade‑in, ex‑fleet/demo or a remarketed ex‑lease with dealer prepping — investigate the car’s full history and ask why the ultra‑low odometer reading. If it’s an ex‑insurance write off or has a cloned/replaced odometer, the dealer should disclose it. (NSW data provided above)
- Price band & depreciation:
  - Broader used market: online aggregators show wide pricing swings for the Discovery depending on year and kilometres — average used Discovery prices hover in the high tens of thousands to mid‑$40k+ range depending on model year. Sites like CarGurus and iSeeCars highlight significant depreciation vs BMW/Audi rivals. CarEdge / iSeeCars note a strong depreciation curve — around ~58–65% over 5 years in aggregated analyses. ([CarEdge depreciation](https://caredge.com/land-rover/discovery/depreciation); [iSeeCars resale value](https://www.iseecars.com/car/land-rover-discovery/resale-value))
  - Practical takeaway: you buy one for capability and cachet, not for capital preservation. The Discovery will depreciate more than German rivals on average.
- Depreciation example:
  - Kelley Blue Book-type assessments and CarGurus trends show that the Discovery drops steeply in the first 3–5 years, often losing ~40–60% of new cost depending on spec and market conditions. ([KBB depreciation example](https://www.kbb.com/land-rover/discovery/2023/p360-metropolitan/))
- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy:
  - Use a simple framework: purchase price + financing + insurance + expected servicing/repairs + fuel + depreciation over ownership period.
  - Sources to build the numbers:
    - Edmunds TCO tool shows modern Discoveries have a high 5‑year TCO driven by depreciation and anticipated repair/maintenance costs — example 5‑year totals for 2025 models approach ~$90–100k USD for higher spec models under US assumptions. ([Edmunds True Cost to Own — Discovery 2025](https://www.edmunds.com/land-rover/discovery/2025/cost-to-own/))
    - CarEdge provides make‑level ownership expectation tables that show Land Rover ownership costs skew higher versus mainstream rivals. ([CarEdge costs](https://caredge.com/land-rover/costs))
  - Fuel & electrified options:
    - Mild‑hybrids (MHEV) used across the Discovery line improve refinement and cut some consumption but don’t transform ownership economics the way full hybrids/PHEVs do. PHEVs on Discovery Sport demonstrate huge WLTP/EPA numbers when regularly charged, but real‑world savings depend on regular charging and driving patterns. JLR’s PHEV PR for Discovery Sport highlights that PHEVs are only that economical if you charge often and use EV mode for daily commutes. ([JLR PHEV press release — Discovery Sport P300e](https://www.jlr.com/news/2020/04/bestselling-evoque-and-discovery-sport-suvs-now-available-plug-hybrids-all-electric))
    - Edmunds TCO comparisons show fuel savings from electrified options are frequently offset by higher depreciation, insurance and (sometimes) higher financing costs — you must model your kilometres and charging habits. ([Edmunds TCO Discovery 2025](https://www.edmunds.com/land-rover/discovery/2025/cost-to-own/))
  - Practical TCO advice:
    - If you do <20,000 km/year and can charge a PHEV regularly, a PHEV (Discovery Sport P300e as proxy) can save fuel dollars — but the full‑size Discovery PHEV variants are rare in Australia and premium depreciation can negate fuel savings unless you keep the car long term and charge every day.
    - If most of your driving is mixed highway and you tow regularly, a diesel MHEV or the I6 petrol MHEV can be the best compromise — better longevity under load than a PHEV that never gets charged.
- Final buying recommendation (clear and blunt):
  - Proceed with caution. The Discovery is brilliant at what it does — big family SUV that can tow, seat seven and go off‑road — but it comes with complexity and higher running costs than mainstream rivals.
  - If you want one: aim for a 2019–2021 model with a documented dealer service history, completed BCM/security updates (post‑2018 security rollouts), and all ADAS/infotainment software updates applied. Avoid cars with sketchy service records or unresolved ADAS/display faults.
  - The NSW listing at AUD $31,950 for a 2017 Discovery with 2,017 km looks like a potentially good deal on paper — but that’s the exact scenario where you lean on paperwork and inspection:
    - Ask for full dealer service records, VIN history, any insurance/accident/title notes and recall completion printouts.
    - Get a pre‑purchase inspection from a Land Rover‑experienced technician (not just a general mechanic).
    - Confirm the touchscreen and ADAS work on a road test, and that there are no “AEB not available” warnings (recall N227 history).
  - Sweet spot: If you want the best value/peace‑of‑mind mix, buy a well‑maintained 2019–2021 example (mid‑range trim) with ~80–140,000 km and a full dealer service history — you’ll avoid early depreciation cliffs and most first‑owner teething electronic work. If you can find a low‑km 2017 example in dealer warranty or with a recent major service and confirmed security/ADAS fixes, that can be a bargain — but verify everything.

Final words — no sugarcoating: the Discovery is a desirable Australian SUV that will happily tow your caravan to the Snowies and swallow three car‑seats plus a dog. It is not a cheap car to maintain if you let small faults compound into big ones. Buy carefully, do your homework, get the software/security updates confirmed in writing and budget for maintenance like you mean it. If you love the Discovery’s capability, you’ve got to love the paperwork too.
