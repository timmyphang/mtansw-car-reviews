#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

- Generation, timeframe and internal codes
  - The cars you’re looking at in the NSW dealer snapshot are the 2nd‑generation Audi A5 (commonly referred to as the B9 family, facelifted through the 2017–2023 cycle) — the 2021 cars sit squarely in that B9/face‑lift era. The new A5 generation (B10 / MY24–25 onwards) is already rolling out globally but the used market in Australia is still thick with B9 examples. See Audi’s model pages and the historical summary on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_A5) and Audi’s recent press materials for the newer generation [here](https://www.audi.com/en/presskits/the-new-audi-a5-models-16102).

- Product hierarchy
  - The A5 is essentially Audi’s stylish coupe/grand‑tourer variant of the A4 range — think A4 underpinnings dressed-up in coupe/Sportback form. It shares showroom real estate and a buyer pool with the A4 (same bones) and sits below the larger A6 class. If you want more space but the same badge, look at the A4/A6/Avant family; if you want quieter prestige, the A6 sits above it.

- Shared global platform architecture and other models sharing it
  - The A5 is derived from the A4 platform heritage — Volkswagen Group’s modular platforms historically underpinning Audi’s mid‑size models. That shared architecture means parts and servicing knowledge are widely available across the Audi dealer network and independent VW/Audi workshops. See the A5/A4 relationship on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_A5) and Audi’s model drive‑technology pages for the new family [here](https://www.audi.com/en/the-new-audi-a5-models-modern-sportiness-meets-premium-proportions-16261/drive-technology-16281).

- Market‑specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drive layouts (what you’ll see in Australia)
  - Common petrol engines you’ll encounter in Australian used A5s:
    - **2.0‑litre turbo four** (badged TFSI on many cars) — typically the workhorse for most A5 Sportback/Coupé/Sportback lines, fitted to 7‑speed **S tronic** dual‑clutch or 8‑speed **Tiptronic** in quattro variants. Official combined WLTP figures for a contemporary 2.0 A5 sit in the mid‑6–7.5 L/100 km band ([Auto‑Data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/audi-a5-model-506), [Audi drive technology](https://www.audi.com/en/the-new-audi-a5-models-modern-sportiness-meets-premium-proportions-16261/drive-technology-16281)).
    - **3.0‑litre V6** in S/S5 derivatives (and higher‑output petrol units) — typically quattro AWD with Tiptronic automatics for stronger acceleration and torque.
    - Diesel variants (2.0 TDI) appear in some markets and on earlier B9 cars, but Australian uptake was modest compared with Europe.
    - Plug‑in e‑hybrid variants and 48V mild‑hybrid (MHEV) systems are now part of the A5 family in newer model years — Audi provides details on mild‑hybrid tech and the e‑hybrid numbers in their specs [presskit](https://www.audi.com/en/presskits/the-new-audi-a5-models-16102) and e‑hybrid spec PDFs ([MY26 Avant spec](https://emea-dam.audi.com/adobe/assets/urn%3Aaaid%3Aaem%3A28f7a93a-37e1-48ac-bec7-667711f33288/original/as/A5-Avant-MY26-Specifications1.pdf)).
  - Drive configurations: front‑biased for base 2.0 models, **quattro** AWD for mid/high trims and V6 models.
  - Transmissions commonly present on Australian examples: 7‑speed **S tronic** (DCT) and 8‑speed **Tiptronic** torque‑converter automatics.

- Marketplace rivals
  - Direct competitors: BMW 4‑Series, Mercedes‑Benz C‑Class / C‑Class coupe or coupe‑style variants, Lexus IS, Volvo S60/V60 — see comparison lists at [The Car Connection](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/audi_a5_choices) and broader competitor roundups ([Carwow](https://www.carwow.co.uk/editorial/best-cars/alternatives/best-audi-a5-alternatives)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Audi A5?

**Exterior**
- Panel fit & trim:
  - Expect generally tidy German fitment but don’t be surprised to find panel‑gap oddities or trim misalignments on older or dealer‑traded cars — owner threads call this out for the B9 family ([AudiWorld](https://www.audiworld.com/forums/audi-a5-s5-rs5-b9-220/fit-finish-issue-2923503/)). Inspect door gaps, bonnet seams and rear bumper clips closely.
  - Black glossy B‑pillar and piano‑black trim on Sportback/coupe variants can peel, fade or have clear‑coat flaking — a recurring cosmetic gripe documented for 2018–2024 cars ([Go‑Parts trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-audi-a4-audi-s4-audi-a5-2009-2025)).
- Paint durability:
  - Clear‑coat flaking has shown up on older Audis in owner forums; not endemic but visible on high‑sun, coastal cars ([Audizine clear coat thread](https://www.audizine.com/threads/clear-coat-peeling.506050/)).
- Recalls and body campaigns:
  - Use VIN recall searches — Audi/authorities maintain active portals — check USA recall lists or NHTSA if buying import or thinking about outstanding campaigns ([Audi USA recalls](https://www.audiusa.com/en/recalls/) and [NHTSA lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)). Aggregators also list A5 recalls historically ([RecallExplained](https://recallexplained.com/audi/a5/)).
- ADAS/sensor quirks:
  - **Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC)** and lane assist warnings aren’t rare on later B9 models — dealers have issued TSBs (software updates) for intermittent ACC and lane‑assist failures. If the cluster flashes “Adaptive Cruise Control unavailable” or similar, that isn’t necessarily an expensive sensor swap — start by checking for a software update/TSB compliance ([TSB 2079926/1 on DOT.report](https://dot.report/bulletins/11026253); summary at [carandproblems](https://carandproblems.com/bulletins/audi/a5/11027415-audi-a5-driver-assist-systems)). Front radar units and cameras require professional recalibration after replacement and can be costly if damaged ([Go‑Parts ADAS guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/video-camera-audi-a5-audi-q5-audi-s5-2017-2025)).

**Interior**
- Wear & durability:
  - Premature wear shows up on centre‑console trim, armrests and leather in older/real‑used cars or rideshare duty. There are multiple owner reports of scruffed trim near the shifter and faded touchpoints ([AudiWorld forum threads](https://www.audiworld.com/forums/audi-a5-s5-rs5-b9-220/worn-interior-trim-next-gear-shifter-3084027/)).
- Infotainment & electronics:
  - **MMI** screen freezing, rebooting or restricted functions are commonly reported across the A5 family (owner complaint clusters and TSBs). A 2025 TSB addresses infotainment/connectivity faults with software fixes — check if the dealer applied those updates ([TSB 2079981/1](https://carandproblems.com/bulletins/audi/a5/11026270-2025-audi-infotainment-restricted-functions); MMI troubleshooting guide [DriversAdvice](https://driversadvice.com/audi-mmi-not-working/)).
  - Phone Box/USB/Wireless charger modules can fail — symptoms include non‑recognition, intermittent charging or faults which sometimes need module replacement ([Go‑Parts USB/Phone Box guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/usb-port-audi-q7-audi-q8-audi-a3-2016-2026)).
- Entry/egress & practicality:
  - Coupe and Sportback doors mean slightly higher sills than the A4 saloon — older owners report respectable entry/exit but check door check angles and sill scuffs if the vehicle has high‑km use or frequent passenger swapping (practical observation; compare A4 vs A5 doors via Audi spec pages).
- Electrical drain patterns:
  - Infotainment modules, phone boxes and aftermarket devices are common culprits for 12V battery drain on modern Audis. If the car sits for days, check the 12V voltage retaining and start cycles; a healthy battery and recent service history help. If you see multiple aftermarket alarm/phone modules, ask why.

**Mechanically**
- Brand engineering reputation:
  - Audi’s engineering is broadly sophisticated — lots of aluminium, tight tolerances and electronics — but that complexity raises repair bills once out of warranty. Expect good durable chassis bits but premium parts & labour from dealers.
- Security & theft vulnerabilities:
  - Modern A5s with keyless or connected modules can be targeted by relay attacks or by the newer CAN injection technique (thieves physically access wiring and inject CAN frames to spoof the key/immobiliser). This is an industry‑wide problem affecting many keyless cars — Audi included. Read the technical write‑up and coverage here: [Ken Tindell on CAN Injection](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/) and aggregated reports ([Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update)). Layered mitigation is required — see the buy/advice section below.

- Suggested practical anti‑theft measures (cheap and proven):
  - Fit an OBD‑port lock or tamper plate, high‑quality steering wheel lock (visible deterrent), an independent GPS tracker with tamper alert, and consider an immobiliser upgrade from a reputable installer. Park under cameras or at well‑lit facilities; don’t rely only on OEM passive security.

**Engines**
- General note: timing chain/tensioner problems are the headline gearbox for many modern turbocharged Audis — especially around the 1.8/2.0/3.0 families. Multiple technical posts and owner reports flag timing‑system wear, oil consumption and turbo/HPFP complaints on certain TFSI engines ([timing chain problems overview](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-audi/audi-a5/timing-chain-problems-with-audi-a5-tfsi-engines/), [EnginePatrol common problems](https://enginepatrol.com/audi-a5-common-problems/)).

- _*1.8 I4*_
  - Key issues: timing‑chain guide/tensioner wear (rattle on cold start), oil consumption in older high‑miles, intake valve carbon on direct injection engines. Owner‑reported failures often appear in the 80,000–150,000 km window if maintenance was light ([TimingChainInfo summary](https://www.timingbeltinfo.com/audi-a5), [carnews timing chain article](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-audi/audi-a5/timing-chain-problems-with-audi-a5-tfsi-engines/)).
  - Preventative maintenance: service oil changes ideally at 10,000 km intervals with OEM spec oil; inspect for rattle and have chain/tensioner checked if cold start rattle appears.

- _*2.0 TFSI*_
  - Key issues: the 2.0 turbo is ubiquitous but comes with complaints: excessive oil consumption, timing‑chain tensioner failures, turbocharger wastegate/actuator issues and carbon buildup on intake valves (direct‑inject engines). Timing‑chain symptoms typically appear from ~80,000 km in poorly maintained cars ([CarJustify 2.0 TFSI problems](https://carjustify.com/audi-2-0-tfsi-problems/), [Autodoc guide](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-audi-a5)).
  - Preventative maintenance: strict 10,000 km oil change service interval; pre‑purchase compression/leak‑down and oil‑consumption checks. If the chain/tensioner has been replaced, ask for receipts.

- _*3.0 V6*_
  - Key issues: smoother and stronger but more expensive to repair. On higher‑miles watch oil leaks, timing/chain guides on some early V6 variants, and the usual turbo/intercooler plumbing in forced‑induction versions. When fitted with Tiptronic and quattro, watch transmission and transfer case service history.
  - Preventative maintenance: 10,000 km oil services, inspect for oil leaks around head gaskets & timing covers.

- _*TDI diesels*_
  - Key issues (mainly European market): EGR/clogging, DPF regeneration problems if used mostly short trips; injector and turbo wear on high km cars. Diesels are less common in Australian A5s but do show up.
  - Preventative maintenance: long‑run highway drives to clean DPF; regular AdBlue and DPF monitoring.

- _*e‑hybrid / MHEV variants*_
  - Key issues: battery health, high‑voltage system software and charging system reliability; the hardware is newish so long‑term reliability curves aren’t fully matured in the used market. Audi published WLTP ranges and battery specs for e‑hybrids in official PDFs ([MY26 specs show e‑hybrid battery & range](https://emea-dam.audi.com/adobe/assets/urn%3Aaaid%3Aaem%3A28f7a93a-37e1-48ac-bec7-667711f33288/original/as/A5-Avant-MY26-Specifications1.pdf)). Auto‑Data lists combined WLTP electricity consumption and fuel numbers for e‑hybrid A5s ([Auto‑Data e‑hybrid entry](https://www.auto-data.net/en/audi-a5-avant-b10-2.0-tfsi-367hp-e-hybrid-quattro-ultra-s-tronic-54003)).
  - Preventative maintenance: check hybrid battery health logs, charging cycles, and get a dealer read for HV battery capacity; if the car is mainly urban and you can charge frequently, hybrids can be sensible — more on that in the TCO section.

**Transmission**
- Known behaviours & failure modes:
  - **7‑speed S tronic (DCT)**: can show mechatronic faults, abrupt clutch engagement or shudder under certain conditions; expensive to fix if clutch packs or mechatronics fail. Regular software updates and clutch adaptations can extend life.
  - **Tiptronic torque‑converter automatics**: known for torque‑converter lock‑up shudder, delayed engagement or slipping if fluid maintenance is neglected. Owner reports and community diagnostics point to torque converter clutch wear or valve body issues as common causes ([700r4 transmission shudder article](https://www.700r4transmissionhq.com/audi-a5-torque-converter-shuddering-causes/), owner case reports ([MyCarly community](https://www.mycarly.com/community/audi-a5-torque-converter-clutch-stuck-causing-shudder-at-cruise-e715d661-9c87-40be-876e-0827d75bf01d/))).
  - Transmission fluid change intervals matter — neglected fluid ages the clutch and valve bodies. Several transmission‑specific analyses recommend proactive fluid changes and software updates where available ([TransmissionKnowledge overview](https://transmissionknowledge.com/audi/audi-a5-transmission-problems-facts-and-insights/)).
- Pre‑purchase checklist:
  - Check for smooth cold‑and‑hot gear changes, no shudder at cruising speeds, and no delayed engagement. Get OEM diagnostic logs for DCT clutch count/adaptations if possible and check service receipts for transmission fluid changes.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Audi A5?

  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 2
  - Bottom line: the NSW dealer market for 2021 A5s is narrow but the median ~AUD $43.5k for a 2021 with ~77k km is a realistic trading price in that snapshot.

- Depreciation metrics
  - Historical figures indicate Audi A5s depreciate materially but not catastrophically: iSeeCars puts a 3‑year depreciation around ~29.4% and a five‑year fall close to ~45–47% ([iSeeCars depreciation summary](https://www.iseecars.com/car/audi-a5/resale-value)). CarEdge and Edmunds track similar declines in the luxury compact segment — expect faster early depreciation than mainstream cars but better residuals than some marques when well specified and maintained ([CarEdge A5 maintenance and costs](https://caredge.com/audi/a5-sportback/maintenance), [Edmunds 2021 values](https://www.edmunds.com/audi/a5/2021/appraisal-value/)).

- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) considerations & hybrid vs petrol math
  - Use published TCO tools: Edmunds’ True Cost to Own is a solid starting point; they estimate five‑year ownership totals factoring depreciation, fuel, insurance and repairs for current A5 configs ([Edmunds TCO](https://www.edmunds.com/audi/a5/2025/cost-to-own/)). CarEdge provides a complementary multi‑year maintenance outlook ([CarEdge costs](https://caredge.com/audi/a5-sportback/costs)).
  - Fuel consumption baselines:
    - **2.0 TFSI A5**: WLTP combined ~6.8–7.6 L/100 km ([Auto‑Data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/audi-a5-model-506)).
    - **A5 e‑hybrid**: combined WLTP varies dramatically with mix — as low as ~2.2 L/100 km if you mostly run electric for short trips, or comparable to petrol if you never charge. Electricity consumption ~15 kWh/100 km with pure electric ranges listed on Audi specs ([Auto‑Data e‑hybrid](https://www.auto-data.net/en/audi-a5-avant-b10-2.0-tfsi-367hp-e-hybrid-quattro-ultra-s-tronic-54003), [Audi e‑hybrid battery spec](https://emea-dam.audi.com/adobe/assets/.../A5-Avant-MY26-Specifications1.pdf)).
  - Practical TCO rule of thumb for Australia:
    - If you do lots of short urban trips (daily commute <50 km round trip), have reliable home/work charging and run <30–40k km/year, a PHEV/e‑hybrid can cut fuel bills and may justify a modest premium. If you’re a regular highway driver or don’t charge, the e‑hybrid premium rarely pays back in fuel savings.
  - Maintenance & repair risk premium:
    - Factor in higher repair costs for Audi parts and scheduled dealer servicing every ~10,000 km (recommended to keep turbo engines and timing chains healthy). CarEdge projects increasing maintenance/repair costs by year 5 compared with a mainstream sedan ([CarEdge maintenance](https://caredge.com/audi/a5-sportback/maintenance)).

- Buying recommendation — the short, blunt version
  - If you want an A5 for looks, quality interior, and a German chassis with reasonable running dynamics, a well‑looked‑after 2021 A5 at ~AUD $43k with a full dealer service history is an acceptable buy — provided you do your homework.
  - Critical musts before signing:
    - Full, dated service history to 10,000 km intervals (or better). If previous owner stretched services to 15,000+ km, treat the car as higher risk.
    - Evidence that any ADAS/infotainment TSBs and software updates have been applied (ask the dealer for records of software campaigns, e.g., driver‑assist and MMI fixes referenced in the 2025 bulletins).
    - A pre‑purchase inspection (PPI) focusing on: timing‑chain noise (cold start rattle), oil consumption rate, S tronic/Tiptronic behaviour, ADAS radar/camera health, and battery/phone box diagnostics.
    - Check for aftermarket alarm, OBD locks or trackers (if none and the car is keyless, consider upgrading security — see CAN injection mitigation notes above).
  - Which year/trim is the best value sweet spot?
    - From a financial and risk perspective in the NSW snapshot, the 2021 2.0‑litre A5 with quattro only if the car has solid service history and <100k km represents the sweet spot. Avoid high‑km (>120k km) 2.0 TFSI cars without chain/tensioner receipts. If you can find a low‑km e‑hybrid with proofs of battery health and charging habits, that’s attractive only if your driving matches urban recharge‑heavy use.
  - Final bottom line:
    - Buy if: dealer PPI is clean, timing chain service/history is good, ADAS/MMI TSBs applied and price matches the market (the NSW median ~AUD $43.5k is a good benchmark).
    - Walk away if: service records are thin, the car makes timing‑chain rattle, transmission symptoms are present, or it’s heavily optioned/modified in ways that hide past issues.

Final practical checklist (print and take to the dealer)
- Service book receipts to 10,000 km intervals (or documented dealer service).
- Ask dealer to confirm TSB/software update history for ADAS & MMI.
- Cold start engine listen (timing chain rattle) and documented oil consumption history.
- Road test for DCT/Tiptronic behaviour (no shudder, smooth shifts).
- VIN recall/TSB check and full vehicle history (accident, ownership).
- If keyless, ask about any aftermarket security fitted — if absent, budget for layered anti‑theft measures.

Buy smart: the A5 is still a very attractive used buy if the mechanical boxes are ticked. Ignore badge‑snobbery and focus on receipts, software updates, and whether the car’s past life proves it wasn’t abused — do that and you’ll have an A5 that looks sharper than the price suggests, without the wallet trauma that comes from ignoring timing chains and dodgy transmissions.
