#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

- Generation: Second‑generation Renault Koleos (MK2 / Phase II facelift) — production 2016–present with facelift updates from 2019 onwards. Internal platform family: CMF‑C/D from the Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi alliance. (See Renault–Nissan CMF notes and Koleos timeline.) [Wikipedia CMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault%E2%80%93Nissan_Common_Module_Family) | [Koleos overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Koleos)

- Product hierarchy (Australia):
  - Sits above: smaller Renault Captur / Arkana in Renault’s line-up (smaller‑segment crossovers).
  - Sits below: larger or more premium 7‑seat rivals in other brands (e.g., Skoda Kodiaq / Hyundai Santa Fe when you need 7 seats).
  - Locally the Koleos is pitched as Renault’s mid‑size, more “premium‑feeling” 5‑seat SUV with generous rear space and higher equipment on upper trims. See Renault press material and local launch reporting. [press kit / Renault UK](https://press.renault.co.uk/assets/documents/original/17936-RE37517KoleosPressKitV1.pdf) | [Carsales MY25 notes](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/flagship-2025-renault-koleos-arrives-149914/)

- Shared global platform / other models using the same architecture:
  - CMF‑C/D platform — shared DNA with Nissan X‑Trail / Rogue family (helpful for parts availability, drivetrain components and gearbox provenance). [Wikipedia Koleos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Koleos)

- Market-specific powertrains, outputs and drivetrains (summary of options found in regional specs and data):
  - Petrol:
    - **1.3 TCe (H5H-family)** — small turbo‑petrol, mid‑power outputs (around 150–158 hp depending on tune), usually FWD with 7‑ or 8‑speed auto or EDC dual‑clutch in some markets. [Auto‑Data 1.3 TCe listing](https://www.auto-data.net/en/renault-koleos-ii-phase-ii-2019-generation-7265)
  - Diesel:
    - **2.0 dCi (M9R / R9M family)** — turbodiesel options from about 150 hp up to 190 hp (the 190 hp often paired with AWD/4x4). Typically uses Jatco X‑Tronic CVT style automatic in many variants. [Auto‑Data / M9R summary](https://www.auto-data.net/en/renault-koleos-ii-phase-ii-2019-generation-7265) | [M9R engine notes](https://motoranimal.com/renault-nissan-m9r-engine-2-0-dci-problems/)
  - Transmissions:
    - FWD/AWD configurations across trim grades; higher spec trims tend to restore AWD (as seen in later MY25 Techno reintroduction for Australia). [carsales MY25](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/flagship-2025-renault-koleos-arrives-149914/)
  - Note: local Australian offerings and exact engine/transmission trims changed across years and facelifts — check the build sheet on the vehicle you’re shopping. [Renault technical specs / user manual](https://www.user-manual.renault.com/en/content/renault-koleos/technical-specifications)

- Marketplace rivals:
  - Toyota RAV4, Mazda CX‑5, Hyundai Tucson, Nissan X‑Trail (platform sibling), Kia Sportage, Skoda Kodiaq (larger competitor), Mitsubishi Outlander. See CarsGuide comparison hub. [CarsGuide comparisons](https://www.carsguide.com.au/renault/koleos/vs)

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Renault Koleos?

This is the “what to look for” checklist. Be brutal: buyers’ market, so use it.

Exterior:
- Panel fit & paint:
  - Panel-gap specs exist in the workshop manual, but owner reports show mixed panel alignment consistency — some vehicles require small adjustments for symmetry after crash repairs or heavy use. [Panel gaps manual reference](https://www.rkoleos.com/137/vehicle_panel_gaps_adjustment_value.html)
  - Paint and chip complaints are present in owner forums and complaint aggregators — stone chips and early corrosion in coastal climates are not unheard of. [CarComplaints body/paint](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Renault/Koleos/body_paint/index.shtml) | [AutoGuru rust repair NZ/AU note](https://www.autoguru.com.au/repairs/rust-repair/renault/koleos)
- Trim vulnerabilities:
  - Gloss black and centre‑console trims reported as scratch‑prone; centre console panels can creak or loosen under heavy duty (rideshare/taxi) wear. [AUTODOC interior faults](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-renault-koleos)
- Recalls & safety‑system hardware:
  - Check for outstanding recalls/quality campaigns via Renault’s recall tool — Koleos has historical recalls (varied by market) including ADAS or steering related campaigns in some model years. [Renault recall campaigns](https://www.renault.co.uk/recall-campaigns.html) | [Car‑Recalls summary](https://car-recalls.eu/common-problems/renault/koleos/)
- ADAS issues:
  - Adaptive Cruise Control and AEB rely on radar and a front camera mounted behind the bumper.
  - Known symptoms: radar/camera errors that disable AEBS/ACC — owners report false warnings and system cancellation; some firmware/driver‑camera software updates have been issued in related TSBs to cure ACC cancel‑on‑the‑move issues. If the car shows ADAS faults, expect diagnostic time and possible sensor replacement (radar sensor behind bumper can be damaged by minor impacts). [Renault ADAS manual](https://www.user-manual.renault.com/en/content/renault-koleos/driving/additional-driving-aid-functions/active-emergency-braking) | [NHTSA TSB re ACC](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10211111-0001.pdf) | [MyCarly radar reports](https://www.mycarly.com/community/koleos-radar-sensor-issue-61d6f198-6e56-4c06-95ef-6aed624f599e/)

Interior:
- Durability under heavy use:
  - The cabin is roomy and comfortable, but some hard plastics and console trim are reported to wear and rattle under heavy, repetitive use (rideshare). WhatCar and AutoExpress note the Koleos is comfortable but not the segment heavyweight in durability. [WhatCar used review](https://www.whatcar.com/renault/koleos/estate/used-review/n18253/reliability) | [AutoExpress used review](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/renault/koleos/358106/used-renault-koleos-mk2-2017-date-review)
- Entry/exit ergonomics:
  - High‑set seats and reasonably low door sills make entry/exit easy for families and older adults. Check door check angles and rubber seals for wear if unit has been garaged in hot climates.
- Electrical & infotainment:
  - R‑Link infotainment system is the recurring gripe — freezing, CarPlay/AndroidAuto dropouts and occasional blank screens. Factory soft resets sometimes fix it; other times modules need reflash or replacement. [AUTODOC R‑Link note](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-renault-koleos) | Reset walkthroughs exist (YouTube how‑to). [YouTube reset guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=asN0dsg4uvE)
  - Wireless charging pad: can overheat or fail to charge; manual warns not to leave metal/credit cards in pad as it triggers errors. [Renault wireless charger manual](https://www.user-manual.renault.com/en/content/openr-link-1/phone/wireless-charger)
  - 12V battery drain: owner reports exist of parasitic drains tied to module behaviour (infotainment modules and telematics). Check for clean battery health report and ask the dealer for a recent battery/charging system check.
  - Screen sizes differ across trims — entry/mid trims have smaller units; top trims get larger screens and more cameras. [Marque review Zen/Intens differences](https://marquenews.com.au/renault-koleos-zen-review/)

Mechanically:
- Brand engineering reputation:
  - Koleos sits on proven alliance architecture (CMF). That gives parts commonality with Nissan X‑Trail but also brings Jatco transmission hardware and Renault‑Nissan diesel engines with known weak points.
- Security vulnerabilities:
  - Industry‑wide CAN bus “headlight/CAN injection” theft techniques can affect many modern vehicles that expose wiring in accessible locations. The Koleos uses an immobiliser system and ECU topology common to alliance vehicles — any exposed wiring around the front bumper or headlight areas is a potential attack vector. Use layered mitigations (secure parking, physical steering lock, aftermarket CAN‑firewall/immobiliser). [NVD CVE description / CAN injection discussion](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2023-29389) | [The Register writeup](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how_thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/) | [Autoblog explainer](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update) | Technical locations for immobiliser components in service manual. [Koleos immobiliser list](https://www.rkoleos.com/655/immobiliser_system_list_and_location_of_components.html)
- Recommended aftermarket measures:
  - Physical: high‑quality Thatcham‑approved steering wheel lock (visible theft deterrent), secure parking, wheel‑to‑ground alarms.
  - Digital/technical: install an aftermarket CAN‑firewall/immobiliser unit from a reputable Australian installer if you plan to leave the car unattended in higher‑risk areas. Use VIN/rego checks and ensure the vehicle’s immobiliser history is clean.

Engines (every engine variant discussed in the research — italics and bold engine code names):

- *H5H / 1.3 TCe (petrol turbo)*
  - Known points: small‑capacity turbo engines are generally sensitive to oil‑change intervals and are prone to carbon build‑up if used predominantly on short trips. Owners report that the 1.3 TCe is generally smooth but demands quality oil and timely services.
  - Failure points: turbocharger oil supply sensitivity (look for oil leaks at turbo flange, excessive smoke on cold start), coil pack/ignition lead wear on higher kilometre cars.
  - Preventative maintenance: stick to 10,000 km oil service cycles with manufacturer‑spec synthetic oil if the car is used in town/stop‑start; for pure highway use 15,000 km can work but I would still favour 10,000 km in Australia due to ambient temps and urban driving. [Auto‑Data 1.3 listings](https://www.auto-data.net/en/renault-koleos-ii-phase-ii-2019-generation-7265)

- *M9R / 2.0 dCi (diesel 150–190 hp family)*
  - Known points: this is the widely‑used Renault‑Nissan 2.0 diesel with a reputation for longevity — but not without expense. Common diesel failure modes include DPF clogging, EGR coking, high‑pressure common rail injector wear, and turbocharger faults.
  - Specific failure modes reported:
    - DPF regeneration failure or frequent soot accumulation if mostly short trips are performed.
    - EGR valve carbonisation causing rough running / limp mode.
    - High‑pressure fuel pump or injector wear causing misfire and expensive repairs.
    - Differential and AWD transfer case issues have been reported on 2.0 dCi AWD cars around ~80k–100k km. [AUTODOC diesel notes](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-renault-koleos) | [M9R engine overview](https://motoranimal.com/renault-nissan-m9r-engine-2-0-dci-problems/)
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Oil & filter: every 10,000 km for severe use; max 15,000 km only for honest highway duty with top‑quality oil.
    - Fuel filter: replace every 30,000–40,000 km (diesel), more frequently with poor fuel sources.
    - DPF/EGR checks: at 60k km inspect EGR and DPF status on diagnostic — plan for cleaning before symptoms escalate.
  - If you’re looking at a high‑kilometre diesel Koleos, insist on a full injector/fuel system history and recent DPF regen evidence.

- *R9M / R9N family (diesel variants reported in owner summaries)*
  - Known points: variants of Renault/Nissan diesel families reported across markets with similar weaknesses to M9R—EGR/DPF/injection wear and occasional oil cooler or water pump failures that can cascade. What‑Breaks and owner forums list these engine codes as part of the Koleos common fault matrix. [What‑Breaks Koleos engines](https://what-breaks.com/renault/koleos-2/) | [Car‑Recalls engine logs](https://car-recalls.eu/common-problems/renault/koleos/)

Transmission:
- CVT / X‑Tronic and torque‑converter behaviour:
  - The Koleos uses a Jatco‑built CVT/X‑Tronic in many diesel and AWD variants; owners report whining, slipping sensations, jerking and delayed engagement, typically surfacing around ~80,000–120,000 km. Transmission metal debris on sensors is a classic sign of internal wear. [Nissan CVT problems background](https://gearfixes.com/nissan-cvt-transmission-problems/) | [Go‑Parts P1715 CVT debris note](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p1715-renault-koleos-2007-2016)
  - Torque converter lock‑up clutch trouble: owners report fault codes and limp modes where the lock‑up clutch is failing (not merely low fluid). [MyCarly torque converter thread](https://www.mycarly.com/community/koleos-auto-transmission-torque-converter-issue-b2d253e4-6aca-48d9-9a53-82dec5b91e9a/)
- Known failure modes:
  - Valve body / stepper motor wear on CVT, belt/pulley wear and cooling insufficiency leading to overheating.
  - Transfer‑case or AWD differential issues: coolant ingress and mechanical failure reported on AWD diesels. [AUTODOC transmission list](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-renault-koleos)
- Service advice:
  - If the car has a CVT, insist on a documented fluid change history and ask for scan‑tool transmission health info. Beware cars with “adaptation reset” histories — they can hide ongoing clutch/belt slippage.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Renault Koleos?

- NSW dealer market snapshot (live from MTA NSW scraper):
  - Active listings: 8
  - Price range: AUD $14,888 – $39,990
  - Median price: AUD $20,940
  - Year range: 2019 – 2024
  - Median odometer: 64,585 km
  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 8
  - Use this as the bargaining map: low end covers older 2019/2020 vehicles with higher spec maybe mid‑20s km; the top end is nearly‑new or low‑km 2023–24 examples.

- Depreciation & resale reality:
  - The Koleos does not hold value like segment leaders (Toyota / Mazda). Reviews and market observers note it sold in smaller volumes and its depreciation is stronger than the class average. Expect used value to fall faster than Toyota RAV4 and Mazda CX‑5 equivalents; that’s the opportunity for buyers — you can buy a well‑spec’d Koleos for good money now. [WhatCar used review](https://www.whatcar.com/renault/koleos/estate/used-review/n18253/reliability) | [AutoExpress used guide notes low volumes](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/renault/koleos/358106/used-renault-koleos-mk2-2017-date-review)
  - Ballpark depreciation: typical mid‑size non‑luxury European SUVs frequently lose ~30–50% of new value over the first 3 years depending on spec and market. Given the Koleos’s sales volume and used listing spread above, expect the Koleos to be towards the higher end of that band — useful if you’re value hunting. (For precise forecast use a depreciation calculator like TheMoneyCalculator or CarEdge.) [TheMoneyCalculator depreciation tool](https://www.themoneycalculator.com/vehicle-finance/calculators/car-depreciation-by-make-and-model/RENAULT/KOLEOS/)

- Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy — how to calculate for your situation:
  - Step 1 — Fuel: pick the engine. Official/real figures:
    - Petrol (representative): combined ~8.1 L/100 km (varies by engine/trim). [CarsGuide fuel numbers](https://www.carsguide.com.au/renault/koleos/fuel-consumption)
    - Hybrid (E‑Tech/FHEV where available in newer Grand Koleos III or hybrid badging): Renault claims combined ~5.6 L/100 km for some hybrid e‑tech variants. [Renault hybrid spec page](https://renault.cw/cars/new-koleos-en/engine.html)
  - Step 2 — Consumption × annual km = litres/year. Example: 12,000 km/year × 8.1 L/100 km = 972 L/yr (petrol) vs 672 L/yr (hybrid 5.6 L/100 km).
  - Step 3 — Multiply by your local fuel price to get annual spend. (I’m avoiding naming a fuel price since it fluctuates — multiply litres by your local per‑litre price.)
  - Step 4 — Add servicing, tyres, insurance and anticipated repairs:
    - Servicing: budget for 10,000 km services every 12 months in Australian conditions (engine oil, filters) — Renault dealers and independents will quote different prices; Renault fixed price servicing sometimes exists depending on campaign.
    - Diesel owners: add potential DPF and injection maintenance costs; inspect service history carefully.
    - Transmission: if it’s CVT/X‑Tronic, expect the risk of expensive repair beyond 120k km on unchecked units.
  - Step 5 — Depreciation: factor the median $20,940 NSW asking price into 3–5 year resale estimates — if you can buy below median with full service history you’ve already captured some depreciation buffer.
  - Hybrid vs petrol payback:
    - If you mostly do short urban trips, hybrid fuel economy advantage may be real, but you must weigh higher used‑buy price and potential battery/complexity risk. Renault’s claimed hybrid figure (5.6 L/100 km) looks attractive versus 8.1 L/100 km petrol, but calculate litres saved × your fuel cost and compare against the price premium (and potential battery servicing costs later). [Renault hybrid claim](https://renault.cw/cars/new-koleos-en/engine.html) | [CarsGuide combined figures](https://www.carsguide.com.au/renault/koleos/fuel-consumption)

- Practical buying recommendation — the blunt Aussie answer:
  - If you want a comfy, roomy, well‑equipped mid‑size SUV with relatively low purchase prices on the used market, the Koleos is a solid value buy — provided you pick the right car and do the checks below.
  - Avoid:
    - High‑mileage 2.0 dCi AWD cars with unknown differential/transfer case service history (there are documented reports of differential issues around 80k–100k km). [AUTODOC diff & drivetrain issues](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-renault-koleos)
    - CVT boxes showing slip/whine or cars with no transmission service history. [CVT failure notes](https://gearfixes.com/nissan-cvt-transmission-problems/) | [MyCarly threads](https://www.mycarly.com/community/koleos-auto-transmission-torque-converter-issue-b2d253e4-6aca-48d9-9a53-82dec5b91e9a/)
  - Buy:
    - 2020–2022 mid‑range to high‑spec cars with full dealer service history (these often sit near the NSW median price and median odometer of ~64k km). These years hit the sweet spot: not brand new; many dealer‑maintained examples still under some warranty or cost‑effective.
    - If you cruise 15–20k km/year and want the absolute economy, consider a hybrid E‑Tech—but only if you can verify battery health and you accept a higher purchase price. [Renault hybrid specs and claimed economy](https://renault.cw/cars/new-koleos-en/engine.html)
  - Trim advice:
    - Zen / Life: sensible spec, smaller screens, FWD — good for private buyers who want comfort without the AWD premium. [Marque Zen review / trim differences](https://marquenews.com.au/renault-koleos-zen-review/)
    - Intens / Techno (top trims): better seats, larger screen, AWD on some MYs — buy one if you value kit and don’t mind paying a bit more, but be stricter on service history and ADAS function checks. [CarExpert MY25 trim rename & AWD return](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/2025-renault-koleos-price-and-specs-ageing-suv-gets-tweaked-flagship)

Final checklist before you sign:
- Get a full independent mechanical inspection that focuses on:
  - CVT/transmission scan‑tool read (look for adaptation histories, torque converter codes).
  - Diesel health if it’s a dCi: DPF soot load, injectors, high‑pressure pump evidence.
  - ADAS self‑test: evidence of radar/camera repairs, warning lights, and a dealer software update history.
  - Full service history and receipts; reputable NSW dealers should be able to show service stamps. The MTA NSW market snapshot shows opportunity — use it to negotiate below median. (MTA NSW data as provided.)
- Confirm no open recalls with Renault and check the VIN for outstanding quality campaigns. [Renault recall lookup](https://www.renault.co.uk/recall-campaigns.html)
