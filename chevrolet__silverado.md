### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

Meet the Chevrolet Silverado – the full-size Yank-tank that’s been crashing Australian barbecues since General Motors Specialty Vehicles (GMSV) started officially converting them to right‑hand drive. We’re talking the T1XX platform generation (2019+ in the US, landing locally from 2021 as a 2022 model). In the GM food chain, it sits below the heavy‑duty Silverado HD and above the mid‑size Colorado. Platform twins include the **GMC Sierra 1500**, **Chevrolet Tahoe/Suburban** and the **Cadillac Escalade** (all sharing chassis bones), so parts cross‑pollination is decent despite the limited local dealer network.

**Australian market engines (2022–2026):**
- **5.3L V8 (L84):** 265 kW / 519 Nm, 10‑speed auto, RWD or part‑time 4WD.
- **6.2L V8 (L87):** 313 kW / 623 Nm, 10‑speed auto, 4WD only.
- **3.0L Duramax inline‑6 turbo‑diesel (LM2):** 207 kW / 624 Nm, 10‑speed auto, 4WD.

Its explicit rivals? The **Ford F‑150** (also locally remanufactured), **Ram 1500** (the DS/DT generation), and the **Toyota Tundra** (3.5‑litre twin‑turbo hybrid V6). With a median used price of **$142,990** (NSW dealer snapshot) and most examples sitting on **1,041 km median** – basically new demonstrators – you’re paying a “Yank‑tax” premium for exclusivity.

### 2. What goes wrong with a used Chevrolet Silverado?

#### Exterior

Panel alignment on the Mexican‑built T1XX Silverado is typically GM‑standard: good enough for a worksite but don’t inspect it with a caliper. The gloss black trim around the grille and lower bumpers (especially on LTZ and High Country) scratches if you look at it sideways – the same plastic Toyota uses on a HiLux is more durable. Paint durability is average; stone chips on the leading edge of the bonnet appear quickly, and the “Blue Glow Metallic” seems to be a chip magnet.

**ADAS quirks:** The forward‑collision alert and lane‑keep assist are overly sensitive on early Australian builds (2022). Some owners report phantom braking when approaching a gentle curve or a steel sign. Later firmware updates (2023.5+) supposedly fixed the sensitivity, but check if the dealer has applied TSB **PIT5720H** before buying.

**Known recall history (US‑sourced – apply to local conversions):**
- Trailer brake control module may fail – recall N232418850.
- High‑voltage battery cable (on Duramax) chafing risk – recall N232425920.
- Check [lemonlawexperts.com/recalls-on-chevy-silverado](https://lemonlawexperts.com/recalls-on-chevy-silverado/) for full list.

#### Interior

Cabin durability is decent for a $140K truck, but the leather on LTZ trims shows wear on the driver’s outer bolster after 20,000 km – rideshare duty would annihilate it. The door check angles are generous: climbing into a lifted Silverado is easier than a Ram 1500 thanks to the lower sill height, but the massive steering wheel can graze your thighs.

**Electrical gremlins:**
- **12V battery drain:** The Silverado’s computers don’t sleep properly if you leave it parked for two weeks; owners report flat batteries after holidays. A battery maintainer is mandatory if you don’t drive weekly.
- **Infotainment:** The 13.4‑inch touchscreen (standard from 2022 LTZ upwards) runs Google built‑in. It’s fast, but wireless Apple CarPlay drops connection about once a day – a hard reset via the “End Call + Home + Fast Forward” button combo fixes it. Lower trims get an 8‑inch screen with the older system; it’s laggy.
- **USB ports:** The centre console USB‑C ports blow fuses if you plug in high‑power devices. Check they all work.

#### Mechanically – Security & Theft

The Silverado is a high‑theft target in the US, and the same vulnerabilities apply here. The **CAN bus injection** attack – thieves access the headlight wiring, slice into the CAN network, and talk the truck into unlocking and starting – is well documented ([Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/2023/04/18/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update/)). Some early Australian conversions lack an engine immobiliser as standard (the 2022 base models), making them stealable with a USB cable ([Carlock](https://www.carlock.co/blog/en/2024/02/12/car-security-anti-theft/)).

**Counter‑measures:**
- Install an OBD‑II port lock (like the *OBD Saver*).
- Add a **PCM security box** (available from aftermarket for 2014‑2023 Silverado/Sierra) – sold by audio shops for $200‑400.
- Always use a steering wheel lock (Disklok) as a visible deterrent.

#### Engines

*L84 – 5.3L V8 (EcoTec3)*
This is the Achilles’ heel of the range. The **Active Fuel Management (AFM)** lifters are notorious for collapsing, causing misfires and catastrophic failure. Oil consumption – up to 1 litre per 1,600 km – is a common warranty claim ([Lemon Firm](https://www.lemonfirm.com/vehicles/2021-2024-chevrolet-silverado-common-problems)). The root cause is the AFM lifter pin shearing. **Preventative move:** Have the AFM system disabled via tune (voids powertrain warranty) and change oil every **7,500 km** using Dexos1 Gen2 5W‑30. The high‑pressure fuel pump on the direct injection (GDI) engines also fails; listen for a rattle from the rear of the engine on cold start.

*L87 – 6.2L V8 (EcoTec3)*
Same AFM issues as the 5.3. The 6.2 is also prone to **coolant bypass valve** failure – the plastic valve at the front of the engine cracks, causing coolant loss and overheating. Part **12677693** is a common replacement. The **oil cooler lines** – braided steel lines at the oil filter housing – can leak after 60,000 km. Service interval: **10,000 km** with Dexos1 Gen2 0W‑40. If you want to keep it past 100,000 km, budget for a lifter delete kit (around $4,000 fitted).

*LM2 – 3.0L Duramax I6*
This diesel is far more reliable. Early 2021‑2022 units had a problem with the **coolant vent filter** (part #55488955) – it plugs, causing coolant to overflow. Also, the **crankcase ventilation heater** can fail if the truck does lots of short trips. Service interval: **15,000 km** or 12 months. No major AFM‑style failures – the Duramax uses a simpler, non‑defueling valvetrain. Expect **8.5 L/100km** on the highway, but around **11 L/100km** in town.

#### Transmission

The 10‑speed automatic (Hydra‑Matic 10L80/10L90) is shared with the F‑150 and Ram. It’s generally robust, but the early production (2022) had a **torque converter shudder** between 6th and 8th gear. GM issued TSB **#23‑NA‑001** for a torque converter replacement and trans fluid flush. The 8‑speed (8L90) from pre‑2021 trucks was a known lemon – a class‑action lawsuit in the US highlighted hard downshifts and premature failure ([GM Authority](https://gmauthority.com/blog/2023/03/gm-8-speed-automatic-transmission-lawsuit-granted-class-action-status/)). Fortunately, Australian Silverados from 2022 use the 10‑speed, but if you find a rare 2020‑2021 demo, run away.

### 3. Should you buy a used Chevrolet Silverado?

**Pricing:** From the NSW dealer data, you’re looking at **$94,990 – $154,990** for 2022‑2026 examples with around 1,000 km. That’s basically new‑car money. The cheapest entry point is a 2022 LT 5.3L – but you’re buying into the worst engine and the outdated 8‑inch screen. The sweet spot is a **2023 LTZ 6.2L** (around $130k‑$140k) or a **2023‑2024 Duramax** ($140k+) – the diesel holds value better and avoids the lifter gamble.

**Depreciation:** A new Silverado 1500 6.2L costs around $155k drive‑away. After 3 years, expect a 25–30% drop – on par with the Ram 1500, but the Ford F‑150 (with its more modern hybrid powertrain) has been holding closer to 22‑25%. The Duramax diesel version loses about 15‑18% over 3 years because diesel demand is strong in regional Australia.

**Total Cost of Ownership (TCO):**  
- **6.2L V8:** Fuel at 14 L/100km average over 20,000 km/year = $5,600/year (at $2/L).  
- **3.0L Duramax:** 9.5 L/100km = $3,800/year.  
- The $10k premium for the diesel pays back in 5.5 years if you drive 20k km. If you tow regularly, the diesel’s torque and longer service intervals make it the smarter buy.  
- Insurance: Expect $1,800–$2,500/year – it’s a high‑risk vehicle.  
- Servicing: $800‑$1,200 annually at a GMSV dealer (e.g., for oil + filter + inspection). Plus a $1,500 major service every 4 years (transmission fluid, diffs, etc.).

**The final verdict:** A used Chevrolet Silverado is a brilliant truck if you **need** a full‑size American ute and can stomach the fuel bills. Avoid the 5.3L – the 6.2L is the heart, but it’s haunted by AFM. The **2023+ Duramax LM2** is the headache‑free goldilocks option. Just budget for aftermarket anti‑theft gear and a battery maintainer. If you can snag a 2023 Duramax for under $140k, you’re laughing – until you refill the 91‑litre tank.
