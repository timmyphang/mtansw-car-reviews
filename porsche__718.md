#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- What you’re looking at: the Porsche 718 (982-generation Cayman & Boxster), the mid‑engine two‑seater introduced for the 2017 model year after the 2016 production launch. The 718 nameplate marks the 982 chassis family (official launch details in Porsche’s newsroom and the model pages). [Porsche international 718 hub](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/718/) | [Porsche production launch note](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-cayman-model-series-718-sport-coupe-production-launch-12591.html) | [Wikipedia: 718 (982)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_718_Boxster_and_Cayman_%28982%29).

- Product hierarchy (where it sits in the Porsche ladder):
  - Above: nothing really above in raw sporting focus except the 911 — the 718 is the cheaper, lighter, more mid‑engine‑focused sports car.
  - Below: there is no “lower” pure two‑seater from Porsche; the 718 sits below the 911 in prestige but above Porsche’s SUV and four‑door lines when the discussion is pure driver engagement.

- Shared global platform architecture:
  - The 718 is the Porsche 982 platform family — both Cayman (coupe) and Boxster (roadster) share this architecture. That platform logic improves parts availability for items shared across body styles and model years. See Porsche model pages and the 982 write‑up. [Porsche 718 models](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/718/) | [Wikipedia 982 details](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_718_Boxster_and_Cayman_%28982%29).

- Engines, outputs, transmissions and drivetrains sold in-market (Australian buyers will see most of these):
  - **2.0‑litre turbo flat‑4** — ~300 PS (base 718 variants), 7‑speed PDK or 6‑speed manual in some markets. [Porsche specs overview](https://www.porsche.com/usa/models/718/) | [Auto‑Data 2.0 specs](https://www.auto-data.net/en/porsche-718-boxster-982-2.0-300hp-pdk-41390).
  - **2.5‑litre turbo flat‑4** — higher output (~350 PS) for S variants (model availability by year). [Porsche international models list](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/718/).
  - **4.0‑litre naturally aspirated flat‑6** — GTS 4.0 / GT4 lineage, ~400 PS; available in higher‑performance trims and lauded for more traditional Porsche character. [Porsche international models](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/718/) | [GTS 4.0/GTS notes via The Car Connection](https://www.thecarconnection.com/specifications/porsche_718_2025).
  - Drivetrain: RWD layout is standard for the 718 range (mid‑engine, rear‑driven) — no FWD. AWD is not typical for the Cayman/Boxster 718 stable. [Porsche specs](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/718/).

- Direct marketplace rivals (what buyers cross‑shop):
  - Toyota GR Supra, BMW Z4 / M2, Chevrolet Corvette (price/performance competitors), and lightweight sports cars like Subaru BRZ / Toyota GR86 for budget buyers. [The Car Connection competitor overview](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/porsche_718_choices) | [Autoblog & comparisons](https://www.autoblog.com/news/report-next-porsche-718-could-support-both-gas-engines-and-evs).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Porsche 718?

Exterior
- Panel fit & cosmetic stuff:
  - Expect generally excellent fit and finish, but community threads show owners occasionally pick up uneven panel gaps after delivery or post‑repair — not catastrophic, but worth checking closely on any used car inspection. [718 forum discussion of panel gaps](https://www.718forum.com/threads/help-request-panel-gaps-photos.34292/).
  - Trim vulnerability: roof‑trim, A‑pillar and other moulded trim pieces can warp, delaminate or lose clips (especially after sun exposure or in cars used with the soft top/hood often). These are largely cosmetic but lead to rattles and wind noise. [Go‑Parts roof trim & A‑pillar guides](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/roof-trim-molding-panel-porsche-718-cayman-porsche-cayman-2014-2024) | [Go‑Parts A‑pillar trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/body-a-pillar-trim-panel-porsche-718-boxster-porsche-718-cayman-porsche-718-2017-2025).
  - Paint and clearcoat: there have been owner reports of clearcoat peeling / chipping on certain early model years (2016–2020) — inspect roofs, antenna surrounds and leading edges carefully. [Clearcoat defect reports (legal firm summary)](https://www.guardianlemonlaw.com/2016-2020-porsche-718-cayman-paint-defects-peeling-or-chipping-of-the-paint-layer/) | [Rennlist forum reports of blemishes](https://rennlist.com/forums/718-forum/1454123-25-gts-clearcoat-defects-and-blemishes.html).
- Body recalls & ADAS external sensors:
  - Check Porsche’s official recall lookup by VIN — always do this before purchase. [Porsche recall lookup](https://recall.porsche.com/).
  - ADAS sensors (cameras and radar) need correct alignment after windscreen replacement — owners and service shops report calibration sensitivity; improper recalibration can trip warnings or reduce functionality. [Bang Autoglass ADAS calibration guide](https://bangautoglass.com/blog/porsche-718-cayman-adas-calibration-warning-signs-owners-should-not-ignore) | [NHTSA ADAS TSBs](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2025/MC-11020513-0001.pdf).

Interior
- Durability under heavy use:
  - Interior panels can rattle or delaminate under frequent ingress/egress and rough use (rideshare/taxi use is rare for a 718, but if it’s been used hard look for trim wear and loose fasteners). [Go‑Parts interior trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/interior-rear-body-panel-porsche-718-boxster-porsche-718-cayman-porsche-718-spyder-2012-2025).
  - Entry/exit: low sills and a mid‑engine floor make the 718 sporty but fiddly to get in/out of — check door‑check angles and sill scrapes on low‑km cars that look “unworn” but might have hidden damage.
- Electrical & infotainment:
  - PCM touchscreen (PCM 4.0 era) can suffer freezes, reboots or blank screens. Community guides exist for reboots or module replacement; persistent faults often require dealer diagnostics. [PCM 4.0 troubleshooting guidance](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/info-gps-tv-screen-porsche-718-boxster-porsche-718-cayman-porsche-911-2017-2023).
  - Wireless charging units and inductive chargers have user‑reported quirks; OEM wireless charging units have power limits and sometimes require software fixes/updates. [NHTSA TSB on wireless chargers](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2024/MC-10251249-0001.pdf).
  - 12V battery parasitic drain: not universally documented in search results, but high‑feature cars can show battery drain symptoms if software updates/ECU sleep strategies weren't correctly applied by service history — insist on a recent battery health check and service records.

Mechanically
- Brand engineering reputation:
  - Porsche engineering is generally high‑quality, but that doesn’t make the 718 immune — the downsized turbo four‑cylinder engines introduced in the 982 brought new failure modes vs older naturally aspirated units. [Wikipedia & Porsche specs overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_718_Boxster_and_Cayman_%28982%29) | [Porsche model pages](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/718/).
- Security vulnerabilities:
  - Modern Porsche CAN networks are complex. There are public write‑ups showing how the 718 CAN bus can be probed; this is a reminder that high‑end cars can be targets for relay, cloning or message‑injection attacks. Consider layered aftermarket protection (immobiliser, CAN‑bus compatible immobiliser, tracking). [PlanetKris CAN‑bus post](https://planetkris.com/unlocking-the-porsche-718-can-bus/) | [Autowatch Ghost 2 immobiliser product for Porsche](https://www.carsoundsecurity.com/product/ghost-2-can-bus-immobiliser-by-autowatch-for-porsche-fully-fitted/).
  - European cybersecurity rules created headwinds for the 718 platform — Porsche paused certain sales in regions due to cyber rules, a signal that some older architectures may be harder to patch long term. It’s an indirect caution for future software support. [Forbes on EU cybersecurity impact](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelharley/2024/03/28/eu-cybersecurity-laws-kill-porsches-718-boxster-and-cayman-early/) | [Carscoops on local impact](https://www.carscoops.com/2024/04/porsche-718-cayman-and-boxster-axed-in-eu-due-to-cybersecurity-rules/).
- Aftermarket anti‑theft measures (practical):
  - Fit a CAN‑compatible immobiliser (Autowatch Ghost 2 style) and a hardwired GPS tracker. If you want extra: mechanical steering wheel lock, replaceable VIN‑locked wheel nuts, and park under CCTV at home. [Autowatch Ghost 2](https://www.carsoundsecurity.com/product/ghost-2-can-bus-immobiliser-by-autowatch-for-porsche-fully-fitted/).

Engines
- For every important variant — italicised section and specific failure points, with preventative guidance and realistic service cadence.

  - * **2.0L turbo flat‑4** *
    - Known issues: turbo wastegate and **turbocharger** control problems (actuator/wastegate), oil consumption complaints and engine management warnings have been reported. Turbo hardware and actuator wear can show as loss of boost, limp‑home modes or check‑engine lights. [Go‑Parts turbo overview](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/turbocharger-porsche-718-boxster-porsche-718-cayman-2017-2025) | [MyEngineSpecs summary](https://myenginespecs.com/problems/porsche-718-engine-problems-what-you-need-to-know/).
    - Typical symptoms: whistling turbo noises, intermittent power loss, elevated oil consumption or oil leaks around turbo oil feed/return lines.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Oil change frequency: for spirited Australian use, aim for oil & filter at least every 10,000 km (preferable) — stretch only if you have documented low‑stress driving and OEM‑grade fluids. (If you prefer a relaxed schedule, many owners extend to 15,000 km on long‑distance highway miles — but that’s a gamble on a turbo engine.)
      - Pay attention to oil quality and low‑speed/oil‑temperature cycles; allow turbos to cool down after track runs.
    - When buying: get full DSG/engine debug logs from a Porsche dealer scan if possible and inspect for turbo codes (wastegate/boost faults).

  - * **2.5L turbo flat‑4 (S variants)** *
    - Known issues: similar turbo/wastegate vulnerabilities, plus higher stress on cooling circuits and potential for earlier water‑pump or coolant system plastic part degradation.
    - Preventatives: follow the same oil change discipline, and inspect coolant hoses, intercooler plumbing and water pump condition at service intervals (see cooling issues below). [CM Autohaus notes on cooling/waterpump](https://cmautohaus.com/blogs/porsche-cayman-boxster-resources/porsche-718-cayman-boxster-common-problems).

  - * **4.0L naturally aspirated flat‑6** *
    - Known issues: overall more robust historically, fewer turbo‑related faults. Community notes say the 4.0 avoids the turbo/wastegate headaches and typical cylinder wear seen in very high‑boost four‑cylinders; however catastrophic failures have been reported on isolated GTS/GT4 engines leading to dealer replacements (rare but serious). [CarChecker on 4.0 reliability](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/porsche_718_cayman_982_gts_4.0.html) | [PistonHeads catastrophic failure thread](https://www.pistonheads.com/gassing/topic.asp?t=2071767).
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Oil every 10,000 km with high‑grade synthetic; valve adjustment intervals and cam-chain tensioner checks per service schedule (confirm with a dealer).
      - The 4.0 is the pick if you want the least turbo‑related complexity.

- Cooling system & water pumps (applies across variants):
  - Water pump failures and coolant system weaknesses (plastic impellers, failing bearings) are recurring owner reports — symptoms include whining/pump noise, coolant loss or overheating. Check service history for a replaced water pump and evidence of cooling system work. [CM Autohaus cooling/water pump notes](https://cmautohaus.com/blogs/porsche-cayman-boxster-resources/porsche-718-cayman-boxster-common-problems).

Transmission
- Gearbox types in the 718 line:
  - **7‑speed PDK (Porsche Doppelkupplung)** — the most common modern choice; fast shifts and excellent for lap times and daily driving.
  - **6‑speed manual** — traditional choice for purists; lighter and cheaper to maintain in some ways.
  - (Tiptronic torque‑converter automatics are not the primary box for the 718 — PDK dominates.)
  - [Porsche technical data on transmissions & PDK/manual options](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr%3A2e05fcd8-e8ce-4b60-8611-9ce844a8242e/PCNA18_1003_us.pdf.PDF).
- Known PDK behaviors & failure modes:
  - PDK is generally robust but complex: many reported failures are linked to sensors, valve bodies, hydraulic control issues or software/calibration — not always catastrophic gearset failure but expensive to repair. These often manifest as harsh shifts, limp‑home modes or stored gearbox error codes. [Department69 PDK repair guide](https://www.department69.com/articles/the-complete-guide-to-repairing-porsche-pdk-transmission-problems) | [PcarWise PDK notes](https://www.pcarwise.com/local-help/porsche-common-problems/porsche-pdk-transmission-problems/).
  - Preventative: insist on documented PDK service (fluid & filter) per Porsche schedule; clutch packs and mechatronics can last a long time if fluids and seals are healthy.
- Manual gearbox:
  - Mechanically simpler but watch for clutch wear (expensive if replaced by a Porsche dealer); the manual 6‑speed is less common on later models — a genuine manual car will usually be more desirable for an enthusiast and possibly cheaper to repair in some shops.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Porsche 718?

- NSW dealer snapshot (live MTA NSW scraper): Active listings 4 — Price range AUD $95,990 – $157,900 — Median price AUD $140,900 — Year range 2017–2024 — Median odometer 14,912 km — Transmission mix: Automatic: 4 (PDK dominant).
  - That median and the narrow market depth in NSW say two things: (1) good 718s with low km are holding value, and (2) availability is thin — be prepared to travel or broaden your search. Use this NSW snapshot as your baseline.

- Current market entry and ceiling (NSW reference):
  - Entry price in NSW snapshot: AUD $95,990 — Ceiling: AUD $157,900 — Median: AUD $140,900 (dealer listings, Automatic/PDK). Use those numbers when negotiating; private sales may be slightly cheaper but often have less warranty protection.

- Depreciation metrics & how the 718 holds value:
  - Porsche 718 models hold value comparatively well for the segment. iSeeCars analysis shows new 718 depreciation is relatively shallow versus segment rivals — roughly a 9.5% drop after five years in their dataset (much better than many luxury sports cars). That’s a headline reason buyers tolerate higher running costs — strong residuals. [iSeeCars Porsche 718 depreciation notes](https://www.iseecars.com/car/porsche-718-cayman/resale-value).
  - Hagerty and Porschestats commentary also underline a wide price spectrum across variants; GT/limited/RS trim levels can buck depreciation trends and even appreciate depending on rarity. [Hagerty market guide](https://www.hagerty.com/media/market-trends/2017-25-porsche-718-many-flavors-many-price-points/) | [Porschestats depreciation tool](https://www.porschestats.com/depreciation).

- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy — the real talk:
  - Fuel: factory/technical references put the 2.0–2.5 turbo variants around ~8.1 L/100 km in combined figures for certain models, though real‑world numbers will be higher depending on driving style and PDK use. The EPA pages for similar models cite a combined ~22 mpg (which converts to ~10.7 L/100 km) for some 2025 variants — use these as a range; expect 9–12 L/100 km for normal use on a 718 depending on engine and driving. [Auto‑Data fuel figures](https://www.auto-data.net/en/porsche-718-boxster-982-2.0-300hp-pdk-41390) | [FuelEconomy 2025 Cayman listing](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=48810).
  - Running costs to factor: premium fuel (98RON commonly in Australia for best performance), insurance (sports car premiums), tyre replacement (wide low profile tyres), brakes (big‑car pads/rotors), regular Porsche servicing (dealer labour rates), and potential major repairs (turbo, water pump, PDK mechatronics).
  - Use calculators: If you want to nerd out on payback for hybrid/EV alternatives vs ICE 718, model your kilometres per year and energy/fuel unit costs using tools like the AFDC vehicle cost calculator — plug in local fuel/energy prices and expected yearly km to see break‑evens. [AFDC Vehicle Cost Calculator (US reference)](https://afdc.energy.gov/calc/).
  - Bottom line math: unless you do very high annual kilometres (20k+ km/year) or have access to very cheap electricity and home charging, buying a hybrid/EV instead of a used ICE 718 won’t pay back quickly because the 718’s appeal is pure petrol performance. Porsche is launching electric successors, but used ICE 718s still look like an enthusiast asset for drivers who want that specific experience. [Car Magazine on EV + ICE future 718s](https://www.carmagazine.co.uk/spy-shots/porsche/electric-boxster-and-cayman-sports-car/) | [Electrive on upcoming electric successors](https://www.electrive.com/2026/03/09/porsche-likely-to-continue-development-of-electric-718-successors/).

- Warranty & service considerations:
  - Dealer‑backed warranty and recent major service history (water pump, timing‑component checks, PDK service, turbo servicing) will materially reduce risk. Insist on a Porsche service history or an independent Porsche specialist with Porsche PIWIS scan logs.
  - Check recalls/TSBs by VIN at Porsche’s recall portal. [Porsche recall lookup](https://recall.porsche.com/).

- Which model year / trim is the sweet spot?
  - If you want everyday reliability and fewer turbo‑headaches: aim for a low‑km **4.0 GTS/flat‑6** example if your budget stretches — the 4.0 is seen as mechanically simpler (no turbocharger complexity) and is generally regarded as robust. Expect to pay a premium, though. [CarChecker 4.0 reliability notes](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/porsche_718_cayman_982_gts_4.0.html).
  - If you want the best value for everyday sports driving: look in the 2018–2021 model years for well‑maintained **PDK** cars with full service history and under ~50,000 km. The NSW median (AUD $140,900) is a realistic guide to what dealers expect; you should be able to find earlier base S or 2.0/2.5 models near the lower end of the NSW range if you expand search across states.
  - Specific buy recommendation:
    - Buyer on a budget but wants the 718 experience: a 2017–2019 **2.0/PDK** with documented cooling/water pump work and a pre‑purchase scan — negotiate toward the low NSW entry number but budget for immediate consumables (tyres, brakes, oil service).
    - Buyer after longevity and fewer turbo risks: save up for a later **4.0 GTS** or GT4 variant — pricier up front but lower risk on turbo/HPFP/wastegate issues.
  - Avoid: cars with unclear service history, signs of cooling system repairs not done, PCM/infotainment that reboots often, or evidence of front/rear damage hiding via aftermarket panels (check panel gaps closely).

- Final verdict — should you buy one?
  - If you want a pure driver’s car, a Porsche 718 remains a top choice in Australia: sharp chassis, mid‑engine balance and strong resale make it a compelling used buy. However, it’s a sports car with the maintenance and ownership profile to match — higher running costs, potential turbo and PDK repair bills, and the need for dealer‑level diagnostics for some electronic faults.
  - Buy if: you prioritise driver feel, are comfortable budgeting for premium servicing and tyres, and you insist on service history and dealer/PIWIS scans before purchase.
  - Walk away or negotiate hard if: the car has patchy service records, unexplained coolant top‑ups, recurrent PCM/infotainment reboots, evidence of bodywork with mismatched gaps, or the price is near NSW ceiling without full documentation.

Final straight talk: the 718 is a brilliant driver’s car — buy one if you want a mid‑engine Porsche experience and you accept the premium servicing / consumables. Buy carefully (service history, PIWIS scan, check water‑pump/turbo and PDK service) and you’ll have one of the best handling two‑seat sports cars on Australian roads. If you’re after a daily without compromise on insurance, tyres and periodic dealer bills — maybe look elsewhere.
