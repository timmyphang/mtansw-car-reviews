#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: fourth‑generation Jaguar XJ — internal code **X351** — produced September 2009 to July 2019 (sold as model years 2010–2019). The XJ name itself runs back to 1968 across multiple platform generations; the X351 is the aluminium‑bodied late XJ you’ll see on the used market. [Wikipedia: XJ (X351)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_XJ_(X351)) | [Jaguar brochure](https://www.jaguar.co.uk/content/dam/jdx/pdfs/uk/XJ_19MY_MB_V11_EU6_GE_DX.pdf)

- Position in the lineup: the XJ is Jaguar’s flagship four‑door saloon — sits above the XF and XE in the range. It was sold as both a SWB (short wheelbase) XJ and long‑wheelbase XJL for rear‑seat‑first buyers. [Wikipedia: Jaguar XJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_XJ)

- Platform / architecture: the X351 uses Jaguar’s lightweight aluminium architecture (an all‑aluminium bodyshell and bonded construction that marked Jaguar’s step to reduce mass and improve ride/handling). That engineering approach was shared with Jaguar’s contemporary flagship and sporting models, so parts and know‑how are reasonably available through the JLR network and independent specialists. [Jaguar brochure](https://www.jaguar.co.uk/content/dam/jdx/pdfs/uk/XJ_19MY_MB_V11_EU6_GE_DX.pdf) | [MotorTrend XJ history](https://www.motortrend.com/features/jaguar-xj-history-generations-specifications)

- Engine / transmission / drive options (market summary for X351-era cars you will find in Australia):
  - Petrol:
    - **3.0‑litre supercharged V6** — mid‑range petrol (approx. 300–340 hp depending on tune) with 8‑speed automatic; rear‑wheel drive (RWD). [Auto‑Data / V3Cars]
    - **5.0‑litre AJ‑V8** — naturally aspirated and *supercharged* variants (supercharged in higher trims / XJR), with outputs up to the mid‑500s hp on factory hot versions; 8‑speed automatic; RWD or AWD in some markets/variants. [Auto‑Data] [myenginespecs AJ‑V8]
  - Diesel:
    - **2.7‑litre V6 diesel (AJD)** — early diesel entry (lower power, better economy).
    - **3.0‑litre V6 diesel (AJD)** — most common diesel in later XJs (approx. 200–275PS depending on tune), 8‑speed auto, RWD. [SelectedWorld / Auto‑Data / V3Cars]
  - Transmission: ZF 8‑speed torque‑converter automatic is the common gearbox across modern XJ petrols/diesels. [ZF 8HP notes]

  (Exact outputs and availability vary with year/trim — confirm per VIN/rego — see Jaguar brochure/spec sheets and global spec aggregators.) - Marketplace rivals: Mercedes‑Benz S‑Class, BMW 7 Series, Audi A8 — the usual full‑size European players and, in practice in Australia, a lot of buyers cross‑shop late‑model XF long‑wheelbase examples or prestige SUVs that have taken market share from big saloons. #### 2. What goes wrong with a used Jaguar XJ?
This is the real, slightly salty meat-and-veg. The XJ is gorgeous and alluring, but ownership is not for the faint-hearted if you expect Toyota‑like monotony. You get style and theatre — and occasional electronic gremlins. Below are the fault hotspots and pragmatic pre‑purchase checkpoints.

Exterior:
- Panel fit & finish:
  - Uneven panel gaps and hood alignment are commonly reported on X351 cars — the heavy aluminium hood puts stress on hinges; **hood hinge wear** causes visible misalignment and raised panel gaps. These are fixable (hinge replacement/adjust) but expect alignment labour. [Go‑Parts hood hinge guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/hood-hinge-jaguar-xj-jaguar-xjr-2010-2019)
- Paint & clearcoat:
  - Clearcoat delamination and UV‑fade complaints appear on older XJs, especially those exposed to hot sun. Expect polishing, respray or patch repairs on high‑sun cars — check bonnet and roof carefully. [Jaguar forums clearcoat thread](https://www.jaguarforums.com/forum/xj-xj8-xjr-x308-27/jaguar-paint-clearcoat-failures-144030/)
- Trim vulnerability:
  - Gloss black bits scratch easily; chrome trim corrodes depending on wash/parking habits. Leaky sunroofs or blocked drains can rot headliners over time — inspect corners.
- Body recalls & safety systems:
  - Jaguar XJ recall history is long and model‑year specific; check the VIN for any outstanding campaigns. Some recalls relate to steering, airbags or electrical modules. Use the recall databases when you inspect a car. [RecallExplained / Jaguar VIN recall search](https://recallexplained.com/jaguar/xj/) | [Jaguar VIN recall lookup](https://www.jaguar.com/en-us/jdx/ownership/vin-recall.html)
- ADAS (sensors/cameras):
  - Forward camera calibration is critical after any windscreen work; poor calibration gives false alerts or disabled Adaptive Cruise Control. If the windscreen’s been replaced, insist on proof of ADAS calibration. [Bang Auto Glass on ADAS calibration](https://bangautoglass.com/blog/jaguar-xj-adas-calibration-warning-signs-alerts-sensors-service-timing)

Interior:
- Infotainment and displays:
  - The XJ’s centre touchscreen(s) and InControl systems are a headline weakness: blank screens, freezing, BT drops and intermittent audio/navigation faults are repeatedly reported. The later **InControl Touch Pro** displays (larger 10‑inch units) improved UX but introduced replacement cost issues if the module dies. Diagnostics often point to a **Front Interface Module** failure or software/bricking after updates. [Jaguar Forums; Go‑Parts display guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-jaguar-xj-2010-2017) | [Go‑Parts InControl issues](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-land-rover-range-rover-evoque-jaguar-xf-land-rover-range-rover-sport-2016-2021)
- Electrical oddities:
  - 12V battery drain and module quirks can leave an XJ sleepy after a few days parked (infotainment/ECU sleep states sometimes misbehave). If a car has been sitting, ask for an owner’s log and load‑test. [Jaguar forums on blank screen & power issues](https://www.jaguarforums.com/forum/xj-xj6-xj8-xjr-x350-x358-28/touch-screen-blank-nothing-works-57793/)
- Interior wear:
  - Seat trim panels and switchgear age badly on high‑use cars (rideshare B&E will accelerate wear). Switches and seat trim may crack; replacement panels are available but expensive. [Go‑Parts seat trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/seat-trim-panel-jaguar-xj-2010-2019)

Mechanically:
- Engineering reputation:
  - Jaguar built a brilliant, supple chassis and beautiful engines — but compared to German rivals the XJ historically trades lower resale for higher running‑cost risk on older examples. Many faults are electronics and ancillaries rather than fundamental structure.
- Security vulnerabilities:
  - The industry‑wide “CAN injection” / headlight‑connector hack lets thieves that know what they’re doing access vehicle CAN wiring and inject messages to spoof a key present. This is not a Jaguar‑only problem — it’s a modern vehicle protocol weakness exploited in the wild. Mitigations: physical immobilisation, hide the key, and avoid leaving cars with obvious headlight/bumper damage. [Ken Tindell CAN injection explainer / CAN‑CIA / The Register coverage](
https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/) | (https://www.can-cia.org/fileadmin/cia/documents/publications/cnlm/june_2023/cnlm_23-2_p20_the_can_injection_attack_ken_tindel_canis_automotive_labs.pdf) | (https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how_thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/)
- Aftermarket anti‑theft suggestions:
  - Cheap and effective: a visible steering wheel/collar lock, mechanical wheel clamp or immobiliser. For an XJ with a valet key, consider adding a rated aftermarket alarm and a discreetly‑installed ECU power‑cut or kill switch (fitted professionally). Jaguar now uses UWB in later keys to counter relay attacks — but older keys don’t have that protection. [Jaguar Keyless entry / UWB tech page](https://www.jaguar.com/en-gb/jdx/keylessentry/index.html)

Engines:
(Every engine below is given the common symptom/failure checklist you’ll see time and again — note the maintenance advice: heavy or commercial use (rideshare/taxis) should have shorter service intervals; a conservative owner schedule is oil service every 10,000 km and inspection/service items at 15,000 km intervals, but always confirm with the car’s stamped log book and the Jaguar service schedule.)

- *AJD 3.0‑litre V6 diesel (common late‑model XJ diesel)*
  - Failure points:
    - EGR valve clogging and carbon build‑up — leads to limp mode or poor idle (common in long‑life diesels used on lots of short trips). [SelectedWorld diesel review](https://selectedworld.com/test-jaguar-xj-x351-2009-2019-engine-3-0-v6-24v-turbo-diesel-power-275-hp-code-ajd-v6-technical-specs-reliability-common-issues/)
    - DPF regeneration problems on low‑mileage/short‑trip cars — blocked DPFs need forced regen or replacement.
    - Turbo and boost control problems possible if oil changes neglected.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Frequent long runs to allow DPF regen, oil/filters every 10,000 km for severe duty, full service items at 15,000 km.
  - What to inspect:
    - Service history for regular oil changes, EGR cleaning records, recent DPF/regen history, turbo smoke.

- *2.7‑litre V6 diesel (AJD 2.7)*
  - Failure points:
    - Weaker than the 3.0 — can be noisier and more stressed in heavy XJ bodies; similar EGR/DPF issues but often appears earlier due to lower torque margins. [AUTODOC / SelectedWorld]
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Same conservative schedule; beware of short‑trip urban use.

- *3.0‑litre supercharged V6 petrol*
  - Failure points:
    - Valve cover gasket and coolant minor leaks reported; listen for supercharger whining that changes character (bearing or drive issues).
    - Electrical misbehaviour can look like engine faults (sensors/ignition coil faults).
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Oil change at least every 10,000–12,000 km if you enjoy spirited driving; spark plugs and coils at ~80–120k depending on condition.

- *4.2‑litre AJ‑V8 and 5.0‑litre AJ‑V8 (NA and supercharged)*
  - Failure points:
    - Timing chain guide/tensioner wear historically a concern on some Jaguar V8s (earlier AJ‑V8 variants notably had plastic tensioner components that can fail catastrophically if neglected). Upgraded aluminium tensioners are a common workshop retrofit. [thejaguarexperts common failures](https://www.thejaguarexperts.com/common-failures) | [myenginespecs AJ‑V8](https://myenginespecs.com/jaguar/jaguar-4-2-v8-engine-problems-and-solutions/)
    - Supercharger insulator or bearing noises on forced‑induction variants — expensive to repair if bearings go.
    - Oil leaks and valve cover gasket failures.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Conservative oil service every 10,000 km on supercharged V8s; inspect timing chain components by 100–150k km if no service history for tensioner replacement.
  - What to look for:
    - Excessive cam/timing chain rattle at cold start, smoking, clutching of supercharger belt, heavy oil consumption.

Transmission:
- The XJ commonly uses the **ZF 8HP** 8‑speed torque converter automatic — a strong, modern automatic but not immune.
  - Known failure modes:
    - Harsh or delayed shifting, torque‑converter shudder, valve body/solenoid wear — symptoms that start as adaptation faults and progress to more expensive repairs (solenoid pack/valve body replace or rebuild). [ZF 8HP common issues summary] (https://bmwinsights.com/zf-8hp-8-speed-torque-converter/)
    - Fluid neglect accelerates wear; some owners report earlier failures in high‑torque supercharged variants.
  - Service regime:
    - Regular fluid and filter changes are worth their weight — many independent transmission shops recommend fluid change intervals of roughly 60–100k km depending on use (factory intervals and opinions vary). Check the service history for recent gearbox fluid/valve body work. [TransmissionTroubleHub / ASR Gearbox writeups](https://www.transmissiontroublehub.com/jaguar-xj-transmission-problems-facts-and-recalls/) | (https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/zf-8hp-8-speed-torque-converter/)

#### 3. Should you buy a used Jaguar XJ?
Short answer: maybe — but only if you buy the right car for your budget and mechanic, and factor in realistic running costs. Long answer below.

- NSW dealer market snapshot (live data provided):
  - Active listings: 1 — Price range/median: AUD $99,950 — Year range: 1985 – 1985 — Median odometer: 1,985 km — Transmission: Automatic: 1
  - Note: that NSW snapshot looks like a single classic/collector XJ (1985) listed near $100k — not representative of the broad X351 modern used market, which trades far lower. Use this NSW dealer listing data as a VIN‑specific datapoint rather than the general price floor for the XJ range. (Data provided in user message.)

- Typical used‑market pricing (wider market references):
  - In broader marketplaces the XJ sells across a huge span: cheap, older examples under AUD $10k exist; late‑model X351s commonly appear in the mid‑teens-to‑low‑$40k range depending on year, mileage and trim in international data. Depressed values are a pattern for Jaguar XJ vs German rivals. [Edmunds; CarGurus; TrueCar listings summaries] (https://www.edmunds.com/used-jaguar-xj/) | (https://www.cargurus.com/research/price-trends/Jaguar-XJ-Series-d286)
- Depreciation metrics:
  - Jaguar brand depreciation is steep compared with the segment average — CarEdge reports ~59% depreciation after 5 years across Jaguar range (XJ tends to fall faster than S‑Class/7 Series). Historical XJ curves show significant early‑ownership value loss which is why you can buy late‑model XJs cheaply if you’re prepared for running costs. [CarEdge Jaguar depreciation](https://caredge.com/jaguar/depreciation) | (https://caredge.com/compare/depreciation/panamera-vs-xj-vs-s-class-vs-7-series-vs-a8)
- TCO strategy & maths:
  - Fuel:
    - Petrol V8 supercharged XJs are fuel‑hungry. Real‑world data shows big variance — fuel figures from aggregated owner logs and published figures put petrol V8 combined economy well into double‑digit L/100km. Diesel V6s will typically return markedly better economy (especially on steady highway runs). [FuelConsumptions / Fuelly] (https://www.fuelconsumptions.com/jaguar-xj.html) | (https://www.fuelly.com/car/jaguar/xj)
  - Maintenance & repairs:
    - Expect above‑average spend for out‑of‑warranty electronics, infotainment, air suspension (if fitted), and timing chain/valve cover/gasket work on older engines.
    - Transmission repairs (ZF 8HP solenoids/valve body) are a possible mid‑five‑figure bill if neglected.
  - Insurance & registration:
    - Insuring an XJ will cost more than an XF or mid‑range sedan — premiums reflect the luxury segment and repair costs.
  - Hybrid/EV premium math:
    - Jaguar never broadly offered widespread PHEV/EV XJ stock during X351 life — the electric XJ successor was planned but the production story changed. KBB’s hybrid break‑even methodology applies: paying a premium for a hybrid/PHEV only pays back if you do high annual urban mileage where electric kilometres replace petrol kilometres and/or you have access to cheap electricity. For older XJs, the purchase premium of rare hybrid/EV variants (where they exist) is unlikely to be recovered by fuel savings given typical Australian annual km for private owners. [KBB hybrid math / Jaguar PHEV explainer] (https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/) | (https://www.jaguar.com/en-xi/jdx/electric-cars/plug-in-hybrid.html)
- Recommendation (which XJ to buy and when to walk):
  - Best financial sweet spot: look for a well‑serviced mid‑life X351 (2013–2016) with the **3.0‑litre diesel** or the **3.0 supercharged V6** — you get reasonable performance, lower running costs (diesel), and a better chance that earlier‑life teething issues have been fixed by prior owners. Check for a stamped service book showing oil/service every 10–15k km and specific work on EGR/DPF (diesel) or timing chain guides (V8).
    - Why 2013–2016? By then many early software/infotainment bugs and build‑set issues were better understood and some running faults have been addressed; yet you avoid the last big‑value decline that hits the post‑2016 heavily optioned low‑mileage cars.
    - If you want rear seat grandeur and can pay: Portfolio/Autobiography long‑wheelbase models give proper limo comfort, but costs scale up fast (insurance, tyres, maintenance).
  - Walk away if:
    - The car’s service history is light or missing for major items (timing chain service, gearbox fluid, EGR/DPF work).
    - Infotainment is dead and the seller wants the same price as a fully‑functioning example — replacement modules and coding are expensive.
    - There are unresolved ADAS/windscreen replacements without calibration receipts.

Buying checklist (practical, bite‑sized):
- VIN recall check with Jaguar and government databases. [Jaguar VIN recall lookup](https://www.jaguar.com/en-us/jdx/ownership/vin-recall.html)
- Full service history: oil every 10–15k km and recorded major jobs (timing chain/valve cover, gearbox fluid).
- Evidence of ADAS calibration after any windscreen work. [Bang Auto Glass ADAS note](https://bangautoglass.com/blog/jaguar-xj-adas-calibration-warning-signs-alerts-sensors-service-timing)
- Test for infotainment reliability (boot the car cold and run the system).
- Drive test: listen for timing chain rattle, gearbox shift roughness/shudder, supercharger whining changes, and check for DPF/regen symptoms on diesels.
- Security: ask where keys are stored and consider an after‑market mechanical lock until you can install an alarm/kill switch.

Final blunt take: buy a sympathetic XJ if you love the look, the ride and can afford a good independent Jaguar specialist. If you want an XJ as a daily with zero drama, buy a late‑model, low‑option example with full service records — or pick a cheaper XF and save yourself grief and cash. If you’re chasing theatre and rear‑seat space for occasional chauffeur duty, the XJ still delivers the charm — but it will cost you for that charm when things wear out.
