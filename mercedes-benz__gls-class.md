#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: Third‑generation GLS (internal X167), produced from 2019 → present (Australian used‑market examples in the NSW snapshot are 2020–2021).
- Where it sits: Mercedes‑Benz flagship three‑row SUV — above GLE/X167 and under S‑Class in the SUV/brand hierarchy (the GLS is Mercedes’ full‑size, 7‑seat luxury contender) ([Mercedes‑Benz USA GLS overview](https://www.mbusa.com/en/vehicles/class/gls/suv), [Wikipedia X167 summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_GLS_%28X167%29)).
- Global platform / parts commonality: X167 shares Mercedes’ latest large‑car architecture and many drivetrain/electronics modules with other full‑size Mercedes SUVs and saloons — which helps parts availability but also means shared failure modes across the range (MB tech pages & third‑party spec aggregators summarise the common 9‑speed gearbox, 48‑V mild‑hybrid assistance on petrol engines and shared ADAS hardware) ([Edmunds GLS specs](https://www.edmunds.com/mercedes-benz/gls-class/2025/features-specs/), [Auto‑Data GLS tech](https://www.auto-data.net/en/mercedes-benz-gls-model-2084)).
- Engines / outputs / drivetrains present in the Australian used market (2020–2021 examples you’ll see):
  - **3.0L inline‑6 mild‑hybrid** (GLS450) — turbocharged I6 with 48‑volt assist; typically paired to 4MATIC AWD and a 9‑speed automatic ([Edmunds GLS trims/specs](https://www.edmunds.com/mercedes-benz/gls-class/2024/trims/), [MotorTrend GLS coverage](https://www.motortrend.com/cars/mercedes-benz/gls-class/2024)).
  - **4.0L V8 bi‑turbo** (GLS580 / AMG derivatives) — twin‑turbo V8 with mild‑hybrid systems on later models, AWD and a high‑performance 9‑speed (AMG) gearbox on AMG models ([The Car Connection specs](https://www.thecarconnection.com/specifications/mercedes-benz_gls-class_2024), [Wikipedia X167](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_GLS_%28X167%29)).
  - **3.0L OM642 diesel V6** — diesel GLS derivatives are common in fleet/long‑distance usage markets (DPF and turbo system caveats apply) ([Mercedes OM642 guide](https://en.mercedesassistance.com/mercedes-om642-engine/)).
  - Transmission: 9‑speed torque‑converter automatic (Mercedes 9G‑Tronic family) is the norm across petrol/diesel variants ([The Car Connection](https://www.thecarconnection.com/specifications/mercedes-benz_gls-class_2024)).
  - Drive: All current Australian examples are all‑wheel drive (4MATIC) as standard.
- Market rivals: BMW X7, Audi Q7 (though smaller), Range Rover, Cadillac Escalade and Lincoln Navigator sit in the same consideration set — full‑size, three‑row luxury SUVs buyers compare against the GLS ([The Car Connection rivals comparison](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/mercedes-benz_gls-class_choices), [Edmunds GLS overview](https://www.edmunds.com/mercedes-benz/gls-class/)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Mercedes‑Benz GLS‑Class?

Exterior:
- Panel fit & trim:
  - Generally solid panel alignment for a vehicle this size, but owners report recurring finish failures on exterior mouldings and arch trims — fading, peeling or fitment issues on wheel arch and roof mouldings have been reported on GLE/GLS common panels ([Go‑Parts exterior trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-mercedes-benz-gle-class-mercedes-benz-gls-class-mercedes-benz-gle-class-coupe-2020-2026)).
  - AMG/appearance gloss or painted trim (black exhaust tips, painted mouldings) can peel or fade — inspect tips and rear bumper surrounds closely on AMG/AMG‑line cars ([Go‑Parts exhaust tip guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exhaust-tail-pipe-tip-mercedes-benz-gle53-amg-mercedes-benz-amg-gt-mercedes-benz-gls63-amg-2018-2023)).
- Paint & corrosion:
  - No systemic rust epidemic reported for X167 — but check paintwork around lower doors and boot sills on high‑use vehicles (common sense applies; look for overspray or respray lines).
- Recalls & safety hardware:
  - The GLS has accumulated several recalls and campaigns across model years — always run the VIN through Mercedes’ recall portal or NHTSA to confirm outstanding actions before purchase ([Mercedes‑Benz recall lookup](https://www.mbusa.com/en/recall), [NHTSA recall portal](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)). Aggregators show numerous campaigns covering different years ([VinCheckEngine GLS recalls list](https://vincheckengine.com/recalls/mercedes-benz/gls)).
- ADAS (Active safety) quirks:
  - Radar/camera sensor faults and ADAS false positives have been reported — symptoms include adaptive cruise (DISTRONIC) false braking or sudden intervention and radar sensor errors that disable multiple driver‑assist systems. Some incidents required sensor/module replacement or re‑calibration; in several cases cleaning or firmware updates resolved the problem ([CarComplaints AEB reports](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Mercedes-Benz/GLS_450/2024/brakes/emergency_breaking_constantly_malfunctions.shtml), [CarsafetyDB driver assist reports](https://carsafetydb.com/mercedes-benz/gls-class/2022/driver-assist), [Go‑Parts radar sensor guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/radar-sensor-mercedes-benz-cls-mercedes-benz-e-class-mercedes-benz-amg-gt-2019-2023)).
  - Practical check: ensure all ADAS warnings are clear, test adaptive cruise on a low‑risk road and request service history for radar/camera calibrations or software updates.

Interior:
- Build & wear:
  - High‑quality materials, but the large expanses of soft leather/trim and coated dash surfaces can show wear — threads on owner forums and complaint threads cite dash finish peeling or sun damage in hotter climates (dealer repair bills can be significant; one owner reported ~AUD$1,900 for dash replacement on earlier models) ([MBWorld dash peeling thread](https://mbworld.org/forums/gl63-amg-gls63-x166-x167/896333-gls-dash-peeling.html)).
  - Third‑row access is generous for the class; check door check arms and sill wear on high‑use people‑carrier examples (rideshare/taxi duty).
- Electrical / infotainment:
  - Infotainment (MBUX) problems are the most common interior complaint: frozen screens, blank displays, random reboots, Bluetooth pairing problems and wireless charging module failures appear frequently on X167 GLS/related GLE models. Dealer software reflash or module replacement are typical remedies ([MercedesBlue MBUX problems guide](https://mercedesblue.com/mercedes-models/suvs/gls-class/mercedes-gls-infotainment-problems/), [Go‑Parts MBUX black screen guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-mercedes-benz-gle-class-mercedes-benz-gls-class-2020-2023), [MBWorld infotainment threads](https://mbworld.org/forums/gls-class-x167-255/)).
  - 12‑V battery and 48‑V mild‑hybrid battery behaviour: owners report occasional parasitic drain symptoms and 48‑V system warnings — make sure the battery(s) have documented service records; high mileages and short‑trip urban use are worse for battery life in these systems ([MBWorld forum reports](https://mbworld.org/forums/gls-class-x167-255/)).
  - Screen sizes/versions: MBUX dual‑screen layouts and central displays vary by trim; confirm whether your car has the larger portrait screen or a smaller unit as part of the options list. Ask for proofs of software updates in service history — many problems are firmware‑fixable.
- Practical test points:
  - Test every button, touch area and rear‑seat USB/12V point. Check seat motors, memory functions, panoramic roof seals and the third‑row folding motors (slow or noisy actuators are an expensive surprise).

Mechanically:
- Engineering reputation: Mercedes’ diesel and petrol large‑capacity engines are generally well engineered but complex; the GLS shares drivetrain and electronics bus components with many Mercedes models — meaning spare parts and dealer expertise are widely available but repairs can be expensive.
- Security vulnerabilities:
  - Modern Mercedes networks are not immune to CAN‑bus injection attacks and keyless‑related bypasses. Research shows thieves increasingly use external wiring access (headlight/taillight connectors) to inject CAN frames and emulate a key present — this is a manufacturer‑level vulnerability across makes, not GLS‑specific, but the GLS’ value makes it a target ([CAN Injection technical write‑up](https://www.can-cia.org/fileadmin/cia/documents/publications/cnlm/june_2023/cnlm_23-2_p20_the_can_injection_attack_ken_tindel_canis_automotive_labs.pdf), [The Register coverage](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how_thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/), [Autoblog on headlight CAN injection](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update)).
  - Practical countermeasures: mechanical steering‑wheel locks, immobiliser upgrades with secure GPS tracking, and hardening of exterior wiring (ask an automotive electrician about sealing vulnerable headlight/taillight connectors) are sensible. For high‑value GLS, consider dealer‑available anti‑theft packages or reputable aftermarket alarm/immobiliser systems with CAN‑intrusion detection.
- High‑priority security checks at purchase:
  - Confirm that all PATS/immobiliser warnings are absent.
  - Verify key fob history (spare keys present) and request recent locksmith/dealer pairing records.

Engines:
(italicised subsections for every engine variant discussed)

- *3.0L inline‑6 mild‑hybrid (commonly found in GLS450)*  
  - Known failure points: electrical/ISG (48‑V) system warnings and battery/charger issues reported by owners; software/ECU updates have addressed some stalling/shift issues on related models. The mild‑hybrid system reduces fuel use but adds electrical complexity — ensure the 48‑V system shows no fault codes and has a healthy battery state in service records ([Edmunds 3.0L I6 spec notes](https://www.edmunds.com/mercedes-benz/gls-class/2025/features-specs/), [CarComplaints recall on stalling related to downshift behaviour](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2024/mercedes-recalls-gle-450-gls-450-stalling-engines.shtml)).  
  - Preventative maintenance: service at Mercedes’ recommended intervals (dealer schedules often 10,000 km service cycles for inspections/fluids; confirm logbook), pay attention to software updates for engine/transmission and check battery health every 2 years/40,000 km if used in short‑trip urban driving.
  - Typical symptoms to watch for: check engine light/limp mode, intermittent stalling while slowing (recall/TCU software fixes exist for the stalling downshift scenario) — ask the dealer to confirm TSB/recall compliance before purchase.

- *4.0L V8 biturbo (GLS580 / AMG GLS models)*  
  - Known failure points: turbocharger oil feed/coolant routing and exhaust manifold heat cycling are common wear areas on high‑power twin‑turbo V8s across manufacturers; AMG variants add complexity with performance cooling and engine electronics that cost more to fix. Expect higher running costs and expensive servicing for turbo/exhaust or intercooler hardware.  
  - Preventative maintenance: strict oil‑change discipline with correct spec synthetic oil, typically 10,000–15,000 km intervals depending on service plan. On used buys insist on full dealer service history — neglect on these engines shows quickly as smoke, oil consumption or turbo whine.

- *3.0L OM642 diesel V6*  
  - Known failure points: DPF (diesel particulate filter) clogging, EGR valve soot buildup and turbocharger failures are the classic diesel headaches — especially if the vehicle has many short trips or urban use. OM642 has an established failure profile and recommended routines exist for long‑route maintenance ([OM642 guide](https://en.mercedesassistance.com/mercedes-om642-engine/)).  
  - Preventative maintenance: regular highway runs to allow DPF regeneration, timely oil changes with low‑ash diesel oil, and EGR/Turbo inspections at ~80–120k km intervals. Check for high‑cost repairs like DPF replacement or turbo exchange on high‑kilometre examples.

Transmission:
- The GLS uses Mercedes’ 9‑speed torque‑converter automatic across petrol/diesel lines. Common failure modes and behaviours to know:
  - Harsh downshifts, shudder on torque‑converter lock‑up, and abrupt shifts reported by owners — some are fixed with TCU software updates or torque‑converter adaptations, others require component replacement (clutch packs or torque converter). A large recall/campaign addressed stalling due to incomplete downshifts on some GLS/GLE 450 models via TCU/ECU software updates ([CarComplaints recall note](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2024/mercedes-recalls-gle-450-gls-450-stalling-engines.shtml), [Go‑Parts 9G‑Tronic shudder guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/torque-converter-mercedes-benz-metris-mercedes-benz-e-class-mercedes-benz-cclass-2016-2023)).  
  - Practical checks pre‑purchase:
    - Test-drive in all modes (Comfort, Sport, Off‑road if fitted) and under a variety of throttle loads. Listen for shudder at low speeds (torque converter lock‑up), heavy lurches on downshifts, or delayed engagement from standstill.
    - Ask the dealer for any TSB/TCU re‑flashes and have a reputable independent trans shop scan for pending transmission adaptations/learning faults.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Mercedes‑Benz GLS‑Class?

NSW dealer snapshot (live): Active listings 3 — Price range AUD $73,800 – $152,990; median AUD $113,395; Year range 2020–2021; Median odometer ~80,218 km; Transmission: Automatic only. (Use this as your local market comparator — you’re looking at high‑value, relatively recent examples with solid kilometres.)

Price & depreciation reality:
- Current NSW asking bands for 2020–2021 GLS: AUD $73.8k–$153k (median AUD $113,395). High variance reflects spec (GLS450 vs GLS580/AMG), options and provenance (dealer‑backed vs private). Use the median as a realistic market value for a mid‑spec 2020–2021 GLS with ~80k km.
- Depreciation context: GLS suffers steep early depreciation relative to segment staples. Aggregators estimate ~34% drop in 3 years and ~48–57% over 5 years in many scenarios — Maybach/AMG variants fall faster in absolute dollars because they start at much higher MSRPs ([CarEdge depreciation summary](https://caredge.com/mercedes-benz/gls/depreciation/), [TheMoneyCalculator 3‑year drop estimate](https://www.themoneycalculator.com/vehicle-finance/calculators/car-depreciation-by-make-and-model/MERCEDES-BENZ/GLS_CLASS/), [CarsCounsel value note](https://carscounsel.com/mercedes-benz-gls-best-worst-years/)).
- Bottom line: you’ll buy a near‑new luxury flagship for a mid‑six‑figure discount compared with new list prices, but residuals keep falling — budget for that.

Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy:
- Fuel and consumption:
  - Official combined figures for big V8s are fuel‑hungry: a GLS580 lists at ~16 mpg (US) combined — that converts to roughly 14.7 L/100km. Use the EPA source as a benchmark ([FuelEconomy.gov GLS580 2025 data](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=48139)). Real‑world from owner reports: GLS450 owner averages are often in the high teens mpg (~19 mpg ~ 12.4 L/100km) on Fuelly users — expect real consumption ~12–15 L/100km depending on driving style and load ([Fuelly GLS450 owner data](https://www.fuelly.com/car/mercedes-benz/gls450)). Convert these to your annual km to estimate fuel spend.
  - Hybrid/mild‑hybrid systems reduce consumption modestly on stop/start and light‑load street driving but don’t make the GLS an economical commuter. Given the high fixed costs (insurance, servicing), fuel savings alone rarely recover a premium paid for a hybrid variant within short ownership windows.
- Maintenance & repairs:
  - Expect dealer servicing costs higher than mainstream SUVs. High‑cost items — turbos, DPFs, transmission work, ADAS recalibration and infotainment module replacement — are realistic repairs on older/worked GLS examples.
- Insurance & registration:
  - Top‑end premiums and luxury vehicle insurance rates apply; factor higher insurance and luxury plate/rego costs into annual TCO.
- Depreciation vs fuel trade:
  - Paying a premium for a GLS450 mild‑hybrid vs a used V8 GLS580 can make sense if your driving is long‑distance highway (diesel or mild‑hybrid returns more benefit) and you care about fuel economy. But in urban mixed driving, the modest fuel savings rarely offset higher purchase premiums once you include servicing and potential repair risk. Use real‑world MPG (Fuelly) vs EPA to model expected litres per year.
- Practical TCO approach:
  - Calculate annual km × expected L/100km × local fuel cost to estimate yearly fuel spend.
  - Add annual service/maintenance (dealer logged service quotes, plus a cushion for one mid‑life expensive repair every 3–5 years).
  - Add insurance and registration.
  - Compare to the price differential between trims (e.g., buy a well‑optioned GLS450 over a tired GLS580 if savings in purchase price and lower insurance beat fuel differences).

Should you buy one?
- Strong yes — if:
  - You value three‑row space, S‑Class‑level tech and on‑road manners in a large luxury SUV.
  - You can afford higher servicing and are buying from a dealer with a full service history and completed recalls/TSBs (essential).
  - You want the brand cache and a comfortable long‑distance cruiser (diesel or I6 mild‑hybrid GLS450 are compelling for long trips).
- Lean no — if:
  - You’re budget constrained and can’t absorb surprise repairs or high insurance.
  - You need a purely economical people‑mover — there are cheaper large SUVs and premium‑value alternatives (older Land Rover/Volvo/BMW alternatives may be cheaper to maintain).
- Best value pick (sweet spot): For 2020–2021 used market in NSW the GLS450 (the 3.0L I6 mild‑hybrid) is the financial sweet spot — it balances performance, efficiency and lower buy‑in price vs V8 or AMG cars, and Edmunds/owners recommend starting at GLS450 as the sensible baseline ([Edmunds recommends GLS450 as a starting point](https://www.edmunds.com/mercedes-benz/gls-class/2025/trims/)). Look for a 2020–2021 GLS450 with full Mercedes service history, TSB/recall compliance, two keys, and ADAS radar re‑calibration stamp.
- Examples of what to avoid:
  - High‑mileage AMG/GLS580 without full service history or with a history of DPF/turbo work — these can hide expensive repairs.
  - Vehicles showing unexplained ADAS warnings, intermittent infotainment failures, torque‑converter shudder or recall non‑compliance.

Final buying checklist (practical, quick):
- Ask the seller for VIN and run a recall/TSB check via Mercedes’ recall portal and NHTSA tools ([Mercedes recall lookup](https://www.mbusa.com/en/recall), [NHTSA recall portal](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)).
- Request full dealer/service history and confirmation that any transmission/engine/stalling campaigns were completed (2020–2023 GLS450/GLE450 campaign exists) ([CarComplaints recall article](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2024/mercedes-recalls-gle-450-gls-450-stalling-engines.shtml)).
- Test drive: look for transmission smoothness in all modes, no ADAS false braking, clear MBUX operation, and no torque‑converter shudder.
- Inspect: clean radar housing, no paint/trim peeling, no dash delamination, check wireless charging/phone connectivity.
- Security: fit a mechanical lock or upgrade alarm/immobiliser if the car will park street side — smart thieves know luxury SUVs are worth the extra effort (read up on CAN injection methods and take sensible hardening steps) ([CAN injection research](https://www.can-cia.org/fileadmin/cia/documents/publications/cnlm/june_2023/cnlm_23-2_p20_the_can_injection_attack_ken_tindel_canis_automotive_labs.pdf), [Autoblog theft coverage](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update)).
