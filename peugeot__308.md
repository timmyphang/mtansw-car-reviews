#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: this review centres on the second‑generation Peugeot 308 (the model you’re most likely to find from 2014–2021 on the Australian used market) — the newer third‑generation arrived in 2021/2022. The 308 has been the compact / C‑segment hatchback staple for Peugeot. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_308).
- Internal/platform codes: the second‑gen 308 sits on PSA’s EMP2 modular underbody (the EMP2 family first appeared on mid‑sized PSA models from 2013). That matters: the platform is shared across many Stellantis/PSA cars so parts and mechanical know‑how aren’t rare. [EMP2 platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMP2_platform).
- Where it sits in the Peugeot line-up: the 308 is the compact family hatch — above the 208 and below the 508 in the Peugeot range. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_308).
- Platform siblings (parts availability advantage): EMP2 underpins multiple PSA/Stellantis models — helping with parts commonality and aftermarket supply. See the EMP2 overview for model lists. [EMP2 platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMP2_platform).
- Typical Australian market engines / outputs / transmissions / drive layouts you’ll see on used 308s:
  - Petrol: **1.2 PureTech** 3‑cyl turbo (popular small petrol), **1.6 THP** (earlier turbo petrol on higher trims) — usually paired with 6‑speed manual or automatic/8‑speed auto depending on year. Known outputs range from ~110–165 hp across petrol variants. Sources note these families and their presence across years. [Auto‑Data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/peugeot-308-model-573), [Carknownfaults](https://www.carknownfaults.com/peugeot/308).
  - Diesel: **1.6 BlueHDi** diesels are common on late‑2010s examples (torque for touring, common in fleet models). [Auto‑Data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/peugeot-308-model-573).
  - Hybrid / PHEV / EV: third‑gen and later 308s introduced hybrids, PHEV and e‑308 electric variants. If you’re looking at a 2017 car, these are not relevant — but they affect residuals and parts cross‑support now. [Stellantis spec guide (new 308)](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uploads/uk/attachment/4984/0244508peugeotspecguidesjuly308eversion20-650a0a92a995a.pdf).
  - Drivetrain: all of the above are front‑wheel drive for mainstream hatch variants; e‑variants use different electrified drivetrains. [Auto‑Data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/peugeot-308-model-573).
- Marketplace rivals in Australia: Volkswagen Golf, Ford Focus, Honda Civic, Mazda 3 and the newer small premium hatches (A‑Class/A3/1 Series) — the 308 straddles mainstream and slightly upmarket compact hatch territory. [Top Gear](https://www.topgear.com/car-reviews/peugeot/308-0), [What Car?](https://www.whatcar.com/peugeot/308/hatchback/review/n17380), [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/peugeot/308/vs).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Peugeot 308?
This is the part where we’re blunt: the 308 is charming to drive in the right trim, but it’s got a few recurring faults that will bite your wallet if you ignore them.

Exterior
- Panel fit and trim:
  - Owners report door/trunk gaps and rattle‑type trim complaints on various years — not catastrophic but annoying and a telltale sign of rough life or poor post‑accident repair. Forum threads are full of panel/trim repair threads. [Peugeot forums](https://www.thepeugeotforums.com/forums/peugeot-308.26/).
  - Gloss/black trim and matt plastics can show scuffs and lifting; check around door handles, tailgate lips and lower bumper trims for wear.
- Paint durability / corrosion:
  - No systemic Australian rust epidemic noted for the 308, but older examples (pre‑2015) show normal panel stone chips and scuffs — check sills and wheelarches.
- Recalls and safety campaigns:
  - There are multiple recall entries across the 308 lifespan (airbag/ECU/engine/fuel system items appear in public recall databases). Always VIN‑check any 308 you’re inspecting. [Regit recalls summary](https://www.regit.cars/recalls/peugeot-308) and aggregated recall lists show dozens of campaigns. [Vehicle-Recall](https://vehicle-recall.co.uk/recalls/cars/peugeot/308).
- ADAS/sensors:
  - Adaptive cruise, AEB and parking aids on later 308s are not immune to faults. Owners report intermittent ACC failures, radar and camera calibration issues (especially after minor impacts), and dashboard ADAS error lights requiring dealer recalibration or module replacement. Some ADAS‑related recall campaigns have occurred. If the car has ADAS, make sure the system functions faultlessly on a test drive and check for recent calibrations. [Peugeot forums ADAS threads](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/adas-malfunction.384752/), [Vehicle‑Recall example R/2025/542](https://vehicle-recall.co.uk/recall/r-2025-542).

Interior
- Cabin electronics & infotainment:
  - Recurrent touchscreen freezes and unresponsive infotainment behaviour across late‑2010s 308s — owners report temporary fixes (soft resets) but persistent faults often need dealer firmware or module swaps. Wireless Android Auto/CarPlay connection issues are a common complaint; wired is more reliable. [Peugeot forums — touchscreen freezing](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/308-touchscreen-freezing-and-more.359538/), [Hjalp infotainment troubleshooting](https://www.hjalp.ai/article/peugeot-308-infotainment-system-issues-and-fixes/).
  - Peugeot provides a USB update tool for map/firmware updates — insist on the latest update and ask the dealer for records of software actions if the screen misbehaves. [Peugeot map update PDF](https://www.peugeot.co.uk/content/dam/peugeot/master/media/manual/1/en/map-software-nac-en.632380.pdf).
- Durability under hard use:
  - For rideshare / high‑mileage duty: upholstery and plastics wear as you’d expect. The 308 isn’t a workhorse, so check door sills (height and check‑strap wear), seat bolsters and boot carpet. Expect to replace cheap interior trims if the car’s been flogged.
- Electrical quirks:
  - Look out for 12V battery drain symptoms and central BSI‑type faults (unexplained warning lights, central locking oddities). Forum owners recommend scanning for stored ECU faults before purchase. [HonestCarCheck common problems](https://honestcarcheck.co.uk/cars/peugeot/308/common-problems/).

Mechanically
- Brand engineering reputation:
  - Peugeot/PSA engineering is competent and parts are common due to platform sharing — but the company is not a reliability saint; there are specific engine and transmission problem clusters (below).
- Security vulnerabilities:
  - Modern Peugeots, like many manufacturers, are susceptible to keyless relay and CAN‑bus injection attacks if left unprotected. Thieves increasingly use OBD/CAN hacks to start the car. Consider layered security: Faraday pouches for keys, steering‑column locks, and a CAN‑bus‑aware immobiliser / tracking device. [CAN bus attack overview](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how-thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/), and aftermarket CAN‑bus immobiliser products for Peugeot exist (eg Autowatch Ghost 2). [Autowatch Ghost 2 product](https://www.carsoundsecurity.com/product/ghost-2-can-bus-immobiliser-by-autowatch-for-peugeot-fully-fitted/).
- Recommended anti‑theft measures:
  - Fit a professionally installed CAN‑aware immobiliser / tracker (Ghost‑style), use key Faraday pouches overnight, and park in well‑lit/secure areas. These are practical mitigations against relay/OBD/CAN hacks. [HelpNetSecurity summary on keyless thefts](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/05/keyless-car-theft-protection/).

Engines
- Below are the engine variants highlighted repeatedly in owner reports and technical guides — I’ll flag the common failure points and what to check.

  *1.2 PureTech (3‑cyl turbo)*
  - Reputation: widely fitted across PSA small to mid cars; light and frugal but not invincible.
  - Failure points:
    - Timing‑system wear / chain tensioner symptoms reported on some PureTech variants (rattle on cold start, issues emerging around 80–120k km in some reports). [AUTODOC timing chain/owner notes](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-peugeot-308), [AUTODOC common faults](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/peugeot-308-common-faults-what-every-driver-in-the-uk-needs-to-know).
    - Oil consumption — owners report checking oil more often than the service schedule suggests.
  - Prevention:
    - Check service records for regular oil changes and any timing‑system work. Listen for rattles at cold start, and have a compression/timing check if unsure.
    - Service interval advice: follow the logbook; many owners and independent workshops service small turbo petrols at ~10,000–15,000 km or 12 months — check the factory manual for exact mileage/time. If in doubt, inspect oil history and ask for a mechanical inspection.

  *1.6 THP (EP6 / turbo petrol)*
  - Reputation: older THP/EP6 engines are well known in the PSA world for timing chain/tensioner vulnerability (particularly early EP6 variants) and turbo issues if oil service has been neglected. [Euspares overview](https://www.euspares.co.uk/blog/peugeot-308-common-problems-a-comprehensive-guide-to-issues-and-solutions/), [Carnews EP6 notes](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-peugeot/peugeot-308/).
  - Failure points:
    - Timing-chain/tensioner failure can be catastrophic and often shows as rattling at start or engine management lights.
    - Turbocharger failures on high‑mileage or oil‑neglected examples.
  - Prevention:
    - Verify full service history and oil service intervals; avoid cars with service gaps. If budget allows, ask the seller for a dealer inspection focusing on timing system status.

  *1.6 BlueHDi (diesel)*
  - Reputation: frugal and torquey; common on higher‑mileage or fleet‑used cars.
  - Failure points:
    - DPF blockages and EGR valve carbon build‑up on vehicles used mostly for short urban trips. These can cause loss of power, warning lights and expensive cleaning/replacement if ignored. [Autodoc DPF/EGR notes](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-peugeot-308).
    - Turbo wear on long‑service‑interval, oil‑neglected cars.
  - Prevention:
    - Check for a healthy service log, ask about typical use (long trips vs just city). If the car has been urban‑only, budget for potential DPF/EGR service in the short term.

  *PHEV / Hybrid variants (e‑EAT8, P51 etc)*
  - These are newer and appear on later 308s — PHEV tech gives excellent headline economy if you charge, but battery condition, hybrid module software and the e‑AT automatic behaviour are new failure areas to watch. WLTP numbers are impressive but only if you charge regularly. [WLTP petrol‑hybrid data](https://www.wltpinfo.com/model/peugeot/308_2024-2025/Petrol-Hybrid.html), [Ultimatespecs PHEV range/figures](https://www.ultimatespecs.com/car-specs/Peugeot/127869/Peugeot-308-2022-GT-HYbrid-225.html).
  - Failure points:
    - Hybrid battery degradation (check warranty, state of health if possible).
    - PHEV e‑AT software quirks and electrified‑gearbox driveability issues reported on some electrified PSA cars.
  - Prevention:
    - For used PHEV/hybrid buys: request battery pack history, ensure vehicle has been regularly charged and serviced at a reputable dealer.

Transmission
- The 308 has been fitted with a range of automatic gearboxes across its life:
  - **EAT6 / EAT8 (Aisin torque‑converter automatics)** — mainstream on later petrol and PHEV variants. Mechanically robust but susceptible to:
    - Low‑speed jerks (1→2 / 2→3), torque‑converter shudder in stop‑start traffic, and electronic TCU/software calibration issues. Many owners report that a fluid change and TCU software update fixes symptoms. [ASR Gearbox guide](https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/peugeot-308-common-gearbox-problems/), [ProPeugeotSpare EAT6/EAT8 guide](https://www.propeugeotspares.co.za/blog/peugeot-eat6-eat8-transmission-guide/).
  - **Older AL4 / EGS boxes** on earlier 308s — rough shifts reported from 1st to 2nd; fluid and valve‑body wear cause problems if not maintained. [French‑Car Forum AL4 thread](https://frenchcarforum.co.uk/forum/viewtopic.php?t=82739).
- Practical check when inspecting:
  - Test low‑speed acceleration (parking lot and slow traffic) and listen/feel for harsh 1→2 shifts, shudder or slipping.
  - Ask for ATF service history and any TCU updates done by the dealer. Most problems are resolved or improved with ATF replacement and software updates — full rebuilds are often avoidable. [ASR EAT6/EAT8 solutions](https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/eat6-eat8-aisin-psa-group/).

#### 3. Should you buy a used Peugeot 308?
Short answer first: yes — if you get the right car, with the right history, and don’t expect German‑car durability without the servicing discipline. If you want cheap, stylish, comfortable C‑segment transport with a bit of French flair, the 308 is worth considering. If you want a bulletproof, flog‑proof commuter that ignores maintenance, look elsewhere.

Depreciation metrics
- The 308 depreciates faster than premium German rivals but is comparable to other mainstream European compact hatches in the used market. Broad aggregator snapshots show large numbers of used 308s with wide price spreads — they tend to lose value quicker in the first 3–4 years and then settle. See AutoUncle/Aggregator listings for wider market range. [AutoUncle market overview](https://www.autouncle.co.uk/en-gb/used-cars/Peugeot/308).
- Ballpark: expect steeper early depreciation than a Golf or Civic. If you buy a 2017 example at $8,990, you’re already into the steeper portion of the depreciation curve — that’s good for used buyers: less likelihood of losing much more money in short term compared to buying near‑new.

Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy
- How to calculate TCO for a used 308: add purchase price + scheduled servicing + expected consumables (tyres, brakes) + likely near‑term repairs (timing chain/turbo/DPF risk depending on engine) + insurance + fuel over your ownership horizon.
- Fuel economy notes:
  - Petrol 308s can be frugal but real‑world economy depends on engine and driving. Hybrid/PHEV official figures look brilliant, but only if you charge often. [Parkers running costs](https://www.parkers.co.uk/peugeot/308/review/mpg-running-costs/), [WLTP hybrid figures](https://www.wltpinfo.com/model/peugeot/308_2024-2025/Petrol-Hybrid.html).
  - PHEV headlines (very low combined L/100km) only apply if you charge regularly; if you don’t, fuel use can be worse than a regular petrol because of extra weight/complexity. [Carbuyer on PHEV real‑world caveat](https://www.carbuyer.co.uk/peugeot/308/mpg).
- Maintenance comparison:
  - Paying a small premium for a well‑maintained petrol 308 with full service history is often better value than buying the cheapest car with gaps — the latter commonly leads to costly engine or DPF/turbo repairs.
  - Hybrid/PHEV as a used buy: mathematically sensible only if (a) you can charge at home or work most days, (b) the battery is in good health and the warranty status is acceptable. [Ultimatespecs PHEV figures](https://www.ultimatespecs.com/car-specs/Peugeot/127869/Peugeot-308-2022-GT-HYbrid-225.html), [WLTP hybrid figures](https://www.wltpinfo.com/model/peugeot/308_2024-2025/Petrol-Hybrid.html).
- Quick TCO checklist:
  - Always add $1,000–$3,000 as a near‑term contingency fund for a 2017‑era European hatch if service history is incomplete (timing chain, turbo, DPF).
  - Include a $500–$1,200 initial check and minor service at a trusted independent European specialist if buying from a dealer — swap fluids, change filters, check timing chain tensioner, scan for ECU codes.

Buying recommendation — which year/trim to aim for
- Optimal sweet spot (financial + reliability): Late‑model second‑generation cars (2016–2018) with a complete Peugeot dealer/service history, under ~120,000 km, and with a good‑service history for the timing system/turbo are the best buys. The NSW snapshot (2017 at 74,035 km for $8,990) could be an absolute steal — but only if its history is clean.
- Trim pick:
  - Mid‑spec Allure / GT Line trims often hit the balance between equipment and value — enough tech to make the car enjoyable (10‑inch touchscreen, safety kit) without paying GT premiums where PHEV powertrains and complicated electrics appear.
  - Avoid unloved base cars that have been ridden hard by fleets unless the price reflects a known, serviceable issue.
- Non‑starter cases:
  - Skip cars with only cosmetic fixes but no service history or cars with unresolved ADAS or recurring infotainment faults unless you price these in for repairs.
  - Be cautious with diesels used only for short urban work (DPF/EGR risk) and early THP engines with partial service histories.

Final buy checklist (what to demand before handing over AUD)
- VIN recall check for open recalls (use Peugeot dealer, Regit/vehicle recall pages). [Regit](https://www.regit.cars/recalls/peugeot-308).
- Dealer or independent European‑car specialist pre‑purchase inspection (pay for a thorough scan and test drive).
- Service book matching stamped invoices for oil changes at ~10k–15k km intervals (or whatever the logbook prescribes).
- Specific checks:
  - Cold‑start engine rattle (timing chain symptom).
  - Smoothness of 1→2 and low‑speed shifts (ATF behaviour).
  - Evidence of DPF regen or EGR cleaning if diesel and used for urban trips.
  - Infotainment/ADAS full function test (camera/radar accuracy, lane/acc settings).
- Negotiate: take the NSW $8,990 listing as a strong bargaining tool — ask for a price reduction or a fresh service if any of the above is marginal.
