#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

- Generation: second‑generation CLS — internal code **C218** (facelifted 2014–2017 production within the C218 run). The CLS line continued later with the **C257** (2018+; MRA platform) before the model was discontinued in 2023. [Wikipedia – C218](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_CLS-Class_(C218)) | [Wikipedia – C257](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_CLS_(C257))
- Where it sits: a niche “four‑door coupé” above the E‑Class in Mercedes’ range (style + luxury over outright practicality). The E‑Class is the natural alternative if you want more rear‑seat access/boot practicality; the AMG‑branded CLS sits above for performance variants. [Mercedes classic CLS overview](https://www.mercedes-benz.com/en/vehicles/mercedes-benz-classic/classic-cars/cls-coupe/)
- Shared platform / parts bleed: the C218 is heavily E‑Class‑derived — it shares chassis and many systems with the W212 E‑Class of the same era which helps parts availability and independent workshop familiarity. Later C257 cars moved onto Mercedes’ MRA architecture shared with newer E‑Classes. [Wikipedia – C218](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_CLS-Class_(C218)) | [Wikipedia – C257](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_CLS_(C257))
- Engines, outputs, transmissions and drive layouts (market‑relevant summary):
  - Petrol V6/V8 and diesel V6 offerings were available across the C218 life. 4MATIC (AWD) was offered on many versions; vehicles commonly use Mercedes’ multi‑link rear suspension and electronic systems.
  - Transmission: Mercedes’ 7‑speed automatic (commonly the **722.9 / 7G‑Tronic**) or later 9‑speed in the newest generation. C218 models you’re likely to see in NSW (2014–2017) use the 7‑speed automatic. [Wikipedia – C218](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_CLS-Class_(C218)) | [MB Medic – 722.9](https://www.mercedesmedic.com/722-9-7g-tronic-automatic-transmission-problems-solutions/)
- Marketplace rivals (what Aussies cross‑shop): Audi A7, BMW 6‑Series Gran Turismo / 6‑Series GT, Porsche Panamera, Volvo S90. See comparative roundups: [The Car Connection / rivals list](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/mercedes-benz_cls-class_choices) | [WhatCar? comparison page](https://www.whatcar.com/news/audi-a7-sportback-vs-bmw-6-series-gt-vs-mercedes-cls/n16964)

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Mercedes‑Benz CLS‑Class?

Exterior
- Panel fit and paint:
  - Early‑generation owner threads and DIY forums flag panel‑gap oddities around the bonnet/A‑pillar area — not every car, but worth checking closely on a 360° inspection, especially bonnet shut lines and headlight alignment. [MBWorld forum on panel gaps](https://mbworld.org/forums/c218/914283-these-panel-gaps-normal.html)
  - Trim vulnerabilities: gloss black/metallic trim and rubber seals can fade, and aftermarket polishing can leave swirl marks on full‑length coupe roofs. Expect normal luxury‑car stone chips on the leading edges of the bonnet and lower bumper.
- Body recalls & safety components:
  - Official recall lookups must be VIN‑checked — Mercedes has had recall campaigns affecting related models; always check the VIN with manufacturer portals or NHTSA (if the car is a re‑import). [Mercedes‑Benz recall lookup](https://www.mbusa.com/en/recall) | [NHTSA recall tool](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)
- ADAS — the tricky bit:
  - Complaints exist about AEB/Distronic adaptive cruise quirks — radar/camera systems can go inop when sensors are contaminated, the windshield has been replaced or calibration is lost, or software fails. There are TSBs and casework on AEB/detection false activations for the CLS450 era. [TSB summary – AEB problems (CLS450)](https://www.aboutautomobile.com/Technical-Service-Bulletin/2022/Mercedes-Benz/CLS450/Automatic-Emergency-Braking) | [Mercedes Assistance – Radar dirty](https://en.mercedesassistance.com/mercedes-radar-sensor-dirty-how-to-fix/)
  - Practical check: make the car perform a radar‑based emergency‑brake check in slow traffic and watch for “Radar sensors dirty”, “Distronic inoperative” or inconsistent behaviour. If repeated faults appear, ask the dealer for evidence of sensor recalibration after any windscreen or bumper work.

Interior
- Durability in heavy use:
  - The CLS is a luxury‑leaning car — leather, stitch trims and door cards age like leather should. For rideshare/taxi‑style heavy use, expect more wear to driver’s seat bolsters, scuffed sill plates and fatigued rear legroom usability because of the coupe roofline.
  - Door sill height and entry: coupe roofline reduces rear headroom and the sill is slightly higher than an E‑Class — good to trial entry/egress for your family, particularly for kids/older passengers.
- Electronics and infotainment:
  - Infotainment black screens, reboots or frozen MBUX/COMAND units were reported across related Mercedes models, with many fixes being software updates or module reprogramming (2015–2019 era faults referenced). Check the system for software version and a recent dealer update record. [Go‑parts – 2015–2019 display failures](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/info-gps-tv-screen-mercedes-benz-b-class-mercedes-benz-cla-class-mercedes-benz-gla-class-2015-2019) | [BeefyTools – Mercedes infotainment overview](https://beefytools.com/mercedes-infotainment-issues-common-problems-solutions/)
  - Wireless charging modules and phone pairing: intermittent faults are common as phone standards change — verify the handset pairs and charges reliably. [Go‑parts – wireless charging issues](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/wireless-device-charging-system-module-mercedes-benz-ml400-mercedes-benz-gl63-amg-mercedes-benz-2019-2025)
- Electrical oddities:
  - 12V battery drain patterns: while not unique to CLS, modern‑Mercedes with multiple control units can exhibit parasitic draws if modules fail to sleep — ask for a battery test and for any “vehicle network” or body‑control module history.

Mechanically
- Brand engineering reputation: Mercedes builds sophisticated hardware with a corresponding maintenance and repair cost profile. Luxury features (air suspension, electro‑valves, adaptive damping) mean higher repair bills when things fail.
- Security and digital attack surface:
  - Modern Mercedes vehicles are part of a growing set of targets for CAN‑bus/CAN injection attacks and keyless relay hacks. Papers and reporting show attackers can inject messages by tapping exposed wiring (headlight harnesses, OBD connectors) to emulate a valid key signal. [Autoblog – CAN injection via headlights](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update) | [Dr Ken Tindell write‑up – CAN injection](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/)
  - Practical countermeasures:
    - Fit a mechanical deterrent (club/locking steering wheel) for street‑parked cars.
    - Physically block the OBD‑II port or fit an OBD lock to reduce quick device-based reprogramming; see OBD port lock reviews. [OBDadvisor – OBD2 port locks roundup](https://obdadvisor.com/best-obd2-port-locks/)
    - Keep keys in a Faraday pouch at home; consider aftermarket CAN‑firewalls offered by security specialists.
- High‑priority vulns: ask any selling dealer if the vehicle has had software updates to address security advisories; demand recent ECU/infotainment security/OS updates where applicable.

Engines
- The C218-era CLS used several petrol and diesel engines. Owners’ reports and technical writeups note recurring faults in families used in CLS/E‑Class cars. Below are the engine codes and failure points referenced in the public record.

  *M272* (older V6 family; relevant to early‑generation CLS derivatives)
  - Known problems: balance‑shaft sprocket / idle‑gear wear leading to timing drift, rattles, misfires and potential catastrophic damage. This is a documented long‑running Mercedes V6 issue. [Motronix – M272 guide](https://motronix.net/blog/mercedes-m272-m273-balance-shaft-timing-chain-wear/) | [MB Medic – M272 balance shaft repair](https://www.mercedesmedic.com/m272-balance-shaft-timing-chain-repair/)
  - Preventative maintenance: regular oil changes on shorter intervals (every 10,000 km) using manufacturer‑grade oil; listen for timing rattle at cold start. If you hear it, prioritise inspection — repairs are not cheap.

  *M273* (V8 sibling of M272)
  - Known problems: similar chain/sprocket wear issues as M272; higher repair costs due to V8 layout. See class‑action and owner reports. [TopClassActions – M272/M273 class action summary](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/lawsuit-news/mercedes-m272-a-m273-engine-class-action/)

  *M276* (V6 petrol: twin‑turbo family used widely across V6 Mercedes in this era)
  - Known problems: camshaft adjuster (phaser) failures and cold‑start rattles where phasers bleed down; symptoms include rattling at cold start, cam position codes, and misfires. Repairs often require phaser replacement and correct oil control. [Motronix – M276 camshaft adjuster failure](https://motronix.net/blog/mercedes-m276-camshaft-adjuster-failure/) | [Automatika – M276 phaser detail](https://automatika.tech/mercedes-m276-engine-camshaft-adjuster-failure/)
  - Preventative maintenance: shorter oil‑change intervals (10,000 km or even 7,500 km if the car is used for many short trips) and use of the correct low‑ash, manufacturer‑spec oil. Watch for oil pressure/level anomalies.

  *M256* (newer inline‑6, 48V mild‑hybrid tech — more relevant to C257+)
  - Known issues (from user reports): oil consumption and carbon build‑up risks typical of direct‑injection engines; electrical complexity (48V mild‑hybrid systems) increases diagnosis difficulty. [Mercedes Assistance – M256 guide](https://en.mercedesassistance.com/m256-engine-mercedes/)
  - Preventative maintenance: strict oil‑change discipline and intake cleaning (where warranted), dealer software updates for hybrid systems. Recommended service interval: follow Mercedes’ schedule but err on the side of 10,000 km oil services for long life.

Transmission
- The C218 largely used the **722.9 / 7G‑Tronic** 7‑speed automatic on petrol & diesel variants — a mature unit but not immune to faults. Common failure modes reported:
  - Rough/sharp shifts, delayed engagement, torque‑converter lock‑up clutch (TCC) shudder or failure, valve‑body and solenoid issues at higher mileage. [MB Medic – 722.9 guide](https://www.mercedesmedic.com/722-9-7g-tronic-automatic-transmission-problems-solutions/) | [BenzWorld transmission threads](https://www.benzworld.org/threads/transmission-torque-converter-issue.3112874/)
  - There are modern recall/service bulletins related to transmission wiring harness issues in Mercedes product lines — always VIN‑check for any open campaigns. [MBUSA customer letter – transmission wiring harness recall example](https://www.mbusa.com/content/dam/mb-nafta/us/customer-letters/2024060013.pdf)
- Buyer checklist for tranny health:
  - Ask for a recent transmission fluid service history and filter change (some workshops perform a pan drop and filter; others recommend a full fluid and torque‑converter flush).
  - Test for slipping, shuddering on take‑up and hold speeds for 1st→2nd and torque‑converter lock up engagement. Any hesitation on a cold pull is a red flag.

Summary checklist — diagnostic failures to watch for (quick scan)
- Exterior: panel gaps at bonnet/A‑pillar; stone chips; ADAS sensor miscalibration.
- Interior: frozen/black infotainment screens; wireless charging faults; scuffed bolsters.
- Mechanical: oil leaks, air suspension faults (AIRMATIC‑equipped cars), engine chain/phaser rattle.
- Engines: M272/M273 balance‑shaft timing wear; M276 cam phaser cold‑start rattle; M256 oil consumption (if present).
- Transmission: 7G‑Tronic rough shifting, TCC shudder, wiring harness recall history.
- Security: potential CAN‑bus injection/OBD hacks — fit mechanical and OBD countermeasures.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Mercedes‑Benz CLS‑Class?

Price & NSW market reality
- Current NSW dealer snapshot (provided): active dealer listings: 2; asking range AUD $23,950 – $27,950; median AUD $25,950; model years 2014–2017; median odometer 2,015 km. That pricing is the real‑world dealer floor/ceiling at the time of the scraper snapshot (MTA NSW dataset you supplied).
- What this means: mid‑$20k buys you a facelifted C218 from the later years — often low recorded kms in dealer stock suggest demonstrators, recent imports or cars that have had odometer resets (rare but worth verification). Always ask for full service and title/VIN history.

Depreciation — where CLS sits vs rivals
- Mercedes as a brand averages heavier depreciation than mainstream brands: studies show Mercedes can lose roughly ~46% of new value over five years (brand average per CarEdge). [CarEdge – Mercedes depreciation overview](https://caredge.com/mercedes-benz/depreciation)
- CLS in particular trades more on looks than practicality, so it tends to depreciate faster than the practical E‑Class and similarly to Audi A7 or Panamera relative to MSRP. Use CarEdge’s model‑level tools to compare depreciation curves for the CLS vs A7/Panamera. [CarEdge – CLS depreciation](https://caredge.com/depreciation/mercedes-benz/cls-class) (model page)

TCO strategy & math (how to decide petrol vs diesel vs hybrid)
- TCO resources to use:
  - Edmunds’ True Cost to Own (US sample) shows CLS ownership totals — use it to estimate depreciation, insurance, servicing and fuel cost buckets then adapt to AUD and local fuel prices. [Edmunds – CLS TCO example](https://www.edmunds.com/mercedes-benz/cls-class/2023/cost-to-own/)
  - KBB’s hybrid vs gas analysis gives you a break‑even framework: hybrid premium only pays if you do enough km/year and fuel prices stay high relative to the hybrid premium. [KBB – hybrid vs gas break‑even](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/)
- Practical Aussie example framework (how to run your own numbers):
  - Step 1: pick a baseline petrol C218 fuel figure — Parkers reports petrol CLS fuel economy roughly 29.7–36.2 mpg (US) which converts to about 7–8.5 L/100km; diesels convert to roughly 5.2–6.3 L/100km. Use these as a range estimate for older CLS models. [Parkers – CLS running costs & mpg](https://www.parkers.co.uk/mercedes-benz/cls/review/mpg-running-costs/)
    - Convertor: L/100km ≈ 235.21 / (mpg US). Example: 30 mpg US ≈ 7.8 L/100km.
  - Step 2: estimate annual km you drive (e.g., 15,000 km vs 25,000 km). Higher kms favour diesel or hybrid.
  - Step 3: compare fuel cost differences over a realistic ownership window (3–5 years), then add expected servicing and likely big‑ticket repairs (air suspension, transmission work, engine timing/phaser work).
  - Step 4: add depreciation — using dealer median ask (~AUD $25,950) vs what similar condition A7/6‑GT are asking in your area.
- Hybrid/48V mild‑hybrid math: newer mild‑hybrid sixes (M256) may save modest fuel in stop/start conditions but carry complexity and likely a price premium. KBB and CleanFleetReport show hybrid payback is only reliable if you do very high urban km or buy the hybrid at a small premium. [KBB hybrid analysis](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/) | [CleanFleetReport comparative TCO](https://cleanfleetreport.com/5-year-total-cost-of-ownership-comparison-ev-vs-hybrid-vs-gas/)
- Practical verdict: at mid‑$20k for a 2014–2017 C218, the buy can make sense if you:
  - Buy a car with full documented service history and proof of major items serviced (e.g., cam phaser work, transmission fluid change if recommended).
  - Keep expectations for running costs realistic: budget accordingly for premium tyres, high‑octane fuel (if turbo petrol), and high labour rates for Mercedes systems.

Buying recommendation — yes/no and which model years/trim to pick
- Yes, if:
  - You want coupe styling with luxury appointments and you can accept the running‑cost profile of a prestige German car.
  - You buy a well‑documented, low‑issue example (full stamp‑book service history at Mercedes dealers or reputable independent Mercedes specialist).
  - You factor in a contingency service and fund for likely medium‑term items (suspension/air springs, cam phaser work on M276 if any, potential transmission service).
- No, if:
  - You need an inexpensive, low‑maintenance daily that’s cheap to repair or you’ll do many high‑km years without willing to pay for premium servicing.
- Best financial sweet spot (for NSW market / C218 buyers):
  - Aim for late‑facelift C218 cars (2014–2016) with a comprehensive service history and preference for diesel V6 or the V6 petrol with documented timing/cam work history — they are cheaper to buy than earlier rarer AMG V8 cars and avoid some of the older M272 timing‑sprocket legacy.
  - Given the NSW dealer median of AUD $25,950, shoot for a private sale or dealer price below that median if you find any service or history gaps — a good target is AUD $20–26k for a 2014–2017 car with clear service history.
  - Avoid unproven ultra‑low km deals that cannot document service/ownership (those low odometer figures in dealer feeds can sometimes hide import paperwork oddities).

Final quick buying checklist (walkaround/testdrive & pre‑purchase)
- VIN check: confirm via manufacturer or CleanVIN/NHTSA where helpful for recalls. [CleanVins – recall aggregator](https://www.cleanvins.com/recalls/mercedes-benz-cls)
- Full service history: stamps, oil types used, documentation of any timing‑chain/phaser work, transmission service history.
- Road test: listen for cold‑start rattles (phaser/timing), check smoothness of 1→2 shifts, test ADAS functions and infotainment, check for radar sensor warnings.
- Electronics: run infotainment, wireless charging and phone pairing; request evidence of recent ECU/infotainment software updates.
- Security: ask if dealer fitted any immobiliser/anti‑theft upgrades; consider adding an OBD lock and a visible mechanical steering lock if the car will be parked on street.
- Specialist pre‑purchase: pay for a Merc‑savvy mechanic to do an inspection (they’ll know to look at phasers, 7G gearbox solenoids, Airmatic valves etc).

Final note (straight talk): the CLS is a gorgeous, low‑volume halo car for people who value style. Buying one used at the mid‑$20k NSW bracket is a fair punt — but treat it like a high‑quality appliance with expensive parts. If you love the silhouette and accept a disciplined maintenance budget (and a pre‑purchase inspection by a Mercedes‑savvy tech), you’ll be rewarded with one of the most elegant four‑door coupés Mercedes built in the 2010s. If you want a lower‑stress long‑run ownership ledger, the E‑Class or a well‑specified Audi A7 will usually cost you less in the long run.
