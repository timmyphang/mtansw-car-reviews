#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

- Generation: This review treats the modern Audi A6 family (recent C8 / latest‑generation models through to the current MY24/25 refresh cycle) — Audi’s executive/midsize sedan slot produced across multiple iterations since 1994. The current combustion A6 is built on Audi’s Premium Platform Combustion (PPC) while the full‑battery A6 e‑tron runs on the PPE electric architecture. See Audi’s official platform notes here: [Audi A6 - PPC/PPE](https://www.audi.com/en/audi-a6-39) and the e‑tron PPE page at Audi.com for the BEV variant: [A6 e‑tron](https://www.audi.com/en/audi-a6-e-tron-16109). The MY24 product guide has full specs and WLTP figures: [A6 Product Guide (MY24) PDF](https://media.audi.com/is/content/audi/nemo/ie/Downloads/ProductGuides/MY24/A6%20Product%20Guide%20MY24.pdf).

- Internal codes you’ll see in workshops and classifieds: the recent combustion A6 generation is commonly referenced as the C8 platform. Engine codes you’ll encounter in parts lists and reliability write‑ups include codes such as **DMTA**, **DETA**, **DEZE**, **DEMA** (details later).

- Product hierarchy: the A6 sits above the A4 and below the full‑size A8 in Audi’s sedan ranking — it’s the executive grade that buyers cross‑shop with BMW 5 Series and Mercedes‑Benz E‑Class. Sources comparing class rivals: [U.S. News / MotorTrend / KBB comparisons](https://cars.usnews.com/cars-trucks/audi/a6), [MotorTrend history and comparisons](https://www.motortrend.com/features/audi-a6-history-generations-specifications).

- Shared platform and parts availability: the PPC combustion A6 shares VW Group modular architectures across many models (so many electrical bits, sensors and even suspension components are common with other VW/Audi/Skoda/SEAT platforms), while the A6 e‑tron runs on the PPE EV architecture shared with other high‑end VW Group EVs — good for parts supply on both sides, but expect some Audi‑specific modules to be pricey. Audi’s platform notes: [Audi A6 platform & product guide PDF](https://media.audi.com/is/content/audi/nemo/ie/Downloads/ProductGuides/MY24/A6%20Product%20Guide%20MY24.pdf).

- Engines, outputs, transmissions and drive layouts sold in markets that include Australia:
  - 2.0‑litre turbo petrol (TFSI) — four‑cylinder, mild‑hybrid support on later MYs; power varies by tune (roughly mid‑to‑high 150–195 kW / ~200–260 kW depending on market and tune). Paired commonly with a 7‑ or 8‑speed automatic or 7‑speed S‑tronic in certain markets. Source: [Edmunds 2024 A6 specs](https://www.edmunds.com/audi/a6/2024/features-specs/) and Audi product guide.
  - 3.0‑litre V6 petrol (TFSI) — higher output, typically with Quattro AWD and 8‑speed Tiptronic automatic where fitted. Source: [The Car Connection specs](https://www.thecarconnection.com/specifications/audi_a6_2024).
  - 3.0‑litre diesel (TDI) — in markets that still sell diesel A6s (Australia has had diesels in the past), with AdBlue aftertreatment on later units.
  - Plug‑in hybrid TFSI e (PHEV) — combined WLTP fuel figures as low as ~1.9–2.1 L/100 km when regularly charged; useful for city commuters: [A6 TFSI e page & WLTP data](https://www.audi.com/en/audi-a6-sedan-tfsie-until-2025-12170).
  - Drivetrains: FWD (limited), Quattro AWD common on higher trims; automatic transmission is the norm. See trim/spec pages: [Cars.com / KBB trim listings](https://www.cars.com/research/audi-a6-2024/trims/), [KBB](https://www.kbb.com/audi/a6/2025/specs/).

- Marketplace rivals you’ll be cross‑shopping in Australia: BMW 5 Series, Mercedes‑Benz E‑Class, Lexus ES, Genesis G80, Volvo S90 (see comparative lists at [U.S. News / The Car Connection / MotorTrend](https://cars.usnews.com/cars-trucks/audi/a6), [The Car Connection competitor comparisons](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/audi_a6_choices)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Audi A6?

Note: Audi A6 history is long and issues vary by generation and engine. Below are the real‑world problem areas buyer’s guides and owner forums repeatedly flag — sources are cited with practical repair and diagnostic guidance.

- Exterior:
  - Panel alignment & trim:
    - Older A6s and some mid‑cycle cars show misaligned trim, sagging door seals, and brittle trim clips that pop loose after road‑wear. Community threads and trim guides document recurring problems and DIY fixes: [Go‑Parts trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-audi-q7-audi-q8-audi-sq7-2015-2026), [AudiWorld forum threads](https://www.audiworld.com/forums/a6-s6-c6-platform-discussion-58/how-do-you-fix-trim-gap-2947177/).
  - Paint & clearcoat:
    - Clearcoat peeling and localised chipping is well reported on older model A6s — check bonnet leading edge, door tops and boot lid. Owner threads dating back many years show this as a recurring cosmetic complaint: [AudiWorld peeling clear coat threads](https://www.audiworld.com/forums/a6-c7-platform-discussion-7/peeling-clear-coat-1719018/), aggregated here: [au7o.io known issues](https://au7o.io/known-issues/audi-a6).
  - Body recalls & safety campaigns:
    - Recalls do happen across recent model years — always run a VIN check with Audi or NHTSA for US vehicles. NHTSA recall lookup is useful to spot active campaigns: [NHTSA A6 recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2024/Audi/A6/recalls).
  - ADAS physicality & quirks:
    - Forward camera and radar sensor faults are common trouble spots — front‑camera failures will disable Audi Pre‑Sense, AEB and lane assist features until the camera or its cabling is repaired and recalibrated. Go‑Parts documents camera failures and the need for professional recalibration: [Go‑Parts ADAS camera guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/video-camera-audi-s8-audi-a8-audi-a6-2015-2026). Owners also report false AEB activation and ACC errors — class action and owner complaint pages compile many instances: [CarProblemZoo AEB reports](https://www.carproblemzoo.com/audi/a6/automatic-emergency-braking-problems.php), [ClassAction on VW/Audi AEB](https://www.classaction.org/volkswagen-audi-aeb-brakes-lawsuits).
  - Practical check at inspection: look for cracked/peeling trim, corroded sensor surrounds, evidence of front bumper repairs (may indicate previous impact that could affect radar alignment).

- Interior:
  - MMI infotainment, screens and Phone Box:
    - The cabin is screen‑heavy; MMI black screens, reboots, frozen units and loss of audio are common faults — sometimes due to the MIB head unit failing or breaks in the MOST fibre‑optic loop. Replace the correct module only after isolating the fault (diagnose loop, connectors, fuses): [Go‑Parts MMI failures overview](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/audio-system-audi-e-tron-audi-q8-audi-a6-2017-2026), practical triage: [Drivers Advice MMI troubleshooting](https://driversadvice.com/audi-mmi-not-working/).
    - Wireless charging in the Phone Box can overheat or fail depending on phone size and alignment — NHTSA TSBs and manufacturer bulletins cover Phone Box charging behaviour and are useful diagnostics if a Phone Box is reported faulty: [NHTSA Phone Box TSB summary](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2021/MC-10202219-0001.pdf).
  - Cabin wear & seating:
    - Driver seat leather wear and scuffing at low mileages is a recurring owner complaint; check seat bolster wear, steering wheel gloss loss and switchgear rub. See owner threads: [AudiWorld premature leather wear](https://www.audiworld.com/forums/a6-c7-platform-discussion-194/premature-leather-wear-2828099/).
  - Ergonomics & entry/exit:
    - Door sills are typically low‑ish (executive sedan), easy ingress/egress for adults — check door check angles and hinge play on high‑mileage cars; older check‑angle springs can be fatigued making doors flap.
  - Electrical & parasitic drains:
    - 12V battery drain patterns can show if there are sleeping module faults (MMI, telematics, Okta/comfort modules). When buying, ask for a recent health check and that modules are up‑to‑date with dealer firmware (modules can draw current after aftermarket installs or telematics faults). Troubleshooting MMI/phone integration and USB/charging ports: [Go‑Parts USB & Phone Box guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/usb-port-audi-a3-audi-e-tron-gt-audi-q3-2016-2026) and TSBs on wireless charging.

- Mechanically:
  - Brand engineering reputation:
    - Audi builds well‑engineered cars with strong chassis and drivetrain tech, but complexity equals expense — advanced electronics, turbocharging, and integrated drivetrain modules mean big bills when things go wrong. Independent reliability surveys and aggregated owner complaints back up a pattern of worthwhile driving experience but higher running costs vs mid‑market cars.
  - Security and theft vulnerabilities:
    - Modern Audis (A6 included) are targets for sophisticated thefts. Techniques like CAN‑bus injection via headlight wiring have been documented; they require physical access and mechanical skill, but are real. Mitigations include dealer software patches, adding a CAN firewall/immobiliser or mechanical locks: [Autoblog headlight CAN injection summary](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update), [The Drive detailed write‑up](https://www.thedrive.com/news/shadetree-hackers-are-stealing-cars-by-injecting-code-into-headlight-wiring).
    - Aftermarket countermeasures: retrofit CAN‑firewalls and hidden “ghost” immobilisers are available for A6 platforms — look at specialist retrofitters like Wibutec and Retrofit.nu for product and installer references (example retrofit devices): [Wibutec CAN‑Bus immobiliser](https://wibutec-shop.com/AMPIRE-CAN-Bus-immobilizer-CAN-FIREWALL-for-Audi-A6-4G), [Retrofit.nu Ghost immobiliser](https://shop.retrofit.nu/en/theft-protection/audi-a6-a7-c8-ghost-immobilizer).
  - Physical anti‑theft basics: wheel locks, concealed alarm/siren, steering column locks and tracking system (loJack or smartphone‑based trackers) — cheap and effective layers.

- Engines:
  - Note: Below are the specific engine codes flagged in reliability research and forums. Each subsection is italicised and includes the most common failure points and recommended preventive service cadence. Source for comprehensive engine lists and engines breakdown: [What‑Breaks Audi A6 C8 engines & problems](https://what-breaks.com/audi/a6-c8/), and aggregated owner reports at [au7o.io and EngineCrux](https://au7o.io/known-issues/audi-a6), (https://enginecrux.com/audi-a6-engine-problems-facts-and-owner-insights/).

  - *DMTA — 2.0 TFSI (modern 2.0L turbo 4‑cyl, mild‑hybrid variants)*  
    - Failure points: oil consumption on some builds, PCV/boost hose hardening and vacuum leaks, high‑pressure fuel pump wear when poorly serviced, turbo actuator faults. MHEV 48V systems add starter‑generator modules that can cause accessory faults if battery health is poor.  
    - Preventive maintenance: oil and filter every 10,000 km (Audi dealer standard in many markets and in the MY24 product guide interval recommendations), inspect PCV hoses and intake for oil fouling every 30,000 km, change spark plugs at 60,000–90,000 km depending on service history. Reference engine list: [What‑Breaks DMTA notes](https://what-breaks.com/audi/a6-c8/).

  - *DETA — 3.0 TFSI (supercharged/turbo V6 variants depending on model year)*  
    - Failure points: timing chain guide/tensioner wear on certain V6s (rattle on cold start), oil leaks from cam covers and valve‑cover gaskets, and turbocharger/actuator issues in higher‑output tunes. Stretched chains can lead to catastrophic engine damage if ignored. See timing‑chain warnings: [CarNews timing chain warning](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-audi/audi-a6/timing-chain-problems-audi-a6-v6-engines/), and broader engine concerns: [EngineCrux](https://enginecrux.com/audi-a6-engine-problems-facts-and-owner-insights/).  
    - Preventive maintenance: strict oil‑change discipline (10,000 km or 12 months), listen for chain rattle at cold starts, and have timing‑chain tensioner and guide condition confirmed at ~100,000 km if no service history.

  - *DEZE — 3.0 TDI (diesel)*  
    - Failure points: AdBlue system faults, particulate filter (DPF) blockages on low‑use cars, turbocharger and EGR valve soot build‑up, and occasional injector or high‑pressure pump failures. DPF/regeneration problems are common if the car has mostly town driving. See aggregated diesel issues: [MyCarFault / FixMyCar diesel & AdBlue notes](https://www.whocanfixmycar.com/advice/common-problems-with-the-audi-a6), [What‑Breaks diesel list](https://what-breaks.com/audi/a6-c8/).  
    - Preventive maintenance: service every 10,000–15,000 km with DPF and AdBlue health checks; if you do lots of short runs, favour petrol or PHEV versions.

  - *DEMA — hybrid/PHEV (TFSI e)*  
    - Failure points: PHEV systems need battery state checks — degradation is slower than early hybrids but replacement battery cost is high. Charging hardware and junction relays can fail if regularly abused; WLTP figures (1.9–2.1 L/100 km) assume regular charging: [Audi TFSI e WLTP data](https://www.audi.com/en/audi-a6-sedan-tfsie-until-2025-12170).  
    - Preventive maintenance: same oil/service intervals as petrol (10,000 km); ensure battery charging routine is used regularly to keep the system healthy. If you do >30,000 km/year predominantly long distances without charging, the PHEV premium may not stack up (see TCO section).

- Transmission:
  - Multitronic / CVT (older models): Multitronic CVTs carried risk of premature wear, juddering, slipping, and expensive repairs — flagged in multiple transmission retrospectives: [EngineCrux Multitronic CVT problems](https://enginecrux.com/audi-multitronic-transmission-problems-facts-and-insights/), [TransmissionTroubleHub overview](https://www.transmissiontroublehub.com/audi-multitronic-transmission-problems-facts-and-insights/). Avoid early Multitronic on high‑mileage buys unless fully serviced/rebuilt with documented parts claim.
  - Torque‑converter automatics (common on V6/TFSI and Quattro models): common symptoms reported are slipping, torque‑converter failure, leaks and limp‑home modes. If the gearbox has hard shifts, slipping or a burning smell, get a transmission specialist scan — torque‑converter repair alone can be several thousand dollars: [EngineCrux torque converter guide](https://enginecrux.com/audi-a6-torque-converter-problems-facts-insights/).
  - S‑tronic dual‑clutch: in stop‑start city driving DCT clutches can wear faster; look for judder in low‑speed creeping and ensure clutch‑servicing history if the car is high‑kilometre.

  - Practical pre‑purchase transmission checks:
    - Cold start and warmed‑up test drives to spot shift quality at different temperatures.
    - Service stamp for gearbox oil change where applicable (some ZF/7‑8 speed boxes are “lifetime” in books but perform better with fluid changes).
    - Scan for transmission fault codes (U and P codes).

- Current used market pricing band (national context & depreciation):
  - Broad market studies show the A6 loses substantial value in the first 5 years — expect roughly 45–55% depreciation at 5 years depending on trim and market: [CarEdge depreciation analysis](https://caredge.com/audi/a6/depreciation), [iSeeCars 5‑year drop stats](https://www.iseecars.com/car/audi-a6/resale-value). That leaves a buyer in the used market with strong value propositions but higher ongoing costs.
  - Local NSW dealer data in this snapshot isn’t giving a usable price range — if you’re shopping in NSW, expand to private listings and interstate dealers; KBB and Cars.com are useful to benchmark retail/wholesale ranges: [KBB used listings](https://www.kbb.com/cars-for-sale/used/audi/a6).

- Depreciation metrics:
  - Expect the A6 to depreciate slightly more than mainstream sedans but roughly in line with other German executive sedans — CarEdge and iSeeCars put 5‑year declines in the high‑40s to mid‑50s percent band. Audis often retain slightly less value than Lexus but similar to BMW/Mercedes across comparable years: [CarEdge comparison tools](https://caredge.com/compare/depreciation/industry-average-vs-5-series-vs-e-class-vs-a6).

- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy & hybrid math:
  - TCO elements to model: depreciation, servicing, repairs, fuel, insurance, rego and finance. Edmunds’ ‘True Cost to Own’ tools are an excellent template (US‑centric but methodology applies): [Edmunds 2026 A6 TCO overview](https://www.edmunds.com/audi/a6/2026/cost-to-own/).
  - Fuel & charging maths: for a PHEV A6 TFSI e, WLTP figures as low as ~1.9–2.1 L/100 km assume frequent charging and lots of short electric commutes — if you don’t charge regularly, PHEV fuel use reverts toward petrol figures (7–9 L/100 km in mixed driving), meaning the PHEV premium isn’t recouped. See Audi WLTP numbers: [A6 TFSI e WLTP](https://www.audi.com/en/audi-a6-sedan-tfsie-until-2025-12170) and hybrid payback calculus: [KBB hybrid vs gas break‑even guide](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/).
  - Practical rule: if you do >20,000 km/year and can’t reliably charge daily, a petrol 2.0 TFSI with careful servicing will often be the cheaper lifetime option. If your driving is heavy city and you can charge nightly, the PHEV can be cost effective — run the numbers with local fuel (AUD) and electricity prices using the AFDC/vehicle cost calculators and add in probable battery health costs for vehicles >6 years old: [AFDC vehicle cost calculator](https://afdc.energy.gov/calc/).

- Buying recommendation (clear, blunt, Aussie‑practical):
  - Avoid early Multitronic CVT cars unless they have documented transmission rebuilds. Avoid high‑mileage 2012‑era cars without a full service history and mechanical inspection — that NSW 2012 offering with 220,040 km screams “buy at your own risk” unless the price is rock‑bottom and you’re mechanically handy.
  - Best value sweet spot: aim for a well‑maintained 2018–2022 A6 with the **2.0 TFSI** (DMTA‑type family) and Quattro if you want all‑weather reassurance. These cars balance fuel economy and mechanical simplicity vs the V6s (which can bring chain risks and higher repair bills). If you commute mostly in town and charge daily, a **TFSI e PHEV** in the same model years will give the best TCO if the battery test checks out. Sources shaping this view: [What‑Breaks engine guide](https://what-breaks.com/audi/a6-c8/), [Edmunds TCO & specs](https://www.edmunds.com/audi/a6/2024/features-specs/), [Audi WLTP TFSI e data](https://www.audi.com/en/audi-a6-sedan-tfsie-until-2025-12170).
  - Minimum pre‑purchase checks:
    - Full service history (insist on stamped records every 10,000–15,000 km and receipts for major jobs). See Audi service recommendations in the product guide: [MY24 product guide PDF](https://media.audi.com/is/content/audi/nemo/ie/Downloads/ProductGuides/MY24/A6%20Product%20Guide%20MY24.pdf).
    - ECU scan with an Audi‑level scanner to uncover stored ADAS or gearbox faults, and verify that any ADAS camera/radar replacement was properly calibrated (failure to calibrate kills AEB/ACC). Go‑Parts ADAS docs: [Go‑Parts front camera & radar guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/video-camera-audi-s8-audi-a8-audi-a6-2015-2026).
    - Transmission check: cold + hot test drive, scan for P‑codes and check fluid condition (where accessible).
    - PHEV buy: battery health report and proof of charging routine.
    - Security: ask the seller if the car has had any immobiliser or security module repairs; consider adding a CAN‑firewall or hidden immobiliser if the car doesn’t already have robust aftermarket theft defences (see Wibutec / Retrofit.nu retrofit options).
  - Final verdict: Buy if:
    - It has a full service history, no outstanding ADAS/airbag/ABS recalls, a clean title and acceptable gearbox behaviour. The A6 rewards buyers who accept higher running costs for premium chassis and comfort. For most buyers in Australia, a late‑model 2.0 TFSI or TFSI e with documented dealer services represents the best financial/usage sweet spot.
  - If you want a simple bite‑size takeaway:
    - “If the A6 is your dream executive sedan and you can stretch to a well‑serviced 2018+ 2.0 TFSI or TFSI e, go for it. If you find a lonely 2012 with 220,000 km (nsw dealer snapshot), walk away unless the price reflects every likely repair.” (NSW snapshot: Active listings 1 — 2012 — 220,040 km — Automatic.)
