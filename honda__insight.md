#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: third-generation Honda Insight (third‑gen sedan launched 2018; broadly 2019–2022 model years). Previous Insight generations include the earlier IMA‑based hatchbacks (second generation 2009–2014). See Honda's model hub and history for generation splits. [Honda Newsroom specs hub](https://hondanews.com/en-US/honda-automobiles/channels/honda-insight?selectedTabId=honda-insight-specs), [Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Insight).
- Internal codes: the 2019–2022 Insight is often referenced in spec databases as the Insight III / 2019–2022 model (the earlier ZE2/2010–2014 second‑gen is commonly discussed separately). See Auto‑Data and Honda press material for generation IDs. [Auto‑Data Insight III page](https://www.auto-data.net/en/honda-insight-iii-1.5-151hp-hybrid-e-cvt-34795).
- Product hierarchy: The Insight sat above the Civic in hybrid focus/efficiency positioning but below Honda’s larger Accord in size and equipment. It was pitched as a more "premium‑leaning" compact hybrid sedan compared with Toyota’s Prius family (a direct efficiency rival) and Hyundai/Kia hybrid offerings.
- Platform architecture & shared models: the third‑gen Insight uses Honda’s compact car architecture closely related to the 10th‑generation Civic (commonality helps parts availability and servicing). See Honda’s technical specs and press summaries for platform relationships. [Honda Newsroom 2019 specs & features](https://hondanews.com/en-US/honda-automobiles/releases/release-99b9311b568240639b28482eb820d667-2019-insight-specifications-and-features).
- Market-specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drive:
  - 1.5‑litre Atkinson‑cycle inline‑4 + electric drive (two‑motor full‑hybrid setup); combined system output ~151.5 hp (approx 113 kW). Uses a lithium‑ion pack (small ~1.2 kWh) and front‑wheel drive. Transmission: electronic continuously variable transmission (e‑CVT) with shift‑by‑wire. [Honda Newsroom 2022 specs](https://hondanews.com/en-US/honda-automobiles/releases/release-53541be6030b25a47a2899aba1122785-2022-honda-insight-specs-features), [Auto‑Data Insight III](https://www.auto-data.net/en/honda-insight-iii-1.5-151hp-hybrid-e-cvt-34795).
  - Older 2010–2014 Insights (common in Australian used market) used Honda’s IMA hybrid with a 1.3‑litre petrol and NiMH battery pack beneath the cargo floor and were paired to a CVT‑style belt transmission in some markets. [Car From Japan spec lists](https://carfromjapan.com/specifications/honda/insight), [Go‑Parts IMA battery notes](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p3000-honda-insight-2010-2014-lea-mf6-1).
- Main rivals in Australia (used market): Toyota Prius (all generations), Toyota Corolla Hybrid, Hyundai Ioniq Hybrid, Kia Niro Hybrid, Mazda3 (for comfort/drive balance) — comparison pages and reviews repeatedly pitch these near the Insight. [The Car Connection competitor list](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/honda_insight_choices), [KBB comparison Insight vs Prius](https://www.kbb.com/comparison/honda-insight-vs-toyota-prius/).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Honda Insight?
You want the hard truth — here it is, triaged by system. Read this like you’re buying with a spanner in one hand and a stout cuppa in the other.

Exterior
- Paint and clearcoat: early reports and owner threads call out clearcoat/paint failure on multiple model years — some owners saw peeling well under 100,000 km. Repair costs are non‑trivial (example: ~AUD $1,500 reported on owner complaint aggregators). Do a daylight inspection for bubbling/flake around wheel arches, bonnet and tail. [CarComplaints paint problems summary](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Honda/Insight/body_paint/index.shtml), [owner forum post on clearcoat](https://www.insightcentral.net/threads/paint-issues-on-2013-w-16-000-miles.66801/).
- Panel fit and trim: later sedans generally have acceptable panel alignment (Civic‑level fit), but some rear door trim panels on 2019–2022 Insights are known to pop or rub — replacement part fitment guides exist because they wear in ride‑share/taxi duty. Look for loose rear door cards and scratched gloss black trim. [Go‑Parts rear door panel guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/trim-panel-rr-dr-honda-insight-2019-2022).
- Recalls: multiple recall campaigns across model years — always VIN‑check before purchase. NHTSA pages and recall aggregators show several campaigns and investigations. [NHTSA vehicle page for 2022 Insight (recalls/investigations)](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2022/HONDA/INSIGHT/), [CleanVins recall list](https://www.cleanvins.com/recalls/honda-insight).
- ADAS issues (important): the Insight is part of a large federal probe into phantom/sudden braking from the Automatic Emergency Braking (Collision Mitigation Braking System, CMBS) on 2019–2022 models. Owners reported sudden decel events which led to an NHTSA preliminary evaluation and related complaints and legal action. If you buy an affected year, check that dealer software/firmware updates have been applied and log any test‑drive behaviour for “phantom braking.” [NHTSA PE24008 on 2019 Insight](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2019/HONDA/INSIGHT), [CarComplaints ADAS investigation reporting](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2024/honda-automatic-emergency-braking-problems-investigated.shtml), [class action coverage](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2025/honda-sudden-braking-class-action-lawsuit.shtml).

Interior
- Durability under heavy use: the Insight interior is relatively well built for a compact hybrid but long hours as a rideshare/taxi will expose the usual wear points — driver seat bolsters, cloth/leather inserts, and rear door trim clips that can pop loose. See trim part guides for replacement options. [Go‑Parts door panel guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/trim-panel-rr-dr-honda-insight-2019-2022).
- Entry/egress: lower sedan sill compared with SUVs — easy to get in/out for most adults, less of a problem than on older cramped hatchbacks.
- Electrical / infotainment:
  - Frozen screens and infotainment hangs are commonly reported across recent model years — simple soft resets (press/hold power/volume) sometimes cure it, but repeated freezes mean dealer software updates or head unit replacement. [JustAnswer frozen infotainment fix steps](https://www.justanswer.com/honda/ow9xi-infotainment-screen-frozen-stuck-screen.html), [KevinsAutos user reports on infotainment glitches](https://kevinsautos.com/faq/what-are-the-problems-with-the-honda-insight.html).
  - Wireless charger pads are hit‑and‑miss — phone compatibility, pad faults and fuse issues can be the culprit. Look for the 15‑amp backup fuse behaviour and test with your phone. [Go‑Parts wireless charger module notes](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/wireless-device-charging-system-module-honda-accord-honda-cr-v-honda-hr-v-2022-2025).
  - 12‑V battery drain / hybrid 12‑V issues: owner reports and dealer writeups show hybrid 12‑V system gremlins — keep an eye on battery health and ensure jump‑start points are intact. [AutoNation common issues list](https://www.autonationmobileservice.com/i/blog/common-issues-on-the-honda-insight-3rd-generation/).
- Practicality / boot: the hybrid battery footprint reduces underfloor storage in older IMA cars more noticeably than the later compact Insight sedan, but the 2019–2022 boot is still useable for daily family needs. Spec sheets detail cargo numbers. [Honda 2019 specs sheet](https://hondanews.com/en-US/honda-automobiles/releases/release-99b9311b568240639b28482eb820d667-2019-insight-specifications-and-features).

Mechanically
- General engineering reputation: Honda builds robust engines and drivetrains, but hybrid complexity shifts fault modes from purely mechanical to electro‑hybrid electronics and battery subsystems. Repair costs for hybrid‑specific components can be higher and require specialist hybrid technicians.
- Security / theft: modern Hondas (including Insights) are vulnerable to the same CAN‑bus/OBD attack techniques that have been publicly demonstrated (headlight/park lamp wiring access for CAN injection; OBD relay hacks). These aren’t Honda‑only problems — they’re architecture problems. Factory immobilisers exist, but physical CAN access can be abused. Defensive steps below. [CAN injection technical background and examples](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/), [Autoblog headlight CAN injection writeup](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update), [Honda Immobilizer description](https://www.hondainfocenter.com/Terms-and-Technologies/_content/Terms-and-Technologies/Immobilizer-Theft-Deterrent-System-All/).
- Aftermarket anti‑theft suggestions (pragmatic):
  - use a steering‑wheel club or visible mechanical lock as a cheap, effective deterrent;
  - fit an OBD port lock or smart OBD guard (blocks quick reflash via OBD);
  - use a Faraday pouch for key‑fob overnight or install key‑signal blockers at home/work;
  - consider an alarm system with immobiliser relay cut or a GPS tracker.
  These are practical mitigations against relay/CAN/OBD style thefts and common in Australia’s theft environment.

Engines
(italicised subsections for each engine variant discussed)

- *1.3‑litre IMA petrol (second‑generation / ~2009–2014 Insight)*  
  - Known failure points:
    - **IMA battery / NiMH pack degradation** — P3000 codes and module sticking are common as cells age; replacements or module refurb can be expensive. [Go‑Parts IMA battery P3000 explainer](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p3000-honda-insight-2010-2014-lea-mf6-1).
    - **Excessive oil consumption and engine failures** reported in owner complaint databases (2010–2013 highlighted). [HybridComplaints and CarComplaints engine trends](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Honda/Insight/engine/index.shtml), [HybridComplaints model page](https://www.hybridcomplaints.com/models/insight/).
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Hybrid system health checks every 20,000–30,000 km as cars age (get a specialist to scan IMA module voltages and individual cell voltages).
    - Engine oil change interval: follow Honda manual (practical Australian servicing tends to be every 10,000 km for routine checks on Hondas — validate against the owner’s manual) — keep oil levels topped and record oil consumption.
- *1.5‑litre Atkinson full‑hybrid (2019–2022 Insight)*  
  - Known failure points:
    - **Hybrid‑system warnings & sensor/controller glitches** — owners report hybrid warnings and occasional stop/start or EV‑mode faults. [AutoNation common issues](https://www.autonationmobileservice.com/i/blog/common-issues-on-the-honda-insight-3rd-generation/), [RepairPal reliability summaries](https://repairpal.com/problems/honda/insight).
    - **12‑V battery and associated sensor/controller issues** — expect 12‑V replacements like any modern hybrid; check accessory power behaviour. [AutoNation & RepairPal notes](https://www.autonationmobileservice.com/i/blog/common-issues-on-the-honda-insight-3rd-generation/), [Consumer Reports reliability notes](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/honda/insight/2019/reliability).
    - **Occasional head gasket / stalling reports** — low frequency but documented in owner complaint databases. [CarComplaints 2019 engine complaints](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Honda/Insight/2019/engine/engine.shtml).
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Follow hybrid‑specific service schedules: scan hybrid control unit codes at major service intervals; change engine oil and filter per Honda schedule (practical routine at 10,000 km in Australia; confirm with the owner’s manual and service book).
    - 12‑V battery life: expect replacement sooner than conventional cars in some cases — monitor cranking assist and accessory behaviour.
  - Fuel economy (real and converted to Australian units): EPA combined ~52 mpg ≈ 4.5 L/100 km; real‑world owner fuelly averages show mid‑to‑high 40s mpg ≈ 5.1 L/100 km in many owner logs. Use these to set fuel cost expectations. [EPA fuel economy data](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/bymodel/2019_Honda_Insight.shtml), [Fuelly owner data](https://www.fuelly.com/car/honda/insight).

Transmission
- e‑CVT behaviour (2019–2022): The Insight’s hybrid drive uses an e‑CVT feel (electronically simulated ratios, smooth acceleration, "rubber band" sensation at times). It’s generally refined but lacks the crisp feel of a conventional gearbox — that’s the tradeoff for efficiency. [Honda Info Center two‑motor hybrid description](https://www.hondainfocenter.com/2022/Insight/Feature-Guide/Engine-Chassis-Features/Two-Motor-Hybrid-System/).
- Known failure modes:
  - CVT shudder/judder and slipping have been reported on older Insights and on CVT‑equipped hybrids broadly; causes can include degraded CVT fluid (loss of friction modifiers) or component wear. A notable NHTSA/TSB (A23‑078) relates to torque converter clutch capacity issues on some Honda products and Honda extended warranty coverage on certain torque converter failures — check VIN‑specific TSBs. [NHTSA TSB A23‑078](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10244188-0001.pdf), [CVT problem primers](https://cvtexpert.com/honda-insight-cvt-transmission-problems-explained/).
  - For older ZE2 cars the belt‑type CVT can be vulnerable at high mileages; ensure service history includes CVT fluid changes where recommended. [TransmissionAudit CVT analysis](https://transmissionaudit.com/cars/honda/insight-2010-2014/), [gearfixes CVT advice](https://gearfixes.com/honda-cvt-transmission-problems/).
- Pre‑purchase transmission checks: test for shudder at low speed, hesitation under load, unusual noises and ensure service history includes CVT/HCF‑2 fluid changes if applicable.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Honda Insight?
Short answer: maybe — but buyer beware and pick the year/trim and inspection carefully.

- NSW dealer market snapshot (live): Active listings: 1 | Price range: AUD $14,990 – $14,990 | Year range: 2011 | Median odometer: 44,247 km. That single 2011 listing is cheap because the market expects hybrid IMA age‑related faults; low kms are attractive but expect near‑term hybrid battery risk. Use VIN checks and pre‑purchase scanning. (Data provided by MTA NSW scraper — your current live market). Keep that number in your pocket as a starting negotiation point.
- Typical used market pricing & depreciation:
  - Recent used 2022 Insights retailed around USD values that translate to substantially higher new‑car prices; valuation services show the Insight can hold value slightly better than some competitors in short windows. Examples: KBB data notes a 24% drop over three years for a 2022 example; iSeeCars shows a 5‑year depreciation around mid‑30% range in some comparisons. These are US figures but useful for trend context when looking at relative depreciation versus Prius/Ioniq. [KBB 2022 depreciation note](https://www.kbb.com/honda/insight/), [iSeeCars comparative depreciation](https://www.iseecars.com/compare/honda-insight-vs-hyundai-ioniq_hybrid).
  - Practical Australian guidance: older IMA cars (2010–2014) have already taken the large depreciation hit — $5–15k market depending on condition/mileage — with the NSW example at $14,990 for a 2011 low‑km car being towards the stronger end of that band.
- Depreciation metric to expect (rough guideline):
  - New to 3 years: ~20–35% (depends on trim/year); 5 years: mid‑30s%. Hybrids can retain slightly stronger used demand if battery health is proven, but that premium disappears if the IMA pack is suspect. Sources: CarEdge, iSeeCars, KBB. [CarEdge depreciation overview](https://caredge.com/honda/insight/depreciation?m=12000&o=3&p=25000&y=1).
- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy:
  - Fuel economy: use real‑world combined ~5.0–5.5 L/100 km for a 1.5L Insight in everyday Australian driving (EPA 4.5 L/100 km is optimistic; owner logs nearer 5.1 L/100 km — convert US mpg to L/100km with the cited sources). [FuelEconomy EPA numbers](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/bymodel/2019_Honda_Insight.shtml), [Fuelly logs](https://www.fuelly.com/car/honda/insight).
  - Estimate expected fuel savings vs a petrol Corolla/Civic with 6.5–7.5 L/100 km: roughly 1.5–2.5 L/100 km savings. Multiply by annual km (e.g., 15,000 km) and current ARD fuel price to see yearly payback. If you don’t rack up kms, the hybrid premium may not pay off.
  - Hybrids vs petrol break‑even: KBB and Drive‑Insight analysis show used hybrids often win on 5‑year TCO for moderately high usage (typical Aussie commuter ~15,000–20,000 km/year) because fuel savings plus slightly better resale offset the purchase premium. But those math outcomes rely on a healthy hybrid battery; a battery replacement (IMA pack or battery controller) can wipe out years of savings. [KBB hybrid break‑even primer](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/), [Drive‑Insight used hybrid TCO study](https://drive-insight.net/posts/used-hybrid-vs-gas-tco/).
- So, how to decide (practical checklist):
  - If the car is a 2011 IMA model (like your NSW sample): buy only if price reflects likely future IMA battery work OR the seller can prove a recent module rebuild/replacement and provide a warranty. The NSW listing at AUD $14,990 for a 2011 with 44,247 km might be a rare, low‑km find — but verify the IMA pack health (specialist scan) and engine condition. [Go‑Parts P3000 IMA guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p3000-honda-insight-2010-2014-lea-mf6-1).
  - If you’re looking at 2019–2022 cars: the sweet spot is typically an EX trim around 2019–2020 with a clear service history and confirmed ADAS firmware recalls addressed. Touring trims are nice but may cost more without proportional fuel or reliability upside. [Honda 2019 trims & specs](https://hondanews.com/en-US/honda-automobiles/releases/release-99b9311b568240639b28482eb820d667-2019-insight-specifications-and-features), [Edmunds trims](https://www.edmunds.com/honda/insight/2019/trims/).
- Final recommendation (crystal clear):
  - If your driving is high‑km (15,000+ km/year), a healthy late‑model Insight (2019–2020 EX/Touring with dealer‑documented hybrid checks and ADAS updates) can be an economical buy — better fuel economy (real‑world ~5.0 L/100 km) beats conventional petrol in TCO over ~4–6 years, assuming no major hybrid battery work. Check recall/firmware status before buying. [Fuelly real‑world data](https://www.fuelly.com/car/honda/insight), [NHTSA ADAS investigation pages](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2019/HONDA/INSIGHT/).
  - If you are offered a 2010–2014 IMA Insight (like the NSW $14,990 2011 listing): proceed cautiously. The low odometer is attractive, but IMA battery age and paint/clearcoat history mean you must get a specialist hybrid scan and negotiate hard. If the vendor warranties the IMA pack or the price drops into the mid‑$8k–$12k range, it becomes a defensible buy for urban commuters who want cheap running costs and don’t mind potential DIY/hybrid repairs. [Go‑Parts battery advice](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p3000-honda-insight-2010-2014-lea-mf6-1), [CarComplaints paint notes](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Honda/Insight/body_paint/index.shtml).
  - Avoid any Insight with unexplained check‑engine/hybrid faults on the dash, CVT shudder or ADAS phantom interventions that cannot be demonstrated as fixed by the dealer.
