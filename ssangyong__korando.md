#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: Korando (Fourth generation sometimes marketed under KGM SsangYong / KGM Korando — the “Korando” revived as a compact crossover from 2019 onward). Production timeframe for this body/architecture: 2019–present (C300/Korando IV / Korando C300 family in global listings). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ssangyong_korando) and global model pages document the fourth‑gen Korando as the current unibody compact SUV (launched 2019 onward).  
- Internal codes you’ll see in threads and parts lists: generically referred to as Korando C300 / Korando IV; diesel engines appear under the **e‑XDi** family and the petrol turbo under **1.5 T‑GDI** in lots of spec sheets (see Auto‑Data and Autotijd). [Auto‑Data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/kgm-korando-1.5-t-gdi-163hp-4wd-50485) | [Autotijd](https://autotijd.be/en/specs/ssangyong/korando-2021-2024)

- Product hierarchy (where it sits in the range): compact / C‑segment crossover. Below it sit smaller SsangYong/KGM crossovers such as the Tivoli; above it sit larger SUVs like the Rexton in the brand range. The Korando is pitched against mainstream compact SUVs rather than micro‑SUVs, so it’s a step up from Tivoli in size and kit. [Drive showroom listing notes segment position and warranty positioning in Australia](https://www.drive.com.au/showrooms/ssangyong/korando/).

- Platform and parts commonality: the modern Korando is a unibody compact crossover architecture (a move away from older ladder‑frame SUVs in earlier generations). That platform is shared with other KGM/SsangYong small/compact crossovers from the same era — the unibody architecture and drivetrain families are used across KGM’s compact range, improving parts availability relative to one‑off niche platforms. (See Wikipedia/KGM model pages for generation/platform notes.) [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ssangyong_korando) | [KGM official model page](https://ssangyong.com.my/models_kgm/korando/)

- Engines, outputs, transmissions and drivetrains sold into Australia and relevant export markets:
  - Petrol: **1.5 T‑GDI** turbocharged petrol — quoted ~163 hp (120 kW) and ~280 Nm; paired with a 6‑speed automatic in many markets and available in FWD or AWD layouts in higher spec levels. [Auto‑Data model page](https://www.auto-data.net/en/kgm-korando-1.5-t-gdi-163hp-4wd-50485)
  - Diesel: **1.6 e‑XDi** diesel family (and older 2.0 e‑XDi variants in earlier Korando derivatives) — diesel outputs typically around 136 hp for the 1.6 diesel variants; paired to 6‑speed automatics/manuals depending on market, with 2WD or AWD options. [Autotijd](https://autotijd.be/en/specs/ssangyong/korando-2021-2024) | [EngineCode e‑XDi family notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGM_e-XDi_engine)
  - EV: Korando e‑Motion (fully electric variant in some markets) — battery ~61.5 kWh gross, WLTP range ~330–340 km, consumption ~16.8 kWh/100 km in WLTP testing. [EV‑Database](https://ev-database.org/car/1589/Ssangyong-Korando-e-Motion)
  - Transmissions observed across the range: 6‑speed automatic (common), 6‑speed manual available in some petrol trims in export markets. Drive layouts: FWD standard on most petrol fronts, AWD optional on higher trims. [Auto‑Data / CarsGuide spec listings](https://www.auto-data.net/en/kgm-korando-1.5-t-gdi-163hp-4wd-50485) | [CarsGuide Korando page](https://www.carsguide.com.au/ssangyong/korando/price/2025)

- Marketplace rivals (explicit): Hyundai Tucson, Kia Sportage, Skoda Karoq, Ford Kuga / Escape, MG HS and other compact crossover players. Comparison tools and reviews put Korando squarely in this compact‑SUV battleground. #### 2. What goes wrong with a used SsangYong Korando?
- Exterior:
  - Panel fit and finish: generally acceptable for the money — reviewers call interior/exterior build a step up on older SsangYong models, but not quite premium. Expect good basic build but cosmetic trim panels (lower bumper inserts, gloss black bits) to scratch or scuff in city use. [Parkers interior notes / Carbuyer fit‑and‑finish context](https://www.parkers.co.uk/ssangyong/korando/review/interior/) | [Carbuyer](https://www.carbuyer.co.uk/ssangyong/korando/interior)
  - Recalls and structural items to check: there have been manufacturer recalls affecting Korando builds in various markets — notable items include a fuel pipe cracking recall (identified in several recall databases) and, more recently, a steering‑column mounting bracket issue reported to affect builds around early 2020–2021 in at least one territory. If you’re shopping, always VIN‑check for outstanding recall work and ask for recall completion proof. [Vehicle‑Recall summary](https://vehicle-recall.co.uk/recalls/cars/ssangyong/korando) | [AutoInsider recall summary and steering column issue](https://www.autoinsider.co.uk/recalls/ssangyong/korando)
  - ADAS and sensors: buyer beware — owners report ADAS nags, false braking, phantom warnings and sensor faults. Some complaints describe whole systems (ABS/ESC/AEB/Auto‑hold) failing at low miles, sometimes leaving the car undrivable until repaired. Dirty sensors, misalignment after bumper/windscreen work or low system voltage are common causes; in other cases a software update or module replacement is required. Often parts lead‑times and dealer support can slow repairs. [HonestJohn owner report of ABS/ESC/AEB failures](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/owner-reviews/ssangyong/korando-2019/?review=18338) | [Autodoc ADAS/electrical overview](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-ssangyong-korando)  
  - Practical check: inspect wheel arch liners and bumper clips for previous repairs (cheap fixes hide accident history), verify VIN vs. service records for recall completion, check AEB/ACC operation on a short test drive and ask for pre‑purchase scan of ADAS modules if you suspect faults.

- Interior:
  - Durability: the cabin trims are pragmatic — good for family and commuter work, but not as hard‑wearing as top rivals. If you’re thinking rideshare or courier duty, expect wear to cloth/leather trim and scuffing to hard plastics within 80–150k km depending on use; owners rate the Korando as “a lot of car for the money” but built to a value price point. [HonestJohn owners / Parkers](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/ssangyong/korando/), [Parkers review](https://www.parkers.co.uk/ssangyong/korando/review/interior/)
  - Entry/egress / ergonomics: seats sit at a conventional crossover height; door checks and sill geometry are competent — nothing to write home about but no major complaints in owner forums. If you have mobility concerns, test the door opening angles yourself as Korando door checks are standard‑pattern rather than wide‑swing.
  - Electrical and infotainment:
    - Infotainment stability: documented issues — the wireless phone charging pad and infotainment stability have been reported as problematic in long‑term tests, with crashes and required reboots that can kill radio, navigation and even climate controls until the system resets. Firmware updates have helped some owners but not eliminated reoccurrence in forum threads. [Autocar long‑term test mentions wireless charger issues](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-review/ssangyong/korando-e-motion/long-term-reviews/ssangyong-korando-e-motion-2023-long-term) | [GoingElectric forum thread on e‑Motion infotainment crashes](https://www.goingelectric.de/forum/viewtopic.php?t=93685)
    - Screen sizes and hardware versions: base and mid trims have smaller infotainment screens and analogue/digital mix; upper Ultimate packs include larger screens and digital instrument clusters. Higher trims also tend to get more driver‑assist kit. Check the specific hardware version if screen stability matters and ask for evidence of software updates applied by the dealer. [HonestJohn / CarsGuide trim breakdowns](https://www.honestjohn.co.uk/ssangyong/korando/) | [CarsGuide Korando price/spec list](https://www.carsguide.com.au/ssangyong/korando/price/2025)
    - 12V battery & parasitic drain: owners report starter battery and electrical‑gremlin symptoms in community threads and reliability roundups — unexplained drains or modules staying awake can occur, especially after ADAS or infotainment faults. A pre‑purchase meter test and 12V drain test (parasitic draw) should be part of your PPI. [Autodoc electrical fault aggregation](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-ssangyong-korando)

- Mechanically:
  - Brand engineering reputation: SsangYong/KGM historically focused on value and ruggedness rather than premium refinement. The modern Korando is a much improved product-quality step compared with older models, but engineering quirks (electronics, targeted powertrain weak spots and periodic parts delays) remain a consistent theme in owner threads and long‑term tests. [Drive showroom & AutoExpress long‑term reviews](https://www.drive.com.au/showrooms/ssangyong/korando/) | [AutoExpress long‑term](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/kgm/korando/352278/ssangyong-korando-ultimate-long-term-test-review)
  - Theft & security vulnerabilities:
    - Immobiliser basics: Korando uses a conventional transponder key immobiliser — if the key isn’t recognised the engine will not run or will cut out after starting. Replacement keys should be sourced through authorised dealer channels. [StartMyCar immobiliser guide and owner manual references](https://www.startmycar.com/ssangyong/korando/guides/immobilizer-anti-theft-system) | [Owner manual repository](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1584600/Ssangyong-Korando.html)
    - Digital threats: like most older architecture vehicles, the Korando’s CAN bus lacks modern cryptographic protections — researchers and aftermarket writeups warn about CAN‑bus injection and relay attacks as general threats to legacy systems. For high‑risk owners (urban areas with opportunistic thieves) fit a mechanical steering lock, upgrade to a GPS tracker with immobiliser cut‑out and keep keys away from doors/windows to blunt relay thefts. [CAN bus security overview](https://tibbsforge.com/can-bus-security-news/) | practical advice from auto forums and aftermarket markets.

- Engines:
  - For clarity, I’ll list each engine variant referenced in market/spec sources and summarise the high‑priority failure points owners and repair aggregators report. (Each subsection is italicised and the engine code / name is bolded as requested.)

  *- **1.5 T‑GDI** (petrol turbo, ~163 hp / 280 Nm)*  
    - What to watch: owners and spec sheets show the 1.5 T‑GDI as the common modern petrol offering. The usual turbo‑petrol vulnerabilities are software/TMAP tuning, occasional misfire/roughness complaints in some owners’ threads, and any fuel‑system items that can be sensitive to low‑grade fuel. Fuel consumption quoted in technical specs ~8.1 L/100 km (WLTP entries), so moderate thirst for a compact petrol turbo. [Auto‑Data 1.5 T‑GDI spec & consumption](https://www.auto-data.net/en/kgm-korando-1.5-t-gdi-163hp-4wd-50485)  
    - Preventative maintenance: follow scheduled servicing, which typically involves oil and filter at standard intervals and spark plugs/air filters per the service manual. If you tow or run frequent short trips, shorten oil service intervals and use manufacturer‑grade oil to prevent turbo‑soot and oil coking. Confirm exact service intervals in the owner manual or with an authorised dealer.

  *- **1.6 e‑XDi** (small diesel ~136 hp)*  
    - What to watch: diesel‑specific failures reported across the e‑XDi family include EGR congestion, turbocharger wear and injector issues after higher mileages (100–150k km bands in community reports). Diesel models typically need attention to intercooler and intake ducts where soot collects, and regular fuel filter changes to avoid injector damage. [EngineCode e‑XDi family notes / Autodoc diesel fault summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGM_e-XDi_engine) | [Autodoc common problems](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-ssangyong-korando)  
    - Preventative maintenance: regular fuel filter replacement, diesel system cleaner intervals and EGR inspection around 60–100k km depending on driving mix. Diesel owners should use quality low‑sulphur diesel and keep up with turbo oil/air service steps.

  *- **e‑XDi 200 / 2.0 diesel** (older/larger diesel families used in some Korando derivates)*  
    - What to watch: similar to the 1.6 family — injectors and turbocharger complexity are the recurring themes in owner posts. Watch for loss of power and rising fuel consumption as early symptoms. [Autodoc diesel fault aggregations](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-ssangyong-korando)

  *- **Korando e‑Motion (EV)** (61.5 kWh battery ~WLTP 339 km)*  
    - What to watch: EV variant owner threads and long‑term tests mention infotainment and electrical stability issues more often than drive‑unit failures; the battery / motor architecture itself uses conventional EV tech and WLTP consumption is ~16.8 kWh/100 km. Key checks for used e‑Motion: battery health/capacity report from dealer or authorised workshop, charging port and onboard charger diagnostics, and full software update history. [EV‑Database Korando e‑Motion](https://ev-database.org/car/1589/Ssangyong-Korando-e-Motion) | [Autocar long‑term infotainment notes](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-review/ssangyong/korando-e-motion/long-term-reviews/ssangyong-korando-e-motion-2023-long-term)

  - Service interval guidance (practical): always follow the factory schedule in the owner manual and the dealer’s recommended service plan. If you can’t confirm the exact interval from paperwork, treat Korando like most modern passenger SUVs and budget for oil/filter service every 10,000–15,000 km under normal driving — shorter if you do many short trips or heavy load/towing. (Check the official manual or dealer for exact mileage service items.) [Owner manual repository for reference](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1584600/Ssangyong-Korando.html)

- Transmission:
  - Torque‑converter automatics (most common 6‑speed auto): known failure modes and symptoms — jerky gear changes, delayed engagement, and in some cases locking in a single gear or limp‑home mode around ~60,000–80,000 km in several owner reports and technical aggregators. Diagnostic trouble code P2757 (torque converter clutch pressure control solenoid) has been reported on some Korando examples; the usual first steps are checking transmission fluid level/quality and solenoid/wiring/connector condition. [Autodoc automatic transmission issues summary](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-ssangyong-korando) | [JustAnswer torque converter solenoid DTC discussion](https://www.justanswer.com/car/rgzl3-11-23-ssangyong-korando-ex-2wd-1-5-turbo-engine-light-code.html)  
  - Service & prevention: if you get a used Korando, insist on a transmission scan and fluid sample analysis if the dealer can provide it — and budget for a service that replaces the transmission fluid/filters if records don’t show regular fluid changes. Aggressive diagnosis and early action avoids clutch/solenoid damage that becomes expensive quickly.
  - Other gearbox types (DCT/eCVT): not relevant to the mainstream Korando spec in most markets; the Korando line sticks to conventional torque‑converter automatics or manuals in common trims.

- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) — how to think about it:
  - Fuel / energy use:
    - Petrol 1.5 T‑GDI baseline: factory WLTP figure ~8.1 L/100 km (you will likely see 8–10 L/100 km in mixed Australian driving). [Auto‑Data 1.5 T‑GDI spec](https://www.auto-data.net/en/kgm-korando-1.5-t-gdi-163hp-4wd-50485)
    - EV Korando e‑Motion: WLTP consumption ~16.8 kWh/100 km, range ~339 km (61.5 kWh gross). [EV‑Database](https://ev-database.org/car/1589/Ssangyong-Korando-e-Motion)
  - How to compare: To judge whether to pay a premium for an e‑Motion or hybrid (if available), run these steps:
    1. Compute per‑100 km energy cost: petrol price (AUD/L) × L/100 km versus electricity price (AUD/kWh) × kWh/100km.  
    2. Add servicing differences: EVs have lower scheduled mechanical servicing (no oil changes) but battery/cooling/charger checks and potential battery warranty coverage to verify. Petrol/diesel have regular oil, filter and intermittent fuel system costs.  
    3. Add depreciation: EVs sometimes retain value better depending on market demand — but for a Korando EV/vs petrol you must check local demand in NSW — at present mainstream uptake for non‑Tesla EVs varies and dealer support matters. [EV‑Database consumption figure](https://ev-database.org/car/1589/Ssangyong-Korando-e-Motion)  
  - Example (illustrative only): at 8.1 L/100 km, if petrol is $1.70/L then fuel cost ≈ $13.77/100 km. For EV at 16.8 kWh/100 km and home electricity at $0.30/kWh, energy cost ≈ $5.04/100 km. Savings sound big on the energy line, but you must fold in purchase premium and depreciation differential and access to home/work charging. Use your real local petrol and electricity rates to decide. (Use the AFDC or your own spreadsheet to run break‑even analysis — AFDC calc is a good model template though it’s US‑centric.) - Final buying recommendation — should you buy one?
  - Short answer: Yes — but only if you buy the right example, at the right price, and with due diligence. The Korando is a value‑packed compact SUV that gives a lot of kit for the money. It’s well worth considering if you prioritise features and space over brand prestige — but you must be disciplined on pre‑purchase checks because the brand’s electrical and transmission quirks can turn a cheap buy into a costly repair.
  - Ideal buy scenario: a late model (2022–2024), low to mid‑20k km Korando on a full dealer service history, with evidence of recall completions and software updates, and ideally still under the manufacturer’s transferable warranty (7‑year where applicable). From the NSW snapshot, a 2023 with 18,757 km at $27,990 looks like the sort of example that merits a close PPI. [NSW dealer snapshot — your live data]
  - Which trim/year is the sweet spot: aim for mid‑spec (Pioneer/Ultimate mid‑grade equivalents depending on local naming) that includes the larger infotainment and most ADAS but avoid entry‑level stripped cars where key comfort/safety items are absent and high‑mileage older diesels where injectors/turbo work is looming. Check for the 1.5 T‑GDI petrol as the sweet spot for light urban use (reasonable economy and less diesel‑system headache), or the e‑Motion EV if you can verify battery health and you have reliable home charging. [CarsGuide trim and pricing split](https://www.carsguide.com.au/kgm-ssangyong/korando/2025) | [EV‑Database e‑Motion specs](https://ev-database.org/car/1589/Ssangyong-Korando-e-Motion)
  - Walk‑away points: no full service history, outstanding recalls, active ADAS warnings on test drive, signs of transmission jerking or torque‑converter fault codes, or no proof the car’s software/infotainment was ever updated. If any of those are present, price must fall dramatically or you should keep walking.
  - Negotiation leverage: use the brand’s market perception — good for discounting. The NSW market snapshot shows weak supply (single listed dealer example) so investigate nearby states for comparable stock and compare prices to sharpen offers.
