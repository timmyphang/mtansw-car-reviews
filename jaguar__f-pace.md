#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: X761 (first launched 2016; production run 2016–present with mid‑cycle updates and facelifts through the 2020s). See Jaguar’s model pages and the Wikipedia overview for the X761 lineage. [Jaguar official specs](https://www.jaguar.com/en-xi/jdx/jaguar-range/f-pace/specifications/index.html), [Wikipedia X761](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_F-Pace).
- Product hierarchy: The F‑Pace sits as Jaguar’s compact luxury performance SUV — above it is the larger E‑Pace/other crossovers in price/size mix (and below are sportier two‑door models in pricing). It’s Jaguar’s mainstream SUV offering that competes for buyers who’d otherwise look at BMW X3, Mercedes‑Benz GLC, Audi Q5 and Porsche Macan.
- Platform: Built on Jaguar Land Rover’s D7 (D7a) modular aluminium architecture shared with a handful of JLR models — this underpins parts-commonality and structural repairs. [Wikipedia (platform list)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_F-Pace).
- Common global stablemates using the same foundation: other Jaguar and Land Rover models that share D7 derivatives (look at Jaguar model family pages for specifics). Manufacturer details are on the Jaguar specs page: [Jaguar F‑PACE specs](https://www.jaguar.com/en-xi/jdx/jaguar-range/f-pace/specifications/index.html).
- Market-specific engines / outputs / transmissions / drivetrains sold in Australia over the years (summary):
  - **2.0‑litre Ingenium petrol** (badged P250 / P300 in various markets) — four‑cylinder turbo, ~180–221 kW depending on tune. Paired to an 8‑speed automatic, normally AWD on Australian spec cars. [Evo engine roundup](https://www.evo.co.uk/jaguar/f‑pace/engine-and-gearbox), [Jaguar specs](https://www.jaguar.com/en-xi/jdx/jaguar-range/f-pace/specifications/index.html).
  - **3.0‑litre inline‑6 mild‑hybrid** (P400) — petrol I6 with MHEV belt‑integrated starter/boost tech, 8‑speed auto, AWD. [Evo summary](https://www.evo.co.uk/jaguar/f-pace/engine-and-gearbox).
  - **Plug‑in hybrid PHEV (P400e)** — petrol/electric hybrid with ~50+ km WLTP electric range on early PHEV versions; 8‑speed auto + electric drive. [Jaguar PHEV info](https://www.jaguar.com/en-gb/jdx/electric-cars/plug-in-hybrid.html).
  - **5.0‑litre supercharged V8 (SVR)** — high‑performance variant on 8‑speed auto, AWD.
  - Diesels: **2.0D / 3.0D** turbodiesels in earlier Australian ranges (availability varies by year/market).
- Rivals: BMW X3/X4, Mercedes‑Benz GLC, Audi Q5, Porsche Macan, Genesis GV70 and Range Rover Velar. See comparison rundowns and class lists: [The Car Connection competitors](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/jaguar_f-pace_choices), [MotorTrend competitive context](https://www.motortrend.com/cars/jaguar/f-pace/2026).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Jaguar F-Pace?
Short version: it’s glamorous until the electronics and small trim bits remind you British premium often means brief thrills and fiddly gremlins later. Below is the nuts‑and‑bolts diagnostic for what to watch when inspecting a used F‑Pace in Australia.

Exterior:
- Panel fit & finish: Early production examples show inconsistent panel gaps and alignment quirks — owners and technicians have logged SSMs and forum threads on front end and rear panel gapping (see SSM73381 discussions). Look closely at rear light joins, wheel arch seams and bonnet gaps for uneven lines. [SSM73381 thread](https://www.fpaceforum.com/threads/ssm73381-jaguar-xf-xe-and-f-pace-front-end-panel-gapping-profiles.31713/), [owner reports](https://www.jaginfo.org/threads/f-pace-panel-fit.80409/).
- Exterior trim vulnerabilities: Long roof drip mouldings and other gloss/painted trims are known to loosen, fade or peel. Go‑Parts runs through common fading/peeling parts for Jaguar/Land Rover that affect F‑PACEs (roof mouldings can ‘fly off’ when clips fail — part numbers and clip replacements are documented). Inspect the drip rail moulding and clip condition (Part No. T4A13514 mentioned in owner guides). [Go‑Parts trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/door-molding-jaguar-f-pace-2017-2023).
- Paint durability: Not a chronic systemic peel issue, but chips on leading edges and stone chips are common on performance SUVs — check for resprays and overspray.
- Recalls & safety systems: There have been numerous campaigns for the F‑PACE (recall trackers list many). Always run the VIN through Jaguar’s VIN/recall lookup and national recall portals. [Jaguar VIN recall lookup](https://www.jaguar.com/en-us/jdx/ownership/vin-recall.html), [RecallExplained summary](https://recallexplained.com/jaguar/f-pace/).
- ADAS behaviour: Forward camera/radar faults and unexpected AEB or adaptive cruise behaviour have been frequently reported. Community threads document radar module faults and adaptive cruise malfunctions; Go‑Parts details JLR camera/radar diagnosis. Confirm ADAS sensors are calibrated and free of damage; recalibration after panel repairs frequently required. [Forward radar failure thread](https://www.fpaceforum.com/threads/forward-looking-radar-failure.46079/), [Go‑Parts camera guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/video-camera-land-rover-range-rover-evoque-land-rover-discovery-sport-land-rover-range-rover-sport-2009-2022).
- Software fixes: Some ADAS quirks and wireless charging issues have firmware/SOTA patches — check service history for recent software updates. Jaguar documents software update capability and specific SOTA actions. [Jaguar software updates](https://www.jaguar.com/en-xi/jdx/ownership/software-update/index.html).

Interior:
- Build & wear: Interior materials are mostly premium, but owners report variability — hard armrests, thinning door panel padding, and sagging A‑pillar fabric or rattles on higher‑mileage or heavy‑duty examples (rideshare use will accelerate wear). Forum notes and Go‑Parts guides show these bits fail more often than you’d expect. [A‑pillar trim sag](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/body-a-pillar-trim-panel-jaguar-fpace-2017-2026), [interior quality forum](https://www.fpaceforum.com/threads/interior-quality-comparison.27698/).
- Spatial ergonomics: Entry/exit is generally comfortable; door sills are mid‑height typical of compact/luxury SUVs. Check door checks and hinges for stiffness on older/neglected cars.
- Electrical & infotainment:
  - Infotainment displays have a known history of blank/frozen screens, “ghost” touches and total failures across Jaguar/Land Rover models. Expect interim resets and sometimes replacement screens — confirm the screen boots reliably and phone Bluetooth/Apple CarPlay works. [Infotainment failure guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-land-rover-range-rover-sport-land-rover-range-rover-jaguar-e-pace-2015-2026).
  - Wireless charging pad has been the subject of SSMs and owner complaints (intermittent operation; later SOTA updates attempted fixes — Jaguar has a bulletin and SOTA action to improve pad performance). If the fitted pad doesn’t work, check for software updates and that the dealer has applied them. [SSM74606 (wireless pad)](https://dot.report/manufacturer-communications/10168909), [SOTA bulletin 2025 FSA](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2025/MC-11015314-0001.pdf).
  - 12V battery drain patterns: owner threads report parasitic drains from infotainment modules and passive entry electronics if vehicles sit unused; check battery condition and test accessory draw.
  - Screen sizes and hardware: earlier models have Jaguar’s InControl TouchPro systems; later mid‑cycle cars upgraded screens and SOTA capabilities. Confirm the hardware variant and if navigation maps and software have been updated.
- Air‑con quirks: owners report intermittent HVAC faults (blower, actuators) on some model years — check for consistent heating/cooling and odour from evaporator (sign of blocked drain or mould).

Mechanically:
- Brand engineering reputation: Jaguar makes engaging, well‑sorted chassis and engines — but complexity, electronics and tight tolerances mean maintenance costs and specialist labour can bite. Expect premium dealer labour rates and specialist independent Jaguar/Land Rover workshops to be the cheaper alternative for common servicing.
- Theft/security vulnerabilities:
  - Jaguar Land Rover has publicly acknowledged relay/relay‑style attacks and invested in Ultra‑Wide Band (UWB) tech in recent models to reduce relay thefts. However mid‑2010s F‑Pace models may not have all the newer protections. [JLR security investment](https://media.jaguarlandrover.com/news/2023/11/jlr-invests-vehicle-security-reduce-thefts).
  - CAN injection/headlight‑access hacks are a real and rising technique thieves use to spoof CAN messages and defeat immobilisers. This is not unique to Jaguar. Owners should assume an attacker can exploit exposed wiring on older vehicles if motivated. [CAN injection primer](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/), [Autoblog coverage](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update).
  - Practical countermeasures: mechanical steering‑wheel lock, hardwired GPS tracker with remote immobilise, OBD port blocker, alarm upgrades that monitor interior movement, and physically securing keys in Faraday‑shielded pouches overnight. Newer JLR cars benefit from UWB, but older F‑Paces do not — take precautions.
- High‑priority security fixes: verify passive entry/authentication action messages (dealers may have performed hardware recalls or service actions) — check VIN recall page. [Jaguar VIN recall lookup](https://www.jaguar.com/en-us/jdx/ownership/vin-recall.html).

Engines:
(Each engine variant below is an italicised subsection and includes specific failure points and recommended preventative maintenance intervals.)
- * **2.0‑litre Ingenium petrol/diesel (commonly badged P250/P300 / 2.0D)** *
  - Known issues: timing‑chain stretch and tensioner wear (more documented on diesel variants but also noted on petrol Ingenium family), oil dilution in some diesel/turbo duty cycles, turbocharger faults and DPF regeneration problems on diesel variants. Early Ingenium engines — especially when used in short‑trip urban cycles — can show accelerated wear or DPF/turbo issues. [LRDirect timing chain guide](https://www.lrdirect.com/technical-guides/2-0-ingenium-timing-chain-faults), [Stedmans overview](https://stedmansgarage.co.uk/common-faults/top-8-jaguar-f-pace-problems/).
  - Failure points to inspect: cold‑start rattle (timing chain/tensioner), high oil consumption, blue smoke, persistent engine fault lights and turbo performance drop‑off.
  - Preventative maintenance: be conservative — oil & filter every 10,000 km (or 12 months), valve/timing chain inspection if cold‑start rattle appears, use high‑quality low‑ash oils recommended by Jaguar, and avoid prolonged low‑load urban use without occasional higher‑RPM/longer drives to help DPF regen. If the car spent most life as short trips, factor potential DPF/turbo service costs into the price.
- * **3.0‑litre I6 mild‑hybrid (P400)** *
  - Known issues: generally better behaved than the early 2.0 Ingenium, but MHEV belt‑integrated starter generator systems add complexity — monitor for belt drive or electrical assist issues. Some owners report cooling and oil consumption concerns in early examples.
  - Failure points: accessory belt/ISG wear, cooling leaks at junctions, intermittent warning lights related to electrified systems.
  - Preventative maintenance: oil & filter every 10,000–15,000 km depending on duty and oil grade; inspect MHEV belt and connectors at 40,000 km intervals; check for any dealer SOTA fixes for MHEV controllers.
- * **P400e Plug‑in hybrid (PHEV)** *
  - Known issues: PHEV systems require owner diligence to realise economy benefits — battery degradation is possible if not used/charged regularly; PHEV packs in these cars are not immune to costlier repairs. Long‑term tests show real-world electric range can drop vs WLTP numbers if you don’t charge consistently. [Jaguar PHEV overview](https://www.jaguar.com/en-gb/jdx/electric-cars/plug-in-hybrid.html), [Auto Express long‑term note](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/jaguar/f-pace/358732/jaguar-f-pace-pheve-long-term-test).
  - Failure points: battery health decline, charging port/cable wear, hybrid cooling circuits, and inverter/charger module faults.
  - Preventative maintenance: battery health checks (state of charge retention) every 2–3 years if buying used, yearly inspection of charging hardware, and a home charge routine if you intend to keep running costs low.
- * **5.0‑litre supercharged V8 (SVR)** *
  - Known issues: V8s are robust but expensive to maintain — supercharger drive, high‑pressure fuel system checks, and higher oil consumption are expected on performance use cars.
  - Failure points: supercharger oiling/drive, ignition coil/fuel injector wear, and higher brake/tyre replacement bills.
  - Preventative maintenance: oil & filter every 10,000 km (preferably high‑grade synthetic), frequent gearbox/transmission oil checks given high torque, and track‑day or heavy use service history scrutiny.
- * **Diesels (2.0D / 3.0D)** *
  - Known issues: 2.0D engines have timing‑chain wear documented; 3.0D crankshaft and turbo concerns have been reported in some forums; DPF issues are common for short‑trip users. [Stedmans engine faults](https://stedmansgarage.co.uk/engine-transmission/2-0-ingenium-engine/), [CarBrain summary](https://carbrain.com/blog/jaguar-f-pace-issues).
  - Preventative maintenance: for diesels, aim for oil/filters every 10,000 km, use correct low‑ash oil, and ensure regular extended highway driving to allow proper DPF regeneration.

Transmission:
- Most F‑Paces use the ZF 8HP 8‑speed torque‑converter automatic family — overall a smooth, efficient gearbox but with known failure modes if service is neglected. Owners report harsh downshifts, hesitant behaviour and torque‑converter lock‑up clutch issues in stop‑start city driving — symptoms often traceable to fluid condition, solenoids or clutch pack wear. [ASR gearbox history & faults](https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/zf-8hp-8-speed-torque-converter/), [DK Transmission notes](https://www.dktransmission.com/zf-8hp-845re-850re-8hp75-8hp95-transmission-problems-fixes).
- PHEV transmissions include hybrid coupling; failure or inverter issues are expensive.
- Preventative checks: request transmission service records (fluid and filter changes). If none, plan for a fluid change soon after purchase by a specialist; test-drive for smoothness under cold start, mid‑range acceleration and low‑speed creep.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Jaguar F‑Pace?
- NSW dealer market snapshot (live): Active listings: 1 — Price range: AUD $22,950 (single listing) — Year: 2016 — Median odometer: 2,016 km — Transmission: Sports Automatic. (This is the live MTA NSW scraper summary you provided — use it as the local market reality.)
- Pricing context: nationally, used F‑Pace prices vary widely by year, engine and condition — U.S./UK listing aggregators show large spreads (examples on KBB, Edmunds and CarGurus). Expect a steep depreciation curve in early years versus German rivals. [KBB used listings](https://www.kbb.com/cars-for-sale/used/jaguar/f-pace), [Edmunds used inventory](https://www.edmunds.com/used-jaguar-f-pace/).
- Depreciation metrics: industry trackers show Jaguar F‑Pace depreciation is steep relative to segment average — iSeeCars reports ~32.8% drop after 3 years, and CarEdge/CareEdge give 5‑year drops around 56–65% depending on dataset. That’s part of why you can find 2016‑2018 examples for bargain prices if you’re comfortable with potential repair bills. [iSeeCars depreciation stat](https://www.iseecars.com/car/jaguar-f-pace/resale-value), [CarEdge depreciation](https://caredge.com/jaguar/f-pace/depreciation/).
- TCO and running‑cost strategy (how to calculate for Australia):
  - Depreciation: already the largest single owner cost for premium SUVs; Jaguar F‑Pace tends to lose value quicker than BMW/Audi equivalents — factor this into trade‑in planning.
  - Fuel: use L/100km figures (convert EPA/WLTP where necessary). Example MHEV combined EPA ~21 mpg → about 11.2 L/100km (21 mpg US = 235.214 / 21). Use official WLTP/EPA numbers from Jaguar and FuelEconomy entries for the exact variant when calculating. [FuelEconomy F‑PACE MHEV](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=47735).
  - Maintenance & repairs: budget for premium servicing, likely higher parts and labour — factor a contingency of AUD $1,500–$3,000/year for older examples without warranty depending on usage and condition.
  - Insurance: higher for luxury/performance models — shop around.
  - Tyres/brakes: performance tyres and upgraded brakes on R‑Dynamic/SVR variants add running costs.
  - Example TCO tradeoff — Petrol vs PHEV:
    - PHEV models (P400e) can be mathematically cheaper to run only if you charge regularly and do a lot of short city trips where electric drive does the heavy lifting. Jaguar’s PHEV claims ~33 miles/53 km WLTP electric range — real world tends to be lower (Auto Express long‑term test recorded lower ranges in cold climate scenarios). If you don’t charge at home/work, you’ll mostly carry extra weight/complexity for no fuel gain. [Jaguar PHEV info](https://www.jaguar.com/en-gb/jdx/electric-cars/plug-in-hybrid.html), [Auto Express PHEV long term](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/jaguar/f-pace/358732/jaguar-f-pace-pheve-long-term-test).
    - Quick arithmetic: if a petrol P300 returns ~9–11 L/100km in mixed use and a charged PHEV returns the equivalent of 2–5 L/100km in urban commute (when charged each day), the PHEV can save fuel costs — but the premium paid for the PHEV (and potential future battery maintenance) must be amortised over ownership to see the benefit. Use your annual km, electricity cost vs petrol price and charging availability to decide.
  - Dealer warranty & service history: in Australia, dealer‑maintained cars with full Jaguar service are worth a premium. For an out‑of‑warranty purchase (like that 2016 example at AUD $22,950 in NSW) you should budget an independent PPI (pre‑purchase inspection) and immediate fluids/filters change.
- Practical recommendation — buy or walk?
  - When to buy it: Buy if you want Jaguar styling, a sporty chassis and you can:
    - Either secure a low‑kilometre, fully documented example from a dealer with a short certified warranty (better) or;
    - Buy a cheap high‑depreciation older example with a strict independent inspection budgeted for (mechanical checks, transmission service, soft‑trim fasteners and electronics).
  - When not to buy: Avoid high‑mileage, short‑trip diesel 2.0D cars with poor service history (DPF and timing chain risk), and avoid PHEVs if you don’t have ready daily charging access.
  - Best year/trim financial sweet spot: mid‑range petrol R‑Dynamic or S trims from 2018–2020 typically balance kit, reasonable engine choices (P250/P300) and not the ultra‑expensive SVR upkeep. Post‑facelift 2018+ cars addressed some early fit/finish and software issues and are worth paying a small premium for. Sources that suggest later P300/D300 models are more reliable: [EngineFinders guide](https://enginefinders.co.uk/blog/used-jaguar-f-pace-buying-guide-reliability-engines/).
  - If you want an outright bargain: that NSW listing (2016, AUD $22,950, 2,016 km) looks like a rare low‑odometer find — but confirm provenance: is that low odometer genuine? Why is a 2016 only one listing left? Always do VIN checks, logbook/service history checks and an independent pre‑purchase inspection.

Final checklist before you buy (scannable):
- Check VIN for open recalls and service actions: [Jaguar recall lookup](https://www.jaguar.com/en-us/jdx/ownership/vin-recall.html).
- Get a full PPI from a Jaguar/Land Rover specialist (transmission, engine, ADAS calibration, infotainment health).
- Confirm software SOTA updates applied (infotainment/wireless charging/ADAS).
- Inspect exterior trim clips and roof drip moulding for loose/missing clips (Go‑Parts guidance). [Drip moulding guidance](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/door-molding-jaguar-f-pace-2017-2023).
- For diesel/Ingenium cars: listen for timing‑chain rattle at cold start and request compression/oil analysis if in doubt. [LRDirect timing chain guide](https://www.lrdirect.com/technical-guides/2-0-ingenium-timing-chain-faults).
- For PHEV: request battery health/charging cycle report or get an ESS (battery) health test; ensure charger and cable are present and functional.
- Security: fit a visible mechanical deterrent and a hardwired GPS tracker if the car lacks modern UWB protection.

If you’re after cheap ownership and low worry, a German rival with a stronger dealer support/resale reputation might save you money in the long run — but where’s the fun in that?
