#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: Second‑generation Peugeot 5008 (the P87 / 2017–2024 model run in Australia; note Peugeot has launched a new STLA‑based 2024/2025 generation globally). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_5008), [STLA media](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_5008_III)
- Internal codes: commonly sold as the P87 (MP/EMP2‑based family shared with the 3008) for the 2017–2024 cars; the newest 2024/25 cars move to the STLA‑Medium architecture. [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/peugeot/5008/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_5008)
- Where it sits in the Peugeot line: It’s the seven‑seat, long‑wheelbase sibling above the 3008 (5008 = family/7‑seat offering; 3008 = five‑seat compact crossover). Think: 3008 is the house‑mate, 5008 is the one that brings the in‑laws. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot_5008)
- Platform and parts commonality: earlier P87 models share the EMP2 modular platform with the Peugeot 3008 and several Stellantis group models—good news for parts availability and cross‑compatibility of consumables. The newest generation sits on STLA‑Medium. [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/peugeot/5008/), [STLA/Peugeot new 5008](https://qtr.peugeot.com/en/our-models/new-peugeot-5008/all-new.html)
- Market‑specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drive:
  - Petrol: 1.2‑litre PureTech (~130 PS) — manual/automatic or mild‑hybrid support in later updates; 1.6‑litre PureTech/THP (~180 PS) on higher‑spec cars (turbocharged). [Carbuyer](https://www.carbuyer.co.uk/peugeot/5008/156241/peugeot-5008-review-a-capable-and-frugal-7-seater/engines), [autotijd](https://autotijd.be/en/specs/peugeot/5008-2021-2024)
  - Diesel: 1.5‑litre BlueHDi (~130 PS) and 2.0‑litre BlueHDi (~180 PS) on some markets/years. [autotijd](https://autotijd.be/en/specs/peugeot/5008-2021-2024)
  - Transmission: Aisin‑built torque‑converter automatics (EAT6/EAT8) are common across petrol and diesel auto models. [ProPeugeotSpares / ASR](https://www.propeugeotspares.co.za/blog/peugeot-eat6-eat8-transmission-guide/), [ASR Gearbox Repairs](https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/eat6-eat8-aisin-psa-group/)
  - Drivetrain: Predominantly front‑wheel drive for the Australian market examples; no common factory AWD on the mainstream 5008 P87 line. [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/peugeot/5008/)
- Key marketplace rivals (Australia): Skoda Kodiaq, Hyundai Santa Fe, Kia Sorento, VW Tiguan / Tiguan Allspace — the large, seven‑seat family crossover set. [CarsGuide comparisons](https://www.carsguide.com.au/peugeot/5008/vs), [Auto Express Skoda Kodiaq twin test](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/car-group-tests/357248/skoda-kodiaq-vs-peugeot-5008-2022-twin-test-review)

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Peugeot 5008?

Exterior:
- Panel gaps and build finish: Multiple owner threads report uneven panel gaps, bonnet and wing alignment oddities and occasional bonnet clatter—dealers sometimes claim tolerances, owners stay grumpy. Inspect panels side‑on and check bonnet latch engagement. [Peugeot Forums (alignment)](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/alignment-quality-issues.359234/), [Peugeot Forums (bonnet alignment)](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/bonnet-and-passenger-door-alignments.271442/page-3)
- Exterior trim and gloss black bits: Reports of black trim peeling and weather‑strip deterioration—common on models with black plastic lower trims and high‑contact areas (door sills, tailgate seals). Expect some replacement costs post‑warranty. [Peugeot Forums (trim peeling)](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/black-trim-peeling-yet-another-update.372910/)
- Paint & clearcoat: No single Australia‑wide paint recall in the sources, but standard advice applies: check lift‑gates and tailgate edges for bubbling/peeling; PPG primer/clearcoat failures are typical diagnostic steps. Corrosion is rarer with newer cars but check usual salt/sea‑air hotspots. [PPG Paint Defects Guide](https://www.ppg.com/en-US/refinish/support/user-guides-and-manuals/common-paint-defects-guide)
- ADAS and safety system quirks:
  - Radar/camera faults and adaptive‑cruise gremlins are commonly reported — intermittent amber spanner/adaptive cruise faults can appear on cold starts and dealer fixes can be hit‑and‑miss. [Peugeot Forums (adaptive cruise)](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/adaptive-cruise-control-fault-with-amber-spanner.384938/)
  - There have been software/recall actions for telltales and front‑camera/AEB behaviour in later models (DVSA/UK recall listing for VSM/AEB telltale). This underlines that some ADAS bugs are firmware/software fixes — check VIN recall history. [DVSA recall entry](https://product-guru.org/recall/uk_dvsa-r-2025-542-peugeot-5008-v3-vsm-software-missing-aeb-telltale-in-case.html), [Peugeot recall tool](https://www.peugeot.co.uk/tools/recall-campaigns.html)
  - Practical check: verify that AEB, lane‑assist and adaptive cruise lights appear and self‑test; road‑test with and without lane markings where safe to confirm behaviour.

Interior:
- Infotainment hardware/software: The touchscreen/radio on 3008/5008 models has a reputation for freezes, black screens and slow response. A hard reset (hold PHONE button ~10s) often recovers it, but recurrent failures can require module replacement or re‑programming. Expect intermittent Bluetooth/USB quirks and flaky CarPlay/Android Auto in older software builds. [Carnews.nl (infotainment faults)](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-peugeot/peugeot-5008/solving-infotainment-faults-in-the-peugeot-5008/), [YouTube reset walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z28vCThwTPI), [Peugeot forums (frozen screen)](https://www.peugeotforums.com/threads/touchscreen-failure-frozen-unresponsive-tsb.347307/page-2)
- Cabin durability / rideshare duty: Materials are generally good quality for a mainstream European car but repeated heavy‑duty use (rideshare/taxi) will expose scuffing on door sills, seat bolsters and rear‑seat sliding/locking hardware. Check third‑row seats for stiffness of sliders and locking latches — owners report wear on moving seats. [Carbuyer interior review](https://www.carbuyer.co.uk/peugeot/5008/interior)
- Ergonomics & access: The 5008 is built to carry people — door opening angles and sill heights are family‑friendly, but third row access requires folding the middle row (typical of class). Inspect door‐check stops for worn clicks and check tailgate struts. [Carbuyer interior](https://www.carbuyer.co.uk/peugeot/5008/interior)
- Electrical behaviour & Start‑Stop: Owner aggregated reports include Start‑Stop failures and general electrical/display faults; 12V drain problems aren’t a universal consensus in the snippets, but owners should check the battery state and ask the dealer to prove a latent parasitic draw is absent during multi‑day park tests. [Autodoc common problems](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-peugeot-5008)

Mechanically:
- Brand engineering reputation: Peugeot/Stellantis parts are well‑engineered in many respects, but the company’s turbo‑petrol and small diesel units have had a history of timing chain and DPF/AdBlue issues on some families. Regular servicing and prompt fault diagnosis makes a huge difference to ownership cost. [What‑Breaks catalogue](https://what-breaks.com/peugeot/5008-p87), [Autodoc](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-peugeot-5008)
- Security vulnerabilities: Modern PSA/Peugeot CAN networks can be targeted by "CAN injection" attacks where thieves access exterior wiring to inject CAN messages and impersonate keys. This isn’t Peugeot‑only — it’s a broad industry vulnerability — but some Peugeot models are vulnerable to relay/key‑cloning and CAN attacks. Aftermarket electronic immobilisers (e.g., Autowatch Ghost 2) are proven countermeasures to stop key cloning and CAN injection spoofing. Consider mechanical locks (wheel clamp) plus an aftermarket CAN‑secure immobiliser if you live in a high‑risk area. [CAN Injection analysis](https://can-cia.org/fileadmin/cia/documents/publications/cnlm/june_2023/cnlm_23-2_p20_the_can_injection_attack_ken_tindel_canis_automotive_labs.pdf), [Autowatch Ghost 2 product page](https://www.carsoundsecurity.com/product/ghost-2-can-bus-immobiliser-by-autowatch-for-peugeot-fully-fitted/)

Engines:
- Note: below are the engine variants discussed in owner reports and technical write‑ups. Each engine subsection is italicised as requested and the major engine families/code names are bolded.

  - * **1.6 PureTech / EP6 / EP6DT (turbo petrol, ~180 PS)** *
    - Known failure points: premature timing‑chain/tensioner wear causing rattle on cold start and (in extreme) catastrophic valve/piston damage if ignored; higher oil consumption and carbon build‑up possible on direct‑injection turbo variants. [EngineDNA on EP6 family](https://enginedna.com/psa-bmw-1.6-ep6-ep6dt-n12-16-engine-problems-and-faults/), [Carnews timing chain summary](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-peugeot/peugeot-5008/timing-chain-defects-in-the-peugeot-5008/)
    - Preventative maintenance: insist on service records showing regular oil changes (high‑quality synthetic oil) and ask for camchain tensioner inspection if the vehicle is noisy at cold start. Arrange pre‑purchase compression and cam‑timing checks if >80,000 km. Given known risk, treat this engine as higher‑risk unless impeccably maintained.
  - * **1.2 PureTech (three‑cylinder, ~130 PS, later mild‑hybrid support)** *
    - Known failure points: fewer catastrophic timing‑chain headlines than the EP6, but watch for turbo/intercooler oil leaks, intake tract soot and classic small‑turbo wear if mostly short trips. Later small PureTech units benefit from mild‑hybrid updates which reduce load cycles. [Carbuyer engine summary](https://www.carbuyer.co.uk/peugeot/5008/156241/peugeot-5008-review-a-capable-and-frugal-7-seater/engines)
    - Preventative maintenance: rigorous oil changes (every 10k–15k km) and periodic induction cleaning if you do lots of city miles. Confirm service intervals in the vehicle handbook and logbook. [Peugeot handbook (service info)](https://public.servicebox.peugeot.com/APddb/modeles/5008/eGuide_5008_5008n_ed01-22/pdfs/9999_9999_441_en-GB.pdf)
  - * **1.5 BlueHDi (DV5 series diesel, ~130 PS)** *
    - Known failure points: camshaft timing / chain concerns were serious enough that Stellantis introduced extended coverage for earlier 1.5 BlueHDi engines built Oct 2017–Jan 2023 to address premature camshaft timing‑chain wear; DPF clogging, EGR and AdBlue/SCR faults show up if the car is used on many short trips. [Stellantis press on extended coverage](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/peugeot/press/stellantis-extends-special-coverage-for-1-5-bluehdi-engines-to-10-years-or-150-000-miles-and-introduces-retroactive-reimbursement-for-previous-issues-for-european-consumers), [Autoricambitritella DPF/EGR notes](https://autoricambitritella.it/en/blogs/problematiche-e-guasti-delle-vetture/motore-1-5-bluehdi-problemi-comuni-soluzioni)
    - Preventative maintenance: the diesel anti‑pollution system needs occasional motorway runs to complete regeneration cycles; check for forced regen history, AdBlue injection and DPF records. Confirm whether the specific VIN is covered by the Stellantis special program.
  - * **2.0 BlueHDi (DW10 series diesel, ~180 PS)** *
    - Known failure points: DPF, EGR and turbocharger wear on heavy urban duty; the 2.0s are robust when used correctly but expensive to fix if an EGR/DPF/AdBlue system goes bad. [What‑Breaks engine catalog](https://what-breaks.com/peugeot/5008-p87)
    - Preventative maintenance: as per 1.5 BlueHDi — regular long runs, AdBlue refills, and periodic servicing of DPF/EGR components. Dealer service history is vital.

Transmission:
- The 5008 uses Aisin torque‑converter automatic boxes badged as EAT6/EAT8 (depending on spec and year). They are mechanically robust but not invincible — common user problems revolve around aged ATF, software calibration/TCU updates and torque‑converter shudder or low‑speed jerks. Symptoms: harsh low‑speed shifts, shudder on lock‑up, occasional slipping or limp‑home mode. [ProPeugeotSpares / ASR guides](https://www.propeugeotspares.co.za/blog/peugeot-eat6-eat8-transmission-guide/), [ASR gearbox repairs](https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/eat6-eat8-aisin-psa-group/)
- What to check on a used car:
  - Insist on a clear service record showing ATF changes where recommended. If there’s no evidence, budget for a fluid and filter service (and software/TCU reflash) — cheaper than a gearbox rebuild.
  - Road test across urban, suburban and highway speeds. Look for firm, predictable shifts and an absence of torque‑converter shudder during slow pulls.

Pricing and where the market sits:
- You're looking at roughly AUD $18.7k–$29k in NSW for 2018–2022 cars, with the market medians sitting around AUD $23.8k and ~71,000 km — realistic numbers for a seven‑seat European family SUV. (NSW dealer snapshot above.)
- Depreciation posture: the 5008 tends to depreciate faster than many mainstream rivals; usedcarstats lists the model as losing value faster than most cars (it ranks better than only 27% of models on three‑year retention). That means a steep early drop but also potential bargain buys for patient buyers. [UsedCarStats depreciation summary](https://usedcarstats.co/cars/peugeot-5008)

Depreciation metrics (practical interpretation):
- Expect sharper-than‑average value loss in the first 3 years versus Japanese/Korean rivals (Hyundai/Kia retain better), and roughly average running costs once the purchase is made. Use online depreciation calculators for personalised numbers, but treat the 5008 as a value pick if you want premium looks for less money later in the ownership cycle. [TheMoneyCalculator depreciation tool / Parkers for valuations](https://www.themoneycalculator.com/vehicle-finance/calculators/car-depreciation-by-make-and-model/PEUGEOT/5008/), [Parkers used prices](https://www.parkers.co.uk/peugeot/5008/used-prices/)

Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy — hybrid vs conventional:
- Official WLTP figures for the newer hybrid 5008 claim combined consumption figures as low as ~5.8 L/100 km for some hybrid derivatives, and PHEVs claim substantial electric ranges (c. 48 miles / ~77 km WLTP for big PHEV versions on some territory specs). But those numbers only translate into savings if you actually charge the PHEV and run many short, urban trips on battery. [Peugeot hybrid WLTP page](https://www.peugeot.ie/our-models/new-peugeot-5008/hybrid.html), [Autocar on PHEV range and real‑world caveats](https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-review/peugeot/5008)
- Practical Aussie maths: if you do <20 km/day local runs and can charge at home/work, a PHEV can cut fuel significantly — but payback depends on the premium you pay for the PHEV used example. Use calculators (e.g., MyCarCalc or AFDC’s vehicle cost tools) and plug in your km/year, local electricity (AUD/kWh) and petrol prices to model break‑even. [MyCarCalc hybrid vs gas calculator](https://mycarcalc.com/fuel-and-mpg/hybrid-vs-gas-calculator), [AFDC vehicle cost tool](https://afdc.energy.gov/calc/)
- For the P87 used market (2018–2022), pure PHEV examples are rare; you’ll mostly find petrol and diesel autos. Given the premium and technical complexity of PHEVs, they’re only worth the extra if you can document consistent charging and can verify battery capacity/condition on a test charge.

Buying recommendation — who should and shouldn’t buy:
- Buy one if:
  - You want seven seats in a stylish, relatively compact footprint and don’t need a burly tow vehicle.
  - You’re prepared to be picky about service history and to do a specialist pre‑purchase check (infotainment/ADAS diagnostics, engine timing chain check for petrols, DPF history for diesels, gearbox fluid/service history).
  - You like the cabin styling and tech features and want a near‑premium look for less money.
- Don’t buy one if:
  - You want a worry‑free, low‑maintenance family wagon and you don’t want to manage software/engine quirks — consider Kodiaq or Sorento for lower long‑term risk and better value retention.
  - You can’t verify maintenance history — the 1.6 PureTech / EP6 family and some diesels have specific expensive failure modes if neglected.

Best engine/trim for a used‑buy (practical sweet spot):
- If you can find a well‑serviced **1.5 BlueHDi** example with confirmed coverage/repairs and full logbook, that’s a sensible choice for longevity and lower chance of the notorious 1.6 timing‑chain drama. The Stellantis special coverage is a strong safety net on early 1.5 diesels — ask the selling dealer to confirm VIN eligibility and documented remedial work. [Stellantis press (1.5 BlueHDi coverage)](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/peugeot/press/stellantis-extends-special-coverage-for-1-5-bluehdi-engines-to-10-years-or-150-000-miles-and-introduces-retroactive-reimbursement-for-previous-issues-for-european-consumers)
- If you prefer petrol, favour later upgrades of the small **1.2 PureTech** (with mild‑hybrid updates if available) over the older **1.6 PureTech EP6** unless you’ve got perfect service history proving early timing‑chain attention.
- Trim: mid‑range Allure / Allure Premium is the sweet spot — good tech and spec without the dealer‑premium of GT/GT‑Premium.

Buying checklist (pre‑purchase; insist these are verified)
- Full stamped service history and VIN recall check via Peugeot dealer. [Peugeot recall tool](https://www.peugeot.co.uk/tools/recall-campaigns.html)
- Confirm gearbox service history (ATF changes) and ask for TCU software reflash history if available. Road‑test for smooth shifts, no shudder or low‑speed jerk.
- PETROL: detailed cam‑chain/tensioner inspection for EP6/1.6 variants; listen for cold‑start rattle. [EngineDNA / Carnews timing‑chain articles](https://enginedna.com/psa-bmw-1.6-ep6-ep6dt-n12-16-engine-problems-and-faults/), [Carnews timing chain summary](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-peugeot/peugeot-5008/timing-chain-defects-in-the-peugeot-5008/)
- DIESEL: DPF/AdBlue/regen history; confirm Stellantis coverage eligibility on 1.5 BlueHDi. [Stellantis press](https://www.media.stellantis.com/uk-en/peugeot/press/stellantis-extends-special-coverage-for-1-5-bluehdi-engines-to-10-years-or-150-000-miles-and-introduces-retroactive-reimbursement-for-previous-issues-for-european-consumers)
- Infotainment: verify screen boots cleanly and that CarPlay/Bluetooth/USB function consistently. Try a forced reboot on‑site. [Carnews / YouTube reset](https://carnews.nl/en/car-problems/car-problems-peugeot/peugeot-5008/solving-infotainment-faults-in-the-peugeot-5008/), [YouTube reset walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z28vCThwTPI)
- ADAS: check adaptive cruise, AEB and lane assist self‑tests; request dealer ADAS calibration history if any camera/radar work was done. [DVSA recall / ADAS reports](https://product-guru.org/recall/uk_dvsa-r-2025-542-peugeot-5008-v3-vsm-software-missing-aeb-telltale-in-case.html)
- Security: consider fitting an aftermarket CAN‑bus immobiliser (e.g., Ghost 2) if you live in a high‑theft area. [CAN injection background](https://can-cia.org/fileadmin/cia/documents/publications/cnlm/june_2023/cnlm_23-2_p20_the_can_injection_attack_ken_tindel_canis_automotive_labs.pdf), [Autowatch Ghost 2 product page](https://www.carsoundsecurity.com/product/ghost-2-can-bus-immobiliser-by-autowatch-for-peugeot-fully-fitted/)

Final verdict (short and blunt)
- The 5008 is a beautiful family package that looks and feels a class above many rivals — you get seven seats, a proud cabin and European handling. But it’s a pick‑and‑choose car: good for the buyer who will do their homework, insist on service records and get a proper PPI. If you’re lazy about maintenance history, steer toward a more bulletproof rival with better long‑term retention.
- Best buy: a 2019–2021 Allure (or equivalent mid‑grade) with the **1.5 BlueHDi** and clean service history — or a later small‑turbo 1.2 PureTech with confirmed oil/chain attention — priced under the NSW median of AUD $23,835 and with less than ~80,000 km on the clock.
- If you’re chasing bargains right now, the NSW market snapshot (AUD $18.7k–$29k) shows opportunity — but treat the cheapest examples like salvage: verify repairs, DPF/gearbox history and ADAS function before you sign.
