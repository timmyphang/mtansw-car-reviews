#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: T6 / T6.2 family (global mid‑size Ranger line engineered by Ford Australia). Production timeframe for the modern global Ranger started in 2011 (T6) with continual updates through the 2024 redesign and 2025–2026 technical refreshes covered in Ford spec sheets. See the T6 background and the latest tech specs: [Wikipedia – Ford Ranger (T6)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Ranger_%28T6%29) and Ford’s official 2025/2026 technical PDFs ([2025 Tech Specs PDF](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/content/dam/fordmediasite/us/en/library/2025/specs/2025_Ford_Ranger_Technical_Specifications.pdf), [2026 Tech Specs PDF](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/content/dam/fordmediasite/us/en/library/2026/specs/2026_Ford_Ranger%20Technical_Specs.pdf)).

- Product hierarchy:
  - Immediately above the Ranger in Ford’s global pickup portfolio is the full‑size F‑Series (F‑150) family (not a direct mainstream Aussie offering but the reference point globally).
  - The closest Ford sibling in Australia is the Ford Everest SUV — mechanically related and built from the Ranger’s platform/architecture in many years. The Ranger is Ford’s mid‑size workhorse; above that is the full‑size world of F‑Series; below it there’s no direct smaller ‘Ford’ ute in Australia that competes on the same scale (Maverick isn’t a mainstream Aussie player). See platform lineage: [Wikipedia – Ford Ranger (T6)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Ranger_%28T6%29).

- Shared global platform architecture and parts‑availability advantages:
  - The Ranger is a globally engineered platform (T6/T6.2) so parts and knowledge are widely available across markets — commonality improves aftermarket support and parts supply. The Everest shares the base architecture; many running gear and body components are common or closely related ([The Ranger Station specs](https://www.therangerstation.com/articles/ford-ranger-specifications/) summarises cross‑market specs and parts commonality).

- Market‑specific engine, power and drivetrain line-up (Australia‑relevant summary from global spec releases and contemporary coverage):
  - Petrol turbos:
    - **2.3L EcoBoost inline‑4** — ~270 hp / 310 lb‑ft class (market figures vary by tune and model). Paired with a 10‑speed automatic; RWD standard with optional 4x4 on many trims ([CarWeek 2024 specs overview](https://www.carweek.com/research/ford/ranger/2024/specs), [Ford 2025 tech PDF](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/content/dam/fordmediasite/us/en/library/2025/specs/2025_Ford_Ranger_Technical_Specifications.pdf)).
    - **2.7L EcoBoost V6** — available on higher trims and towing‑oriented variants; also mated to the 10‑speed automatic ([EngineNeeds / SpecsData coverage of 2024 lineup](https://engineneeds.com/2024-ford-ranger-specifications/), https://specsdata.com/2024-ranger-specs/).
    - **3.0L units** appear in performance/Raptor variants in some markets (Raptor uses higher‑output V6 on top trims: see trim coverage) ([Edmunds trims summary](https://www.edmunds.com/ford/ranger/2025/trims/)).
  - Diesel and older options (Australia historically and export markets):
    - **3.2L Duratorq (P5AT)** — Euro/ANZ markets historically used the 3.2 TDCi; known in older model years (2011–2018 era and some used examples) — see known failure patterns below ([EngineFinder 3.2 problems](https://www.enginefinder.co.za/blog/problems/ford-ranger-3-2-tdci-engine-problems/), [Unsealed4x4 Australia guide](https://unsealed4x4.com.au/ford-ranger-3-2l-common-problems-and-solutions/)).
    - 2.0/2.2 diesel Duratorq/ EcoBlue variants appeared in various markets (details vary by year).
  - Transmission and drive:
    - Modern Ranger models (2024–2026) use the 10‑speed automatic across petrol options; manual options are rare in the current Aussie market. Ford docs list the 10‑speed SelectShift automatic as standard on many models ([Ford 2025/2026 PDFs](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/content/dam/fordmediasite/us/en/library/2025/specs/2025_Ford_Ranger_Technical_Specifications.pdf), [Ford Authority powertrain summary](https://fordauthority.com/2026/02/2026-ford-ranger-all-available-powertrains/)).
    - Drive configurations: RWD standard, selectable 4x4 on most higher‑spec or optional configurations; electronic locking differentials on specialised off‑road variants.

- Marketplace rivals (local mid‑size ute segment):
  - Toyota HiLux / Tacoma (where available), Mazda BT‑50, Nissan Navara / Frontier, Mitsubishi Triton, Isuzu D‑MAX, Chevrolet Colorado / GMC Canyon (import competition for some buyers). Useful comparisons can be found at The Car Connection and U.S. media comparisons ([The Car Connection competitor list](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/ford_ranger_choices), [MotorTrend head‑to‑head](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/2024-toyota-tacoma-vs-ford-ranger-chevrolet-colorado-midsize-pickup-truck-comparison-test-review)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Ford Ranger?
This is the meat. If you buy a Ranger used in Australia you want a realistic shopping list — what will bite you, what’s an easy fix, and what’s a lottery ticket. I’ve split the faults into Exterior, Interior, Mechanical, Engines (by code/variant) and Transmission.

- Exterior:
  - Panel fitment and trim:
    - Expect minor panel gaps or misaligned bumper‑to‑fender gaps on some builds. Owner forums and community threads regularly show front bumper gaps that can be corrected at modest cost (re‑positioning bolts or replacing clips) — not catastrophic, but a sign production tolerances can vary [Ranger Station forum thread re: bumper gap](https://www.therangerstation.com/forums/threads/front-bumper-gap.212543/), [Ranger5G forum on panel gaps](https://www.ranger5g.com/forum/threads/ranger-panel-gap-bumper-to-fender.11163/).
  - Paint / clear‑coat issues:
    - There are documented reports, especially on some newer gen units (notably 2023–2024 examples), of clearcoat peeling and flaking. These can be expensive if widespread — owners have reported full‑truck respray bills in the many‑thousand‑dollars range. If you see paint bubbling/peeling on a used Ranger, push the dealer for a claim history or shop it thoroughly; tiny chips are normal, widespread peeling is not ([Ranger5G thread on peeling paint](https://www.ranger5g.com/forum/threads/anyone-having-peeling-paint-issues-with-their-gen5-ranger-particularly-2023.39395/), general repair guidance: [Motorist Care clear coat primer](https://motoristcare.com/how-to-fix-peeling-clear-coat/)).
  - Exterior trim fragility:
    - Gloss black trims and polymer covers can scratch, warp or loosen (common on 2019–2025 models in various owner reports). Replacement parts can be model‑specific and pricey ([Go‑Parts external trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-ford-ranger-ford-bronco-ford-explorer-2019-2025)).
  - ADAS hardware sensitivity & recourse:
    - Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC) and radar‑based systems are sensitive to blocked sensor mounting (even aftermarket number‑plate brackets or bullbars can obscure sensors), and bumped/damaged sensors (e.g., from bullbar fitment or impacts) commonly lead to ACC or pre‑collision warnings. There have been official recalls and dealer software remedies for braking/EBB and ADAS‑related matters from Ford (see recall coverage below). Owners commonly report “ACC sensor blocked” messages and intermittent disabling of pre‑collision functions — sometimes resolved by repositioning or dealer recalibration ([Ranger6G ACC sensor blocked thread](https://www.ranger6g.com/forum/threads/acc-sensor-blocked.11350/), [The Ranger Station recall summary](https://www.therangerstation.com/ford-ranger-recalls-2024-and-2025-models/)).
    - Important recalls/bugs: multiple model years of the Ranger have had recall/service campaigns relating to brakes and EBB modules which affect brake assist and ADAS behaviour; remedies have included dealer software updates or hardware fixes. See the BRAKE report on the large 2025 Ford EBB recall and NHTSA/Ford recall lookup for VIN‑specific checks ([The BRAKE Report on 2025 recall](https://thebrakereport.com/2025-ford-brake-issue-prompts-recall/), Ford recall page: [Ford recall lookup](https://www.ford.com/support/how-tos/recall/recalls-and-faqs/is-there-a-recall-on-my-vehicle/)).

- Interior:
  - Cabin durability under heavy use (rideshare/towing/work):
    - Door panels and interior trims can rattle over time if used hard (work truck duty, heavy toolboxes). Door‑panel rattles and trim fit issues are frequently raised in DIY guides and parts notes for 2019–2025 Rangers ([Go‑Parts on door panel rattles](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/door-interior-trim-panel-ford-ranger-2019-2025-2)).
    - Entry/exit ergonomics: tall sill and high step‑in are typical ute behaviour — good for load defence but not ideal for arthritic knees. Check door check angles and scuff wear on used examples.
  - Electrical and infotainment:
    - Infotainment/glitches are a recurring theme, especially with recent SYNC versions. SYNC freezes, blank screens and camera‑display failures have prompted large recalls on Ford vehicles (reports note a major Ford recall over blank/freezing screens across many 2021–2024 models) — freezes can disable radio and camera displays and so affect reversing safety. Factory wireless charging pads and USB/wireless modules have intermittent faults reported by owners; in some 2024–2025 Rangers the wireless charger barely charges or doesn't recognise phones ([Ford SYNC troubleshooting](https://www.ford.com/support/how-tos/sync/troubleshooting/why-is-sync-3-showing-a-blank-screen/), [TFLcar reporting on infotainment recall](https://tflcar.com/2025/05/ford-major-recall-screen-freezes-missing-images-news/), owner‑forum posts on 12" SYNC freeze: [Ranger6G SYNC 12" freeze](https://www.ranger6g.com/forum/threads/sync-12-screen-freezing-up-fixing-in-progress.7588/)).
    - Practical test on inspection: check Apple CarPlay/Android Auto stability, test rear camera under night and day, test wireless charging and all USB ports. If any functions are flaky, verify dealer service history for software updates or recall remedies.
  - 12‑volt battery and phantom drains:
    - With modern electronics and telematics a weak 12V battery will manifest as sleeping drains, central locking/disconnects and failed boot cycles. No single official dataset in the search results, but SYNC and companion modules are a common source of parasitic draws if not updated; always verify battery age and load tests on used vehicles.

- Mechanically:
  - General engineering reputation:
    - Long‑term Ford engineering is competent but the Ranger has had recurring problem clusters depending on engine and transmission choices — some model years are better than others. Community and technical service bulletin data shows a history of TSBs for the Ranger across 2019–2025 ([TheRangerStation TSB index](https://www.therangerstation.com/tsb/)).
  - Security vulnerabilities (keyless/CAN bus threats):
    - Like many modern vehicles, Rangers are susceptible to advanced theft techniques such as CAN injection attacks when physical wiring is exposed (e.g., via headlamp/bullbar removal) — criminals have used CAN injectors to spoof immobiliser messages and start vehicles without keys. Practical mitigations include mechanical steering‑wheel locks, aftermarket immobilisers, and checking that dealer‑fitted wiring for accessories is sealed and not left with exposed CAN access points. See technical coverage of the CAN injection technique and mitigation recommendations ([VicOne CAN bus writeup](https://vicone.com/blog/how-to-get-away-with-car-theft-unveiling-the-dark-side-of-the-can-bus/), [CAN‑CIA paper on injection attack](https://www.can-cia.org/fileadmin/cia/documents/publications/cnlm/june_2023/cnlm_23-2_p20_the_can_injection_attack_ken_tindel_canis_automotive_labs.pdf), SecurityWeek reporting on the theft technique: https://www.securityweek.com/thieves-use-can-injection-hack-to-steal-cars/).
  - Aftermarket anti‑theft measures (practical):
    - Fit a visible mechanical deterrent (club, steering lock).
    - Install an independent immobiliser with a separate kill switch for fuel or starter.
    - Have a professional inspect any aftermarket wiring in the headlamp/bumper area; seal unused connectors and add conduit to make CAN access physically difficult.
    - Consider telematics with secure alerts and GPS tracking.

- Engines:
  - I’ll italicise each engine/variant and detail the common failures, with recommended preventative service cadence (note: Ford Australia service intervals are frequently 10,000 km for modern Ranger ownership patterns — align maintenance accordingly and follow the owner manual).

  - *2.3L EcoBoost (petrol, turbocharged inline‑4)*  
    - Failure points & symptoms:
      - Overheating and coolant leaks reported in owner summaries; potential head‑gasket related symptoms and coolant system issues have been documented in owner reports and legal summaries ([8020automotive on 2.3L EcoBoost issues](https://8020automotive.com/ford-ranger-2-3l-ecoboost-reliability-common-problems/), [Lemon Law firm notes on 2.3 issues](https://lemonlawfirm.com/ford-2-3-liter-ecoboost-problems-how-to-fix-them/)).
      - Carbon build‑up, turbo sensitivity and related misfire or roughness symptoms are a recurring theme on direct‑inject turbos ([CherishYourCar Ranger engine problem roundup](https://www.cherishyourcar.com/ford-ranger-engine-problems/)).
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Oil and filter: change every 10,000 km (strongly recommended for turbo longevity).
      - Coolant system inspection at 10,000 km intervals — pressure test hoses, inspect for external leaks and thermostat behaviour.
      - Intake & spark checks: service every 20,000–40,000 km depending on driving pattern; carbon induction cleaning if you see misfire/fouling.
      - Turbo oil feed & return inspection during major services — turbomachinery fails harder when oil maintenance is ignored.

  - *2.7L EcoBoost V6 (petrol, V6 turbo on higher trims)*  
    - Failure points & symptoms:
      - Less commonly complained about in the search results than the 2.3, but turbocharged V6 duty requires strict oil/coolant maintenance. Expect higher fuel use but stronger torque and towing ability (source: engine spec overviews and trim guides) ([EngineNeeds & SpecsData coverage of the 2.7 availability](https://engineneeds.com/2024-ford-ranger-specifications/), https://specsdata.com/2024-ranger-specs/).
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Oil every 10,000 km with premium spec oil; check turbo and intercooler pipework regularly if used for towing.
      - Brake and cooling system checks before heavy towing.

  - *3.2L TDCi Duratorq (P5AT) diesel — older model years*  
    - Failure points & symptoms:
      - Known for EGR cooler failures, turbocharger wear, injector faults and DPF clogging. “Wet” timing‑belt designs and timing chain/tensioner wear can cause cold‑start rattle and risk of significant failure if not attended to ([EngineFinder 3.2 problems](https://www.enginefinder.co.za/blog/problems/ford-ranger-3-2-tdci-engine-problems/), [Unsealed4x4 Australia guide](https://unsealed4x4.com.au/ford-ranger-3-2l-common-problems-and-solutions/)).
      - DPF clogging is common in work vehicles if lots of short trips or towing without occasional long runs — regeneration failures lead to expensive injector/DPF replacements.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Diesel service every 10,000 km (oil/filter), with DPF and EGR checks every 20–30k kms.
      - If used for towing or heavy duty, schedule forced regeneration runs and monitor turbo/EGR coolant circuits; replace timing components at manufacturer‑recommended intervals or earlier if noise/tensioner issues appear.

  - *Other Duratorq diesel variants (2.0 / 2.2 / 2.0 EcoBlue depending on year/market)*  
    - Failure points & symptoms:
      - EGR and DPF sensitivity, turbocharger wear, and injector seals. Patterns vary by market and tune; older units may show oil leaks and injector‑related misfires.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - As above: strict 10k oil service, DPF/EGR inspection, and follow tow‑use schedules.

- Transmission:
  - The modern Ranger — especially petrol variants — runs a 10‑speed automatic (10R80 family in many US/ANZ applications). Known failure modes across multiple Ranger model years:
    - Harsh/delayed engagements, shudder, whine noises and occasional failures of remanufactured 10R80 units have been documented and attracted NHTSA attention and service bulletins. Some remanufactured 10R80 components in earlier builds were part of advance notices requiring inspection/replacement ([NHTSA reman 10R80 doc](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2025/RCMN-25E070-3531.pdf), technical writeups: [TransmissionKnowledge 10‑speed problems](https://transmissionknowledge.com/ford/ford-ranger-10-speed-transmission-problems-explained/), [TransmissionAudit analysis of 2019–2023](https://transmissionaudit.com/cars/ford/ranger-2019-2023/)).
    - Early life failures reported by owners: some 2024+ owners reported major transmission problems within a few thousand kilometres in forum threads — not universal, but clusters exist ([Ranger6G thread on major transmission problems at 5,000 miles](https://www.ranger6g.com/forum/threads/major-transmission-problems-at-5-000-miles.22780/)).
  - Practical inspection checks for a used Ranger:
    - Cold and hot drive test: note any shuddering, slipping, delayed engagement from park/neutral, whining or unusual vibration on acceleration.
    - Check TSB/service history for transmission software updates and fluid service. TheRangerStation aggregates many TSBs and SSMs you should reference before purchase ([TheRangerStation TSB index](https://www.therangerstation.com/tsb/)).
  - Preventative/service:
    - Transmission fluid changes per dealer recommendations (if your unit is heavy towing, change intervals should be shortened). If the vehicle has a history of 10R80 work, get independent verification of parts used (reman vs factory overhaul) and warranty on the repair.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Ford Ranger?
Short answer first: yes — if you pick the right year/trim and do your homework. The Ranger is a versatile mid‑size ute that remains popular in Australia; but buy one without checking the specific problem clusters and you’ll be the one paying for the warranty work the last owner avoided.

- NSW dealer market snapshot (live):
  - Active listings scraped: 327
  - Price range in NSW dealer market: AUD $0 – $114,990
  - Median price: AUD $56,990
  - Year range: 2011 – 2026
  - Median odometer: 24,506 km
  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 318, Manual: 4  
  This is your market context — plenty of examples, automatic‑dominant, and a healthy spread from older 2011 units through to nearly‑new 2026 stock (data supplied by the MTA NSW snapshot you provided).

- Entry price to ceiling price:
  - The live sample shows median AUD $56,990 with a ceiling of AUD $114,990. Expect older 2011–2015 workhorses under $30k and recent‑model Lariat/Raptor examples north of $80k depending on spec and kilometres.

- Depreciation metrics:
  - Hard numbers vary by trim and powertrain. Use the median price (AUD $56,990) and the model’s high popularity to expect depreciation roughly in line with the mid‑size‑ute segment: decent residuals but not Toyota HiLux levels (Tacoma/HiLux historically outperform in resale in Australia). Sales context: Toyota Tacoma still leads midsize pickup deliveries into 2026 in various market summaries, which pressures Ranger resale a touch ([AutoEvolution H1 2026 sales report](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/h1-2026-sales-report-toyota-tacoma-crowned-king-of-the-mid-size-pickup-trucks-once-again-272562.html)). For a concrete strategy, assume a conservative 3‑year depreciation similar to segment average (work the dealer numbers; use KBB/Edmunds cost‑to‑own tools for US proxies then adjust for AU market specifics): see KBB/Edmunds cost to own references ([KBB Cost-to‑Own 2025 Ranger](https://www.kbb.com/ford/ranger/2025/cost-to-own/), [Edmunds 2024 TCO](https://www.edmunds.com/ford/ranger/2024/cost-to-own/)).

- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy:
  - Don’t just look at purchase price — factor in:
    - Fuel: petrol EcoBoost numbers (EPA lab figures) show combined consumption in the mid‑to‑high teens L/100km for 2.3/2.7 petrols in mixed duty; PHEV variants promise WLTP figures in the low single digits when charged ([FuelEconomy.gov 2025 Ranger page](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/bymodel/2025_Ford_Ranger.shtml), Ford PHEV WLTP claims and owner impressions: [FromTheRoad Ford PHEV writeup](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/eur/en/articles/2025/what-ranger-owners-think-of-the-new-ranger-plug-in-hybrid), [4x4Australia PHEV review](https://www.4x4australia.com.au/reviews/2025-ford-ranger-phev-review)). In real Aussie conditions expect the PHEV to be excellent for short urban runs (if you charge regularly) and return major fuel savings vs petrol over typical suburban km.
    - Servicing & consumables: use the 10,000 km service rhythm as your baseline — plan for brake pads/tyres if used tough and DPF/EGR servicing for diesels.
    - Repairs for known clusters: if buying a 2019–2021 with a reman 10R80 inspection history, budget for potential transmission work unless the vehicle has documented fixes/warranties. If buying 2024+ check for SYNC and ADAS recall/service history.
    - Insurance & rego: midsize utes attract moderate insurance; premium models (Raptor/Lariat) cost more.
  - Hybrid vs petrol TCO calculus:
    - PHEV Ranger: Ford claims WLTP ~3.1–3.2 L/100 km and ~43 km EV range in some markets; that translates to real savings if you do lots of short daily runs and can charge. However, the PHEV premium at purchase must be amortised — check local pricing and weigh against fuel savings and potential battery/service costs. See Ford’s PHEV claims and a hands‑on PHEV review for nuanced trade‑offs ([Ford PHEV owner impressions](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/eur/en/articles/2025/what-ranger-owners-think-of-the-new-ranger-plug-in-hybrid), [4x4Australia PHEV review](https://www.4x4australia.com.au/reviews/2025-ford-ranger-phev-review)). If your weekly kms are high and you tow often, fuel savings from a PHEV may be limited unless you can cruise on electric for many short trips.
  - Practical TCO calculation method:
    1. Get the exact local price for the model you want (use the NSW median as a sanity check).
    2. Estimate annual running km and split into town/highway.
    3. Use realistic consumption figures for your powertrain choice (owner reports and official figures).
    4. Add scheduled service/suspension/tyres and an allowance for known repair clusters (e.g., add a small annual “risk” allowance if the vehicle is from a problem cohort).
    5. Compare net cost vs rivals (HiLux/Navaras) to see whether Ranger’s features justify any price premium.

- Buying recommendation — which years/trims and what to avoid:
  - Avoid: completely avoid blind buys of 2024–2026 near‑new Rangers with unresolved SYNC or ADAS complaints unless the dealer can show the specific VIN has had all relevant software recalls and dealer fixes performed. Recent model years have more software/ADAS/infotainment headaches out of the box (SYNC screens, camera/display and ADAS service campaigns exist) ([TFLcar infotainment recall coverage](https://tflcar.com/2025/05/ford-major-recall-screen-freezes-missing-images-news/), [Ranger6G SYNC freeze threads](https://www.ranger6g.com/forum/threads/sync-12-screen-freezing-up-fixing-in-progress.7588/)).
  - Avoid: 2019–2021 examples without a clear transmission service history or TSB work documented for the 10R80 reman issues — these years had clusters of transmission complaints and were the focus of NHTSA/TSB attention ([NHTSA reman 10R80 doc](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2025/RCMN-25E070-3531.pdf), [TransmissionAudit review](https://transmissionaudit.com/cars/ford/ranger-2019-2023/)).
  - Sweet spot to target:
    - Late 2021–2023 Ranger XLT / Wildtrak (or XLT with dealer‑installed towing packages) represents a practical sweet spot if you can verify documented TSB fixes and transmission checks. These give modern comfort and safety without the very earliest 10R80 reman‑concern cluster and before the 2024 SYNC‑era software upheaval. Look for examples with full dealer service history, explicit TSB completions and a recent transmission scan.
  - Trim choice:
    - Midrange XLT or Lariat balance equipment and TCO; Raptors are fantastic but cost a premium and potential mechanical repair bills scale with capability. Edmunds and MotorTrend recommend XLT as a value pick in comparative trim guides ([Edmunds trims overview](https://www.edmunds.com/ford/ranger/2025/trims/), [MotorTrend trim breakdown](https://www.motortrend.com/features/2024-ford-ranger-trim-levels)).

- Final bottom line (plain talk):
  - The Ranger is a competent, capable mid‑size ute with decent parts availability across Australia and a broad dealer network. Buy smart: inspect the vehicle for the specific fault clusters listed above (infotainment/ADAS history for post‑2023 units; transmission history for 2019–2021/early‑10R80 era; paint/clearcoat on some 2023 examples). If the dealer provides paperwork that shows TSBs/recalls were done and the transmission has a clean service/scan history, a Ranger is a fine buy. If the service history is thin or the seller shrugs about “we didn’t have any problems”, walk away or knock the price down and get an independent pre‑purchase inspection.
