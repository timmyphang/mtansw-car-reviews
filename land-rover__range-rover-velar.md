#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation, production timeframe & internal codes  
  The Range Rover Velar (L560) launched in 2017 and remains in production through the facelifted mid‑cycle updates (2017–present). It's widely referred to by Jaguar Land Rover’s platform code L560 and was built on the D7 aluminium architecture (D7a / iQ[Al]/PLA). [Wikipedia — JLR platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Land_Rover_car_platforms)

- Product hierarchy (what sits above and below)  
  - Sits between the Range Rover Evoque (smaller/cuter) and Range Rover Sport (bigger/brutish). Introduced as the fourth member of the Range Rover family to bridge style and utility. [Land Rover press release (2017)](https://media.landrover.com/en-us/news/2017/03/land-rover-introduces-fourth-member-range-rover-family-range-rover-velar)

- Shared global platform & models sharing it (parts availability)  
  - Built on the D7a aluminium platform shared across JLR products, which improves parts commonality with Jaguar F‑PACE and some Range Rover cousins. This helps when sourcing suspension bits, some body panels and electronic components — but don’t assume everything is plug‑and‑play. [Wikipedia — JLR platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Land_Rover_car_platforms)

- Market-specific engines, outputs, transmissions & drivetrains (Aussie market flavours you’ll see used)  
  - Petrol:
    - **2.0‑litre Ingenium** inline‑4 (badged P250 in some markets) — ~180–190 kW (varies by tune), paired to an 8‑speed automatic; AWD available. [RangeRover models & specs](https://www.rangerover.com/en-us/range-rover-velar/models-and-specifications.html)  
    - **3.0‑litre V6** and **I6** mild‑hybrid options (P400/P400‑equivalent) in higher trims — stronger output, still mated to the 8‑speed automatic; AWD. [Edmunds / MotorTrend summaries](https://www.edmunds.com/land-rover/range-rover-velar/2025/features-specs/)
  - Diesel:
    - **2.0‑litre Ingenium diesel** variants (common in earlier Australian imports depending on delivery specs) — turbocharged with DPF; 8‑speed auto; AWD or FWD in some markets. [Land Rover technical PDF (2024)](https://www.landrover.com/content/dam/lrdx/pdfs/xi/wltp/Land-Rover-Range-Rover-Velar-TD-Insert-1L560255000EN01P.pdf)
  - Plug‑in Hybrid:
    - **P400e (plug‑in hybrid)** — petrol engine + electric motor, official combined WLTP figures can be very low (sub‑2 L/100 km in test cycles), EV range claimed c. 30–60 km depending on cycle; charging and battery condition dominate real‑world efficiency. [Land Rover PHEV page](https://www.rangerover.com/en-ch/electric/plug-in-hybrid/range-rover-velar.html) and technical specs [Auto‑Data P400e entry](https://www.auto-data.net/en/land-rover-range-rover-velar-facelift-2023-2.0-p400e-404hp-plug-in-hybrid-awd-automatic-47392)
  - Transmission: 8‑speed automatic across the line (ZF derivative in many JLR cars); AWD is common at higher trims, some base cars may be FWD depending on market. [RangeRover models & specs](https://www.rangerover.com/en-us/range-rover-velar/models-and-specifications.html)

- Marketplace rivals in Australia  
  - Jaguar F‑PACE, Porsche Macan, Volvo XC60, Mercedes‑Benz GLC, Audi Q5 — the lot. The Velar competes on looks and presence, less so on measured reliability and running costs. [The Car Connection / CarsGuide comparisons](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/land-rover_range-rover-velar_choices), [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/audi/q5/vs/land-rover-range-rover-velar)

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Land Rover Range Rover Velar?

- Exterior
  - Panel fit & paint:
    - Expect occasional inconsistent panel gaps and trim fitment niggles — reports of rattles and uneven gaps are common in used examples; inspect door lines and rear quarter joins closely. [Stedman's Garage summary](https://stedmansgarage.co.uk/common-faults/range-rover-velar-common-issues/)
    - Paint and clearcoat problems have turned up in owner reports. Check for bubbling, peeling or touch‑up paintwork, especially around bumpers and wheel arches. [CarComplaints body/paint reports](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Land_Rover/Range_Rover_Velar/body_paint/index.shtml)
  - Trim vulnerability:
    - Gloss‑black exterior trim (B‑pillar covers, rear quarter extensions and other accents) fades, speckles and loses finish in sun‑exposed cars — very common. Expect to replace or refinish panels if you want the Instagram black. [Go‑Parts trim guides](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-land-rover-range-rover-velar-2018-2025), [LandRoverForums thread](https://landroverforums.com/forum/range-rover-velar-52/range-rover-velar-black-exterior-trim-fading-speckling-issue-128495/)
  - Door handles & moving bits:
    - Early Velar models famously had sticky or failing flush‑door handles — verify every handle cycles cleanly, especially on cold or humid days.
  - ADAS & safety system quirks:
    - Reports and TSBs show occasional messages such as “Autonomous Emergency Braking not available” or AEB/ACC temporarily disabled after sleep/wake cycles — a software/ECU sleep state bug in some JLR vehicles. Dealers can often fix via software update but confirm records. [Vehicle Service Pros TSB summary](https://www.vehicleservicepros.com/tsbs/article/53081532/mitchell-1-solving-the-land-rover-parking-brake-problem) and aggregated recall lists show multiple campaigns. [RecallExplained](https://recallexplained.com/land-rover/range-rover-velar/)

- Interior
  - Durability under heavy use:
    - Centre console armrest scuffs, rear interior panel rattles and wear on common touch points show up fast in rideshare/taxi use — the Velar isn’t built for taxi abuse. Check seat foam sag and switchgear wear if it’s been a heavy user. [Go‑Parts console & rear interior guides](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/console-panel-land-rover-range-rover-velar-2018-2026-2)
  - Entry/egress notes:
    - Low roofline and firm door checks give a roomy feel but watch the sill scrapes on kerbside entry in flashy finishes; heavy doors can be awkward for elderly passengers.
  - Electrical & infotainment:
    - The JLR Touch Pro Duo / Pivi infotainment systems have a long list of blank screens, resets and persistent software bugs (frozen screens, loss of CarPlay/Android Auto, USB/wireless charging faults). These can be fixed by software updates or hardware replacement — but dealer visits are often required. [Go‑Parts infotainment failures guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-land-rover-range-rover-velar-jaguar-e-pace-land-rover-defender-2018-2025), [Land Rover software update page](https://www.landroverusa.com/ownership/infotainment-systems/infotainment/software-updates/index.html)
    - There was a class action/settlement concerning defective InControl systems across JLR vehicles — keep an eye on whether the used car had any remediation. [Claim Depot summary](https://www.claimdepot.com/settlements/jaguar-land-rover-defective-incontrol-systems-settlement)
    - TSBs recorded electrical oddities (radio presets clearing, app disappearance after Pivi updates). Expect intermittent 12V drain complaints; ask for a recent battery test and power‑draw check. [AboutAutomobile TSB list](https://www.aboutautomobile.com/Technical-Service-Bulletin/2024/Land-Rover/Range-Rover-Velar/Electrical-System-Software)
  - Connectivity:
    - Owners report Pivi Pro connectivity issues with some SOTA updates — check current update level and connectivity function. [Forum thread on Pivi Pro issue](https://landroverforums.com/forum/range-rover-velar-52/pivi-pro-connectivity-stuck-activating-after-4-4-0-update-my26-velar-128086/)

- Mechanically
  - Brand engineering & reputation:
    - JLR builds desirable, sophisticated metal but historically has struggled with electrical and some drivetrain reliability. Independent reliability indexes and owner reports cluster faults in electrical, engine and suspension systems. [Consumer Reports reliability summary; MotorEasy analysis](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/land-rover/range-rover-velar/2025/reliability/), [MotorEasy reliability page](https://www.motoreasy.com/land-rover-reliability/range-rover-velar)
  - Security vulnerabilities:
    - Real thefts have occurred using CAN‑bus/OBD/CAN injection techniques where thieves access wiring (often via headlight harness) to inject messages that bypass immobilisers — the Velar has been targeted in documented cases. JLR is rolling firmware and hardware countermeasures, but older cars may still be vulnerable. Lock the OBD port, add a hardware immobiliser or tracker, and use Faraday pouches for keys at night. [DIGITPOL case study](https://digitpol-vehiclecrime.com/stolen-and-seized-a-range-rover-velar-stolen-by-can-bus-methods/), [JLR security update](https://www.jlr.com/news/2023/11/jlr-invests-vehicle-security-reduce-thefts), technical background on CAN injection [Ken Tindell writeup](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/)
  - Recommended anti‑theft measures:
    - Faraday pouch for keys, OBD port lock, VIN‑tagged aftermarket tracking (with immobiliser cut or kill‑switch), and confirm any dealer‑applied BCM firmware updates.

- Engines
  - Notes: below each engine variant is italicised and includes observed failure points and recommended service intervals (Australian context: if you’re a high‑use owner, favour 10,000 km oil services; otherwise follow official Land Rover guidance but err on the side of more frequent oil and filter changes).
  - *2.0‑litre Ingenium petrol (badged P250 on many markets)*  
    - Issues: occasional turbocharger wastegate and boost control faults; electrical sensor glitches; some owners report oil‑consumption trends if servicing is neglected.  
    - Failure points: turbo actuator, high‑pressure fuel pump (rare), and timing‑chain tensioner noises on neglected units.  
    - Preventative maintenance: oil & filter every 10,000 km if used hard, otherwise 12–15,000 km with high‑quality synthetic oil. Check cam/timing noise at cold start and scan for misfires. [RangeRover specs](https://www.rangerover.com/en-us/range-rover-velar/models-and-specifications.html)
  - *2.0‑litre Ingenium diesel*  
    - Issues: timing chain failure and related rattles have been reported in the diesel Ingenium family on long‑running units; DPF blocking and EGR soot build‑up in lots of low‑mile, stop‑start urban use. [LR Engine Specialists troubleshooting](https://www.lrenginespecialists.co.uk/blog/troubleshooting-range-rover-velar-engines-common-problems-and-fixes-2/), [LRDirect technical guide](https://www.lrdirect.com/technical-guides/2-0-ingenium-engine-failure)
    - Failure points: timing chain/tensioners, injectors, DPF/EGR (if urban driven), turbocharger seals.  
    - Preventative maintenance: heavy‑use or diesel‑town drivers should run an oil change every 10,000 km and perform occasional high‑speed runs to clear DPF; injector checks at 100–150k km if rough running appears.
  - *3.0‑litre V6 (supercharged / mild‑hybrid I6 variants / P400 family)*  
    - Issues: camshaft/timing chain and associated tensioner issues noted in some V6 applications; expensive head/timing repairs if neglected. [Go‑Parts camshaft timing guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/engine-camshaft-land-rover-range-rover-land-rover-range-rover-sport-land-rover-range-rover-velar-2013-2020)
    - Failure points: timing chain guides/tensioners, camshaft phasers, and occasional oil pressure issues feeding variable valve timing hardware.  
    - Preventative maintenance: strict oil change discipline — 10,000 km for performance V6/I6s; inspect service history for oil viscosity and frequency. [JLR Engines reliability guide](https://jlrengines.co.uk/blog/range-rover-velar-ingenium-reliability-guide/)
  - *P400e plug‑in hybrid (PHEV)*  
    - Issues: battery health and charging system complexity; real‑world fuel economy depends entirely on charging habits. Official combined WLTP figures can be tiny (e.g., ~1.9 L/100 km) but are test‑cycle numbers that assume regular charging. [Auto‑Data P400e specs](https://www.auto-data.net/en/land-rover-range-rover-velar-facelift-2023-2.0-p400e-404hp-plug-in-hybrid-awd-automatic-47392), [Land Rover PHEV page](https://www.rangerover.com/en-ch/electric/plug-in-hybrid/range-rover-velar.html)
    - Failure points: traction battery degradation (rare but expensive), inverter/charger faults, and hybrid cooling components. Verify battery State of Health and ask for evidence of charging routine.  
    - Preventative maintenance: battery diagnostic check, proper software updates, and maintain scheduled services; if you can’t commit to charging every couple of days for regular commutes, the fuel & maintenance benefit vanishes.

- Transmission
  - Behaviour & common faults:
    - The Velar uses an 8‑speed automatic (ZF family derivatives) across the range. Owners report torque‑converter shudder, harsh or delayed shifts and occasional failure modes that lead to limp‑home modes. CarComplaints and owner threads gather such reports; some require transmission module reprogramming or mechanical intervention. [CarComplaints transmission page](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Land_Rover/Range_Rover_Velar/transmission/index.shtml)
  - Known failure modes:
    - Torque converter shudder under load, solenoid pack faults, valve body wear and occasional fluid overheating if towing/abused.  
  - Service:
    - Check for full transmission service history — fluid and filter changes every 60–100k km recommended by independent specialists; if claim history lacks transmission services, budget for fluid/service and possible reprogramming. Dealer TSBs exist; ask for any transmission module programming records. [LandRoverForums TSB threads](https://landroverforums.com/forum/tsb_recall/tsbs/land%2Brover/range%2Brover%2Bvelar/2025)

#### 3. Should you buy a used Land Rover Range Rover Velar?

  - Year range: 2020 (median odometer: 66,410 km)  
  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 1  
  - Source: MTA NSW scraper live snapshot supplied above (dealer listing pool). Use that as your immediate market pulse.

- Price context & depreciation
  - The Velar experiences heavy depreciation compared to German rivals. CarEdge’s historical data shows deep value loss (example: up to ~62% over five years in some datasets), so bargains exist if you’re willing to accept higher running costs. [CarEdge depreciation overview](https://caredge.com/land-rover/range-rover-velar/depreciation/)
  - Typical Australian used listing variance is large (examples from international aggregators show huge spread), so the NSW $54,880 for a 2020 with ~66k km sits in the market’s middle‑to‑upper used range depending on trim and options. See KBB/CarFax/CARFAX listings for broader price context. [KBB / CARFAX listings](https://www.kbb.com/land-rover/range-rover-velar/), [CARFAX used listings](https://www.carfax.com/Used-Land-Rover-Range-Rover-Velar_w9329)

- Depreciation metric (3‑year baseline vs rivals)
  - Expect a sharper initial drop than German premium rivals — Plan on a 3‑year retention worse than an Audi Q5 or Volvo XC60, which generally hold value better. Use a conservative figure: Velar typically loses 40–55% in the first 3–5 years depending on spec and region. [CarEdge comparisons](https://caredge.com/compare/depreciation/sq5-vs-macan-vs-x5-vs-range-rover-velar)

- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy
  - What to include: depreciation (largest), scheduled maintenance, unscheduled repairs (electrical & powertrain), fuel, insurance (luxury SUV premium), tyres and finance. Use local figures for fuel (L/100 km), service intervals and real‑world consumption.
  - Fuel:
    - Manufacturer/WLTP figures for PHEV look attractive but are often optimistic — test figures (P400e) show ~1.9 L/100 km on cycle; real‑world uncharged operation for petrol variants often sits in the mid‑9 to mid‑12 L/100 km range, while heavier I6s can be worse. Owner‑reported consumption on Fuelly shows mixed results — expect higher than advertised. [Auto‑Data P400e WLTP](https://www.auto-data.net/en/land-rover-range-rover-velar-facelift-2023-2.0-p400e-404hp-plug-in-hybrid-awd-automatic-47392), [Fuelly owner data](https://www.fuelly.com/car/land_rover/range_rover_velar)
  - Maintenance & repair risk:
    - Factor in elevated probability of infotainment, electrical and sometimes transmission or engine work vs mainstream rivals. CarEdge’s 5‑year ownership estimate places the Velar’s medium‑to‑high running cost profile and a total ownership figure that can be large. [CarEdge costs](https://caredge.com/land-rover/range-rover-velar/costs)
  - PHEV math vs petrol:
    - If you can reliably charge daily and your commute is short (many trips <50 km daily), a P400e can reduce fuel spend significantly — but you pay more initially and for potential hybrid battery issues down the line. If you do mostly long trips or cannot charge, the PHEV’s complexity rarely pays off. Use real‑world consumption (Fuelly and owner reports) to model TCO instead of WLTP.
  - Insurance & depreciation:
    - Luxury SUVs attract premium insurance; factor that into monthly cost. Depreciation for Velar is large, which helps used buyers but hits new‑car buyers hard.

- Buy / avoid summary and practical checklist
  - Buy if:
    - You prize style and feel over rock‑solid mechanical simplicity.  
    - You find a well‑documented example with full dealer service history, recent software/recall work completed (infotainment & BCM updates), and can verify the 12V battery & hybrid battery health where applicable. The NSW example (2020, 66,410 km @ $54,880) is sensible only if the car has a clean service ledger, no outstanding TSB/recall items, and all infotainment/ADAS updates are done.
  - Avoid if:
    - The car has unresolved infotainment faults, unexplained 12V battery replacements, or no proof of software/TSB remediation. Also avoid high‑mileage diesels with urban service history and no evidence of DPF servicing or injector work.
  - Best model year / trim sweet spot:
    - Financial sweet spot: 2019–2021 mid‑trim P250 petrol (carefully serviced) — cheaper to run than the V6/I6 and less complex than PHEV, but still carries the desirable Range Rover badge and much of the interior flair. Avoid earlier 2017–2018 cars with lots of owner complaints until you can confirm remedial work. For PHEV buyers: late‑facelift P400e with verified battery health and clear charging history is the only PHEV worth a premium.
  - Pre‑purchase checklist (non‑negotiable):
    - Full dealer/service history and evidence of software/TSB/recall work (infotainment, BCM, ADAS patches). [Land Rover recall lookup](https://www.landroverusa.com/ownership/vin-recall.html) (use VIN)
    - Verify infotainment works: CarPlay/Android Auto, wireless charger, all screens, and rotations.  
    - Scan for outstanding codes and perform a deep battery draw test (overnight). Ask for the latest battery SOC (PHEV) or dealer report.  
    - Test drive on varied surfaces: look for transmission slip/shudder, ABS/DSC lights, and ACC/AEB behaviour. [TSB/owner reports for AEB/ACC](https://www.vehicleservicepros.com/tsbs/article/53081532/mitchell-1-solving-the-land-rover-parking-brake-problem)  
    - Confirm door handles and flush mechanisms work reliably, check glossy trim condition and hood/cowl for panel alignment.
    - Consider adding: OBD port lock, tracker, Faraday pouch for keys, and budget a contingency of $5–10k over 3 years for intermittent electrical or transmission repairs depending on age/mileage.

- Final verdict — clear recommendation
  - If you’re buying for style, low‑to‑medium annual kms and don’t mind dealer visits for occasional gremlins: get one — but only with full dealer history and completed software/recall work. The NSW sample 2020 Velar at AUD $54,880 and 66,410 km could be a fair buy if the car ticks the pre‑purchase checklist boxes.  
  - If you’re a high‑mileage commuter, a fleet user or you want cheap headache‑free ownership, steer towards a rival (Volvo XC60, Audi Q5, or lightly used Porsche Macan) — they generally cost less in surprise repairs and have firmer long‑term reliability profiles.  
  - If you want the PHEV because of WLTP numbers: only buy if you can access charging every day and have proof the battery and charging system are healthy; otherwise the hybrid premium rarely returns value.
