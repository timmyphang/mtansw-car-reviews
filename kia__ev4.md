#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

Kia’s EV4 hits Australian showrooms as a 2026 model‑year proposition, landing as the brand’s new electric sedan (and hatch) entry point. It slots below the EV6 and above the soon‑to‑arrive EV3, effectively replacing the role of a combustion‑engine mid‑sizer in Kia’s EV lineup. The EV4 rides on the **400‑volt version of Hyundai Group’s E‑GMP platform** – a cost‑cutting move versus the 800‑V architecture used by the Ioniq 5 and EV6, but shared with the EV3 and the Niro EV. That same modular bones also underpins the Hyundai Kona Electric and, in various forms, the Genesis GV60.

**Australian line‑up (as of early 2026):**

| Variant | Battery | WLTP Range | Power/Torque | 0‑100 km/h |
|---------|---------|------------|--------------|------------|
| Air Standard Range | 58.3 kWh NCM | 456 km | 150 kW / 283 Nm | 7.4 sec |
| Earth Long Range | 81.4 kWh NCM | ~590 km (est.) | 150 kW / 283 Nm | 7.8 sec |
| GT‑Line Long Range | 81.4 kWh NCM | ~590 km (est.) | 150 kW / 283 Nm | 7.8 sec |

All variants are **front‑wheel drive**, single‑motor, with a single‑speed reduction gear. Pricing before on‑roads starts at **$49,990** for the Air SR, rising to **$59,190** for the Earth LR and **$64,690** for the GT‑Line LR ([evee.com.au](https://www.evee.com.au/blog/new-electric-sedans-australia-kia-ev4-vs-mazda-6e-vs-mg-im5/)).

**Direct marketplace rivals:** Tesla Model 3 (RWD, now starts above $55k), Mazda 6e (arriving 2026), MG IM5 (long‑range sedan), and the BYD Seal – plus, at the lower end, the GWM Ora and the Hyundai Kona Electric.

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Kia EV4?

Because the EV4 has barely touched Aussie roads (median odometer in NSW dealer stock is **30 km**), long‑term failure data is non‑existent. What follows is a platform‑based prediction, informed by early press cars and known quirks of the E‑GMP 400‑V family.

##### Exterior

- **Panel alignment:** Early production units observed at international reveals show consistent gaps – Kia’s Korean build quality has been solid on the EV3 and EV6, and the EV4 appears no different. No recalls flagged yet.
- **Trim vulnerability:** The EV4 goes heavy on gloss‑black lower cladding (wheel arches, bumpers). It swirls if you look at it funny. Expect micro‑scratches after a few automated car washes. The “stone‑like” finishes inside are more durable than piano black, but the exterior plastic bits are a mark‑collecting magnet.
- **Paint durability:** Standard Kia palette; metallic finishes hold up well, but solid colours (especially the lighter tones) show chips quickly, particularly on the low hood and leading edge of the ducktail spoiler.
- **ADAS quirks:** The EV4 packs the full suite: adaptive cruise control, lane‑keep assist, automatic emergency braking, rear cross‑traffic alert with braking. Early journo drives report the lane‑keep can be overly aggressive on narrow country roads – a typical Kia trait. It’s not a “firmware fix needed” issue, but worth checking if the dealer has applied any TSB updates. The i‑Pedal 3.0 regen system is smooth and intuitive, even in winter conditions ([plazakiany.com](https://www.plazakiany.com/blog/2025/december/15/cold-weather-questions-staten-island-ny-eco-minded-drivers-ask-about-the-2026-kia-ev4.htm)).

##### Interior

- **Cabin durability:** Kia has used fabric, matte plastics, and a “nifty stone‑like finish” ([theautopian.com](https://www.theautopian.com/im-worried-the-electric-kia-ev4-might-be-too-weird-for-america/)). The fabric wears well, but spills on the door card inserts will stain. Avoid light‑coloured interiors if you’re a coffee spiller.
- **Spatial entry/exit:** The EV4’s low hood and relatively flat door sills make getting in easy, even for taller folk. The sedan’s sloping roofline means rear headroom is tight for those over 183 cm – the hatchback version (not confirmed for Australia) would be better.
- **Infotainment & 12V battery:** A 30‑inch ultrawide screen spans the dashboard – one continuous panel for gauges and infotainment. Early units have glitched on startup (slow software boot), but over‑the‑air updates should squash that. The 12V auxiliary battery can drain if the car is left unplugged for weeks in deep sleep – typical EV behaviour. No specific CAN bus injection exploits reported yet for the EV4, but the 400‑V architecture is less complex than 800‑V systems, which may reduce attack surface. Still, fit a **steering lock** (old school, but effective) and consider an OBD‑port lock if you park on the street.

##### Mechanically

- **General reputation:** Kia’s electric powertrains have proven remarkably reliable. The EV4’s motor (EM07 series, similar to EV3) is shared across multiple global models, so parts support is excellent.
- **Anti‑theft:** No high‑profile security vulnerabilities for the EV4 yet. However, Kia’s earlier models (pre‑2022) were famously easy to bypass. The EV4 uses digital keys and immobilisers from the ground up – but keyless‑relay attacks are still a threat. Keep your key fob in a Faraday pouch.
- **Battery health:** Kia subjected the EV4 to 6,200 miles of Nürburgring abuse with hypercharging cycles, recording only **5% degradation** – a 95% state‑of‑health afterwards. The official warranty guarantees **70% capacity after 160,000 km / 8 years** ([motoraxle.com](https://motoraxle.com/kia-ev4-nurburgring-durability-battery-test/)). That’s better than many rivals.

##### Engines

There are no engines. But the *electric drive unit* deserves scrutiny:

*Single permanent‑magnet synchronous motor (150 kW / 283 Nm)*
- **Cooling system:** The EV4 uses an oil‑cooled motor; leaks are unlikely but the coolant pump for the battery can fail prematurely in hot climates. Listen for a persistent whine from the front axle after the car shuts down – that’s the pump continuing to circulate coolant. If it stops making noise altogether, you’ve likely got a dead pump.
- **High‑voltage contactor:** The main battery contactors can weld shut if the car is repeatedly charged to 100% and then immediately hammered. Anecdotal from EV6 forums; same part family. Preventative: keep daily charge limits to 80%.
- **Inverter failure:** The **EM07 inverter** (traction motor inverter) has a known batch issue on early 2024 EV3 builds – a software update resolved most cases. If you buy an EV4 built before late 2025, check the service history for software version **V3.12.1** or later.
- **Service intervals:** Kia Australia recommends **12 months / 15,000 km** for EV service (coolant, brake fluid, cabin filter, HV check). Unlike ICE cars, there’s no oil change, but brake fluid must be replaced every 2 years.
- **Regen brake noise:** The i‑Pedal 3.0 system can produce a faint groan during strong deceleration – normal, but if it becomes a clunk, the rear caliper slide pins may need greasing.

##### Transmission

There is no transmission in any traditional sense. The **single‑speed reduction gear** is essentially a fixed gearset. It’s exceptionally robust. The only failure mode seen across the E‑GMP family is a whining differential bearing – extremely rare, but if you hear a metallic howl during coasting (especially between 40‑60 km/h), have the drive unit assembly checked under warranty.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Kia EV4?

**Current used market snapshot (NSW dealer data):**
- **Active listings:** 8
- **Price range:** AUD $52,152 – $70,871
- **Median price:** AUD $63,590
- **Median odometer:** 30 km (effectively new demonstrators or cancelled orders)
- **Year range:** 2025 – 2026

These are essentially “new” cars being sold as used – many are dealer demonstrators with delivery kilometres. You’re not saving much yet, but the supply is limited.

**Depreciation metrics:** Because the EV4 is brand‑new, no three‑year depreciation curve exists. Based on Kia’s EV6 and Niro EV, expect a **20‑25% drop in the first year**, then stabilise around **50% retained value after 3 years** – roughly in line with Tesla Model 3, better than many Chinese rivals. The **GT‑Line** premium (approx. $5k over Earth) tends to hold value slightly better due to higher demand for the “top spec” badge.

**Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy:**
- **Fuel savings:** If you charge at home off‑peak (~25c/kWh), the EV4 Long Range costs about **$4.50 per 100 km** vs **$14 for a Toyota Camry hybrid** (at $2.10/L). Over 20,000 km/year, that’s roughly $1,900 saved annually.
- **Servicing:** Kia’s 7‑year, 7‑service capped‑price plan for EVs costs around $2,600 total – about $370/year. Far cheaper than a Camry’s $450‑$600/year.
- **Which powertrain makes sense?** The Air Standard Range saves $9k up front vs the GT‑Line Long Range. If you do mostly city commuting (< 300 km/day), the Standard Range’s 456 km WLTP is ample. If you regularly drive Sydney–Melbourne, the Long Range’s extra 130‑odd km of real‑world range is worth the premium. The Air SR will likely depreciate at a higher percentage (higher supply for entry‑level), but its lower entry price means less absolute loss.

**Buying recommendation:**
**Go for the Earth Long Range** (mid‑spec, ~$59k new). It gets the bigger battery, decent equipment levels (leather‑trimmed seats, blind‑spot view monitor, premium audio) without the GT‑Line’s styling tax. On the used market, look for a **2026‑built Earth LR** with under 5,000 km – you should snag one for **$55,000‑$58,000** as supply increases. Avoid the Air SR unless you only need a city runabout – the range anxiety on longer trips will hurt resale.

**The EV4 is a bloody good buy for early adopters who don’t mind looking a bit weird.** It undercuts the Model 3 on price, offers a cracking interior with actual physical buttons, and Kia’s 7‑year warranty (transferable) gives peace of mind. Just make sure the dealer has applied the latest OTA software and you’re golden.
