#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: VE-series Berlina (commonly referenced in Australia as the VE Berlina), production for the VE platform ran from 2006–2013; Berlina is the Commodore-derived luxury/mid‑lux trim that sits below the Calais and below the ultimate HSV/SS performance models. See the Berlina/Commodore family history on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_Commodore) and the VE brochure for equipment detail ([Holden VE brochure PDF](https://autocatalogarchive.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Holden-Calais-2006-AU.pdf)).
- Internal codes: VE-series (the platform/series code you’ll hear in forums and workshop manuals). Berlina is a trim level of the Commodore line rather than a separate platform.
- Platform architecture / parts commonality: the Berlina is built on the Holden VE Commodore architecture (the Commodore family platform), so suspension components, many electrics, and engine/transmission hardware are shared across the Commodore/Calais/SS/HSV lines — this broad parts pool helps with availability and aftermarket support ([Holden Commodore – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_Commodore)).
- Market-specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drivetrains commonly found on VE Berlina examples (Australia):
  - 3.0‑litre SIDI V6 (direct injection petrol) — sold in some VE Berlina variants; paired with a 6‑speed sports automatic (FWD/AWD not applicable — Berlina is RWD).
  - 6.0‑litre V8 (in some Berlina variants historically and in other Commodore trims) — also paired with automatic boxes in affected vehicles.
  - Most NSW dealer listings for 2010 Berlina show Sports Automatic transmission (live snapshot from MTA NSW scraper: Active listings: 2; Price range: AUD $11,990; Year range: 2010; Median odometer: 68,282 km; Transmission mix: Sports Automatic: 2).
  - Fuel economy (real-world/claimed): expect mid‑range large‑car fuel use — single digits are wishful thinking; official/real-world combined figures range commonly in the low-to-mid teens L/100 km depending on engine and year ([Drive VE fuel usage](https://www.drive.com.au/news/holden-ve-fuel-usage-consumption-figures/) and historical Carsales spec pages).
- Marketplace rivals:
  - Ford Falcon (G6 / G6E / Ghia trims) — direct segment rival at the time ([carsales Berlina v Falcon comparison](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/holden-commodore-berlina-vs-ford-falcon-g6-18105/)).
  - Toyota/AWD alternatives less directly comparable; the Berlina sits squarely against large‑car local rivals from Ford.

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Holden Berlina?
This is the meat. If you love a big comfy rear seat and a cheap stereo array of rattles, read on.

Exterior:
- Panel alignment & fit:
  - Expect the old Holden foibles: uneven shut-lines and inconsistent panel gaps on some VE/VY cars. Owners have long discussed correcting gapping and radiator support shims in community posts ([Panel Gapping discussion](https://www.fastlane.com.au/Forum/postsm188869_Panel-Gapping.aspx)).
  - Cheap fix? Often shimming or a panel refit at a competent panel shop — but a badly resprayed car with dodgy gaps = red flag.
- Body paint & trims:
  - Berlina paint will show chips and swirl marks after commuter life — stone chips on leading edges and bonnet are common. AutoGuru lists paint repairs as frequent jobs on older Berlina models ([AutoGuru paint repair](https://www.autoguru.com.au/repairs/paint-repair/holden/berlina)).
  - Gloss plastics and black trim scratch easily; chrome trims can pit near coastal cars. Look for patchy refinish work or paint runs.
- Rust:
  - Not immune. Older Holdens suffer corroded seams and sills especially in coastal/salty environments. AustralianHolden’s rundown of common early Holden rust spots is a good checklist — check lower sills, boot floor seams and rear trailing-arm surrounds ([Early Holden Rust Problems](https://australianholden.com/index.php/2026/06/16/early-holden-rust-problems-common-trouble-spots-warning-signs-and-how-to-fix-them/)).
- Recalls & safety system (ADAS) quirks:
  - VE-era Berlinas have fewer modern ADAS sensors than today's cars, but cruise-control and driver‑assist bits have been flaky for some owners. Forums document cruise control disengaging or failing intermittently across the VE/VY line — useful troubleshooting threads live on Just Commodores ([VE Cruise Control fault thread](https://forums.justcommodores.com.au/threads/ve-cruise-control-fault-detailed-investigation.304398/)). Check the car’s VIN against the Australian recall database before you sign ([Vehicle Recalls](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/)).

Interior:
- Wear & finish:
  - Berlina interiors were presented as a step up from the base Commodore — nicer trim, different seat fabrics/leather in higher specs — but expect heavily used examples (rideshare/taxi history) to show seat bolster wear, crease cracking and cheap soft-touch plastics that mark easily. ProductReview threads and Motorsift owner summaries note normal ageing and running-cost gripes ([ProductReview Berlina](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/holden-berlina), [Motorsift Berlina buying guide](https://www.motorsift.co.nz/vehicle/holden-berlina-2006-2010-3715)).
- Entry / egress & ergonomics:
  - Commodore/Berlina cars are lowish for a “large car” — easy to slide in/out for most, but high-hip/older users might find door sills a touch low (no special step-up). Look for creaky door checks and worn door bolts on high‑use cars.
- Electrical & infotainment:
  - Infotainment gremlins are common on the VE platform. Long forum threads show head units going blank, erratic fascia lighting or intermittent touch/display faults in VE-era units ([VE head unit issues](https://forums.justcommodores.com.au/threads/ve-berlina-head-unit-issues-helpp.153229/)). There are specialist repair shops that fix MyLink/LCD modules when screens or touch digitizers fail ([MyLink touchscreen repair](https://getelectronic.net/products/holden-lcd-touch-screen-mylink)).
  - Electrical parasitics and unexplained battery drain are reported occasionally — track wiring mods and aftermarket alarms. Use Holden workshop wiring diagrams to trace fuses and circuits ([Holden workshop repair manuals](https://www.onlymanuals.com/holden)).
- Connectivity:
  - Earlier VE Berlinas pre-date good phone integration. BlueTooth and hands-free tend to be basic and sometimes flaky after years of use — reset and firmware fixes can help, but some faults need head-unit repair or swap.

Mechanically:
- Brand engineering reputation:
  - Holden’s VE Commodore era is mixed: many owners praise chassis dynamics and robustness for everyday use; at the same time, routine items (ignition coils, sensors, timing chain tensioners on some V6s) come up repeatedly in independent repair tallies ([CarsGuide Holden Commodore problems summary](https://www.carsguide.com.au/holden/commodore/problems), [AutoGuru common VE repairs](https://www.autoguru.com.au/car-advice/articles/holden-ve-commodore-5-most-common-repairs)).
- Security vulnerabilities:
  - Electronic theft risk: older Holdens (and many cars) have been targeted by key‑programming gangs and CAN attacks. Australian police reporting highlights the rise of key‑programming tools used in thefts ([ABC News on key programming thefts](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-02-27/victoria-car-thefts-surge-electronic-programming-devices/104987680)). Technical write‑ups show CAN‑injection attacks where thieves inject messages onto the vehicle network to bypass immobilisers — not theoretical any more ([CAN injection explanation](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/), headlight CAN attack coverage ([Autoblog CAN injection story](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update)).
  - Mitigations: add a mechanical steering‑wheel lock, fit an immobiliser that isolates ignition circuits, or get your local specialist to BCM‑lock or OBD port‑protect (see techniques used for VF Commodore BCMs as reference) — aftermarket BCM‑locking approaches exist to limit unauthorised key coding ([BCM theft security / locking](https://obdsync.com/body-control-module-bcm-theft-security-locking-to-prevent-gm-key-coding-for-holden-vf-commodore/)).

Engines:
- Preventative servicing note: owner/independent sources show many owners follow 10,000–15,000 km service intervals depending on the year and oil spec; always defer to the vehicle’s owners manual but be prepared to service every 10–12,000 km if the engine has direct-injection or is used for short trips. Use the factory manual if in doubt ([Holden workshop manuals](https://www.onlymanuals.com/holden)).

Italicised subsections for specific engine variants referenced in research:

- *3.0‑litre SIDI V6*
  - Known failure points:
    - **High‑pressure fuel pump / injectors**: direct injection systems are generally harder on fuel delivery hardware and can show carbon build‑up on intake valves. StartMyCar and VE owners note sensor/fuel‑delivery faults and recommend fuel system checks ([StartMyCar MAP sensor guide](https://www.startmycar.com/holden/berlina/guides/map-sensor)).
    - **Torque converter shudder** (reported on some 3.0‑litre SIDI 2013 examples): AustralianCar.Reviews notes torque‑converter shudder occurred in a batch of 3.0‑litre SIDI V6 vehicles requiring replacement ([Recalls & faults VE Berlina 2006‑13](https://australiancar.reviews/recalls-faults-holden-ve-berlina-2006-13/)).
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Fuel system inspection & cleaning every 60–100k km depending on condition.
    - Change oil every 10–12,000 km with the manufacturer‑specified oil (direct injection engines benefit from cleaner oil regimes).
    - Scan for stored DTCs and monitor fuel trim and MAP readings as sensors age.

- *6.0‑litre V8*
  - Known failure points:
    - **Fuel hoses**: a 2006 recall targeted incorrectly manufactured fuel hoses on selected 6.0‑litre V8 Holdens — check VIN/recall status and inspect hoses for cracking ([australiancar.reviews recall summary](https://australiancar.reviews/recalls-faults-holden-ve-berlina-2006-13/)).
    - More mechanical wear due to higher torque: clutch packs (in manuals) and torque converters (in automatics) can show stress after heavy towing or hard use.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Fuel system check and replace old rubber hoses; inspect fuel lines for chafe.
    - Oil and filter every 10–12,000 km (V8s are more forgiving but still prefer regular changes).
    - Valve‑train and lifter checks if noisy — hydraulic lifter noise is commonly reported on older engines.

Transmission:
- Automatic (Torque‑converter) behaviour and failure modes:
  - Most Berlina automatics are 4/6/6‑speed torque‑converter autos branded as Sports Automatic in dealer ads. Real‑world owner threads report symptoms of shuddering, harsh/thumping shifts, and being ‘stuck’ between gears — classic torque‑converter or TCC (torque-converter clutch) control problems ([VE transmission issues thread](https://forums.justcommodores.com.au/threads/ve-berlina-transmission-issues.289966/)).
  - Legal/class action context: Recent class action coverage and legal campaigns allege defects in certain GM 6L‑series automatics and torque converters causing shudders and vibration issues — read the Maurice Blackburn page for the action and GMAuthority for coverage ([Maurice Blackburn class action](https://www.mauriceblackburn.com.au/class-actions/join-a-class-action/holden-transmission-class-action/), [GMAuthority coverage](https://gmauthority.com/blog/2025/01/gm-hit-with-class-action-lawsuit-over-defective-holden-vehicle-transmissions/)).
  - Diagnostics & fixes:
    - Common remedies range from fluid/service and solenoid replacements to full transmission overhaul or torque‑converter exchange. A TCC solenoid or pressure control valve failure will show shudder under light throttle or when TCC is applied — technical write‑ups explain the TCC PWM solenoid operation and failure signs ([Torque Converter diagnosis write‑up](http://gm.saturn.team/Holden/Commodore_Berlina/2005/50/5/4279/6991) — forum/tech resource).
  - Owner takeaway: a transmission that shifts smoothly and without shudder is a must-have. If the test drive shows slipping, harsh shifts or vibratory shudder, budget seriously for a rebuild or walk away.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Holden Berlina?
Numbers first — then the blunt truth.

  - That is your immediate market reality in NSW: a tight, narrow supply of 2010 Berlinas clustered at ≈$11,990 with reasonable km for age.
- Broader market pricing & values:
  - Historical online listings show Berlina asking prices varying with year/condition; CarsGuide and Carsales show older Berlina examples between ~AUD $7–12k depending on age and kilometres ([CarsGuide Berlina listings](https://www.carsguide.com.au/buy-a-car/holden/commodore/berlina-variant), [Carsales Berlina search](https://www.carsales.com.au/cars/holden/berlina/)). TradeMe NZ historical average around NZ$12k (gives cross‑market idea) ([TradeMe Berlina listings](https://www.trademe.co.nz/a/motors/cars/holden/berlina)).
- Depreciation metrics:
  - Use Carsorted’s Australian depreciation analysis as a benchmark — large local sedans traditionally lose a large chunk of their new value in the first 3–5 years. Expect a typical large Holden to have lost 40–60% of its new value by three years in that era, with slower declines after the mid‑life mark as it becomes an entry-priced used car ([Carsorted depreciation analysis](https://www.carsorted.ai/blog/analysis/car-depreciation-australia)). Applied here: a 2010 Berlina trading at ~$12k in 2026 has already completed the heavy part of depreciation — what you pay now is mostly a function of condition, kilometres, and service history.
- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy:
  - Fuel: real‑world Fuelly logs and Drive’s VE fuel-usage tests show Berlina fuel consumption commonly around 9–14 L/100 km depending on engine, driving style and year. If you do 15,000 km/year and your Berlina returns 11 L/100 km on average, annual fuel = (15,000 km / 100) * 11 L = 1,650 L. At $1.80/L petrol = ~$2,970/year in fuel.
    - Sources: Fuelly owner logs ([Fuelly Berlina page](https://www.fuelly.com/car/holden/berlina)) and Drive consumption tests ([Drive VE fuel usage](https://www.drive.com.au/news/holden-ve-fuel-usage-consumption-figures/)).
  - Maintenance & repairs:
    - Budget for higher‑age Holden servicing: routine service/fluids/tyres/brakes ~ $1,000–$1,500/year on average; add a lump sum reserve for transmission or engine repairs if you buy a car with unknown history (torque converter work can be several thousand dollars).
  - Insurance & registration:
    - Insure and register per NSW rates — older $12k cars attract lower premiums for comprehensive, but still factor in third‑party or agreed‑value top‑ups if you care about replacement.
  - Hybrid/EV premium? Berlina had no factory hybrid; if comparing the Berlina (petrol) to a contemporary hybrid large car, the hybrid premium must be high and payback on fuel alone is unlikely unless you do massive city miles. KBB/consumer guides show hybrids need high annual mileage and urban stop‑start use to pay back premium — same logic applies in Australia ([KBB hybrid vs gas methodology](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/)).
- Is this a good buy? The verdict:
  - Buy if:
    - The car is the $11,990 2010 NSW example with a documented service history and the advertised 68k km is genuine — that’s a very reasonable price for a comfy, well‑equipped large sedan. Two key inspections: transmission function and infotainment/electrics.
    - The price is in line with the local market (your NSW snapshot). Cross‑check CarSales/CarsGuide/RedBook valuations for condition.
  - Avoid if:
    - The car shows transmission shudder, erratic shifting, or has a history of transmission work that’s not documented. With ongoing litigation and known torque‑converter complaints for some VE/GM transmissions, a shaky gearbox is a financial black hole ([Maurice Blackburn class action](https://www.mauriceblackburn.com.au/class-actions/join-a-class-action/holden-transmission-class-action/), [VE transmission issues](https://forums.justcommodores.com.au/threads/ve-berlina-transmission-issues.289966/)).
    - The head unit is dead or intermittently blank — repairs are common but costly if you want original functionality; a replacement aftermarket unit is often cheaper than OEM repair ([VE head unit issues](https://forums.justcommodores.com.au/threads/ve-berlina-head-unit-issues-helpp.153229/), [MyLink repair services](https://getelectronic.net/products/holden-lcd-touch-screen-mylink)).
- Optimal buy / sweet spot recommendation:
  - For most buyers in 2026: the 2010 Berlina listed at AUD $11,990 with ~68k km (the current NSW listings) represents the financial sweet spot if:
    - It has full service history (look for 10–12k km oil/service stamps), no transmission shudder, no major rust in sills/boot, and a clean electrical test.
    - If a 2006–2008 VE Berlina is cheaper but has 200–300k km and uncertain history, you’re buying maintenance risk. Spend a little more and get the later 2009–2011 cars if you want lower short‑term maintenance risk.
  - If you want the best long‑term peace of mind, find a Berlina with a documented dealer service history (Holden service stamps prior to 2017), non‑faulting auto gearbox, and evidence that any recalls (fuel hose etc.) were completed — check VIN against [Vehicle Recalls Australia](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/).

Closing take:
- The Berlina is a characterful, roomy, comfortable large Holden with plentiful parts and a good owner community. If you play the used‑car detective — insist on a clean transmission, head‑unit that works, and a full service log — you can buy one for around AUD $12k in NSW and enjoy a cheap, comfortable cruiser.
- Don’t be sentimental about dodgy shifting or unexplained electrical issues — they are the two most common “wallet killers.” If the test drive and inspection are clean, the 2010 $11,990 Berlinas in NSW are sensible buys for buyers who prioritise space and comfort over premium tech and fuel frugality.
