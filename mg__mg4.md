#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation & codes: First-generation MG4 (EH32 family; launched mid‑2022 export markets). It’s MG’s first model on SAIC’s new Modular Scalable Platform (MSP / “MSP platform”), introduced for the MG4 from 2022–present. [MG brochure (MSP, One‑Pack batteries)](https://cdn.mgmotor.eu/images/model/brochure/MG-Brochure_web_new-EN.pdf) and [Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MG4_EV).
- Where it sits in the MG line-up: compact/family hatchback EV. Above it sit MG’s larger EVs/crossovers (e.g., MG Marvel R in some markets) and below it are more budget city EVs in the brand’s range. The MG4 was positioned as the mainstream, affordable EV hatch — value and packaging are its pitch. [MG European launch](https://news.mgmotor.eu/press/mg4-electric-celebrates-its-european-launch/).
- Shared platform and parts-availability benefits: the MSP platform and SAIC “One‑Pack” battery architecture are used across multiple SAIC/MG EVs and derivatives (MG4 / MG Mulan naming in China). That common platform means parts for electrics, battery modules and chassis are easier to source than a unique bespoke layout. See MG brochure and SAIC press. [MSP / One‑Pack battery explained](https://cdn.mgmotor.eu/images/model/brochure/MG4_MY2024_Brochure_Digital_250212_compressed.pdf), [SAIC press on European launch](https://www.saicmotor.com/english/latest_news/mg/57973.shtml).
- Market-specific powertrains and drivetrains (Australia-market relevance):
  - Battery sizes offered globally: roughly **51 kWh (Standard)**, **64 kWh (Long Range)** and **77 kWh (Extended Range / Premium Extended)** usable-pack variants (usable figures ~61.7 kWh on the 64 kWh gross pack). [MG brochure](https://cdn.mgmotor.eu/images/model/brochure/MG-Brochure_web_new-EN.pdf), [EV‑Database 64 kWh page](https://ev-database.org/car/1708/MG-MG4-Electric-64-kWh).
  - Drive layouts: rear‑wheel drive single‑motor (most mainstream variants), and the performance **XPOWER** dual‑motor AWD (introduced 2023) with ~320 kW (435 PS) combined output in some markets. [XPOWER announcement](https://www.mg.co.uk/media-centre/mg-confirms-thrilling-mg4-ev-xpower), [Auto‑Data XPower specs](https://www.auto-data.net/en/mg-mg4-ev-i-64-kwh-435hp-xpower-awd-49032).
  - Transmission: single‑speed reduction gearbox (electric drive, one fixed ratio). [Workshop manual description](https://workshopmanuals.com/shop/make/mg-mg4-ev-workshop-service-repair-manual-2022-2025-pdf/).
  - Real-world efficiency: typical 64 kWh MG4 efficiency reported ~171 Wh/km (≈17.1 kWh/100 km), translating roughly to a petrol‑equivalent of ~1.8 L/100 km — useful for running‑cost comparisons. [EV‑Database efficiency figures](https://ev-database.org/car/1708/MG-MG4-Electric-64-kWh).
- Marketplace rivals in Australia:
  - Volkswagen ID.3 / ID.4, Renault Megane E‑Tech (hatch), BYD Dolphin/Seal, Kia Niro EV / Hyundai Ioniq 5 family (for compact buyers) and budget EV alternatives like the Atto 3 — mainstream compact EV hatchbacks and small crossovers. [TopGear twin test & competitor lists](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/big-reads/big-electric-car-test-renault-megane-e-tech-vs-volkswagen-id3-vs-mg4-ev), [DriveAuthority Chinese EV roundup](https://driveauthority.com/which-chinese-ev-is-the-best-2026/).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used MG MG4?
Below is the nuts‑and‑bolts trouble map assembled from owner logs, specialist sites and official material. If you’re buying one, read this and pencil in a test‑drive script.

- Exterior
  - Panel fit and paint:
    - Early builds (MY2022–early MY2023) show inconsistent panel gaps and trim alignment — check boot/door closures, bonnet alignment and wind‑noise seals. Community guides flag a handful of finish defects in shipped cars. [CarOS buyer’s guide](https://mycaros.com/cars/mg/mg4), [MGEVs forum fault tracker](https://www.mgevs.com/threads/mg4-fault-tracker-links-to-problems-solutions-and-general-advice.10549/).
  - Trim vulnerabilities:
    - Gloss‑black/soft plastics around the grille, mirror caps and door handles can scuff. Interior door pulls and side garnish clips have been reported loose or rattly with heavy use.
  - Body recalls / water ingress:
    - Owner reports include water ingress and poor sealing in some units — check drains, door seals and under‑carriage splash protection. Forum threads record individual incidents; treat any damp smell or water stain as a red flag. [MGEVs forum threads](https://www.mgevs.com/forums/mg4-ev-2022-to-2025-forum.38/).
  - ADAS & safety systems:
    - AEB / lane‑assistance “phantom braking” and errant Autonomous Emergency Braking activations are the headline issue. Consumer group Which? and Australian press covered owner complaints; MG issued software fixes and worked with certification bodies to push OTA / dealer updates. If the car you’re inspecting hasn’t had the ADAS firmware update, walk away until it has. [Which? coverage of MG software updates](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/mg-issues-software-update-following-which-safety-concerns-with-mg4-adlYn3k5lPXd), [Carsales Q&A on AEB behaviour](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/your-questions-answered-why-does-aeb-on-our-mg4-kick-in-unexpectedly-143737/).
    - Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC) dropouts: forum reports of cruise control turning itself off in clear conditions — typically tied to camera/radar sensors or software. Check for recent dealer software history.
  - Practical check:
    - Test-drive with camera/ADAS on; try ACC and lane‑keep at low speed on a quiet road; listen for “beep then brake” phantom events. If the vendor can’t show evidence of ADAS firmware updates, insist on a documented dealer update before purchase.

- Interior
  - Durability:
    - Under intense use (rideshare/taxi conditions) watch seat bolster wear, cheap upholstery rub, and dashboard creaks around the infotainment bezel. Owners report door cards and storage bites loosening earlier than expected. [MGEVs owner discussions](https://www.mgevs.com/threads/what-is-your-opinion-on-your-mg4-for-build-quality-good-or-bad.9819/).
  - Entry/egress:
    - Low hatchback sill helps easy access; door check angles are broad enough for wide openings. No special issues here — just check scuff plates and lower paint near the sill for curb rash.
  - Electrical / infotainment:
    - Infotainment freezing/black screen is common enough that forums and how‑to videos show simple reboot tricks (hold home button 10–15s) — but that’s a band‑aid. Wireless charging pads cutting out and USB‑cable connectivity issues are frequently reported. [MGEVs thread on screen stuck](https://www.mgevs.com/threads/screen-stuck-or-black.22228/), [wireless charging complaints](https://www.mgevs.com/threads/wireless-charging-issues.10197/), [infotainment reset video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40USYhRsNjA).
    - Screen sizes vary by trim (e.g., 10.25" vs 12.3/12.8" on higher trims in some markets) — confirm trim spec and whether it’s a hardware or software–only problem. [MG UK brochure/specs mention touchscreen sizes](https://www.mg.co.uk/sites/default/files/2022-09/MG4_EV_Quick_Guide.pdf).
  - 12V battery drain:
    - Early MY2022 cars reported 12V drain faults with a TSB/fixes issued in some markets; check for a recorded 12V replacement history or dealer TSB service. [ev.care summary of common problems and 12V TSB note](https://ev.care/en-gb/guides/mg/mg-4-ev/common-problems).
  - Practical test checklist:
    - Test all USB ports, wireless CarPlay/AndroidAuto, wireless charger, instrument cluster and perform an infotainment reboot. Ask for log of any infotainment or 12V repairs.

- Mechanically
  - Engineering reputation:
    - The MG4’s mechanicals are typical EV simplicity: a motor, inverter, battery pack and a single‑speed reduction gearbox. That reduces moving‑part counts versus ICE cars, but MG’s reputation (especially early production) is mixed because most trouble is electronics/software rather than classic engine failure. [DrivingElectric reliability note](https://www.drivingelectric.com/mg/4/reliability), [WhatCar reliability](https://www.whatcar.com/mg-motor-uk/mg4/hatchback/used-review/n26329/reliability).
  - Security vulnerabilities:
    - The industry‑wide threats (relay thefts, OBD/CAN injection) affect any modern keyless vehicle; MG4 owners discuss concerns including CAN‑injection style thefts and possible remote telemetry issues. If you plan to park in high‑theft areas, fit physical barriers. [CAN Injection paper & discussion](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/), [owner forum theft/security threads](https://www.mgevs.com/threads/mg-cyberattack-concerns.19457/).
  - Recommended anti‑theft countermeasures:
    - Faraday pouch for key, steering wheel lock and aftermarket immobiliser/GPS logger. Consider OBD port lock and window etching/visible deterrents — cheap insurance versus the modern theft techniques. Forum owners recommend software updates and physical security in combination.

- Engines (electric motor / battery variants)
  - Note: I will italicise each variant below and list the known weak points / recommended service intervals.
  - *51 kWh single‑motor (Standard Range)*
    - Weak points & owner reports:
      - Shorter real‑world range and more frequent charging cycles can exacerbate degradation if the car is used on fast DC charge often.
      - Charging handshake glitches and DC/CCS starting/stopping have been reported across pack sizes (not unique to 51 kWh). [MG brochure battery options](https://cdn.mgmotor.eu/images/model/brochure/MG-Brochure_web_new-EN.pdf), [owner fault tracker](https://www.mgevs.com/threads/mg4-fault-tracker-links-to-problems-solutions-and-general-advice.10549/).
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Battery/BMS updates when available (dealer or OTA), avoid frequent high‑power DC sessions if you want slower long‑term degradation. Check for updated BMS/thermal management software at dealer.
  - *64 kWh single‑motor (Long Range)* — the common Aussie sweet spot
    - Weak points:
      - Most owner data and reliability guides reference charging handshake faults and occasional high‑voltage system warnings. Real‑world range ~360 km WLTP/city mix, efficiency ~171 Wh/km. [EV‑Database 64 kWh MG4](https://ev-database.org/car/1708/MG-MG4-Electric-64-kWh), [autoguide/top problems summary](https://autoguide.top/mg4-problems-the-complete-real-world-guide-to-common-issues-fixes-and-long-term-reliability/).
    - Preventative maintenance & intervals:
      - Check for any HV system recalls/TSBs and ensure the car’s BMS and charger firmware are up‑to‑date. As a conservative Australian servicing approach use 10,000 km checks for 12V system and general wear items (brakes, suspension), even if MG nominal intervals may be longer. [MG owner manuals & quick guides](https://www.mg.co.uk/owner-manuals).
  - *77 kWh single‑motor (Extended Range / Premium Extended)*
    - Weak points:
      - Larger pack reduces range anxiety but the same reported charging handshake/electrical niggles apply. Thermals: heavier packs can heat quicker under repeated DC sessions — ensure thermal management software is present and tested in your sample car.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Same BMS and software checks; battery health report from dealer strongly advised.
  - *XPOWER dual‑motor AWD (approx 320 kW combined)*
    - Weak points:
      - Performance pack means higher stress on drive units and battery; less common in Australian fleet but watch for drive‑unit oil/cooling TSBs and higher DC charging frequency. Owner specs and press pack show high torque and performance credentials but fewer long‑term owner miles to judge. [XPOWER technical specs](https://www.auto-data.net/en/mg-mg4-ev-i-64-kwh-435hp-xpower-awd-49032).
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - More aggressive driving increases wear on brakes (regenerative limits) and may demand more frequent checks of drive‑unit cooling and software calibration.
  - Cross‑variant housekeeping (applies to all MG4s):
    - 12V battery health should be checked and replaced per symptom; owners have reported unexpected 12V failures. [ev.care common problems & TSB note](https://ev.care/en-gb/guides/mg/mg-4-ev/common-problems).
    - Keep software/BMS/ADAS updated via an MG dealer or OTA where available — a surprisingly large chunk of reported faults are software‑fixable.

- Transmission
  - Mechanical behaviour:
    - The MG4 uses a **single‑speed reduction gearbox** (electric drive reduction unit) — effectively one fixed ratio gearset feeding the rear axle in RWD models; no torque converter or multi‑gear auto. [Workshop manual single‑speed description](https://workshopmanuals.com/shop/make/mg-mg4-ev-workshop-service-repair-manual-2022-2025-pdf/).
  - Known failure modes:
    - Owners have reported catastrophic reduction‑gearbox failures linked to low oil level and leaking seals. There’s documented forum debate and at least a couple of high‑profile cases where the reduction unit failed loudly and left cars immobile. These incidents appear rare but are serious. [MGEVs reduction gearbox thread](https://www.mgevs.com/threads/mg4-reduction-gearbox-broken-first-in-the-world.13243/page-3).
    - Sourcing the exact specified gearbox oil can be a pain; service shops and owners report difficulty finding the manufacturer spec and suitable alternatives. [BobIsTheOilGuy discussion](https://bobistheoilguy.com/forums/threads/mg4-ev-drive-unit-cant-find-correct-oil.397788/).
  - Service advice:
    - Confirm with the vendor that reduction‑unit oil checks were performed at dealer service intervals. If the paperwork is thin, budget to have a competent EV technician inspect the reduction unit oil level and seals before purchase — and insist on a pre‑sale dealer service if the car has high kilometres and no recent service history. [Workshop manual reference](https://workshopmanuals.com/shop/make/mg-mg4-ev-workshop-service-repair-manual-2022-2025-pdf/).

#### 3. Should you buy a used MG MG4?
- NSW dealer market snapshot (live MTA NSW scraping):

  - Price range: AUD $34,990 – $34,990
  - Median price: AUD $34,990
  - Year range: 2023 – 2023
  - Median odometer: 51,616 km
  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 1
  - That single listing at $34,990 with ~51k km is your live NSW market reality — bargain or lemon depends on history and firmware updates. Use the listing as leverage; there’s only one active dealer listing right now, so you’ve got a strong inspection position.
- Depreciation & value retention:
  - The MG4’s resale has been volatile. Analysts flag steep early depreciation in markets where MG aggressively priced new cars, pushing used prices down in a short window. If you buy, expect higher short‑term depreciation than premium European rivals; the MG4 can be good value if you accept that the resale will be weaker. [EVDepreciation deep‑dive](https://evdepreciation.com/mg4-depreciation-the-hidden-cost-of-australias-ev-price-war/).
  - Baseline: expect depreciation faster than the Volkswagen ID.3/ID.4 and mainstream Japanese rivals over the first 3 years — MG’s retail pricing volatility is the main driver.
- Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy
  - How I calculate TCO for an Aussie MG4:
    - Step 1 — Acquire: capital cost (price paid) + dealer prep. Your NSW example: $34,990 purchase price.
    - Step 2 — Running costs: electricity consumption (use 17.1 kWh/100 km as a working average for the 64 kWh car) × your local kWh price. For example, at $0.30/kWh that’s ~ $5.13/100 km (17.1 × $0.30). Adjust for home vs public DC pricing (public is usually higher).
      - Source for efficiency: [EV‑Database MG4 efficiency](https://ev-database.org/car/1708/MG-MG4-Electric-64-kWh).
    - Step 3 — Service/maintenance: EV services are lighter (brakes, cabin filters, 12V battery, tyre rotations, coolant/drive‑unit checks). But budget for unexpected electronics work — forums report long dealer wait times for software/hardware fixes; that downtime can be a hidden cost. Use a conservative servicing cadence (10,000 km inspections) for wear items and a battery/BMS health check every 2–3 years.
    - Step 4 — Insurance, rego & tyres: insurance is typical for hatchbacks but note higher theft risk in some areas; consider higher premiums.
    - Step 5 — Depreciation: include expected faster depreciation; model EVDepreciation’s finding when running your resale assumptions. [EVDepreciation MG4 article](https://evdepreciation.com/mg4-depreciation-the-hidden-cost-of-australias-ev-price-war/).
  - Hybrid/EV premium vs petrol:
    - The MG4 is only an EV — if comparing to petrol/hybrid rivals, calculate break‑even using your km/year:
      - Low km urban drivers (≤15,000 km/year): EVs often win on running costs even with resale uncertainty because electricity is cheap per km and servicing is simpler.
      - High‑km drivers (>25,000 km/year) who frequently use public rapid chargers and have uncertain warranty coverage might find petrol hybrids more predictable long‑term.
    - What the market sources say: industry reviews show MG4’s running costs can be competitive with petrol rivals, but the caveat is MG4’s reliability and software niggles can incur downtime/repair bills that upset TCO. [WhatCar running cost analysis](https://www.whatcar.com/mg-motor-uk/mg4/hatchback/review/n24916).
- Final buying recommendation (clear, practical)
  - Short answer: Buy cautiously — the MG4 is a very good value compact EV and, if you get one with the right spec and documented software/TSB history, it’s a solid daily driver. But it’s not a carefree purchase like a tried‑and‑tested Toyota Corolla Hybrid. Expect to be proactive about software updates, service records and security.
  - If you’re in NSW and seeing the live dealer car at $34,990 with 51,616 km (2023), here’s my verdict:
    - Do not buy it blind. Insist on:
      - Complete dealer service history and a printout (or online record) showing ADAS/firmware updates (AEB / ACC), BMS updates and any TSBs performed. If the car hasn’t had the ADAS/firmware fixes discussed by Which?, this is a deal breaker. [Which? software update story](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/mg-issues-software-update-following-which-safety-concerns-with-mg4-adlYn3k5lPXd).
      - A battery health report and dealer HV-system scan showing no charging handshake errors logged recently.
      - Proof that reduction‑unit oil was checked and topped per the workshop manual, and ask for a physical inspection of the reduction unit for leaks. [Workshop manual reduction gearbox notes](https://workshopmanuals.com/shop/make/mg-mg4-ev-workshop-service-repair-manual-2022-2025-pdf/).
      - A 3‑month dealer warranty extension if the car is out of factory warranty window, or negotiate a lower price to allow for possible software/electrical fixes.
    - Best financial sweet spot:
      - Aim for a 64 kWh Long Range (if you can confirm BMS/firmware history) — it’s the balance of range, efficiency and lower degradation risk vs the smaller pack. Avoid early MY2022 cars with sparse service records; MY2023 with documented updates is the smarter buy. [EV‑Database 64 kWh](https://ev-database.org/car/1708/MG-MG4-Electric-64-kWh).
    - Who should buy?
      - Urban/suburban households who want a roomy small hatch, low running costs, and are comfortable chasing OTA updates or working with an MG dealer for software/support.
    - Who should not buy?
      - People who need absolute bulletproof reliability and minimal workshop visits, long‑distance highway commuters who use a lot of DC fast charging (potentially stressing pack thermals), and anyone planning to park in a high‑theft zone without adding physical security measures.
- Bargain hunting tactics in Australia
  - With only one NSW dealer listing at time of writing, use it as a benchmark. If the dealer won’t commit to firmware/service history, walk. There’s a broader used market online (UK/EU listings) that shows variety in pricing — but Australian warranty and support differences matter.
  - Consider third‑party pre‑purchase specialist EV inspections that can read battery cell data, check charge‑port logs and verify drive‑unit oil level.
