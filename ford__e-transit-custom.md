#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation & codes: the Ford E‑Transit Custom (new, ground‑up BEV van launched 2024–2026 in global markets; often referenced in Ford material simply as the “E‑Transit Custom” series). Ford’s marketing and technical sheets position the E‑Transit Custom as the electric successor to the ICE Transit Custom line [Ford Corporate](https://corporate.ford.com/corporate/corporate/articles/products/ford-e-transit-custom/).
- Where it sits in the Ford range: sits directly below the full‑size E‑Transit and above smaller city vans — the medium (mid‑sized) electric van choice for trades, delivery and fleets. It replaces the Transit Custom ICE in Ford’s commercial ladder and competes with similar mid‑size electric vans.
- Platform architecture & parts commonality: built on Ford’s Transit Custom architecture re‑imagined for BEV packaging (rear‑mounted e‑motor(s), battery pack underfloor). It’s shared closely with Volkswagen’s e‑Transporter family (platformly related) and other Ford Transit derivatives — which helps parts and service access in markets where both Ford and partner brands operate [Ford Authority](https://fordauthority.com/2026/01/ford-e-transit-custom-based-vw-vans-get-updated/), [Ford UK](https://www.ford.co.uk/vans-and-pickups/e-transit-custom).
- Available powertrains, outputs, transmissions & drive layouts (market‑specific summary):
  - **Battery**: early models use a ~74–82.5 kWh gross battery with roughly 64–71 kWh usable depending on spec/updates; Ford later listed a 71 kWh usable upgrade for 2026 models [Autonews](https://autonews.autoua.net/en/novosti/29211-2024-ford-e-transit-custom-specifications-released-217-hp.html), [fromtheroad.ford.com](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/eur/en/articles/2025/whats-new-e-transit-custom-2026).
  - **Motor options** (rear drive primary; AWD option added later):
    - Base rear motor: ~100 kW / 135 PS (~136 hp) — immediate torque ~415 Nm.
    - Mid option: ~160 kW / 217 PS (~217 hp).
    - High‑performance variants: MS‑RT and Sport variants pushing into ~285PS territory planned for later model years [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/609003/ford-e-transit-custom-specifications/), [fromtheroad.ford.com](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/eur/en/articles/2025/whats-new-e-transit-custom-2026).
  - **Transmission**: single‑speed reduction gearbox (electric direct drive). AWD variants add a front e‑motor; overall driveline is electric multi‑motor architecture rather than torque‑converter automatics [UltimateSpecs](https://www.ultimatespecs.com/car-specs/Ford/139097/Ford-Transit-Custom-Van-L1-2024-320-E-Transit-100kW.html), [fromtheroad.ford.com](https://www.fromtheroad.ford.com/corporate/corporate/articles/products/ford-e-transit-custom/).
  - **Charging & range**: targeted WLTP‑style ranges up to ~373–380 km depending on battery/motor/trim; DC charging up to ~125 kW giving 0–80% in ~35–45 minutes in favourable conditions [Ford Corporate](https://corporate.ford.com/corporate/corporate/articles/products/ford-e-transit-custom/), [UltimateSpecs](https://www.ultimatespecs.com/car-specs/Ford/139097/Ford-Transit-Custom-Van-L1-2024-320-E-Transit-100kW.html).
- Marketplace rivals: Mercedes‑Benz eVito / eSprinter, Volkswagen e‑Transporter / e‑Crafter, Ram ProMaster EV / BrightDrop, Maxus eDeliver series and newer Kia/LDV offerings — the mid‑size electric‑van set [FleetRabbit](https://fleetrabbit.com/blogs/post/electric-van-fleet-etransit-esprinter-promaster), [MotorIllustrated](https://motorillustrated.com/2025-chevrolet-brightdrop-vs-ford-e-transit-vs-mercedes-benz-esprinter-vs-ram-promaster-ev-vs-.../158742/).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Ford E‑Transit Custom?

Exterior:
- Panel fit & paint:
  - Generally durable commercial finish but check quarter‑panel seams and wheel‑arch welds — Transit family panels have a history of corrosion spots in harsh climates or where salt/grit is routine (inspect underside seams) [Go‑Parts rust guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/quarter-panel-ford-transit-250-ford-transit-350-ford-transit-150-2015-2025).
  - Early owner reports (forums) mention localized paint cracking/marks on panels on some Transit Custom derivatives; not widespread, but worth a close visual check of bonnet, wings and roof edges in used vehicles.
- Plastic trim & gloss surfaces: expect the usual vulnerability — gloss black plastics scratch easily, rubbers fade under sun and roof‑mounted ladder/rails do paint chafe if the van’s been worked hard.
- Structural/body recalls: check VIN for open Ford recalls — E‑Transit had safety campaigns including high‑voltage battery fastener/washer concerns and earlier halfshaft issues on some E‑Transit/Transit vehicles (see recalls) [NHTSA recalls page](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2026/FORD/E-TRANSIT/recalls), [Ford Authority](https://fordauthority.com/2026/02/2026-ford-e-transit-recalled-over-high-voltage-battery-issue/).
- ADAS & sensor vulnerabilities:
  - Adaptive Cruise/Pre‑Collision can show ‘sensor fault’ messages if radar/camera obstructions or wiring get knocked (common with vans doing urban work). Simple cleaning often helps, but some faults need dealer recalibration [Go‑Parts Adaptive Cruise fault guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p2583-ford-transit-2020-2024).
  - There have been software/firmware remedial campaigns for infotainment/reverse camera freezes and for other ADAS glitches — check for completed SYNC recalls/updates on the vehicle’s VIN [DOT recall summary (SYNC)](https://dot.report/bulletins/11029548), [Ford Owner Support recalls](https://www.ford.com/support/recalls-details/).
  - Bottom line: ADAS reliability partly depends on up‑to‑date firmware and correct sensor mounting. Verify recent dealer updates and look for dash flags such as “Adaptive Cruise Malfunction”.

Interior:
- Fit‑for‑work durability:
  - Tougher than a fancy passenger car, but check wear on seat bolsters, rubberised floor mats and overhead stowage — commercial rigs get slammed around. If the van’s had trades use, expect scuffs on A‑pillars and cargo access scuffing.
  - Entry/egress is a real working metric: check door sill heights and door check stops — heavier door springs and rubber seals are easy to damage if ladders/packages are hooked up when getting in/out frequently.
- Electrical & infotainment:
  - **SYNC infotainment**: multiple reports of frozen screens or reboot loops in earlier SYNC software versions; a formal remedial update (recall/TSB) exists for some transit models — ensure the VIN has the SYNC remediation performed [DOT bulletin on SYNC recall](https://dot.report/bulletins/11029548).
  - **Screen sizes**: twin‑screen layouts up to a 13‑inch central screen are used on Customs in higher trims; lower specs have smaller screens — verify exact spec if Apple CarPlay/Android Auto functionality matters for your fleet of phones [Top Gear interior overview](https://www.topgear.com/car-reviews/ford/e-transit/interior).
  - **12V battery drains**: community threads report intermittent 12V battery behaviour on e‑Transit Customs (some owners noting parasitic drains or early 12V failures) — verify battery age, load tests and ask for a recent service record showing battery health checks [FordTransit forum 12V thread](https://fordtransit.org/forum/viewtopic.php?t=230377).
  - Connectivity & telematics: Ford Pro connectivity is a selling point (OTA updates & fleet telematics), but real world depends on subscription/activation — confirm what’s included and transferability of services.

Mechanically:
- Brand engineering reputation: Ford’s commercial division has decades of Transit support and dealer network depth in Australia — good for parts and warranty support. But commercial vehicles are used hard, and early BEV entrants have teething items (electronics, cooling, halfshafts).
- Security vulnerabilities:
  - Modern Fords are susceptible to the same CAN‑bus injection attacks reported across many brands — thieves have used headlight wiring access points to inject CAN frames and bypass immobilisers. This is an industry issue; physical hardening and perimeter sensor alarms help [Ken Tindell CAN injection analysis](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/), [Autoblog coverage](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update).
  - Practical countermeasures: fit a mechanical steering lock and a hard‑wired alarm that monitors CAN network activity; consider an inline starter immobiliser relay or VIN‑linked telematics that can immobilise vehicle remotely for fleet units.
- High‑priority mechanical checks:
  - Inspect halfshafts and powerdrive mounts — a recall/TSB affected E‑Transit halfshafts on earlier runs where halfshafts could partially disengage, risking loss of drive [CarComplaints halfshaft recall coverage](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2025/ford-e-transit-halfshaft-problems-recall.shtml).
  - Check for loose engine/tray crossmember TSBs (some Transits had unsecured crossmembers that needed rectification) — inspect for loose bolts, unusual drivetrain shudder or clunks [CarComplaints recall list](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2026/ford-recalls-e-transit-and-transit-vans.shtml).

Engines (electric motor variants) — every single variant covered
- *100 kW rear motor (base / Trend/L1 style offerings)*:
  - Failure points: fewer moving parts than ICE, but watch motor bearing whine, inverter/PEM cooling blockages and cabling loom damage in wheel‑arch areas. Early owner reports emphasise software/electronics faults and occasional 12V system problems that can strand a vehicle until dealer reboot/patch [Recharged common problems guide](https://recharged.com/articles/ford-e-transit-common-problems/).
  - Preventative maintenance: keep motor cooling ducts and underfloor battery ventilation clear; annual inspection of high‑voltage cabling and a 12V battery health check every 12 months or every 10,000 km for commercial use (see note on Australian fleet practice below). Ensure OTA updates are applied to motor inverters via Ford Pro or dealer.
- *160 kW / 217 PS rear motor (mid option)*:
  - Failure points: same electrical vulnerabilities as base motor plus increased thermal load under towing/long hill climbs — check for heat soak events, power limiting codes and verify torque delivery under load via a test drive with a payload equivalent to your normal use.
  - Preventative maintenance: verify cooling system servicing and ensure firmware patches are up to date. For frequent towing (E‑Transit Custom targets towing up to 2,000 kg in some configs), confirm towing harnesses and vehicle’s software towing profile were used and not overloaded [Ford Corporate towing claim](https://corporate.ford.com/corporate/corporate/articles/products/ford-e-transit-custom/).
- *MS‑RT / 285PS & Sport variants*:
  - Failure points: higher stresses on halfshafts, mounting points and thermal management; these variants may accelerate wear on drive components if used as daily heavy‑load workhorses.
  - Preventative maintenance: stricter checks on halfshafts and torque settings; make sure dealer has installed any service campaigns aimed at high‑power variants.

Transmission:
- Layout & behaviour:
  - The E‑Transit Custom uses a single‑speed reduction gearbox on the driven axle(s) — effectively no multi‑ratio gearbox to fail like a torque converter or DCT; gear changes are instant feel, regenerative braking tuning matters for driver experience [UltimateSpecs (motor + 1‑speed)](https://www.ultimatespecs.com/car-specs/Ford/139097/Ford-Transit-Custom-Van-L1-2024-320-E-Transit-100kW.html).
  - AWD variants add a front motor driving a simpler mechanical coupling; this adds complexity (extra motor controller, front‑axle halfshafts) and opens opportunities for halfshaft mount / coupling recalls if anything’s been poorly torqued.
- Known failure modes:
  - No classic torque‑converter wear, but look for motor controller faults, inverter error codes, or mechanical noises from reduction gear bearings. Recall history has flagged halfshaft disengagement risks on some E‑Transit families — check for TSBs/recalls and that dealer work was done [CarComplaints halfshaft recall](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2025/ford-e-transit-halfshaft-problems-recall.shtml).
  - Regenerative braking behaviour can feel abrupt or inconsistent if software isn’t updated — a dealer flash often fixes it.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Ford E‑Transit Custom?

- Current NSW dealer marketplace snapshot (live): Active listings: 1. Price range / median: AUD $82,290. Year: 2026 (median odometer 28 km). Transmission: Automatic (1). (This is the MTA NSW scraper snapshot you provided — single live dealer listing at AUD $82,290, near‑new 2026 spec).
- Local pricing context & entry ceiling:
  - If that NSW example is representative, the Australian used market currently prices near new‑delivery numbers for 2026 examples (single low‑km unit shown at AUD $82,290). That’s effectively new‑car money in the used channel — you’re buying almost the car’s retail price in secondhand form.
- Depreciation & residual value reality:
  - Early BEV vans have mixed depreciation trajectories: big fleets often buy new on lease cycles and release well‑maintained units into the trade market; retail supply is thin—so prices can be sticky high on near‑new, low‑kilometre examples. Comparative Europe market listings show used E‑Transit Customs commonly change hands for a large chunk of original price, but UK markets show used offers in the mid‑tens of thousands GBP for earlier years (not a direct AU comparator) [AutoTrader UK used listings](https://www.autotrader.co.uk/vans/used-vans/ford/e-transit-custom), [Parkers used listings](https://www.parkers.co.uk/vans-pickups/ford/e-transit-custom/for-sale/).
  - If you’re comparing depreciation against ICE Transit Customs or rival electric vans, expect an initial 3‑year drop that’s likely shallower than some passenger EVs (fleet demand keeps residual values) but still material — don’t expect miracle low rates. Use fleet running‑cost and residual tools to get model‑specific numbers: Fleet News running costs tool / CAP‑HPI data help fleet buyers benchmark residuals [Fleet News](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/tools/van/running-costs/?Manufacturer=ford&Model=transit-custom-light-commercial).
- TCO strategy & maths (how to run the numbers for Australia):
  - Key inputs for your TCO: purchase price (capex), expected annual kilometres, charging cost (c/kWh or depot kWh), real‑world energy consumption (kWh/100 km), servicing costs, insurance, tyres, and residual after X years.
  - Fleet/real world hints: MotorIllustrated & FleetRabbit tests suggest the E‑Transit tends to deliver better real‑world efficiency and lower running costs per km compared with many rivals, particularly when charging costs are low (off‑peak depot charging) [FleetRabbit](https://fleetrabbit.com/blogs/post/electric-van-fleet-etransit-esprinter-promaster), [MotorIllustrated](https://motorillustrated.com/2025-chevrolet-brightdrop-vs-ford-e-transit-.../158742/).
  - EV premium payback: if the E‑Transit costs you an extra AUD X at purchase but saves Y cents/km in energy and lower scheduled maintenance (no oil, fewer consumables), calculate simple payback: Extra purchase / (annual km × savings per km). Fleet studies show BEV vans often reach fleet payback over diesel within 3–5 years when electricity pricing and duty cycles are favourable — use the ICCT TCO calculator or fleet TCO tools to run scenario analysis [ICCT TCO tool](https://theicct.org/tco-calculator/), [Inspiration Mobility TCO comparison](https://inspirationmobility.com/news/tco-comparison-ford-transit-ice-vs-ford-e-transit-bev).
  - Practical example: if the E‑Transit consumes ~24–28 kWh/100 km (real world with payload), at AUD 0.25/kWh depot rate that’s ~AUD 0.06–0.07/km. Compare to a diesel Transit doing ~9–10 L/100 km at AUD 1.80/L (~AUD 0.16–0.18/km fuel). That’s a potential ~10‑12 cents/km saving on energy alone — annual savings scale with km/year. (NOTE: plug your own local energy & fuelling costs for accuracy).
- Reliability & ownership risk:
  - Things that can cost you serious downtime or money: unresolved recalls (battery high‑voltage washers, halfshaft issues), intermittent 12V/infotainment faults, and any outstanding ADAS recalibrations. For commercial users, downtime is where real costs live — ensure dealer has done recall remedies and that subscription services (Ford Pro) are active or transferable.
- Final buying recommendation — practical, blunt Australian advice:
  - If you run a fleet and you can buy with a dealer warranty, full service history and depot charging in place, the E‑Transit Custom is a strong working EV — good range for daily circuits, well supported by Ford’s dealer network and built for towing/payload more so than many city‑only vans [Ford Corporate specs](https://corporate.ford.com/corporate/corporate/articles/products/ford-e-transit-custom/).
  - If you’re a private buyer or sole trader buying a used unit from the private market: be cautious paying near‑new money. The NSW snapshot shows a single 2026 listing at AUD $82,290 (28 km). If you’re paying that you might as well buy new to get full factory warranty and dealer ordering options unless the used unit has a meaningful discount or specific extras.
  - Best financial sweet spot: for most small fleets the optimal used buy will be a 12–30 month ex‑fleet example with verified dealer‑completed recall work, low‑kilometre service history, and an active Ford Pro telematics package. If you can find a 2024–2025 off‑lease unit with 20–80k km at a meaningful discount to new (not the one NSW near‑new example), that’s the sweet spot: depreciation has been taken, but the battery and drivetrain are still young.
  - If you need a short recommendation: prefer mid‑spec (160 kW / 217 PS) trim for a balance of range and performance unless you constantly tow near‑max; avoid high‑performance MS‑RT spec if you’re strictly focused on payload/efficiency rather than performance. Confirm AWD if you need it for traction — but accept added complexity.

Practical pre‑purchase checklist (Australian buyer):
- Check VIN for open recalls and that all Ford recall remedies/TSBs have been completed (Ford Owner Support & NHTSA recall pages) [Ford Owner Support](https://www.ford.com/support/recalls-details/), [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2026/FORD/E-TRANSIT/recalls).
- Request full dealer service history and proof of SYNC/firmware updates (especially for infotainment/ADAS).
- Load test the 12V battery and request a battery health report for the HV battery if available.
- Ask for demonstration of regenerative braking modes and a test drive with a realistic load or ask the dealer to replicate a payload drive.
- Inspect for corrosion in quarter‑panel seams and underside weld areas.
- Confirm telematics/Ford Pro subscription status and whether transfer to a new owner is supported.
- Fit mechanical theft deterrents (steering wheel lock) and consider hardened alarm/immobiliser if you park in high‑risk zones (CAN‑injection reports show thieves target modern vehicles via light‑harness access) [Ken Tindell CAN injection](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/).
