#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: Fifth‑generation Toyota Supra — internal codes A90 / A91 (marketing) and J29/DB (engineering); produced 2019–2026 and commonly referred to in press as the GR Supra (J29/A90) [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_GR_Supra).
- Product hierarchy: the Supra sits above Toyota’s GT‑marketed coupes (there isn’t a “Supra Lite” beneath it) and below full‑blown Toyota performance halo cars and exotics in price — it’s Toyota’s two‑seat, driver‑focused sport coupe offering. Its nearest in‑house sibling in spirit is the 86/Subaru BRZ (smaller, cheaper, more playful); Supra aims for sharper power and luxury.
- Shared global platform: built on a joint Toyota–BMW platform developed with Magna Steyr; the Supra shares its architecture and many mechanical components with the G29 BMW Z4 and BMW petrol engines/transmissions [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_GR_Supra).
  - Other models on the same foundation: BMW Z4 (G29), and engine/transmission kin with BMW models using the B48/B58 family.
- Engines, outputs, transmissions and drive:
  - 2.0‑litre turbo four — BMW‑sourced **B48** (entry 2.0). Typical quoted power ≈ 255 hp (around 190 kW) depending on market and model year [evo](https://www.evo.co.uk/toyota/supra/engine-gearbox-and-technical-highlights).
  - 3.0‑litre turbo straight‑six — BMW‑sourced **B58** (sports flagship 3.0). Early 3.0 units launched with ~335 hp (250 kW); later updates and special editions push higher outputs and a 6‑speed manual was later offered for the 3.0 [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_GR_Supra), [Toyota USA](https://pressroom.toyota.com/vehicle/2023-toyota-gr-supra/).
  - Transmissions: ZF 8‑speed torque‑converter automatic (standard across most early models) and a Toyota‑tuned 6‑speed manual option introduced for the 3.0 in later model years [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_GR_Supra), [Toyota USA](https://pressroom.toyota.com/vehicle/2023-toyota-gr-supra/).
  - Drive layout: Front‑engine, rear‑wheel drive (RWD).
- Marketplace rivals (Australian context): BMW Z4 (close mechanical twin), Nissan Z (value sports coupe), Porsche 718 Cayman / Boxster (more expensive, sharper chassis), Ford Mustang (cheaper V6/ecoboost or V8 rivals), and entry‑level sports cars like the Toyota 86 for customers prioritising engagement over outright power [MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/cars/toyota/supra/2026), [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/car-reviews/2025-bmw-z4-vs-2024-toyota-gr-supra-manual-review).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Toyota Supra?
Below is the blunt, nitty‑gritty buyer’s checklist. If you’re shopping in Australia, treat this like mandatory reading before you hand over $50k+ of hard‑earned dosh.

- Exterior
  - Panel alignment and trim:
    - Some A90 Supras left the factory with door‑to‑fender and door‑to‑quarter gap borderline‑out‑of‑spec. Owners report doors needing dealer adjustment or a body shop tweak to get gaps right — inspect in daylight and with doors closed and partially open for catches [SupraMKV forum](https://www.supramkv.com/threads/door-panel-gap-issue.26803/).
    - Gloss‑black A‑pillar and pillar trims scratch easily; replacement trim pieces are common in the parts market [Go‑Parts](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/body-a-pillar-trim-panel-toyota-gr-supra-2020-2026).
  - Paint durability / campaigns:
    - Toyota has run customer support programs on paint peeling on several models; check the specific VIN for any paint‑related programs that might cover a respray or repair — use Toyota’s recall lookup [Toyota Owners Recalls](https://www.toyota.com/recall/) and read guidance about Toyota paint programs [CyPaint](https://cypaint.com/article/what-is-the-toyota-paint-peeling-recall-program).
  - ADAS / sensor vulnerability:
    - Pre‑Collision / AEB and adaptive cruise systems have had owner complaints for false activations or unexpected braking. The forward radar sensor is delicate — minor front damage or grille mods can knock it out and replacement needs professional calibration [CarSafetyDB](https://carsafetydb.com/toyota/supra/2023/driver-assist), [Go‑Parts radar guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/radar-sensor-toyota-gr-supra-2020-2022).
    - Aftermarket front‑end changes (bullbars, lower grilles) often mean expensive recalibration to restore AEB/DRCC — I‑CAR documents OEM calibration requirements and the need for post‑repair calibration on ADAS systems [I‑CAR OEM Calibration](https://rts.i-car.com/oem-calibration-requirements-search.html?make=Toyota).

- Interior
  - Wear & fit:
    - Alcantara trim and seat surfaces can show premature peeling on some cars — not universal but reported often enough to check seats and steering‑arm surfaces carefully [Why Supra Alcantara Peels](https://carinterior.alibaba.com/tips/supra-alcantara-peeling-reasons).
    - Small interiors: ingress/egress is low‑slung — tall Aussies will feel the sill and roofline when getting in/out. Door check angles are shallow, so watch for scuffs on trousers and door cards over time.
  - Electrical / infotainment:
    - The Supra’s infotainment has recurring reports of freezing, random resets and Bluetooth/Apple CarPlay dropouts. Soft resets and whole‑car sleep cycles help but persistent reboots often need dealer software updates or module replacement — inspect the car with the system running for a while and test all bluetooth/phone features [AutoreviewNest infotainment reset guide](https://autoreviewnest.com/how-to-reset-infotainment-toyota-supra/), [SupraMKV thread on resets](https://www.supramkv.com/threads/infotainment-system-constantly-resets.20763/).
    - Wireless phone pads (Qi) can overheat or fail; battery/charging modules are a known weak link across platform siblings (BMW parts shared) [Go‑Parts wireless charger module write‑up](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/wireless-device-charging-system-module-bmw-z4-bmw-m340i-xdrive-bmw-328i-2019-2024).
  - Practicality / durability under intense use (rideshare/taxi):
    - This is not a rideshare car. Hard day‑to‑day use will accelerate wear on Alcantara and trim; low boot opening and tight rear parcel area make hauling awkward. For high‑utilisation fleets, look elsewhere.

- Mechanically
  - Engineering pedigree: the Supra wears two badges — Toyota chassis tuning and BMW mechanicals. That combo gives strong performance but also means some BMW‑sourced quirks and parts costs. Overall engineering is robust, but expect BMW‑level service bills for engine and transmission work [Auto‑Data Toyota/BMW supply note](https://www.auto-data.net/en/toyota-supra-model-415).
  - Security (high priority):
    - Modern Toyotas (including the Supra) are vulnerable to CAN‑injection and “headlight plug” style attacks where thieves access exposed wiring and inject CAN frames to spoof the key/fob and start the car. This is a documented, global problem — not just theft fear‑mongering [CAN‑CIA paper on CAN Injection](https://can-cia.org/fileadmin/cia/documents/publications/cnlm/june_2023/cnlm_23-2_p20_the_can_injection_attack_ken_tindel_canis_automotive_labs.pdf), [The Register news analysis](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how_thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/), and consumer reporting [Autoblog theft article](https://www.autoblog.com/news/thieves-are-stealing-toyotas-in-minutes-using-a-simple-headlight-hack).
    - Aftermarket mitigations:
      - Mechanical steering wheel lock or club — simple, visible theft deterrent.
      - Install an immobiliser with a secondary relay or kill‑switch that physically interrupts the fuel pump or starter circuit.
      - Have trusted workshops block off exposed headlight/wiring access points or fit an aftermarket CAN bus intrusion detection system (industrial solutions exist — OEMs prefer IDPS in‑vehicle gateways) [PlaxidityX recommendations](https://plaxidityx.com/blog/blog-post/what-oems-can-do-to-prevent-can-injection-car-theft/).
      - VIN etching and GPS trackers help recovery odds.
  - Under‑the‑skin failures to watch:
    - Suspension bushes and subframe mounts show wear if the car has seen track days — check for uneven tyre wear, clunks over bumps and inspect bushes visually.
    - Braking system: owners report unexpected PCS braking activates and jerking behaviour — verify brake pedal feel and watch for pulsing/jerking at low speed (forum reports) [Braking problems thread](https://www.supramkv.com/threads/braking-problems.6847/).

- Engines (italicised subsection per engine code/variant)
  - *B48 — 2.0‑litre turbo four (BMW‑sourced)*
    - Known failure points:
      - Oil consumption and oil leaks when neglected, especially at higher mileages — monitor oil levels and service records.
      - Turbocharger wastegate and charge piping can be weak on tuned cars; check for boost leaks and listen for turbo bearing noise.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Oil change every 10,000 km is sensible for Australian use (shorter intervals if driven hard). Use BMW‑spec synthetic oil and log receipts.
      - Inspect intercooler and charge pipes at 50,000 km; replace cheap silicone joins if present.
    - Sources: evo trim and engine coverage, common owner reports [evo](https://www.evo.co.uk/toyota/supra/engine-gearbox-and-technical-highlights).
  - *B58 — 3.0‑litre turbo inline‑six (BMW‑sourced)*
    - Known failure points:
      - Reports of crankshaft and cylinder scoring issues exist in the used‑parts aftermarket and owner write‑ups — rare but serious; pre‑purchase compression/leakdown and oil analysis are worth doing on high‑km cars [Go‑Parts B58 crankshaft note](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/crankshaft-bmw-840i-bmw-z4-toyota-supra-2019-2026), [what‑breaks Supra guide](https://what-breaks.com/toyota/gr-supra-a90).
      - Turbo plumbing, oil feed/return line leaks and high‑pressure fuel components can be stressed by tuning/track work.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Oil service every 10,000 km minimum; more often (5–7,500 km) if you plan spirited driving or track days. Use manufacturer‑spec oil and replace the oil filter each service.
      - Coolant checks and thermostat/hoses inspection every 40–60,000 km; turbocharged engines hate old, collapsed hoses.
    - Sources: aggregated problems list and component notes [what‑breaks](https://what-breaks.com/toyota/gr-supra-a90), [Go‑Parts analysis](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/crankshaft-bmw-840i-bmw-z4-toyota-supra-2019-2026).

- Transmission
  - ZF 8‑speed torque‑converter automatic (most Supras)
    - Behaviour: smooth, rapid shifts when healthy; can be stressed by higher torque (tuned) engines and aggressive launch usage.
    - Known failure modes:
      - Torque‑converter shudder and vibration on light acceleration — symptoms of lock‑up clutch wear/failure. Replacement requires transmission removal and is labour‑intensive (expensive) [Go‑Parts torque converter guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/torque-converter-toyota-supra-bmw-z4-bmw-m340i-2020-2025).
      - Owners pushing big power often report transmission heat and clutch wear; check fluid change history and any aftermarket tune notes.
    - Maintenance:
      - Transmission fluid and filter change at intervals recommended by Toyota/BMW service schedules — if there is no record, treat it as overdue. A fluid change every 60–80,000 km is a pragmatic preventive step for Australian conditions (more often for track use).
      - Watch for slipping or flares at shift points; get a transmission diagnostic scanned if present.
    - Sources: ZF 8HP noted as the automatic choice for the Supra [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_GR_Supra), torque converter failure symptoms and owner reports [Go‑Parts](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/torque-converter-toyota-supra-bmw-z4-bmw-m340i-2020-2025).
  - 6‑speed manual (later 3.0 models)
    - Behaviour: Toyota‑tuned 6‑speed with iMT on some models — engages more driver involvement but is rarer in the used market. Fewer mechanical horror stories here, but clutch wear is a used‑car risk; check for chatter, drag, and service history [Toyota USA manual press release](https://pressroom.toyota.com/vehicle/2023-toyota-gr-supra/).

- Typical Australian used price range and depreciation:
  - Market reality: Supras vary a lot by year, engine, mileage and special editions. In broad international datasets the GR Supra has shown mid‑segment depreciation: iSeeCars reports a ~32.1% loss over 5 years compared with segment rivals and CarEdge notes a 5‑year depreciation in the 20–25% range depending on specifics [iSeeCars depreciation comparison](https://www.iseecars.com/compare/bmw-z4-vs-toyota-gr_supra), [CarEdge depreciation summary](https://caredge.com/toyota/gr-supra/depreciation).
  - Australia nuance: Collector buzz around certain Supra badges and low‑km, unmodified 3.0s can lift prices relative to ordinary sports coupes — hence that $99,995 single NSW listing for a near‑new example isn’t impossible, but it’s at the top end for the platform.
- Depreciation metric (definitive example):
  - Baseline: expect around 20–35% value drop over 5 years depending on spec — better than BMW Z4 in some datasets, worse than Porsche Cayman. Use iSeeCars and CarEdge stats for a cross‑check [iSeeCars](https://www.iseecars.com/compare/bmw-z4-vs-toyota-gr_supra), [CarEdge](https://caredge.com/toyota/gr-supra/depreciation).
- Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy — how to think about it in Australia
  - Fuel / running example:
    - Combined fuel use for the Supra is roughly 27 MPG (US combined), which equates to ~8.7 L/100 km (EPA combined figure conversion) — expect real‑world 9–12 L/100 km in Australia depending on engine and driving style [FuelEconomy (EPA figure) summary](https://fueleconomy.gov/feg/PowerSearch.do?action=noform&baseModel=GR+Supra&make=Toyota&pageno=1&srchtyp=ymm&year1=2025&year2=2025).
    - Example calculation: At 12,000 km/year and 10 L/100 km consumption, that’s 1,200 L/year. At AUD $1.80/L fuel (assumption for example), fuel ≈ $2,160/year. Adjust the fuel price and consumption to your local habits for accurate totals.
  - Service & parts:
    - Expect BMW‑level parts and specialist labour for engine/transmission components (higher than Corolla/Auris servicing). Factor in occasional expensive items: turbo, transmission work, ADAS recalibration after panels — budget a premium for scheduled and unscheduled maintenance.
  - Insurance:
    - Sports coupes attract higher premiums — get several insurer quotes to understand your annual cost.
  - Alternative powertrain premium (NOT applicable here — Supra is petrol RWD only):
    - There’s no hybrid/EV Supra to compare; the decision is between 2.0 four or 3.0 six. The 3.0 costs more upfront, but for most Aussie buyers the 3.0’s stronger resale and better performance deliver better long‑term value if you keep the car several years — unless you do huge km where the 2.0’s slightly better economy could matter.
  - TCO verdict:
    - If your driving is low‑to‑moderate km/year (under 15k km) and you value performance and scarcity, the 3.0 can be defended on TCO because strong resale offsets higher running costs. High‑km commuters should favour the 2.0 for fuel economy and lower entry price.
    - Use KBB/Edmunds/CarEdge TCO tools to run your numbers — they break down depreciation and running costs by year [KBB cost tools](https://www.kbb.com/cars-for-sale/all/toyota/supra), [CarEdge costs](https://caredge.com/toyota/gr-supra/costs).
- Buying recommendation — the short, blunt answer
  - If you want a Supra and you use it as a weekend weapon, buy a 3.0 (B58) in good condition. The sweet spot for value vs specification is typically a 2021–2023 3.0 Premium (better infotainment, nicer interior, and stronger market desirability). The 3.0 manual (where you can find one) is the enthusiast unicorn — worth a premium if you want engagement [Toyota press on manual availability](https://pressroom.toyota.com/vehicle/2023-toyota-gr-supra/), [Edmunds trim comparisons](https://www.edmunds.com/toyota/gr-supra/2020/trims/).
  - Don’t buy:
    - Heavily modified/tuned cars unless inspected by a Supra/BMW specialist. Tuning adds stress to the torque‑converter, engine internals and cooling system.
    - Cars without a clear service history or missing ADAS calibration records after front‑end repairs.
  - Pre‑purchase checklist (non‑negotiable):
    - Full service history, oil change records every 10k km (or closer for hard use).
    - Transmission fluid service record (if absent, budget an immediate flush).
    - VIN check for recall/consumer campaigns (Toyota recall lookup) [Toyota recall lookup](https://www.toyota.com/recall/).
    - ADAS function test and evidence of calibration after any panel repairs.
    - Detailed inspection for exterior panel gaps and paint condition (doors and A‑pillar trims).
    - Security check — ask seller whether car has been targeted for keyless/CAN theft; consider fitting additional mechanical locks/immobilisers.
- Final verdict
  - Buy if:
    - You value a sharp, well‑specced RWD sports coupe, can stomach higher running and parts costs, and you prioritise low‑km, unmodified cars with known service history.
    - Prefer the 3.0 Premium (2021–2023) for best blend of features, resale and performance. If you can find a 3.0 manual in good condition, grab it — they’re rare and hold value.
  - Walk away if:
    - The car’s history is fuzzy, it has aftermarket tuning without receipts, or the listing hides ADAS/paint/trim damage. At $100k+ you’re not buying a project.
  - If the NSW single listing at AUD $99,995 is real: treat it like a near‑new, nearly showroom specimen. Get the VIN, an independent inspection and full dealer documentation. Don’t let collector buzz make you buy sight unseen.
