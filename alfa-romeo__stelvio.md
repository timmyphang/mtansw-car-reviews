#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation / production timeframe / internal code  
  The Alfa Romeo Stelvio you see in Australian yards today stems from the 2017–present Stelvio line (often referenced as the 949 platform in owner communities). It was first revealed at the 2016 LA Auto Show and entered production at the end of 2016 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo_Stelvio)). Alfa has iterated the model with mid‑life upgrades and software/TSB fixes through to the 2024–2026 model years.

- Product hierarchy (what sits above / below)  
  The Stelvio is Alfa Romeo’s compact luxury SUV — above it sits the larger and more expensive Alfa Romeo Tonale‑adjacent offerings in future lineups (and the Quadrifoglio‑badged Stelvio sits at the top of this specific model family). Below the Stelvio in Alfa’s showroom hierarchy is the smaller‑sized crossover/compact offerings (the Tonale historically).

- Shared global platform architecture and other models using it  
  The Stelvio shares architecture and many components with the Alfa Romeo Giulia sedan (Giulia/Stelvio parts overlap is common, especially for driveline and infotainment components) — this helps with parts availability in markets that support the brand ([Go‑Parts Giulia & Stelvio guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-alfa-romeo-stelvio-alfa-romeo-giulia-2017-2025)). The 2.0‑litre turbo family and ZF automatic are used across several Stellantis/Alfa models, so some parts and specialist knowledge are reasonably common in independent workshops.

- Market‑specific engine sizes, outputs, transmission types and drive configurations (AU‑market flavours you’ll find)
  - 2.0‑litre turbocharged four‑cylinder — the mainstream petrol across Sprint/Ti/Veloce/Competizione trims (roughly ~280 hp in later tuning, see US/press spec comparisons) with standard 8‑speed automatic and AWD on many trims ([Autotrader trim notes](https://www.autotrader.com/comparisons/2024-alfa-romeo-stelvio-choosing-the-right-trim), [Edmunds trims](https://www.edmunds.com/alfa-romeo/stelvio/2024/trims/)).  
  - 2.9‑litre twin‑turbo V6 **Quadrifoglio** — high‑performance variant (~505 hp on Quadrifoglio spec), special servicing and higher running costs ([Autotrader, trim notes](https://www.autotrader.com/comparisons/2024-alfa-romeo-stelvio-choosing-the-right-trim)).  
  - 2.2‑litre diesel derivatives were available in some markets and are referenced in reliability write‑ups (common issues different from petrols — EGR/DPF related).  
  - Transmission: 8‑speed ZF torque‑converter automatic (**ZF 8HP**) across petrol/diesel variants; some earlier rear‑wheel drive base variants existed in other markets ([Wikipedia transmission note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo_Stelvio)).

- Explicit marketplace rivals  
  If you’re cross‑shopping, your main mainstream rivals are the BMW X3, Audi Q5, Mercedes‑Benz GLC, Volvo XC60 and Jaguar F‑Pace — essentially the compact/mid‑size premium SUV pack ([The Car Connection list](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/alfa-romeo_stelvio_choices), [MotorTrend overview](https://www.motortrend.com/cars/alfa-romeo/stelvio)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Alfa Romeo Stelvio?

Exterior:
- Panel fit and trim vulnerabilities
  - Repeated owner reports of loose or faded wheel‑arch and bumper trim, brittle plastic clips and fasteners that crack with age — the fix is usually inexpensive clip replacements but annoying (and rattles can mask more serious noises) ([Go‑Parts trim guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-alfa-romeo-stelvio-alfa-romeo-giulia-2017-2025), [Primescroll rivet guide](https://primescroll.com/alfa-romeo-stelvio-plastic-rivets/)).  
  - Hatch/boot misalignment and visible rear quarter adhesive marks have been reported in forums; some cars arrive from dealers with larger panel gaps requiring bodyshop attention ([Stelvio forum hatch misalignment](https://www.stelvioforum.com/threads/hatch-misalignment.15860/), [forum rear quarter defects](https://www.stelvioforum.com/threads/rear-quarter-panel-defects.22970/)).
- Paint quality
  - Paint chipping, especially around leading edges (hood near wipers) is a recurring owner complaint; dealers have occasionally declined warranty coverage, leaving DIY or paid repairs as options ([CarComplaints paint reports](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Alfa_Romeo/Stelvio/body_paint/index.shtml), [forum paint chipping](https://www.stelvioforum.com/threads/paint-chipping.14579/)).
- Safety systems and ADAS quirks
  - Multiple campaigns and technical bulletins: forward radar misalignment or temporary faults can cause ACC/FCW to intermittently go unavailable — sometimes a software calibration/cleaning resolves it, sometimes recalibration or hardware replacement is needed ([AboutAutomobile ACC TSBs](https://www.aboutautomobile.com/Technical-Service-Bulletin/2024/Alfa-Romeo/Stelvio/Adaptive-Cruise-Control), [Go‑Parts radar C1417 note](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-c1417-alfa-romeo-stelvio-2023-2025)).  
  - Rearview camera non‑display and other camera/infotainment issues have attracted recalls and dealer remedies (software update or component replacement) — check VIN recall history before purchase ([NHTSA Part 573 recall PDF](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2025/RCLRPT-25V667-9362.pdf), [KBB on camera recalls](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/alfa-romeo-recalls-stelvio-giulia-over-rearview-cameras/), [Cars.com recalls list](https://www.cars.com/research/alfa_romeo-stelvio/recalls/)).

Interior:
- Durability under heavy use (rideshare / family work)
  - The Stelvio’s interior styling is praised, but materials and switchgear that feel premium can hide durability weaknesses. Infotainment head units are tightly networked and VIN‑locked; a failed module can be expensive and may require dealer‑level reprogramming ([Go‑Parts audio/infotainment guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/audio-system-alfa-romeo-giulia-alfa-romeo-stelvio-2017-2026)).  
  - Wireless charging pad complaints (slow charging, 'FOD' foreign object detection with some phones) and a TSB exist for non‑charging pads in 2021–2023 cars ([CarAndProblems Qi TSB](https://carandproblems.com/bulletins/alfa-romeo/stelvio/10242750-alfa-romeo-stelvio-qi-charger), forum threads on slow charging).
- Ergonomics and ingress/egress
  - Door‑sill heights and sporty seat bolstering make ingress/egress slightly less friendly for older passengers — typical for a sporty premium SUV. Door check angles are firm; body trim gaps from earlier panel fit issues can affect perceived build quality.
- Electrical behaviors
  - Weak 12V battery symptoms are commonly tied to infotainment reboots and ADAS “unavailable” warnings; many intermittent electronic gremlins trace back to voltage instability rather than outright ECU failure ([Autoricambitritella ADAS/infotronics summary](https://autoricambitritella.it/en/blogs/problematiche-e-guasti-delle-vetture/alfa-romeo-stelvio-problemi-elettronica-adas-infotainment), forum threads).  
  - Infotainment black screens and reboots have repeated entries across 2020–2022 cars; VIN‑locked replacement parts and programming can make repairs expensive and time‑consuming ([Go‑Parts black screen guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/vehicle-connectivity-module-alfa-romeo-stelvio-alfa-romeo-giulia-2020-2022), forum discussion).

Mechanically:
- Brand engineering reputation and service culture
  - Alfa Romeo delivers sharp chassis and driver engagement, but historically trades off some robustness relative to German rivals — expect more frequent trips to the dealer or an Alfa‑specialist for software/ECU work and warranty fixes ([MotorTrend on character vs rivals](https://www.motortrend.com/cars/alfa-romeo/stelvio), community writeups).
- Theft / security vulnerabilities (real and actionable)
  - Recent global theft trends include CAN‑bus injection and headlight/CAN access exploits that affect many modern vehicles; Alfa Stelvio/Giulia wiring and radar plug access points have been abused in some thefts. Independent vendors market hardened covers and immobiliser add‑ons (e.g., Anti‑Theft Armor for radar plug, aftermarket immobilisers that require a PIN) ([The Register CAN injection story](https://www.theregister.com/2023/04/06/how-thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/), [Autoblog CAN update](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update), [JTD‑Performance anti‑theft armor](https://jtd-performance.com/en/product/anti-theft-armor-giulia-stelvio/), [Alfissimo immobilizer product](https://shop.alfissimo.com/inicio/2831-anti-theft-immobilizer-.html)).  
  - Practical countermeasures: mechanical steering wheel lock + immobiliser with PIN + garage/secure parking; consider the radar‑plug shield and an immobiliser that disables push‑button start — all inexpensive relative to replacing a stolen car.

Engines:
- General note: Alfa’s petrol 2.0 turbo and the high‑output 2.9 V6 have different failure modes; diesels bring a distinct set of EGR/DPF/injector risks. Below are italicized subsections for each engine variant commonly encountered.

  *2.0‑litre turbo (mainstream 4‑cylinder petrol)*
  - Failure points and common faults:
    - **Timing cover and oil leaks**: There is a documented TSB for timing cover oil leaks on certain 2.0L units built on/before May 2, 2024 — oil leaking into ancillaries can trigger engine protection codes ([Timing cover TSB](https://carandproblems.com/bulletins/alfa-romeo/stelvio/11018862-alfa-romeo-stelvio-timing-cover-oil-leak)).  
    - **Oil‑pump / oil pressure faults (P06DD)**: The two‑stage oil pump on this family has produced P06DD oil‑pump‑related failures; symptoms include limp modes and engine protection shutdowns ([Go‑Parts oil pump note](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/engine-oil-pump-alfa-romeo-giulia-alfa-romeo-stelvio-dodge-hornet-2018-2025)).  
    - **Turbocharger and oil feed leaks**: Owners report turbocharger oil feed/return leaks, and related performance loss or smoke; early diagnosis and repair are advisable.  
    - **Higher oil consumption**: Some cars show increased oil consumption over long term.
  - Preventative maintenance and intervals (pragmatic Australian approach)
    - Oil and filter: every 10,000 km with high‑quality synthetic oil (shorter than some OEM recommendations but cheaper than an engine repair).  
    - Inspection for timing‑cover seepage and oil pump codes at every major service (10,000–15,000 km). If you buy a 2018–2022 car, ask service history for any timing‑cover work or TSB compliance.  
    - Turbocharger and charge‑pipe inspection at 30,000 km intervals; if the car has EGR delete or aftermarket tuning, be cautious — these increase risk.
  - Sources: [CarAndProblems timing‑cover TSB](https://carandproblems.com/bulletins/alfa-romeo/stelvio/11018862-alfa-romeo-stelvio-timing-cover-oil-leak), [Go‑Parts oil pump article](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/engine-oil-pump-alfa-romeo-giulia-alfa-romeo-stelvio-dodge-hornet-2018-2025), [What‑Breaks engine guide](https://what-breaks.com/alfa-romeo/stelvio-949/).

  *2.9‑litre twin‑turbo V6 (Quadrifoglio)*
  - Failure points and common faults:
    - High‑performance hardware requires meticulous servicing — turbo and cooling systems are complex and expensive if they go wrong. Expect higher running costs, premium oil, and vigilant cooling system servicing.  
    - No single systemic catastrophic fault widely reported in the dataset here, but used V6s bring higher repair bills and potential for neglected service in trade‑ins that hit the used market.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Service at 10,000 km intervals with strict oil quality and coolant checks; if you intend to push the car hard, inspect turbos and chargecooler plumbing at 30k km.
  - Sources: Trim and performance context ([Autotrader trims](https://www.autotrader.com/comparisons/2024-alfa-romeo-stelvio-choosing-the-right-trim)).

  *2.2‑litre diesel*
  - Failure points and common faults:
    - Typical diesel issues: EGR clogging, DPF problems, high‑pressure injector or fuel‑pump faults — more likely on short‑trip cars that don’t get hot‑soak cleans. Diesel high‑pressure pump/injector failures appear in aggregated owner reports ([Autodoc list](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-alfa-romeo-stelvio)).  
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Longer runs at highway speeds regularly to keep DPF/EGR healthy; injectors and HPFP should be checked at 60k–100k km depending on use.
  - Sources: [What‑Breaks Stelvio guide](https://what-breaks.com/alfa-romeo/stelvio-949/), [Autodoc common problems list](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-alfa-romeo-stelvio).

Transmission:
- The Stelvio uses the **ZF 8HP** eight‑speed automatic in most petrol/diesel models ([Wikipedia transmission note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfa_Romeo_Stelvio)). Known failure modes:
  - Torque‑converter lock‑up clutch issues causing harsh shifts, jerking, and transmission fault, sometimes with codes like P0740/P2764; these can present intermittently and escalate to a limp mode or full transmission replacement if neglected ([CarAndProblems transmission jerking bulletin](https://carandproblems.com/bulletins/alfa-romeo/stelvio/10221097-alfa-romeo-stelvio-transmission-jerking), [forum transmission problems](https://www.stelvioforum.com/threads/transmission-problems.14948/)).  
  - Software updates have fixed some shift maps and adaptative learning behaviour, but hardware torque‑converter wear can recur. ZF service communications and dealer software packs have reduced the frequency but not eliminated it ([Alfa Romeo‑Club ZF note](https://en.alfaromeo-club.com/article/most-common-problems-with-the-alfa-romeo-stelvio-152)).  
- Maintenance and owner strategy:
  - If it hasn’t been done, a transmission fluid and filter service at 60,000–80,000 km at a reputable specialist is a sensible preventative measure (ZF gearboxes tolerate some variation but refreshed oil helps clutch longevity). Documented software updates are also valuable — ask the dealer for the latest transmission software and if TSB updates have been applied.
- Sources: [CarComplaints transmission page](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Alfa_Romeo/Stelvio/transmission/), [CarAndProblems bulletin](https://carandproblems.com/bulletins/alfa-romeo/stelvio/10221097-alfa-romeo-stelvio-transmission-jerking).

#### 3. Should you buy a used Alfa Romeo Stelvio?

- NSW dealer market snapshot (live)
  - Active listings: 13  
  - Price range: AUD $60,888 – $142,990  
  - Median price: AUD $75,939  
  - Year range: 2021 – 2025  
  - Median odometer: 343 km  
  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 13  
  (Seller data supplied in your NSW scraper snapshot — use it to bargain and set expectations.)

- Current used marketplace entry price up to ceiling price  
  - Expect to pay from about **AUD $60k** for an earlier or lower‑spec 2021–2022 Ti/Sprint, through to well over **AUD $140k** for a newer Quadrifoglio or lightly optioned demonstrator — the NSW sample confirms a wide spread (AUD $60,888–$142,990) with a median just under $76k.

- Depreciation metrics (what the numbers show)
  - Alfa Stelvio depreciation is steeper than many German rivals: iSeeCars reports roughly ~45% down at 3 years and CarEdge suggests 67% at 5 years under some assumptions — this means you can buy relatively modern examples cheaper than their German competitors but resale risk is higher ([iSeeCars depreciation data](https://www.iseecars.com/car/alfa-romeo/stelvio/resale-value), [CarEdge 5‑year number](https://caredge.com/alfa-romeo/stelvio/depreciation)).  
  - Pragmatic take: pay less up front and buy into the Stelvio’s driving character — but buy with eyes open to higher running and repair costs and to the fact Alfas historically hold value less well than BMW/Audi/Mercedes.

- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy
  - Fuel: US EPA/MPG figures translate to roughly **~9.4 L/100km** combined for the 2.0L in EPA parlance (25 mpg ≈ 9.4 L/100km) — real‑world Fuelly data and owner reports often show similar or slightly worse consumption depending on driving style ([MPG Buddy / FuelEconomy summary](https://mpgbuddy.com/cars/alfa-romeo/stelvio/2024), [Fuelly actuals](https://www.fuelly.com/car/alfa_romeo/stelvio)).  
  - Servicing & repairs: Alfa dealer labour and parts are pricier than mainstream brands; warranty repairs can shield you initially but second‑owner bills for infotainment or transmission fixes can quickly add thousands. CarEdge estimates 5‑year ownership costs in US metrics that include fuel/depreciation/maintenance — translate that risk to AUD and assume higher dealer labour rates in Australia. See CarEdge for a US baseline cost breakdown ([CarEdge costs page](https://caredge.com/alfa-romeo/stelvio/costs)).  
  - Insurance: Expect premium SUV insurance levels — Quadrifoglio will attract significantly higher premiums.  
  - TCO comparison of petrol vs alternative powertrains: At the moment in Australia the Stelvio’s mainstream 2.0 petrol is usually the cheapest total ownership path unless you do huge suburban mileage where a diesel might pay off (diesel caveats: DPF/EGR costs). There aren’t widely available hybrid or EV Stelvios in current lineups that make a clear TCO win for buyers here — paying a premium for a Quadrifoglio is largely about performance, not long‑term savings. Use your annual km to model fuel spend (example: at 15,000 km/yr and 9.4 L/100km, fuel = ~1,410 L/yr; at AUD $2.00/L = AUD $2,820/yr fuel bill).

- Depreciation vs rivals — short verdict
  - Expect to lose more value over three years compared to a BMW X3 or Audi Q5. If you want character and don't care about maximum resale, the Stelvio gives more driving soul per dollar in the short term. If you want the safest resale, pay a German premium instead.

- Final buying recommendation (clear, practical)
  - If you love this car for how it drives: buy a **2021–2023 Ti or Veloce** with a clean service history and documented completion of any TSB/recall work. These model years capture early fixes while still being deeply discounted relative to new. Aim for examples with:
    - Full dealer service history (ask for proof of any timing‑cover, oil‑pump or transmission software work).  
    - A clean VIN recall check (use NHTSA or Alfa dealer VIN check — US recalls apply as indicative; local AU VIN history matters too) ([NHTSA recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)).  
    - Low to moderate kms and evidence the car hasn’t lived a short‑trip life (avoid diesel short‑trip cars).  
  - Avoid buying:
    - An uninsured Quadrifoglio unless you’re prepared for substantially higher running and repair bills.  
    - Cars with unexplained infotainment or ADAS warnings that the seller cannot prove were fixed — these can be expensive to repair.  
  - Sweet spot: A well‑maintained **2021–2022 Ti/Veloce** with documented dealer work and a median NSW market price around the AUD $70–85k that covers later model year improvements but still offers value relative to new.

Buy one if: you want a compact‑luxury SUV that actually drives like more than a sofa on wheels and you can accept higher potential running costs and a bit of Italian temperament. Don’t buy one if you want the absolute lowest risk, highest resale or cheapest dealer servicing — German alternatives are easier to live with for long, worry‑free ownership.

If you want a short checklist before you sign: full service history, proof of TSB/recall actions, transmission software updated, no persistent ADAS warnings, and a recent diagnostic scan showing no stored drivetrain faults. Get a pre‑purchase inspection with a tech who knows ZF transmissions and Alfa electronics — it will pay for itself.
