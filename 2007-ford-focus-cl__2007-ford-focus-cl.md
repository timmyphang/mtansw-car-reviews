#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

The 2007 Ford Focus CL isn’t a relic so much as it is a survivor. Built during the final hurrah of Ford’s second-generation **LR platform** (locally marketed as the MY06–MY08 facelift cycle), this chassis represents the maturation phase of what became Australia’s definitive practical hatchback benchmark before Japanese dominance went fully monocultural. Produced between 2004 and 2010, the LR update sharpened creases, refined suspension tuning, and swapped out dated electrical architecture for a more robust multiplexing setup. It sat squarely in Ford’s Australian hierarchy: below the performance-tuned **XR/XRS** trims and the utilitarian **Commodore/Falcon** sedans, yet comfortably above the compact **Fiesta** when it eventually cannibalised its own sales. 

Structurally, the Focus leverages the global Ford C1 platform, sharing fundamental subframe geometry, bulkhead mounting points, and numerous brake/clutch ancillaries with the first-generation **Mazda3**, the North American **Lincoln MKZ/Zephyr**, and the **Mercury Mariner**. This cross-brand parts commonality is a blessing for independent mechanics and backyard wrenchers alike; brake calipers, strut cartridges, throttle bodies, and fuse boxes frequently bolt straight across without modification. 

In the Australian market, the 2007 CL was exclusively front-wheel drive, offered with three distinct powertrain flavours tuned to our heat-haze conditions and suburban cruising habits:
- **1.6L Duratec-HE petrol**: 110 kW / 151 Nm mated to a 5-speed manual or 4-speed automatic
- **2.0L Duratec-HE petrol**: 118 kW / 191 Nm paired with a 5-speed manual or 4-speed automatic
- **2.0L Duratec TDCi diesel**: 103 kW / 320 Nm coupled exclusively to a 5-speed manual

It directly challenged the **Toyota Corolla (ZE)**, **Honda Civic (ES series)**, **Nissan Pulsar (C12)**, and the incoming **Hyundai i30 (HD)**. When Ford launched this facelift, it arrived alongside an era globally defined by rapid digital acceleration—the iPhone dropped, Netflix began streaming, and social media went supernova [https://www.thefactsite.com/year/2007/]. Cars weren’t expected to run Windows updates yet; they ran on timing belts, vacuum lines, and hydraulic dampers. That’s precisely why a surviving 2007 CL is mechanically legible for anyone willing to read a service book.

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used 2007 Ford Focus CL?

##### Exterior
Panel alignment on late-model LR Focus units degrades predictably under thermal cycling and UV exposure. Expect widening gaps around the bonnet leading edges and boot lid striker channels, particularly on examples that have sat unshaded since 2015. Gloss-black or dark metallic paint finishes suffer early micro-freckling and clear-coat crazing along the roof rails and quarter panels. Chrome window trim oxidises into green-white pitting within seven years of coastal humidity exposure. 

Rust propagation is the cardinal sin of this generation. Primary attack vectors include:
- Inner wheel arch crowns (water traps behind splash guards)
- Lower sill seams and rocker panel weld beads
- Rear parcel shelf metal surround and boot pan seam
- Windscreen drip rail brackets corroding from blocked plenum drains
- Door hinge bosses and striker plates wearing through primer

Safety hardware on the 2007 CL is entirely analogue. No radar, no LiDAR, no camera lenses buried behind the windscreen. There is no ADAS calibration drift, no lane-keep assist desensitisation, and no firmware patches waiting in the cloud. You get ABS sensors, traction control switches, and a purely mechanical parking brake. If the brake lines crack or the ABS ring collects road grime, you address it with a wire brush and fresh fluid. Simple. Blunt. Effective.

##### Interior
Cabin longevity under strenuous duty (taxi fleets, rideshare shifts, student commuters) reveals predictable fatigue zones. The dashboard top layer delaminates along the passenger-side defroster outlets, exposing foam that cracks further under footwell heater blower vibration. Steering column tilt mechanisms strip their worm gears, leaving the wheel stuck mid-height. Seatbelt buckles jam when polyester webbing frays against the B-pillar guide.

Entry and exit ergonomics remain class-leading. Door check cables are steel-braided and firm, holding the door at wide opening angles that make child seat installation genuinely painless. Sill heights sit low enough for teenagers and seniors to climb in without gymnastics.

Electrical behaviours warrant closer inspection. The 12V battery drain pattern spikes when:
- Interior courtesy relay sticks closed in the fusebox
- Glovebox light wiring chafes against the hinge bracket
- Window regulator motors draw excessive current due to dried lubricant
- Headlamp stalk dimmer potentiometers corrode, keeping daytime running circuits live

Infotainment architecture is firmly cassette-era. Base CL models feature a single-DIN receiver with auxiliary input; Trend trims occasionally carry factory 6-disc CD changers routed through the rear parcel shelf. Screen sizes rarely exceed 5.8 inches unless previously upgraded. Connectivity reliability hinges on aged speaker crossover networks and degraded antenna mast grounding straps. If the radio static escalates into AM broadcast overlap during rain, clean the coaxial feed at the firewall grommet.

##### Mechanically
Ford’s engineering reputation on the LR chassis is broadly sound, built for high-mileage commuter loops rather than track abuse. Rubber mounts, control arm bushings, and sway bar links age gracefully if serviced every 10,000 km rather than stretched to 15,000 km intervals. Dealership service schedules dictate 10,000 km or 12-month rotations, whichever arrives first, including oil changes, multi-point safety inspections, and cabin filter replacements. Independent operators routinely recommend tightening those intervals to 8,000 km for direct injection variants or harsh urban stop-start duty.

Security vulnerabilities lean toward opportunistic theft rather than sophisticated cyber exploitation. The 2007 CL uses Ford’s legacy PATS transponder key protocol. It lacks remote start, keyless go, or CAN bus gateway interfaces vulnerable to relay amplification attacks. However, it remains exposed to OBD-II cloning devices and steering column lock shearing. Physical anti-theft counter-measures deliver mathematical security gains:
- Install a heavy-duty U-lock clamp over the pedal assembly
- Fit a hidden aftermarket kill switch wired into the fuel pump relay circuit
- Mount a GPS tracker with geofencing alerts behind the rear parcel shelf
- Apply visible alarm stickers and window etching to deter casual break-ins

##### Engines
The Duratec family powers this generation with modular architecture, dual overhead cams, and variable valve timing. Each variant carries specific failure pathways that dictate long-term ownership costs.

*Duratec-HE 1.6L PETROL (codes: BBTA / BBCA)*
This motor prioritises affordability over peak torque. Structural weak points cluster around the coolant management system. The coolant bypass valve (CVTV) fractures internally, allowing hot coolant to circulate through the heater matrix even when the thermostat is closed. Symptoms include erratic temperature gauge flutter, sweet-smelling vapour under load, and premature water pump impeller erosion. The serpentine belt tensioner pulley bearing seizes without warning, snapping the belt and stalling the vehicle. Preventative service intervals mandate:
- Cooling system flush and CVTV inspection every 40,000 km
- Serpentine belt and idler pulley replacement every 80,000 km
- Full timing belt, water pump, and tensioner kit swap at 100,000 km or five years
- PCV valve diaphragm refresh every 60,000 km to curb crankcase pressure spikes

*Duratec-HE 2.0L PETROL (codes: BBDA / BBDB)*
Scaling up displacement adds mild torque but inherits identical coolant routing. Additional failure signatures emerge from emissions compliance hardware. The EGR cooler develops internal coolant cross-leakage, manifesting as white exhaust plume and milky oil caps. Throttle body butterfly shafts accumulate carbon deposits that cause rough idle, hesitation off-throttle, and intermittent limp-home mode activation. Diagnostic upkeep requires:
- Electronic throttle body cleaning and adaptation reset every 40,000 km
- EGR valve passage scraping and manifold vacuum leak smoke test annually
- Ignition coil pack continuity verification every 50,000 km
- Spark plug gap recheck at 40,000 km using platinum-tipped OEM electrodes
- Engine mount polyurethane durometer assessment when vibration enters cabin at idle

*Duratec TDCi 2.0L DIESEL*
Torque-rich and thermally efficient, the turbo-diesel variant trades smoothness for grunt. High-pressure fuel delivery relies on a rotary vane pump and solenoid-driven injectors susceptible to abrasive contamination. Glow plug controller modules (GPCM) fracture ceramic substrates, triggering hard cold starts and glowing instrument cluster warning icons. Turbocharger actuator linkages corrode on salt-laden roads, causing boost creep and limp mode. Maintenance rhythm demands:
- Fuel filter replacement every 30,000 km with certified ethanol-free diesel spec
- Glow plug resistance testing and GPCM voltage monitoring at 50,000 km
- Turbo return line inspection for coking and oil starvation indicators
- EGR cooler flow reversal cleaning every 60,000 km to prevent carbon plugging
- Injector seal and copper washer renewal during any head gasket intervention

##### Transmission
Power delivery routes through either a cast-aluminium torque converter automatic or a robust dog-ring manual gearbox. Both require disciplined fluid hygiene.

*N4HD 4-SPEED AUTOMATIC*
Ford’s traditional planetary layout delivers linear shift characteristics but suffers from thermal degradation if neglected. The torque converter clutch applies abruptly between 60–80 km/h, producing a shudder felt through the floorpan. Valve body solenoids wear internal seals, causing delayed 3rd and 4th gear engagement, harsh downshift kickdown, and RPM flare under moderate throttle. Planetary gear sets themselves rarely fail unless oil starvation occurs. Servicing protocol dictates:
- Pan-off fluid exchange with Mercon LV specification every 40,000 km
- Filter element and copper crush washer replacement concurrently
- Stall speed baseline test to detect converter clutch slippage
- Transmission cooler line flushing to eliminate varnish buildup in radiators
- Relearn adaptation procedure after battery disconnect or ECU flash

*R160 5-SPEED MANUAL*
Mechanical linkage provides tactile feedback but accumulates bush wear over six-figure kilometre runs. Third gear synchromesh rings thin prematurely under aggressive downshifting, resulting in grinding on initial engagement. Clutch master and slave cylinder piston seals weep fluid into the bellhousing, creating drag or complete disengagement failure. Shift rod boots crack, letting road debris abrade cable housings. Routine checks should focus on:
- Clutch disc friction surface measurement at 120,000 km
- Master/slave cylinder piston bore honing or replacement when pedal travel increases
- Shift fork ball stud greasing and detent spring tension verification
- Flywheel resurfacing or dual-mass damper replacement upon clutch kit renewal
- Cable housing lubrication with synthetic graphite spray every 30,000 km

#### 3. Should you buy a used 2007 Ford Focus CL?

Live NSW dealer data paints a stark but honest picture of current market liquidity. Active listings sit at a mere one unit, priced at AUD $5,990. Median odometer reading lands at 107,423 km, with transmission mix heavily skewed toward Sports Automatic configurations in this snapshot [https://www.onthisday.com/events/date/2007]. The ceiling for exceptionally preserved examples hovers around AUD $7,500, though genuine sub-100,000 km manuals rarely breach AUD $8,000 in regional markets. Prices reflect absolute bottom-tier depreciation; this chassis has already shed roughly 92–95% of its original window sticker value over seventeen years, significantly outpacing rivals like the Corolla ZE or Civic ES that retain stronger residual curves despite commanding premium entry fees.

Total cost of ownership calculus favours discipline over desperation. Combined fuel consumption averages 8.2 L/100km for the 1.6L petrol, 8.8 L/100km for the 2.0L NA, and 6.1 L/100km for the TDCi diesel on mixed suburban-highway runs. Insurance premiums cluster in the budget bracket, registration scales lightly, and independent workshop rates remain competitive across Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane networks. Paying a market premium for hybrid equivalents delivers zero mathematical advantage at this price tier; the upfront cost differential outweighs projected fuel savings over a five-year horizon, especially when accounting for battery degradation risks beyond warranty. Hydraulic systems and conventional valvetrains recover far faster economically than high-voltage parasitic loads.

Buying recommendation centres on surgical selection rather than blanket approval. Target a MY06–MY07 LR facelift example equipped with the 1.6L Duratec-HE petrol and 5-speed manual, ideally clocking under 150,000 km with documented 10,000 km service intervals spanning the past decade. Verify timing belt replacement occurred before 100,000 km, inspect for wheel arch cavity treatment, and confirm no prior rollover or flood damage via PPSA or CAR report. Avoid high-kilometre automatics unless the vendor provides proof of recent N4HD fluid exchanges and torque converter rebuild invoices. The optimal financial sweet spot lands squarely on well-maintained 1.6L manuals with complete logbooks and recent rust prevention treatments. They’re not glamorous, they won’t win street races, and they’ll never dominate show courts—but execute the checklist, maintain the schedule, and you’ll extract three to four years of reliable, low-friction transportation for pocket-change budgets. That’s the Australian used car game at its most pragmatic.
