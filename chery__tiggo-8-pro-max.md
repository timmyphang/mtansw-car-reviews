#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation / codes: Tiggo 8 Pro Max — the T18 family built on Chery’s modular **T1X** platform (current Pro / Pro Max variants launched 2022–2024 in many export markets).
  - Production timeframe (relevant to Australia): 2023–present for the Pro/Pro Max family; Australian new/used listings are dominated by 2024 examples.
  - Internal architecture name: **T1X** platform. See Chery’s global product page for the Pro Max and platform notes: [Chery International - Tiggo 8 Pro Max](https://www.cheryinternational.com/pc/models/tiggo/tiggo8promaxt18fl3/index.shtml) and [Tiggo 8 (T1X platform)](https://www.cheryinternational.com/pc/models/tiggo/tiggo8/index.shtml).

- Product hierarchy:
  - Sits at the top of Chery’s Tiggo 8 line-up in Australia as the flagship seven‑seat offering (Pro Max > Pro > Tiggo 8 standard variants). The Pro Max is marketed as the “premium” 7‑seat Tiggo 8 with the stronger 2.0 turbo engine and extra tech.

- Shared platform / parts implications:
  - Underpinned by the T1X modular architecture used across Tiggo 8 variants (Pro / Pro Max) — that helps parts commonality across Tiggo 8 family models and improves local parts availability compared with unique bespoke platforms. See Chery T1X notes: [Chery International (Tiggo 8)](https://www.cheryinternational.com/pc/models/tiggo/tiggo8/index.shtml).
  - Supplier partnerships (Bosch, Benteler) are flagged in launch materials, which suggests mainstream component sourcing rather than boutique parts that are impossible to replace locally: [DriveArabia launch notes](https://www.drivearabia.com/news/2024/01/16/chery-launches-the-2024-tiggo8-pro-max-awrostamani-group/).

- Market‑specific powertrains & drivetrains (summarised from export/AU spec sources):
  - **2.0TGDI** — ~254 hp (around 187 kW) and 390 Nm; paired to a 7‑speed dual‑clutch transmission (7DCT) and available with AWD on Pro Max variants in many markets. (Chery spec pages and launch PR: [Chery International](https://www.cheryinternational.com/pc/models/tiggo/tiggo8promaxt18fl3/index.shtml), [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/chery-launches-the-2024-tiggo8-pro-max-redefining-the-future-of-driving-e7t473x6).)
  - **1.6T** — turbo petrol variants exist in some regions (paired to a 7DCT in some specs) — lower output, used across Tiggo 8 family pages: [Parkers / Automotive24](https://www.parkers.co.uk/chery/tiggo-8/suv-2025/specs/), [Automotive24](https://automotive24.center/article/chery-tiggo-8-8-plus-8-pro-pro-max-i-t18-2018-present-engines-transmissions-and-dimensions).
  - **1.5T PHEV / “Super Hybrid” (CSH)** — plug‑in / strong hybrid variants in the Tiggo 8 range offering large electric‑assist and excellent economy (18.4 kWh battery figures and long electric range referenced in spec roundups): [Carwow / Parkers / CarsGuide long‑term notes](https://www.carwow.co.uk/chery/tiggo-8), [Parkers MPG & running costs](https://www.parkers.co.uk/chery/tiggo-8/review/mpg-running-costs/), [CarsGuide long‑term PHEV test](https://www.carsguide.com.au/family/chery-tiggo-8-ultimate-super-hybrid-long-term-p1-100695).

- Typical rivals in the Australian used market:
  - Established seven‑seat and large mid‑size SUVs: Mazda CX‑8, Hyundai Santa Fe, Kia Sorento, Mitsubishi Outlander (PHEV option), Toyota Kluger / Fortuner (depending on buyer needs). Market comparison resources: [CarsGuide Tiggo 8 Pro Max comparisons](https://www.carsguide.com.au/chery/tiggo-8-pro-max/vs) and editorials: [WhichCar review of Pro Max](https://www.whichcar.com.au/reviews/chery-tiggo-8-pro-max-review-seven-seat-large-suv).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Chery Tiggo 8 Pro Max?

- **Exterior**
  - Panel fit and finish:
    - Recurrent owner reports of inconsistent panel alignment, bonnet/door gaps, and trim fit issues across markets — buyer beware and check gaps in daylight and at different angles: community threads and problem aggregators document this as a recurring theme: [PakWheels owner thread](https://www.pakwheels.com/forums/t/chery-tiggo-8-dedicated-discussion-owner-fan-club-thread/2690278?page=19), and consolidated complaints pages catalog similar fit‑and‑finish notes: [Chery kronik issues](https://cherykroniksorunlari.com/au-en/chery-tiggo-8-pro-max/body-panels/).
  - Trim vulnerabilities:
    - Complaints about chrome trim defects and peeling finishes in export markets — check exposed chrome around doors/boot and lower bumper trims. Example consumer thread on chrome defects: [Şikayetvar chrome defect thread](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/chery-us/chery-tiggo-8-pro-max-chrome-trim-defect).
  - Paint durability:
    - Some owners report paint peeling or dulling, especially on early build cars — inspect in direct light and around wheelarches and bonnet edges (discussion: [Gari.pk paint problems](https://www.gari.pk/cars-motors/chery/tiggo-8-pro/paint-problems/)).
  - Recalls / safety items:
    - Official Australian recall: a brake pipe may chafe against the engine and become damaged; this led to a recall for certain VINs across 2023–2026 Tiggo 8 Pro/Pro Max vehicles — verify completion on any used car. See the official recall page and Australian press coverage: [Vehicle Recalls REC-006636](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006636), [Drive article on the recall](https://www.drive.com.au/news/2023-2026-chery-tiggo-8-pro-max-recalled-in-australia-over-brake-fault/), [CarExpert coverage](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/chery-tiggo-8-recalled).
  - ADAS quirks:
    - The Pro Max carries a broad ADAS suite (ANCAP lists AEB, LKA, LDW, ELK, speed assistance and more). However, early owner feedback flagged over‑aggressive AEB and some false braking interventions; Chery has worked to re‑tune software locally and in some markets issued updates. Confirm ADAS firmware has been updated by a dealer and validate behaviour during a test drive: [ANCAP safety page](https://www.ancap.com.au/safety-ratings/chery/tiggo-8-pro-max/28d53e), and reporting on ADAS tuning: [Drive – local ADAS tuning story](https://www.drive.com.au/news/chery-improves-safety-tech-in-new-suvs-through-testing-day-in-day-out-on-local-roads/). Complaints and anecdotes aggregated here: [AEB complaints aggregator](https://cherykroniksorunlari.com/au-en/chery-tiggo-8-pro-max/safety-systems/autonomous-braking-errors/).

- **Interior**
  - Cabin durability:
    - Materials are generally serviceable but not German‑luxury. Repeated owner threads flag trim rattles, loose plastic clips and scuffs when cars are used for high‑turnover duties (rideshare/school runs) — check door sills, map pockets and 3rd‑row seat mechanisms for wear: [PakWheels forums](https://www.pakwheels.com/forums/t/chery-tiggo-8-dedicated-discussion-owner-fan-club-thread/2690278?page=20).
  - Entry / egress:
    - Three‑row access is typical for this class. Check second‑row sliding/tilt function and third‑row seat release catches — cheap‑feeling latch hardware is often the weak link on long‑life fleet cars.
  - Electrical & infotainment:
    - The Tiggo 8 family uses a large infotainment / driver display arrangement (reports cite 12.3‑inch screens in many trims). Owner reports of screen freezes, lag and occasional reboots — simple restarts sometimes fix it, but persistent faults have been raised in complaints across multiple markets: [Automotive24 interior/specs & screen sizing](https://automotive24.center/article/chery-tiggo-8-8-plus-8-pro-pro-max-i-t18-2018-present-interior-cabin-trim-levels-and-updates), troubleshooting video of infotainment restart trick: [YouTube fix video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X0T3s2zmCZs), and owner complaint threads: [Sikayetvar complaint & MyCarFault summary](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/chery-us/chery-hasnt-fixed-my-tiggo-8-pros-infotainment-freezing-and-lagging-how-can-i-make-them-address-it-q-14366), [MyCarFault infotainment summary](https://mycarfault.com/common-problems/chery-tiggo-8-pro).
  - 12V battery / parasitic drains:
    - Several owners report unexplained electrical gremlins and parasitic draws that can flatten the 12V if the car stands for weeks. Confirm battery health and ask the dealer for evidence of software patches or module reprogramming if prior electrical faults were logged: see owner reports aggregated: [MyCarFault / AutoSeeks](https://mycarfault.com/common-problems/chery-tiggo-8-pro), [AutoSeeks common problems](https://autoseeks.com/common-problems-in-chery-models/).

- **Mechanically**
  - Brand engineering reputation:
    - Chery has improved design and supplier sourcing in recent generations, but long‑term reliability and dealer support remain an open question relative to established Japanese/Korean rivals — there are reports of high‑severity failures in isolated cases and frequent calls to dealers for driveline or steering work: community & product‑review reports: [ProductReview owner complaints](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/chery-tiggo-8), [DriveAuthority global review](https://driveauthority.com/chery-tiggo-8-review-global/).
  - High‑priority security vulnerabilities:
    - The Tiggo 8 Pro Max uses passive entry / push‑button start and an immobiliser. Like most modern cars, it is exposed to relay attacks and potential CAN‑bus exploits if a thief has physical or wireless access to your key or OBD port. Academic and industry write‑ups on CAN injection and relay attacks show this is a general class risk — not unique to Chery: [PlaxidityX theft threat report summary](https://plaxidityx.com/resources/report/automotive-theft-threat-report-2026/), [Vicone CAN bus explainer](https://vicone.com/blog/how-to-get-away-with-car-theft-unveiling-the-dark-side-of-the-can-bus/). Practical dealer-level key programming notes (showing dealer PIN requirements) are described in a tech writeup: [MechanicWiki key programming guide](https://www.mechanicwiki.com/2026/06/chery-tiggo-8-pro-max-smart-key.html).
  - Suggested anti‑theft steps:
    - Always fit at least one physical deterrent (visible steering‑wheel lock).
    - Consider an OBD‑port lock and an aftermarket immobiliser or tracker with backup battery.
    - Keep keys in Faraday pouches at night and avoid leaving spare keys in obvious places.

- **Engines**
  - The Tiggo 8 family is offered with multiple engines across markets. Owner reports and diagnostics callouts differ by powertrain; read each closely.

  - *2.0TGDI (2.0 turbocharged GDI, ~254 hp / 390 Nm)*  
    - Owner reports: strong mid‑range performance but some owners note turbo‑era sensitivities — oil consumption or turbo oil feed/return issues have been noted anecdotally in forum posts (monitor oil level closely). Performance spec and economy: Zawya and Chery materials list 254 hp, ~7.39 L/100km official figure: [Zawya launch spec](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/chery-launches-the-2024-tiggo8-pro-max-redefining-the-future-of-driving-e7t473x6), [Chery international page](https://www.cheryinternational.com/pc/models/tiggo/tiggo8promaxt18fl3/index.shtml).  
    - Typical failure points (owner‑reported / forum‑based): turbo oil leaks, oil dilution/emulsification reports in some threads, and sensitivity to low‑quality oil or extended oil change intervals. See forum compilations & owner threads: [PakWheels forum complaint](https://www.pakwheels.com/forums/t/chery-tiggo-8-dedicated-discussion-owner-fan-club-thread/2690278?page=20).  
    - Preventative maintenance: adhere to factory scheduled servicing; for Australian ownership, follow the local spec sheet and dealer program (check the official spec sheet) — recommended checks: oil and filter every 10,000 km with high‑quality synthetic oil, inspect turbo oil feed lines at every service, and track oil consumption. See Chery Australia spec sheet: [Tiggo 8 Spec Sheet (AU)](https://cherymotor.com.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/012_24_Tiggo_8_Spec_Sheet_v112.pdf).

  - *1.6T (turbo petrol variations found in some markets)*  
    - Owner reports: the 1.6 turbo tends to be tuned for efficiency but can show drivability complaints when paired to the DCT (low‑speed jerkiness discussed below). Fuel economy claims vs real‑world vary widely: [Automotive24 engine roundup](https://automotive24.center/article/chery-tiggo-8-8-plus-8-pro-pro-max-i-t18-2018-present-engines-transmissions-and-dimensions), [Parkers specs](https://www.parkers.co.uk/chery/tiggo-8/suv-2025/specs/).  
    - Preventative maintenance: follow 10,000 km oil / filter intervals; when used in stop/start urban duty, inspect intake and PCV systems for soot buildup and perform more frequent oil analysis.

  - *1.5T PHEV / “Super Hybrid” (CSH, plug‑in)*  
    - Owner & test reports: delivers the best running costs if you can charge regularly. CarsGuide long‑term experiences and Parkers/Carwow testing show dramatic fuel reductions with a PHEV, often in the low single‑digit L/100km when charged regularly: [CarsGuide long‑term PHEV test](https://www.carsguide.com.au/family/chery-tiggo-8-ultimate-super-hybrid-long-term-p1-100695), [DriveAuthority / Carwow spec writeups](https://driveauthority.com/chery-tiggo-8-review-global/).  
    - Typical failure points: PHEV complexity adds potential battery/electric drive issues long term — battery thermal management, high‑voltage cable integrity and inverter electronics are the usual risk spots (owner pools are younger; long‑term battery reliability for Chery PHEV is not yet proven in Australia).  
    - Preventative maintenance: follow the same dealer service cadence, but factor in periodic HV system checks and ensure the car has full charging history (charger faults and repeated deep discharges can shorten pack life). Refer to manufacturer guidance in the AU spec sheet: [Chery AU spec sheet](https://cherymotor.com.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/012_24_Tiggo_8_Spec_Sheet_v112.pdf).

- **Transmission**
  - The Pro Max commonly pairs the 2.0TGDI to a **7‑speed DCT (7DCT)**. Across owner forums and independent reviews this gearbox is the single most‑reported annoyance. Reported behaviour includes:
    - Low‑speed jerkiness, delayed take‑off and lurching in stop‑start traffic.
    - Software‑related shift harshness that in some markets was partially mitigated by ECU/TCU software updates — confirm updates have been applied. Owner/issue aggregators and reviews: [Automotive24 on common problems](https://automotive24.center/article/common-drawbacks-and-problems-of-the-chery-tiggo-8-8-plus-8-pro-pro-max-i-t18-generation-2018-present-what-buyers-need-to-know), [DriveAuthority notes DCT issues](https://driveauthority.com/chery-tiggo-8-review-global/), and broader transmission symptom writeups: [MyCarFault DCT summary](https://mycarfault.com/common-problems/chery-tiggo-8-pro).
  - Known failure modes:
    - Early clutch wear (typical of DCTs used in heavy urban duty), mechatronic unit problems, fluid leaks. Some owners report needing clutch or mechatronic attention well before what they'd expect from a traditional torque‑converter auto.
  - Buyer checklist:
    - Test drive through low‑speed manoeuvres, take note of lag, shudder or slip during 0–30 km/h transitions.
    - Ask dealer for TCU firmware update history and for evidence of DCT fluid service (if any) — older cars used for taxi/ride‑share are the highest risk.
  - Maintenance tip:
    - If buying, budget for early DCT fluid and filter service and insist any low‑speed drivability issues were dealer‑addressed.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Chery Tiggo 8 Pro Max?

- NSW dealer snapshot (live MTA NSW scraper provided)
  - Active listings: 2
  - Price range: AUD $30,880 – $34,880
  - Median price: AUD $32,880
  - Year range: 2024 only (in this small sample)
  - Median odometer: 36,423 km
  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 2
  - Use this as your immediate negotiation band if you’re hunting in NSW: target around AU$30–33k for a 2024 example with ~30–40k km, but caveat emptor (see checks below).

- Market context & depreciation:
  - New/demo Tiggo 8 Pro Max prices in dealer ads show new/demo Ultimate AWD examples listed higher (examples on Carsales show 2025 Ultimate AWD at AU$42,990 driveaway and 2024 Urban from ~AU$34,990), so the used NSW listings are offering noticeable price reductions vs some new/demo RRP points: [Carsales Tiggo 8 Pro Max listings](https://www.carsales.com.au/cars/chery/tiggo-8-pro-max/).
  - Expectation: a 2024→2026 depreciation curve for the Tiggo 8 family appears steeper than that of comparable Toyotas/Hondas — a reasonable market expectation is a 20–35% value drop in the first 2–3 years depending on trim, warranty coverage and whether the car has had any warranty/recall work done. Chery’s aggressive new pricing undercuts segment rivals (DriveAuthority comparison) but resale can be weaker because of brand perception: [DriveAuthority price/position](https://driveauthority.com/chery-tiggo-8-review-global/).
  - Practical shorthand: the low entry price is your advantage — you buy features for less — but you’re buying brand and residual risk with it.

- TCO strategy (how to think about total ownership costs and the PHEV premium)
  - Fuel economy roundups:
    - Manufacturer / PR and media figures: the 2.0TGDI is quoted around ~7.4 L/100km official combined in launch material; independent figures range higher in real urban use (~8.1 L/100km or worse in city driving): [Zawya launch spec 7.39 L/100km](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/chery-launches-the-2024-tiggo8-pro-max-redefining-the-future-of-driving-e7t473x6), [CarsGuide comparison lists ~8.1 L/100km](https://www.carsguide.com.au/chery/tiggo-8/vs/chery-tiggo-8-pro-max). WhichCar notes urban consumption can creep toward ~12 L/100km in heavy stop/start city driving.  
    - PHEV / Super Hybrid reality: long‑term tests and WLTP figures show dramatic reductions for PHEV when regularly charged — CarsGuide long‑term recorded runs as low as 6.1 L/100km in blended testing and reviewers report low single‑digit combined numbers when the car is charged frequently: [CarsGuide long‑term PHEV](https://www.carsguide.com.au/family/chery-tiggo-8-ultimate-super-hybrid-long-term-p1-100695), [DriveAuthority / Carwow reporting on low single digit combined figures](https://driveauthority.com/chery-tiggo-8-review-global/).
  - Simple comparative maths (useful planning method):
    - Over 100,000 km:
      - Petrol Pro Max at a conservative blended 8.5 L/100km = 8,500 L of petrol consumed.
      - PHEV blended (charged regularly) at ~4.0 L/100km = 4,000 L of petrol consumed.
      - Difference = 4,500 L saved. Multiply by your local pump price to get dollar savings — the liter saving is the reliable starting point because fuel price varies by region/time.
      - Source reference: blended real‑world figures from CarsGuide / DriveAuthority / Parkers: [CarsGuide PHEV long‑term](https://www.carsguide.com.au/family/chery-tiggo-8-ultimate-super-hybrid-long-term-p1-100695), [Parkers MPG & running costs](https://www.parkers.co.uk/chery/tiggo-8/review/mpg-running-costs/).
    - Interpretation: If you do mostly highway runs with occasional charging, the petrol Pro Max will be reasonable. If your week is city commutes and you can charge at home or at work, the PHEV’s higher purchase premium (if any) pays back quickly in fuel savings.
  - Other TCO factors:
    - Servicing: budget properly for dealer servicing (use Chery Australia schedule) and expect some items (DCT servicing, HV system inspections for PHEV) to add cost. See AU spec sheet for reference: [Chery AU spec sheet](https://cherymotor.com.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/012_24_Tiggo_8_Spec_Sheet_v112.pdf).
    - Repairs & parts: mainstream suppliers for core components exist, but body‑shop panels, unique trim and high‑voltage modules may be more costly than for Japanese peers. Factor in potential outlay for transmissions if you buy a high‑mile DCT example with unchecked driveability.
    - Insurance and theft risk: passive entry and widely documented CAN/relay attack vectors make value for money on tracking and theft cover sensible.

- Buying recommendation — the plain answer
  - If you want features, space and tech for the money and are comfortable managing some risk: buy, but with very specific caveats.
    - Target: 2024 examples with full dealer service history, completed recall work (REC‑006636) and proof of any ADAS/TCU software updates. In the NSW snapshot the median asking AU$32,880 (range AU$30,880–AU$34,880) is the realistic market — use that as your negotiation band.
    - Trim sweet spot: aim for an **Elite** or **Urban** with documented service history (2014‑style picks from AU dealer sheets show Urban as the baseline and Elite/Ultimate as higher spec; the AU spec sheet provides the trim split). If you can find a used **Super Hybrid / PHEV** with a realistic premium and charger history, that is the smarter long‑term financial play for city drivers who can charge often (CarsGuide PHEV testing shows significant real‑world savings): [CarsGuide PHEV long‑term](https://www.carsguide.com.au/family/chery-tiggo-8-ultimate-super-hybrid-long-term-p1-100695).
  - Walk away if:
    - The car shows unresolved DCT low‑speed shudder, has no proof of mechanical remedy or TCU update, or has panel/paint problems that the seller won’t remedy. Don’t buy an example that hasn’t had the brake‑pipe recall completed — that’s non‑negotiable.
    - The dealer won’t show evidence of recent software/firmware updates for infotainment/ADAS or provide a full service history.

- Short pre‑purchase checklist (non‑negotiable)
  - VIN cross‑check recalls: confirm REC‑006636 recall work completed via the dealer or the Australian vehicle recalls portal: [Vehicle Recalls - REC‑006636](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006636).
  - ADAS & TCU updates: ask for dealer printouts showing ADAS and transmission (TCU) software updates.
  - Test drive checklist:
    - Low‑speed 0–30 km/h drivability test for DCT jerks and clutch shudder.
    - Repeated start/stop cycles to test infotainment stability and 12V battery under static conditions.
    - Check AEB behaviour in a controlled setting (annoying false braking or slack response flags).
  - Body & interior:
    - Check panel gaps + paint condition; use daylight and look for corrosion or repaint patches.
    - Inspect 3rd‑row mechanisms and check audio/infotainment screen for freezes; reproduce a freeze if possible.
  - Documents:
    - Full service history; DCT fluid change records (if any); proof of recall/repair completion.
    - HV battery charge logs (PHEV) and charger cable condition if applicable.
  - Walk away if the seller refuses to show any of the above.
