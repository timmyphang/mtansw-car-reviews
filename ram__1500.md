#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: Fifth‑generation Ram 1500 (internal code: **DT**), production introduced January 2018 as a 2019 model and refreshed through to 2026 with updated powertrains and tech. For lineage and generation detail see [Wikipedia — Ram 1500 (DT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_1500_(DT)).
- Where it sits in the Ram lineup:
  - Below: heavy‑duty Ram 2500 / 3500 (work/towing specialists).
  - Above / aside: range stretches from Tradesman work trucks up to luxury‑spec Limited / Longhorn and performance TRX/RHO variants — you pick the personality you want, and Ram gives one.
  - Trim architecture and model ladder documented in Ram’s own trim selector and third‑party trim comparisons: [Ram Trucks Compare](https://www.ramtrucks.com/vehicle-selector.specs.html) and [Edmunds trim chart (2024)](https://www.edmunds.com/ram/1500/2024/trims/).
- Platform/parts availability:
  - The DT 1500 is Ram’s current light‑duty architecture for US/Australia sales since 2019; driveline components commonly use widely adopted suppliers such as the ZF 8‑speed automatic — that transmission is used across many manufacturers, which helps parts and specialist knowledge availability ([ZF 8HP guide](https://houstonengines.com/blog-rams-zf-8hp-transmission-reliability-service-intervals-and-replacement-options)).
- Engines / transmissions / drivetrains (market specifics you’ll see in Australia or in comparable export spec):
  - 3.6‑litre Pentastar V6 with 48‑V mild‑hybrid **eTorque** — paired to an 8‑speed automatic; FWD or AWD options historically available ([Ram 2026 engine line](https://www.ramtrucks.com/towing/engine-performance/2026/ram-1500-4x4.html)).
  - 5.7‑litre **HEMI V8** (returned for 2026 with eTorque mild hybrid on some tunes) — 8‑speed automatic; RWD/4x4 depending on spec ([TruckReportGeeks HEMI writeup](https://truckreportgeeks.com/2026-ram-1500-hemi-v-8-etorque/)).
  - New 3.0‑litre **Hurricane I6** (twin‑scroll/twin‑turbo I6 variants introduced 2025/2026) — high output variants and more efficient than older V8s; 8‑speed auto ([Ram official 2026 page](https://www.ramtrucks.com/ram-1500.html), [CarWeek specs](https://www.carweek.com/research/ram/1500/2026/specs)).
  - Older / optional 3.0‑litre EcoDiesel V6 (on previous model years) — diesel option with specific failure history (see Engines).
- Marketplace rivals in Australia: Ford F‑150, Chevrolet / Holden‑sourced Silverado / GMC Sierra equivalents, Toyota Tundra (and to an extent midsize utes for buyers focused on cost/efficiency). Ram is consistently pitched against the F‑150 / Silverado / Sierra set in comparatives ([Ram competitive compare](https://www.ramtrucks.com/2026/competitive-compare/ram-1500.html), [Edmunds comparison coverage](https://www.edmunds.com/car-news/ford-f150-chevy-silverado-gmc-sierra-ram-1500-toyota-tundra-comparison-test.html)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used RAM 1500?
A quick warning: modern Rams are complex, heavy trucks with a lot of software and expensive hardware. If you want a simple, indestructible ute — you’ll pay for it elsewhere. But if you want comfort, towing muscle and a bit of swagger, Ram is a top pick — provided you know where the landmines are.

- Exterior
  - Panel fitment and paint:
    - Owners report hood/quarter‑panel gap variation and inconsistent panel alignment across the 2019+ DT run — not usually structural, but irritating and costs time at the dealer to fix ([5thGenRams forum thread](https://5thgenrams.com/community/threads/hood-and-quarter-panel-gap-issue.59050/)).
    - Paint durability and peeling issues have been reported — roof/roof‑panel peeling cases have made news (example: a reported 2018 roof paint peel) and aggregated complaints suggest body/paint repairs are a non‑trivial expense ([Autoevolution story](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/the-owner-of-this-2018-ram-1500-shows-how-the-paint-peels-off-the-roof-of-his-truck-237210.html), [CarComplaints body/paint](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Ram/1500/body_paint/index.shtml)).
  - Trim and small parts:
    - Exterior trim panels and fender flares can loosen; Go‑Parts has a practical how‑to for replacement because it’s common enough to warrant community documentation ([Go‑Parts Garage guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-ram-1500-2019-2024)).
  - Recalls and ADAS hardware:
    - Big recall activity over multiple years: large Stellantis campaigns covered many 2019 and 2021–2024 trucks for software and safety modules — check for open campaigns on any candidate vehicle ([USA Today recall summary](https://www.usatoday.com/story/money/cars/recalls/2024/09/07/stellantis-recalls-ram-1500-trucks/75124207007/), NHTSA recall lookup at [nhtsa.gov/recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)).
    - ADAS quirks: owners frequently report radar/camera sensor warnings that render Auto Emergency Braking (AEB), Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC) or Blind‑Spot systems “Unavailable” — sometimes cleared by cleaning the sensor or dealer recalibration, but repeat faults have prompted Technical Service Bulletins (TSBs) and recalls in certain model years ([DOT.Report bulletin re AEB](https://dot.report/bulletins/11019588), [CarComplaints ADAS recall summary](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2024/ram-1500-trucks-recalled-software-errors.shtml)).

- Interior
  - Durability under heavy use:
    - Leather cracking on bolsters and wear on higher‑use vehicles (rideshare, towing work) crops up in owner forums; leather trim quality on mid‑range Laramie/Longhorn examples can delaminate earlier than expected ([5thGenRams leather thread](https://5thgenrams.com/community/threads/2019-laramie-leather-seat-cracking-and-peeling-issue.30768/)).
    - Door entry height and step‑in are higher than a passenger car; the truck does have a generous cabin but large door‑to‑sill gaps can make repeated full‑day in/out physically demanding.
  - Electronics and infotainment:
    - Persistent complaints about Uconnect freezing, blanking, delaminating or suffering “ghost touches” on the larger 12‑inch screens; fixes range from software updates to full unit replacements. Uconnect resets will often clear the problem temporarily; repeated failures are common enough to have community guides ([Go‑Parts infotainment problems guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-ram-1500-2019-2024), [Uconnect hard reset guide](https://truckguider.com/uconnect-hard-reset-ram/)).
    - Wireless charging pad overheating and Qi pad quirks are frequently reported — sometimes phone cases or alignment are the issue, sometimes the pad itself ([Cartipsdaily wireless charging](https://cartipsdaily.com/ram-1500-wireless-charging-not-working)).
    - Instrument clusters and 12" units have owner reports of freezing mid‑trip; dealers often apply software flashes but hardware replacements have been necessary in some cases ([RAM Uconnect complaints summary](https://www.geekdiymechanic.com/CarComplaints/Stellantis_UconnectInfotainment)).
  - Electrical drain:
    - Modern Rams are feature‑heavy; phantom 12‑V battery drains can occur, especially if modules fail to sleep correctly — check vehicle sleep current and recent module replacements on your PPI.

- Mechanically
  - Engineering reputation:
    - In general, Ram’s mechanical hardware (engines, heavy‑duty chassis) is capable, but complexity (48‑V eTorque systems, advanced ADAS, big screens) raises the maintenance bar. Ram’s parts and specialist support in Australia are improving but expect some components to be shipped or wait‑listed depending on trim.
  - Security and theft:
    - Two real problems: the Uconnect hardware is a target for physical theft (screens are removable) and keyless/relay attacks are a known nationwide issue — there are multiple documented thefts and police advisories ([The Drive on screen theft](https://www.thedrive.com/news/so-many-ram-truck-screens-are-being-stolen-because-of-these-two-screws), BackfireNews coverage of relay thefts ([BackfireNews theft clip](https://backfirenews.com/watch-thieves-steal-a-ram-trx-out-of-a-driveway/))).
    - Research and advisories about remote exploits go back years (the Uconnect vulnerability disclosed in 2015) — always assume you need to add aftermarket anti‑theft layers ([CISA advisory on Uconnect vulnerability](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-alerts/ics-alert-15-203-01)).
  - Recommended anti‑theft mitigations:
    - Mechanical steering‑wheel or pedal lock + wheel‑to‑chassis immobiliser.
    - A CAN‑bus immobiliser/IDS solution for high‑risk zones (products such as WFS400 series are designed for this) — consult a specialist installer ([Ampire CAN Immobilizer product page](https://www.ampire.de/CAN-Immobilizer.htm?a=catalog&p=30552&shop=ampire_en)).
    - Faraday pouch for keyfobs or keyfob signal‑blockers at home; physical removal or secure mounting of the infotainment screen if in a high‑theft area.

- Engines
  - Notes before we start: every candidate engine in a used market has known tendencies. Below I list the common failure points reported in owner reports, shop writeups and diagnostic guides. Preventative maintenance: in Australian use, plan heavy‑duty oil and filter changes at 10,000 km intervals if you tow or do short‑trip city use; otherwise maintain at or below manufacturer recommended intervals and monitor oil condition closely.
  - *3.6L Pentastar V6 (including eTorque variants)*
    - Known failure points:
      - Valvetrain ticking, hydraulic lifter/roller and rocker‑arm wear leading to misfires on some early engines.
      - Oil leaks from cam covers/valve‑cover gaskets.
    - Sources and diagnostics: Pentastar problem compendia and owner reports point to left‑bank head issues and valvetrain ticks ([3‑6 Pentastar guide](https://cartipsdaily.com/3-6-pentastar-engine-problems-2026)).
    - Maintenance:
      - Oil change every 10,000 km for mixed/towing duty; inspect lifter noise and compression check if you hear persistent tick.
  - *5.7L HEMI V8 (with and without eTorque)*
    - Known failure points:
      - Lifter and cam wear ('HEMI tick'); MDS (cylinder deactivation) solenoid failures; broken exhaust‑manifold bolts causing exhaust leaks and O2 sensor oddities.
      - eTorque versions are more refined but add complexity (48‑V motor/generator).
    - Source: ASE tech diagnostics and comprehensive HEMI writeups ([HEMI common problems](https://apextechnation.com/articles/ram-1500-57-hemi-common-problems)).
    - Maintenance:
      - Heavy‑use oil change at 10,000 km; watch for lifter noise and oil pressure drops.
  - *3.0L EcoDiesel V6 (older model years)*
    - Known failure points:
      - Serious oil‑cooler failures and oil/coolant contamination, soot/EGR related bottom‑end bearing issues on certain years — can lead to catastrophic failures at surprisingly low mileages under heavy use ([ABCO Diesel shop guide](https://abcodieselinc.com/most-common-problems-of-ram-ecodiesel-2020-2023-ram-1500-3-0l-ecodiesel/), [Cartipsdaily 3.0 EcoDiesel problems](https://cartipsdaily.com/3-0-ecodiesel-problems-2026)).
    - Maintenance:
      - If you buy an EcoDiesel, insist on full service history, recent oil analysis and oil‑cooler replacements if the truck has been used for towing or lots of city driving. Diesel service intervals are not a free pass — frequent long trips at operating temp help longevity.
  - *3.0L Hurricane I6 (newer twin‑turbo inline‑6, 2025/2026)*
    - Known failure points:
      - New engine technology — few long‑term failures publicly documented yet; early reports focus on complexity and expected normal modern turbocharged issues (HPFPs, turbo wastegate actuators, etc.). Monitor recalls and TSBs as this is still being proven in service ([TruckGuider Hurricane chart](https://truckguider.com/ram-1500-engine-options-by-year/), [Ram 2026 engines page](https://www.ramtrucks.com/ram-1500.html)).
    - Maintenance:
      - Conservative oil and filter changes at 10,000 km for Australian conditions until long‑term data proves otherwise.

- Transmission
  - The 8‑speed ZF automatic (often called ZF 8HP) is widely used and generally smooth, but Ram implementations have had issues:
    - Symptoms: harsh shifts, torque‑converter lock‑up judder, delayed engagement from park, or limp‑home behaviour.
    - Root causes: fluid degradation, valve body/solenoid faults, software/TCM calibration and torque‑converter clutch problems. Many fixes are reprogramming, valve body rebuilds or, in worst cases, full replace — which is expensive ([TruckGuider transmission guide](https://truckguider.com/ram-1500-transmission-problems-troubleshoot-repair/), [Ram transmission NHTSA investigation coverage](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2025/ram-truck-transmission-problems-investigated.shtml)).
    - Preventative steps:
      - Check service records for transmission fluid changes, avoid high‑temperature towing without an aux cooler, and have a pre‑purchase test drive include a cold and hot drive to expose shifting problems.
    - Specific owner threads reference torque converter clutch lock issues and valve body solenoid failures as real‑world faults requiring dealer/aftermarket intervention ([RamForum torque converter issue](https://www.ramforum.com/threads/trans-torque-converter-issue.215521/)).

#### 3. Should you buy a used RAM 1500?
- NSW dealer market snapshot (live MTA NSW scraper):
  - Active listings: 38
  - Price range: AUD $0 – $173,888
  - Median price: AUD $131,990
  - Year range: 2020 – 2026
  - Median odometer: 11 km
  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 36
  - Use these to benchmark private vs dealer pricing and negotiate — the market is small but prices are strong for low‑kilometre, late‑model examples.
- Current used marketplace pricing and depreciation:
  - In NSW dealer stock the median AUD $131,990 suggests buyers are paying near new or slightly used values for late‑model Rams — Ram retains value well among full‑size trucks but not quite as strongly as the Toyota Tundra in some studies ([iSeeCars resale ranking](https://www.iseecars.com/resale-value/full-size-trucks)).
  - Depreciation context: studies show that a Ram 1500 retains roughly half to two‑thirds of its value at 3–5 years depending on trim and configuration — CarEdge and CarDepreciation provide detailed curves ([CarEdge Depreciation](https://caredge.com/ram/1500/depreciation), [CarDepreciation Ram page](https://cardepreciation.org/ram/1500/)). Expect a larger percentage hit if you buy high‑spec (Limited, TRX) versus a basic Tradesman.
- Depreciation metrics (practical rule):
  - Baseline: typical loss ~20–30% in first 12 months if heavily optioned; 3‑year drop for mainstream configs ~35–45% versus new, with premium trims holding value better but being more expensive to insure and maintain.
- Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy:
  - Major TCO drivers: depreciation, fuel, insurance and repairs for electronics/ADAS. OwnerKeep’s 5‑year TCO for recent Ram 1500s is in the ballpark of AUD equivalent of USD estimates — roughly $55k–$72k over five years depending on engine and usage ([OwnerKeep TCO page](https://ownerkeep.com/2024-ram-1500-cost-of-ownership)).
  - Fuel math example (Australian context):
    - If you do 20,000 km/year towing and town use, a petrol HEMI 5.7L might average ~14–16 L/100km in mixed towing use (US EPA figures need adjustment for Aussie conditions). A diesel EcoDiesel could deliver ~8–10 L/100km equivalent fuel energy and save you significant diesel dollars if you average high km and heavy towing. MotorTrend and MotorBiscuit show EcoDiesel has clear mpg advantage vs V8 under many conditions ([MotorTrend EcoDiesel vs HEMI](https://www.motortrend.com/how-to/ecodiesel-vs-hemi), additional EcoDiesel real world concerns in diesel guides).
    - Payback: Diesel premium up front is only worth it if you do high annual kilometres and tow frequently. If you do <10,000 km/year and little towing, the V8 or Hurricane I6 may be a better financial and reliability pick.
  - Hybrid / eTorque payback:
    - eTorque mild‑hybrid is a small fuel saver primarily in city/stop‑start use — expect modest gains (1–3 mpg equivalent in city driving). If you live in the city and do lots of short trips, eTorque trims can be worth the small premium; for long‑distance highway work, gains are negligible ([CartipsDaily eTorque notes](https://cartipsdaily.com/dodge-ram-1500-gas-mileage)).
- Buying recommendation — clear and direct:
  - If you want the safest financial and reliability bet:
    - Target: 2021–2023 Big Horn / Laramie spec with a **3.6L Pentastar eTorque** or the **5.7L HEMI eTorque** if you want V8 character. These model years have matured beyond 2019‑2020 teething issues, parts are common and dealer familiarity is high.
    - Avoid buying EcoDiesel examples unless you can verify meticulous service history, recent oil‑cooler work if required, and you plan to tow/drive high kilometres (diesel payback only at high usage) — EcoDiesel can be brilliant but carries known failure risk if not cared for ([ABCO Diesel guide](https://abcodieselinc.com/most-common-problems-of-ram-ecodiesel-2020-2023-ram-1500-3-0l-ecodiesel/)).
  - If you want the cutting edge and don’t mind potential early‑adopter tech issues:
    - The 2025–2026 **Hurricane I6** and return of the eTorque **5.7 HEMI** deliver strong performance and efficiency improvements; buy only with a full warranty or from a dealer who offers certified pre‑owned backing because electronics/ADAS and new engine teething could be costly.
  - Specific trim sweet spot:
    - Mid‑range Laramie or Big Horn (or equivalent in local naming) strikes the best balance: strong equipment, dealer serviceability and lower premiums than Limited/TRX. Laramie gives better interior materials without the price of the top tier.
  - Pre‑purchase checklist (non‑negotiable):
    - Full service history; ask specifically about transmission fluid services and turbo/oil‑cooler work if diesel.
    - Verify all ADAS functions work: ACC, AEB, blind‑spot — if any 'Unavailable' warnings persist, budget for dealer diagnostics.
    - Uconnect screen behaviour: ask to see a long drive with infotainment active; frozen or black screens are expensive to replace.
    - VIN recall check via NHTSA/Mopar and confirm dealer completed recall repairs: [NHTSA recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and [Mopar recall lookup](https://www.mopar.com/en-us/my-vehicle/recalls/search.html).
    - Theft mitigation: confirmed working immobiliser, physical locks if you live in high‑risk areas.

Final verdict in plain Aussie: buy a Ram 1500 if you prioritise ride comfort, towing capability and a truck‑style interior that’s more living‑room than toolbox — but only if you accept the tradeoffs: complexity, potential electronics headaches and higher repair costs than a simple ute. For most buyers the smartest used pick is a low‑kilometre 2021–2023 mid‑trim Laramie/Big Horn with a Pentastar or HEMI eTorque, bought from a dealer who will show completed recall/TSB work and give at least short‑term warranty cover. If you’re after economy and tow a lot, a well‑documented EcoDiesel can still be brilliant — but do your homework.
