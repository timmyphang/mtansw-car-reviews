#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

The Hyundai Mighty operates squarely in Australia’s mid-weight light commercial vehicle (LCV) bracket, bridging the gap between light-duty utes and rigid tippers. While the nameplate dates back to the late 1980s, the current generation—marketed internationally and locally as the **EX Series**—represents a complete architectural refresh. Development commenced internally around 2015, with the third-generation platform officially launching globally in October 1997 and continuously iterating through the EX4, EX6, and EX8 nomenclature to denote payload and GVWM tiers. 

Product hierarchy within Hyundai’s commercial stack places the Mighty below the heavy XCIENT prime movers and hydrogen-cell trucks, sitting directly above the Kona-based commercial conversions and legacy H-100 vans. It shares a global LCV chassis architecture developed jointly with Hyundai Special Vehicles, meaning core rails, suspension mounting points, and steering knuckles are cross-compatible with export markets across Southeast Asia and the Middle East. This shared foundation drastically simplifies sourcing leaf springs, axle housings, and cab mounts compared to fragmented niche imports.

Powertrain mapping for the Australian delivery spec centres on a single displacement family: the **3.9-litre common-rail four-cylinder turbo-diesel**. Output splits across three primary configurations: the base EX4 delivers 140 hp (103 kW) at 2,500 rpm with 392 Nm of torque [1]; the EX6 and EX8 bump outputs to 170 hp (125 kW) and peak 610 Nm [2]. Both are paired with either a robust five-speed manual or a six-speed fully automatic Allison 1000 Series xFE [3]. Drivetrain layout is strictly 4x2 RWD, optimised for suburban delivery corridors, trade fit-outs, and lightweight bulk haulage rather than off-grid excavation duties.

Market rivals are brutally familiar. The Mighty trades blows daily with the Isuzu N-Series, Mitsubishi Fuso Canter, and Hino Dutro [4]. Unlike passenger SUVs fighting for lifestyle buyers, this arena is governed by uptime, parts turnaround times at your nearest Hunter Valley or Campbelltown distributor, and how quickly a mechanic can wrench on the thing at 4am when the tray snaps a bolt mid-load. Hyundai’s positioning leans on modern safety tech, comfortable cabin ergonomics, and a structured warranty regime, aiming to steal share from brands that have historically relied on inertia and decades of dealer goodwill.

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Hyundai Mighty?

**Exterior:**
Panel alignment on the Mighty is generally tight at factory specification, but commercial abuse tells a different story. Watch for mismatched gap tolerances around the front valance and wheel arch cladding—evidence of prior impact compensation or cheap aftermarket tray welding. Gloss black plastic door handles, mirror caps, and windscreen surrounds crack and oxidise rapidly under Queensland sunlight, often splitting along mould lines rather than just scratching. Paint durability on steel cabs holds up well unless subjected to stone chips left untreated, where flash rust migrates under clearcoat layers near the A-pillar weld seams.

ADAS implementation arrives primarily with newer electric and high-spec diesel trims. Forward collision avoidance radar and cameras are standard on recent launches, paired with active lane-keep assist. These systems function competently in daylight but suffer sensitivity quirks when dust coats sensors or when navigating unmarked loading bays. False braking events were common in early firmware calibrations targeting heavy metal grilles; Hyundai pushed targeted software patches to adjust threshold mapping, meaning pre-2023 units may require a dealer-side update to prevent panic stops during routine traffic squeezes.

**Interior:**
Cabin durability gets hammered in rideshare-adjacent fleet duty and courier operations. Upholstery tearing at bolster seams and cracked vinyl dashboards from UV exposure are commonplace after 100,000 km. Spatial entry excels: step-in height sits low compared to traditional rigid trucks, reducing hip strain for veteran operators, and door check mechanisms are tuned for wide swing angles that accommodate toolboxes wedged against the door sill.

Electrical gremlins cluster around parasitic 12V battery drain, particularly when vehicles sit overnight with telematics gateways and telematic ECUs failing to enter proper sleep mode. Aftermarket dashcams and GPS trackers often wire directly to switched accessories, accelerating drain patterns beyond OEM tolerances. Infotainment hardware runs a dedicated 7.0-inch LCD touchscreen paired with a digital supervision cluster, prioritising radio, Bluetooth telephony, reverse camera feed, and trip logging over smartphone mirroring. Connectivity reliability improved markedly post-2015 with revised modem firmware, though occasional Bluetooth pairing timeouts persist on heavier-duty USB ports used for rugged tablet mounts.

**Mechanically:**
Hyundai’s general engineering reputation leans toward conservative, proven architectures rather than cutting-edge experimentation. That translates to predictable wear profiles and fewer catastrophic design flaws, provided owners respect service schedules. However, the brand’s secondary parts supply chain hasn’t yet caught up to Isuzu or Toyota network density outside major metro hubs, meaning some specialists need to order rail components from Korea rather than pulling from a Brisbane warehouse.

Security vulnerabilities remain a pragmatic concern for uninsured yard parking. Earlier MY2012–2016 diesels lack encrypted CAN-bus authentication, leaving physical OBD-II ports open to cloning or relay-style bypass exploits if fitted with smart-key fleet packages. Anti-theft counter-measures aren’t decorative: fit a physical steering column boot lock, install an OBD-II port immobiliser bypass switch, deploy a hidden GPS tracker with movement alerts, and store all fob keys inside Faraday shielding bags overnight. Insurance underwriters actually read policy clauses, and visible deterrents lower premium brackets.

**Engines:**
*3.9L CRDi Turbo Diesel (EX4 / EX6 / EX8 Variants)*
This common-rail unit is the bread-and-butter workhorse, but it carries specific failure signatures depending on production year. Models built between 2010 and 2015 exhibit systemic coolant bypass valve routing flaws combined with EGR cooler degradation, leading to chronic overheating episodes under sustained gradient climbing. Coolant loss manifests as white exhaust vapour and warped cylinder heads if ignored. Exhaust manifold cracks, warping, and internal corrosion trigger audible ticking leaks, misfire codes, and reduced turbo boost efficiency. High-pressure fuel pump (HPFP) seals tend to degrade past 140,000–160,000 km, causing cold-start hesitation, rough idle, and elevated crankcase pressure. Preventative maintenance requires strict 10,000 km oil and filter changes, coolant system flushes every 40,000 km, and air filter replacements at 20,000 km intervals to combat abrasive inland dust infiltration [5].

*Electric Traction Motor & Battery Pack (Mighty EV Variant)*
The all-electric powertrain replaces combustion entirely with a 120 kW permanent magnet synchronous motor delivering 320 Nm instantaneous torque, backed by a 114.5 kWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery pack [6]. Range sits around 200–240 km fully laden depending on payload distribution, top speed governed to 100 km/h for vocational stability. Thermal management relies on active liquid cooling loops that require inspection every 20,000 km or annually, whichever comes first [7]. Common failure points involve DC fast-charging contact pitting, onboard charger thermal runaway warnings under extreme ambient heat, and BMS (battery management system) cell balancing drift past 100,000 km. Scheduled servicing remains lean—first five years/100,000 km clock in under $4,300 total—but out-of-warranty traction battery or motor inverter replacement runs seven figures without corporate fleet coverage [8].

**Transmission:**
*Allison 1000 Series xFE 6-Speed Automatic*
This fully automatic box is engineered for continuous heavy-torque application, yet it demands disciplined maintenance. Primary failure modes emerge around the 3rd-to-4th shift shudder under partial load, delayed kickdown engagement when fluid temperature exceeds 110°C, and gradual solenoid valve package wear manifesting as hunting between gears. Transmission control module (TCM) programming glitches specifically affected 2012–2017 manufacture windows, causing erratic downshifts during mountain descents or rapid deceleration sequences. Remediation involves dedicated software flashing, external oil cooler installation for constant laden operation, and strict adherence to manufacturer-specified synthetic fluid levels. Neglecting cooler purges guarantees sludge buildup, pressure drop, and eventual clutch pack glazing.

*Mechanical Manual Gearboxes (Legacy & Base Trims)*
Five-speed manual variants feature synchronised dog-ring gearsets designed for commercial stop-start duty. Wear concentrates on the primary shaft bearings, thrust washers, and clutch hydraulic master/slave cylinders operating in dusty environments. Throw-out bearing噪音 correlates to improper release bearing preload adjustment. Overall reliability remains high provided operators avoid riding the clutch pedal on gradients and use proper engine braking tactics on long downhills to preserve brake drum integrity.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Hyundai Mighty?

Live NSW dealer market data presents a tight inventory window: two active listings priced between AUD $23,888 and $66,990, landing at a median of $45,439. Year range spans 2017 to 2024, with a median odometer reading of 188,700 km across cab-chassis and fitted-body configurations. The transmission mix reads n/a due to sparse volume, confirming this segment trades largely on individual vendor negotiations rather than mass-market turnover.

Depreciation metrics for mid-LCVs follow a predictable curve: approximately 30–35% shed in calendar year one, followed by a flattening trajectory as residual value stabilises around the $30k–$40k band. Compared to Isuzu N-Series benchmarks—which retain roughly 8–12% higher residual percentages due to ubiquitous parts stock—the Mighty drops slightly faster but compensates with lower entry premiums. Over a standard three-year holding period, expect a baseline 25–30% total depreciation, heavily contingent on recorded service history, tray body corrosion status, and compliance documentation.

Total cost of ownership (TCO) calculation requires hard mathematics, not marketing brochures. Loaded diesel variants consume roughly 10–12 L/100km in urban delivery cycles, settling to ~8 L/100km at steady-state highway speeds. Standard servicing intervals run at 10,000 km or 12 months, translating to annual outlays of $800–$1,200 for oil, filters, brake inspections, and fluid top-ups. The electric pathway offers starkly different economics: capped service costs beneath $4,300 over five years, zero fuel expenditure, and regenerative braking extending pad life [9]. However, paying the ~AUD $150,000+ capital premium for EV configuration only yields mathematically sound long-term savings if your operational envelope matches strict parameters: daily return-to-base loops under 150 km, guaranteed workplace/home charging infrastructure, urban emission zone compliance mandates, and fleet-scale utilisation rates. For standalone tradies, rural contractors, or irregular haulage operators, the ICE diesel remains the rational financial anchor. EV batteries degrade under deep-cycle heat stress, and out-of-warranty replacement expenses instantly erase projected savings.

Buying recommendation targets a narrow window: secure a 2018–2021 EX6 or EX8 cab-chassis with documented Allison automatic fluid services, verified exhaust manifold integrity, and a corrosion-free upper body or tray. The 2018 model year consistently rates highest for structural refinement and resolved ECM calibration bugs [10]. Steer clear of 2012–2015 pre-refresh builds carrying unresolved TCM fault trees and compromised cooling pathways. Verify logbooks match the 10,000 km servicing rhythm, demand dyno pull-test results if buying pre-fitted bodies, and always commission an independent pre-delivery inspection focused on radiator hose permeability, transmission cooler line fittings, and leaf spring center-pin play. The Mighty rewards disciplined buyers with uncompromising daily work ethics; it absolutely punishes neglect with expensive tow-trip invoices.
