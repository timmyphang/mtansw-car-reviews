#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: Third‑generation Mazda 6 (often called the GJ/GL in some markets), production broadly 2013–2024; replaces the second‑gen and precedes the model line being wound down in some markets. See the model overview on Wikipedia for the generation span and SKYACTIV shift: [Mazda6 (third generation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda6_(third_generation)).
- Internal codes & names you’ll see in classifieds: GJ/GL for the mid‑2010s cars, facelifted variants from ~2018 onward sometimes simply listed as “facelift” or “refresh”.
- Product hierarchy: The Mazda 6 sits above the Mazda3 as the brand’s mid‑size (D‑segment) sedan and below the brand’s larger flagship crossovers (such as the CX‑9 for buyers wanting more space/luxury). Wikipedia lays out the line positioning across Mazda’s catalogue: [Mazda6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda6).
- Shared platform architecture: The third‑gen Mazda 6 is built on Mazda’s SKYACTIV‑body and chassis architecture — the same family that underpins Mazda3 and several CX‑series models. That common architecture helps parts availability for suspension, brakes and many interior components; see the SKYACTIV family noted on the third‑gen spec page: [Mazda6 (third generation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda6_(third_generation)) and technical spec aggregation: [mazda‑specs.com](https://mazda-specs.com/6/2013-present/specs/).
- Australia market powertrains and drivetrains (what you’ll actually encounter in NSW classifieds):
  - **SKYACTIV‑G 2.5‑litre (naturally aspirated)** — commonly paired to 6‑speed SKYACTIV‑Drive automatic; front‑wheel drive (FWD). Power figures vary by tune and year but expect ~135–140 kW in the common tuneings (see Mazda spec decks). Source: [mazda‑specs.com](https://mazda-specs.com/6/2013-present/specs/).
  - **SKYACTIV‑G 2.5 Turbo (2.5T)** — introduced around the 2018 refresh in higher trims (Grand Touring and above in some markets); turbocharged 2.5L, 6‑speed or 6‑speed auto depending on market, FWD. See trim and turbo introduction in spec decks: [2018 & 2020 spec decks](https://news.mazdausa.com/download/2018-Mazda6-Spec-Deck_Version-3.pdf).
  - **SKYACTIV‑D 2.2‑litre diesel** — available in some markets and model years; paired to automatic or manual depending on spec, often prized for long‑distance economy. Listing of diesel variants and specs: [mazda‑specs.com](https://mazda-specs.com/6/2013-present/specs/).
  - Transmissions commonly available: 6‑speed SKYACTIV‑Drive automatic is the predominant box across the generation. Technical doc on SKYACTIV‑Drive: [SKYACTIV‑Drive Transmission Specifications](https://mazda-specs.com/6/2013-present/skyactiv-drive-trans/specs/).
  - Drive configurations: primarily FWD; AWD was rare or unavailable on many Mazda6 market variants.
- Marketplace rivals in Australia: Toyota Camry, Honda Accord, Hyundai Sonata (and SUVs like Kia Sportage or Mazda CX‑5 if you’re cross‑shopping for a family wagon/soft‑roader). Comparative notes from Australian buyer guides: [CarsGuide comparison hub](https://www.carsguide.com.au/mazda/6/vs) and broader rival lists: [CarsCounsel rivals](https://carscounsel.com/cars-similar-to-mazda-6/).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Mazda 6?
You want the blunt truth: the Mazda 6 isn’t a lemon, but the third‑gen era brought a mix of really likeable engineering and a few recurring gremlins. Breakdowns and niggles concentrate in paint/trim, infotainment/12V electricals, ADAS radar/software, Skyactiv engines’ particular failure modes, and torque‑converter automatic niggles.

- Exterior
  - Panel alignment: Generally good — Mazda has decent panel fit for the class. No systemic gap nightmares, but older, high‑mile or accident‑repaired cars can show uneven gaps (as with any used car).
  - Trim vulnerabilities:
    - Gloss black and lower‑trim appliqués can scratch and fade; B‑pillar appliqués and mirror caps are known to warp/chew up in sun/heat (owner video coverage highlights warping concerns and service alerts) — owners discuss this on community forums and YouTube resources: [Why Your Mazda’s Trim Is Warping](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aHzj2tLIVcw).
    - Clearcoat and paint: older Mazda 6s have recurring clearcoat peeling or flaking in certain paint colours — owner threads and aggregated complaint sites report repeated examples and DIY repaint costs. See threads and summaries: [clearcoat peeling forum thread](https://www.mazda6club.com/threads/paint-peeling-pretty-sure-its-the-clear-coat.296145/) and paint analysis: [CyPaint coverage](https://cypaint.com/article/have-newer-mazdas-fixed-the-paint-flaw).
  - Body recalls: Always run a VIN check via Mazda’s recall portal — genuine safety recall repairs are done free at dealers: [Mazda Recall Information](https://www.mazdausa.com/owners/recalls).
  - ADAS (advanced driver aids) exterior/sensor fragility:
    - Radar and front sensors can fail or need recalibration after bumper removal — replacement/calibration can be expensive; an aftermarket guide covers common radar unit failures and calibration costs: [Go‑Parts radar sensor guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/suspension-yaw-sensor-mazda-6-mazda-cx-5-mazda-cx-3-2017-2024).
    - Owners report weird adaptive cruise / unexpected braking events and generic front‑radar malfunction warnings — searchable in owner threads and complaint lists: [CarProblemZoo ADAS threads](https://www.carproblemzoo.com/mazda/mazda6/adaptive-cruise-control-problems.php) and community forum threads describing "surprising braking" events: [Mazda6Club FOW thread](https://www.mazda6club.com/threads/fow-and-cruise-control-problems-dangerous-surprise-braking-on-highway.395089/).

- Interior
  - Durability under heavy duty (rideshare/taxi):
    - Mazda 6 cabins are well designed but premium materials in higher trims (softer leathers, stitched trims) can wear if used as a taxi. Base cloth can hold up well, but seat bolsters commonly show wear on higher‑km cars.
    - Door sills and entry: reasonably low sills and wide openings — easier for older passengers and kids than a typical coupe‑sloped sedan.
  - Electrical and infotainment:
    - Mazda Connect screens in a subset of 2014–2016/2016–2021 vehicles experienced “ghost touch”, peeling, spider‑cracks and controller issues; Mazda/NHTSA documentation shows dealer repair kits for the display surfaced as a Service Bulletin/repair kit: [Gen 6 Infotainment Display TSB (NHTSA PDF)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10222452-0001.pdf) and a related TSB covering 2014–2016 Mazda3 and 2016–2021 Mazda6 infotainment concerns: [TSB list PDF](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10215250-0001.pdf).
    - 12‑volt battery drain: older cars with many accessories or aftermarket infotainment add‑ons can show parasitic drain. Soft fixes often work (battery replacement, checking accessory relays); if your test‑drive car has repeated flat batteries, treat it as a red flag.
    - Screen sizes and versions: base units used smaller Mazda Connect displays on lower trims; higher trims got larger glass and sometimes wireless Apple CarPlay later in the lifecycle. Trim‑by‑trim equipment listings are visible in Mazda spec decks: [2021 Mazda6 spec deck](https://news.mazdausa.com/download/2021-Mazda6-Spec-Deck.pdf).
  - Connectivity reliability: Mazda Connect can freeze — a soft reset (hold volume knob) often clears it; community guides explain reboots and resets: [How To Reset Mazda Infotainment System](https://fatboysoffroad.com/how-to-reset-mazda-infotainment-system/).

- Mechanically
  - Engineering reputation: Mazda’s SKYACTIV philosophy pushed lightweight, efficient engines and chassis. They drive well for the class — sharper steering and better handling than many rivals — but some SKYACTIV engines have developed distinct reliability headlines (see Engines below).
  - Security vulnerabilities:
    - Keyless relay/roll‑up theft and CAN‑bus “injection” attacks are industry‑wide threats, not Mazda‑specific. Criminals have exploited physical wiring access in headlight/bumper areas to inject messages and bypass immobilisers — recommended layered security (steering lock, immobiliser dongle, storing keys in RF‑blocking pouches at home) helps. Read the technical coverage: [The Register on CAN bus attacks](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how-thieves-steal-cars-using-vehicle-can-bus/) and background on headlight CAN injection: [Autoblog piece](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update).
    - Suggested owner countermeasures:
      - Use a hardened mechanical steering wheel lock when parked in public.
      - Fit a dealer or reputable aftermarket immobiliser/kill‑switch.
      - Keep keys in a Faraday pouch and park in well‑lit areas.

- Engines
  - The major engine families present in Australian classifieds and discussed in owner reports are covered below. Each engine entry is italicised as requested and cites problem patterns and maintenance intervals where sources exist.

  - *SKYACTIV‑G 2.5 (naturally aspirated)*
    - Notes: The backbone petrol engine for many Mazda6s; lively and efficient with SKYACTIV tuning. Spec lists and recommended oil/specs are in Mazda documentation: [Mazda Owners Manual specifications](https://owners-manual.mazda.com/gen/en/mazda6/mazda6_8fk7ee16f/contents/10020105.html).
    - Common failure points:
      - Front timing cover and associated seal oil leaks have been reported across Skyactiv engines — watch for oil pooling under the front of the car or a slow drip. Workshop guides identify timing cover seal leaks as a recurring issue: [Go‑Parts timing cover leak guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/engine-timing-cover-mazda-3-mazda-cx-5-mazda-6-2012-2025).
      - Carbon build‑up on direct‑injection engines can cause rough idling or misfires after high km without intake cleaning.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Follow Mazda service intervals (factory schedule in owners manual) — and prefer 10,000 km oil service cadence if the car sees frequent short trips or dusty use rather than stretching to 15,000 km between oil changes. Spec/interval detail: [Mazda Owners Manual specifications](https://owners-manual.mazda.com/gen/en/mazda6/mazda6_8fk7ee16f/contents/10020105.html).
      - Watch oil level frequently (top‑up if required) and use recommended oil viscosity and specification.

  - *SKYACTIV‑G 2.5 Turbo (2.5T)*
    - Notes: Added around the 2018 refresh to inject more torque and refinement into the line-up. Spec references in Mazda US spec decks show turbo availability at higher trims: [2018 spec deck](https://news.mazdausa.com/download/2018-Mazda6-Spec-Deck_Version-3.pdf).
    - Common failure points:
      - Increased reports of oil consumption, oil leaks, and in some owner reports even head/engine failures leading to class action coverage in 2024 related to some Skyactiv‑G 2.5 turbo variants — read the reporting on the legal action and owner complaints: [CarComplaints on 2024 class action](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2024/mazda-engine-problems-class-action-lawsuit.shtml) and variant problem breakdowns: [CherishYourCar 2.5 issues](https://www.cherishyourcar.com/mazda-2-5-engine-problems/).
      - Turbo variants can show valve‑stem seal, oil burning, and carbon issues earlier than NA variants due to higher operating pressures and temperatures.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - 10,000 km oil change with OEM‑spec synthetic oil is prudent — turbos don’t forgive neglected oil.
      - Inspect for oil leaks around timing cover and turbo oil feed/return lines; check PCV and intake for oil contamination.

  - *SKYACTIV‑D 2.2 Diesel*
    - Notes: Diesel fans liked the torque and economy on long runs. Diesel DPF and high‑pressure fuel pump failures are common failure categories on many modern diesels and feature in aggregated fault lists: [AUTODOC summary of Mazda6 faults](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-mazda-6).
    - Common failure points:
      - DPF (diesel particulate filter) clogging if primarily used for short trips. Regens not completing will lead to costly diagnostics and potential DPF replacement.
      - High‑pressure fuel pump and injector faults as mileage climbs — AUTODOC and other owner aggregates highlight these as recurring faults on diesel Mazda engines.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Regular highway runs to complete DPF regens; service interval monitoring for fuel filter replacements.
      - Follow Mazda’s recommended service schedule and fuel‑system checks at 20–40k km intervals if used under heavy load or frequent towing.

- Transmission
  - The third‑gen Mazda6 largely uses the **6‑speed SKYACTIV‑Drive automatic**.
  - Behaviour & known failure modes:
    - Symptoms reported: low‑speed jerkiness, delayed engagement, harsh shifts and torque‑converter shudder — many owners report issues around 80,000–120,000 km. Aggregated fault logs and specialists identify torque‑converter lock‑up clutch wear and valve body faults as common culprits: [Transmission problem overview](https://gearfixes.com/mazda-6-transmission-problems/) and [AUTODOC transmission summary](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-mazda-6).
    - Typical fixes range from TCM (transmission control module) resets and fluid/ filter changes to valve body repairs or torque‑converter replacement for higher severity failures.
    - Preventative: regular ATF inspection and change per Mazda interval (use the ATF grade Mazda specifies) can mitigate earlier failure; using correct synthetic ATF and changing at 60–100k km if not serviced before is sensible.
  - Manuals and older 5‑speed automatics: older pre‑SKYACTIV boxes aren’t common in later models but check the gearbox code in the listing.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Mazda 6?
- NSW dealer market snapshot (live): Active listings: 11; Price range: AUD $0 – $38,500; Median price: AUD $10,990; Year range: 2008 – 2024; Median odometer: 28,208 km; Transmission mix dominated by Automatic (9). This is the market you’re buying into — median price and low median kilometres suggest many NSW dealer examples are reasonably young or low‑km: (NSW dealer data: provided).
- Current marketplace entry to ceiling price (NSW snapshot): Expect to see bargain‑basement cars under $5k (usually high‑km, accident salvage, or private unroadworthy listings) through to late‑model low‑km, near‑top spec examples approaching $35–38k. Median in NSW is AUD $10,990 (see your NSW data).
- Depreciation metrics:
  - Broadly the Mazda6 depreciates faster than the Honda Accord and Toyota Camry on average — iSeeCars analysis suggests Mazda6 can lose ~43% over 5 years vs Accord’s ~35% (model comparisons shown): [iSeeCars comparison](https://www.iseecars.com/compare/honda-accord-vs-mazda-mazda6).
  - Expect a steeper drop after the 3–4 year mark if the market moves away from sedans to SUVs — the Mazda6’s resale can be punishing versus its more popular SUV siblings.
- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy — how to think about the numbers:
  - Baseline costs to model into your budget:
    - Depreciation: largest single cost; factor a typical 3‑5 year trade window with a ~35–45% drop depending on model year/trim.
    - Fuel: SKYACTIV‑G 2.5 real‑world economy ranges widely — Fuelly collects owner data you can use to model typical consumption (city vs highway) for the Mazda6: [Fuelly Mazda6 page](https://www.fuelly.com/car/mazda/6). Expect mid‑to‑high teens in heavy urban cycles in L/100km; diesels will be lower on highways.
    - Maintenance & repairs: factor mid‑range servicing (10,000 km oil services if you follow the conservative path), occasional infotainment fixes, and potential transmission or engine repairs if buying high‑km turbo or diesel models.
    - Insurance & rego: standard for D‑segment sedans — shop around.
  - Is paying a premium for turbo or diesel worth it?
    - Turbo 2.5T: if you do long motorway runs and value torque and refinement, the turbo can feel worth it — but you pay more up front and face possibly higher repair risk/repair bills (see class action & owner complaints). If your driving is mostly urban, the NA 2.5 is cheaper to buy and maintain, and its fuel penalty in stop/start driving makes the turbo less justifiable.
    - Diesel: economical on highways and high annual kms, but avoid if 90% of your trips are short urban hops — DPF maintenance costs can kill the savings.
  - Use a cost calculator to plug your km and compare (Australia‑friendly calculators exist; general TCO frameworks are here): [CostMyCar](https://costmycar.com/) or multi‑scenario tools like [best‑calculators EV vs gas tool](https://best-calculators.com/automotive/ev-vs-gas-calculator/).
- Price vs value recommendation (mathematical & pragmatic):
  - If your budget is around/under the NSW median (~AUD $11k), you can pick up a tidy late‑2000s to early‑2010s example or a higher‑year but higher‑km car. These are fine as daily drivers if you accept dated tech and higher potential service bills.
  - If you want the best risk‑to‑reward, aim for mid‑2016 to 2019 facelift models (the “sweet spot”): by then many late‑cycle improvements to infotainment, safety assists and chassis tuning were in place; prices often slide into good value territory while retaining modern kit. The 2018 refresh brought the turbo to higher trims and interior refinement — a well‑spec 2016–2019 model with consistent service history is my pick.
  - Trim pick:
    - For reliability and lower servicing risk: stick with **non‑turbo SKYACTIV‑G 2.5 NA** in a Touring or Sport trim — good equipment, simpler engine, cheaper to run.
    - For driving enjoyment and if you can afford slightly higher risk: a Grand Touring with **2.5T** is fun but budget for potentially higher maintenance and insist on full service history.
- Final verdict — should you buy one?
  - Short answer: Yes, if you value a sharp‑handling, well‑built mid‑size sedan and you pick carefully. No, if you want the absolute lowest long‑term ownership headaches and best resale — a Camry or Accord might age more gracefully in value terms.
  - Buying checklist (practical):
    - Full service history (5‑star). If the seller skipped oil changes or went long intervals, walk.
    - VIN/recall check through Mazda dealer portal — get outstanding recall work done free: [Mazda Recall Information](https://www.mazdausa.com/owners/recalls).
    - Compression/leak‑down and oil‑consumption check for 2.5T cars; check for timing‑cover leaks on Skyactiv engines.
    - Transmission health check: no shuddering, smooth lock‑up, test at both low speed and highway merge.
    - Infotainment test: confirm screen has no ghost touch, peeling. If it behaves badly, ask for proof of TSB repair or a discount: [Infotainment TSB (NHTSA)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10222452-0001.pdf).
    - Check DPF regen status and fuel system if it’s a diesel — avoid a car that has had repeated forced regen cycles without resolution.
- The final buy call:
  - Best financial sweet spot: a well‑serviced 2016–2019 Mazda 6 with the **SKYACTIV‑G 2.5** NA engine, automatic, with around 50–120k km and full service history. You get modern kit, improved software/infotainment, and a lower risk profile than turbo or high‑km diesels.
  - If you’re a driving nerd and you do long open runs at highway speeds with careful maintenance, a 2018+ Grand Touring with the **2.5T** can be a cracker — but price it cheaper than equivalent Camry/Accord and budget for potential engine or turbo work down the line.

If you follow that checklist, you’ll have a very likeable car for a sensible price.
