# Geely EX2 (2026–present): The Electric Hatch That’s Rewriting the Budget Rulebook – But Is It Built to Last?

## 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

The **Geely EX2** is the Chinese automotive giant’s baby electric hatch, launched in Australia from **July 2026**. It represents the brand’s most aggressive play for the entry-level EV buyer – a segment currently dominated by the **BYD Seagull** (sold here as the Atto 1), **MG4 Excite**, **GAC Aion UT**, and the soon-to-arrive **Renault 5 Electric**. Internally, the EX2 is known as the **Geome Xingyuan** in China, and it shares its DNA with the **Proton eMas 5** in Malaysia – making parts availability across ASEAN and China surprisingly robust.

### Product Hierarchy & Platform

The EX2 sits below the **Geely EX5** (a larger electric SUV that launched earlier in Australia) and above nothing – it’s the brand’s entry point. It rides on Geely’s **GEA** intelligent architecture, the same flexible platform used by the EX5. That means shared suspension components, battery modules, and motor designs. Other models on this architecture include the **Geely EX5** and upcoming **Starray PHEV** – so expect cross-compatibility for things like inverters and control modules.

Both use a **single-speed reduction gear** (the EV equivalent of a transmission) and **rear-wheel drive** – a rarity in this class. The Inspire also adds a multi-link independent rear suspension (the Complete uses a torsion beam), which is a key differentiator.

- **Renault 5 Electric** (due late 2026, expected ~$35k+)

## 2. What goes wrong with a used Geely EX2?

Let’s be straight: the EX2 is *so new* that the Australian used market is a ghost town. The MTA NSW data shows **43 active listings** but **$0 price range** – that’s just dealer demo cars being registered, not pre-owned stock. Median odometer: 10 km. So this section is a **predictive analysis** based on the model’s Chinese market history (where the Geome Xingyuan has been on sale since late 2024), common EV teething issues, and Geely’s global reputation.

### Exterior

The EX2 is a handsome little thing – smooth, rounded, with cute “insect-eye” LED headlights. Panel fit on early Chinese examples was **acceptable but not class-leading**. Expect slight misalignments on front bumper to wing joints, typical of the price bracket. The **gloss black trim** around the fake grille and lower air intakes scratches if you look at it wrong. No chrome here, thank goodness.

**Paint durability:** The soft pastel colours (pink, mint green) show swirl marks quickly. The solid whites hold up better. No widespread paint recalls yet, but Geely has had thin paint complaints on the EX5 in South Africa.

**ADAS systems:** The EX2 comes equipped with **Autonomous Emergency Braking (AEB), Lane Keep Assist, and Traffic Sign Recognition** – though only on the Inspire (the Complete misses out on some). Early feedback from the Chinese market indicates the AEB is **overly sensitive at low speeds**, triggering phantom braking when reversing past shrubs. A firmware update (v2.1) apparently improved this, but check if the car has been updated before buying. The Lane Keep Assist is more of a gentle nudge than an aggressive yank – fine for daily duties.

### Interior

The cabin punches above its weight. The **14.6-inch central touchscreen** and **8.8-inch driver display** are crisp and responsive. But there’s a catch: **no physical climate controls**. Everything – including temperature, fan speed, and even the side mirror adjustment – is buried in the touchscreen. This is a problem in a three-year-old car when the screen gets laggy. Owners in cold climates have reported **screen dimming issues** in direct sunlight.

**12V battery drain:** A known gremlin on this vehicle. The EX2’s 12V battery (used for door locks, infotainment standby, etc.) can drain in as little as 72 hours if the car is left unplugged with the proximity key nearby. This is a common issue across many Chinese EVs (looking at you, BYD). A **battery maintainer** or regularly plugging in the high-voltage charger (which keeps the 12V topped up) is the workaround.

**Spatial entry:** The EX2 is surprisingly easy to get into – the door sill aperture is wide, and the hip-point is low, making it accessible for all ages. The **1,320 litres** with seats folded is best-in-class – IKEA flatpack warriors, rejoice.

### Mechanically

Geely’s global reputation has improved massively since the Volvo acquisition. The EX2’s **LFP battery** is a plus: it’s more thermally stable and lasts longer than NMC. But the **cooling system** for the battery (a liquid-cooled plate system) has had **coolant bypass valve failures** in some Chinese-market cars, leading to reduced DC fast-charging speeds. Geely Australia has a seven-year unlimited-kilometre warranty on the vehicle and eight years on the battery – but read the fine print; the warranty covers “defects,” not degradation beyond normal.

**Security vulnerabilities:** There’s no keyless entry on the base Complete (it uses a physical key), but the Inspire has **keyless go and passive entry**. That system is susceptible to **relay attacks** if thieves get within a few meters of the key fob. A simple **Faraday pouch** is cheap insurance. More worrying: there have been **CAN bus injection exploits** reported for Geely’s infotainment system (see the “Dicas Geely EX2” blog about unlocking the head unit via ADB). While that won’t steal the car, it could allow thieves to disable the immobiliser if they gain physical USB access. A **OBD port lock** is recommended.

### Engines

- *Single electric motor (TZ180XS000 – 60 kW in Complete, TZ180XS001 – 85 kW in Inspire)*

These are **permanent magnet synchronous motors** made by Geely’s subsidiary. No catastrophic failures reported, but:

- **Motor bearing noise:** A faint whine between 50-70 km/h has been noted on some examples. Not a failure, just an annoyance.
- **Inverter capacitor degradation:** If the car has been rapid-charged exclusively, the DC-DC converter and inverter capacitors can degrade faster. No real-world data yet, but it’s an EV 101.
- **Coolant leak at the motor-to-gearbox seal:** A few cases in China where the oil seal weeped. Geely addressed it with a revised seal from August 2025 production. Check the build date.

**Preventative maintenance:** Unlike a combustion car, there’s no oil change. But you should **replace the cabin air filter every 20,000 km** (it’s behind the glovebox, DIYable) and **have the coolant system flushed every 4 years/60,000 km**. Geely’s official schedule is **20,000 km/12 months**, whichever comes first. Ignore it at your peril – the battery cooling loop needs clean fluid.

### Transmission

There’s **no transmission** in the traditional sense. It’s a **single-speed reduction gear** with a planetary final drive. The only mechanical issues could be:

- **Differential fluid breakdown** (not really a problem within 100,000 km).
- **Parking pawl failure** (rare, but if you hear a clunk when engaging park, get it checked).

The **multi-link independent rear suspension** on the Inspire is a genuine boon for ride quality – it’s the same type used on the Volvo EX30. It’s robust, but the **sway bar end links** can develop a dull clunk after 50,000 km on rough Australian roads. A $50 fix.

## 3. Should you buy a used Geely EX2?

### Current used market reality

The MTA data shows **no pre-owned examples yet** – everything is dealer demos with sub-100 km. But that won’t last. Expect the first wave of ex-demo EX2s (Complete and Inspire) to hit the classifieds by **early 2027** with prices around:

- **2026 EX2 Complete, ~5,000 km:** $22,000 – $24,000
- **2026 EX2 Inspire, ~5,000 km:** $26,000 – $29,000

*Depreciation baseline:* New cars drop **20-25% in the first year** in this segment. The MG4 Excite has held up better (around 15-18% due to demand), but the BYD Seagull (Atto 1) sheds closer to 30% because of oversupply. The EX2 will likely settle in the middle – maybe **20-22% after 12 months**.

### Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) calculation

Compared to a petrol MG3 or Toyota Yaris:

- **Fuel: ** $0.225/kWh (off-peak rate in NSW). EX2 consumption ~15 kWh/100 km = **$3.38/100 km**.
- **Petrol Yaris:** 6.0 L/100 km at $1.80/L = **$10.80/100 km**.
- **Annual saving at 15,000 km:** ~$1,100.

Now factor in the higher purchase price. A used EX2 at $24,000 vs a used Yaris at $18,000. The $6k premium takes **5.5 years** to recoup in fuel savings – assuming you keep it that long. If you charge exclusively on a standard rooftop solar, the payback can drop to **3.5 years**.

**Service costs:** Geely offers capped-price servicing for the first 5 years – around $350 per visit (every 12 months/20,000 km). A Yaris will set you back about $250 per visit. Net difference ~$100/year more on the Geely.

**Battery degradation risk:** LFP batteries are robust – expect 80% capacity retention after 200,000 km. But if you need a new battery out of warranty (unlikely in the first 8 years), it could be $8-12k. You’re essentially betting on the warranty.

### The sweet spot

**Buy a used 2026 Geely EX2 Inspire** in 2027, after the first wave of demos hit private sale. The **Inspire** gives you the larger battery, better motor, multi-link rear suspension, and ADAS – and the used premium over the Complete will only be about $4,000. That’s money well spent; the base Complete’s 60 kW motor is adequate for city duties but struggles on highway inclines (0-100 km/h takes ~13 seconds). The Inspire’s 85 kW drops that to ~10.5 seconds.

**Avoid the first six months of production** (July-December 2025 Chinese production, arriving here early 2026). The revised oil seal and AC compressor clutch from mid-2025 on are worth having.

### Verdict

The Geely EX2 is a **genuine game-changer** for the budget EV buyer. It’s not perfect – the touchscreen-only operation will age badly, 12V battery drain is a real annoyance, and the AEB can be skittish. But for the price, the space, the RWD handling, and the stellar warranty, it’s a **strong buy** – especially in Inspire spec.

If you can find a **2026 Inspire with under 15,000 km and a service history showing the ADAS firmware update** – snap it up. You won’t get a better small EV for the money in Australia right now. Just don’t forget the Faraday pouch.
