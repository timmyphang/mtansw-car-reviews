#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

- Generation / codes / timeframe
  - The LDV D90 you see in Australian driveways is the ladder‑frame (body‑on‑frame) large SUV first shown as the Maxus D90 from 2016 and sold in Australia from late‑2017 onward (often referenced internally as SV9A / D90 family). The current MY25/MY26 rethink is the latest refresh of that line. [SAIC Maxus global](https://en.saicmaxus.com/car/d90.shtml), [Wikipedia D90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxus_D90), [LDV MY25 product page](https://www.ldvautomotive.com.au/vehicles/ldv-my25-d90-suv/).
- Product hierarchy
  - Where it sits: the D90 is LDV’s large seven‑seat, family/off‑road SUV — above the smaller LDV G10 people mover or mid‑size SUVs; under no direct premium badge in the range (it’s the flagship large SUV for LDV in Australia). See LDV product pages and brochures for full range context. [LDV brochure/specs PDF](https://cdn.mattaki.com/ldv/static-assets/vehicles/brochures/specifications/d90_specs.pdf).
- Platform & parts commonality
  - Platform: ladder‑frame chassis shared with the LDV/Maxus T60 ute. That means many suspension, body and drivetrain mounts are shared with the T60 pickup – a practical plus for parts availability and independent wreckers. [SAIC Maxus D90 global](https://en.saicmaxus.com/car/d90.shtml), [Drive overview](https://www.drive.com.au/showrooms/ldv/d90/).
- Engines, outputs, transmissions, drive layouts (Australia)
  - Current Australian offering (recent model years): **2.0‑litre turbo petrol — 184 kW / 410 Nm** paired to a **ZF 8‑speed automatic (8AT)**, available in 2WD (rear‑drive) and 4WD variants. Older Australian imports and other markets also saw 2.0‑litre turbo diesel options and earlier 6‑speed automatic/manual gearboxes. [4x4Australia](https://www.4x4australia.com.au/ldv/d90), [SAIC Maxus PDF spec sheet](https://en.saicmaxus.com/uploads/month_202411/files/7d84772e7caf4f720c5033d9513397ac.pdf).
- Marketplace rivals
  - Competes with budget large seven‑seat SUVs and ladder‑frame alternatives: Toyota Prado / Kluger (positional competitors), Ford Everest, Isuzu MU‑X, GWM (and Chinese rivals like GWM Tank / SsangYong in the value segment). Media coverage explicitly frames it against mainstream Japanese/Korean rivals for family buyers seeking value. [CarsGuide comparison](https://www.carsguide.com.au/ldv/d90/vs), [CarExpert 2025 review](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2025-ldv-d90-review).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used LDV D90?

Exterior
- Body & panel issues
  - Panel alignment and finish on early builds has had mixed owner feedback: some units show variable panel gaps and lower‑grade trim fitment (common for value‑priced Chinese‑origins early production runs). Owner forums and ProductReview threads highlight an inconsistent finish compared with Japanese rivals. [ProductReview D90](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/ldv-d90), [LDV forum discussions](https://www.ldvforum.com/).
- Trim vulnerabilities & paint
  - Gloss plastics and cheap chrome can scratch or pit faster than premium competitors. Expect scuffs on sill plastics and lower bumper trims on high‑kilometre, worksite or off‑road examples.
- Recalls and safety hardware
  - Official recall: a significant Australian recall (about 4,785 D90s, model years 2017–2024) for a faulty T‑piece connector in coolant piping that can fail and cause coolant leaks, overheating and engine stalling. This is an important safety recall to verify on any Victorian/NSW/Vic‑registered vehicle. [Vehicle recalls database REC‑006141](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006141), [Drive report](https://www.drive.com.au/news/2017-2024-ldv-d90-recalled-in-australia/), [CarExpert recall piece](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/ldv-d90-recalled-because-it-could-overheat).
- ADAS / camera & radar issues
  - The D90 ships with AEB and lane‑departure systems on many Australian examples (ANCAP technical report confirms). However, owner reports show occasional ADAS malfunctions (unexpected braking or “false intervention” from adaptive cruise), and forum chatter suggests firmware/zeroing quirks. Mixed experiences — some owners praise the system; others report random decelerations and camera/software glitches. There’s not a single universal fix reported beyond dealer software updates. [ANCAP technical report](https://s3.amazonaws.com/cdn.ancap.com.au/app/public/assets/3765ac896c2892134d7d98bfa1e2fc5c24011ba9/original.pdf), [ProductReview ADAS complaints](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/ldv-d90).

Interior
- Durability in hard service
  - For rideshare or tradie duty, the D90’s cabin is spacious but some surfaces (entry sills, seat bolsters, switchgear) show accelerated wear. Owner forums and Carsales owner reviews call out “cheap‑feeling” plastics on high‑use cars. [Carsales owner reviews](https://www.carsales.com.au/owner-reviews/ldv/d90/), [Whirlpool forum notes](https://forums.whirlpool.net.au/archive/2685830).
- Access, third‑row practicality
  - Door sills are high because of ladder‑frame design — good for off‑road clearance, worse for small kids or elderly passengers. Third row is usable for kids and short trips; entry/exit requires a decent leg raise.
- Electrical & infotainment
  - Recurring issues:
    - 12V battery drain and sleep‑mode quirks reported in owner threads; aftermarket installs and parasitic draws (alarms, fridges, poor ECU sleep) have been blamed.
    - Infotainment: earlier D90s used older head units with smaller screens; MY25 refresh introduced a larger 12.3‑inch screen and digital cluster. Even so, CarPlay/Android Auto failures and intermittent USB port faults are common enough that owners post DIY fixes and YouTube walkthroughs. [CarExpert interior review of MY25](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2025-ldv-d90-review), [YouTube Android Auto fix video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uvPyfFuMNbQ).
  - Practical tip: test every USB port, Bluetooth pairing and reverse camera during inspection; ask for proof of recent software updates.

Mechanically
- Engineering reputation
  - The D90’s ladder frame and heavy‑duty mechanicals are sensible for towing and off‑road use; LDV’s engineering has improved but earlier builds suffered quality and parts‑fit inconsistencies, and dealer network coverage historically lagged larger brands. [Drive summary](https://www.drive.com.au/reviews/2025-ldv-d90-executive-4x4-review/).
- Security vulnerabilities
  - Like many modern vehicles, immobiliser, remote and CAN‑bus attack vectors exist. There are anecdotal immobiliser issues and public discussion of bypass techniques on aftermarket forums — treat these as real risks in high‑theft areas. Recommended hardening:
    - Fit a physical OBD‑port lock and a proven aftermarket immobiliser or GPS‑plus‑immobiliser solution.
    - Consider UWB digital key retrofit or a second‑stage relay prevention device for keyless entry models.
    - If you value the vehicle, a steering‑column lock and covert tracking are cheap insurance.
  - Sources discussing CAN/OBD risks and immobiliser conversations: TibbsForge CAN‑bus analysis and OBD2 port lock guides; JustAnswer thread on LDV immobiliser discussions. [CAN‑bus security overview](https://tibbsforge.com/can-bus-security-news/), [OBD2 port lock advice](https://obdadvisor.com/best-obd2-port-locks/), [JustAnswer immobiliser discussion](https://www.justanswer.com/uk-car/78tqt-convoy-minibus-ldv-workshop-does.html).

Engines
- Overview
  - The D90 family runs a handful of 2.0‑litre turbocharged engines across markets — petrol and diesel flavours — and outputs differ by market/year. For Australian buyers the most relevant are the 2.0 turbo petrol in current models and earlier diesel variants found on used stock. Manufacturer specs show petrol at ~184 kW / 410 Nm and diesel quoted around 158–160 kW / 480–500 Nm. [SAIC Maxus spec sheet](https://en.saicmaxus.com/car/d90.shtml), [LDV D90 brochure](https://cdn.mattaki.com/ldv/static-assets/vehicles/brochures/d90_ebrochure.pdf), [SAIC overseas PDF](https://en.saicmaxus.com/uploads/month_202411/files/7d84772e7caf4f720c5033d9513397ac.pdf).
- What to watch for — engine variant notes (each italicised below)
  - *2.0‑litre turbo petrol (≈184 kW / 410 Nm)*
    - Failure points / owner complaints:
      - Throttle lag/hunt and intermittent hesitation reported on some owner threads.
      - Carbon build‑up on direct‑injection engines (intake valves, EGR) if mostly urban running — common petrol‑direct issues; owners use carbon cleaning services. [Expert Carbon Cleaning LDV page](https://expertcarboncleaning.com.au/vehicle-manufacturer/ldv/).
      - Cooling system vulnerability: if the recalled T‑piece applies to petrol models in your VIN range, a failure can cause sudden overheating and power loss. Check VIN history. [Vehicle recall REC‑006141](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006141).
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Follow a conservative servicing rhythm: oil + filter every 10,000 km (or yearly), inspect/clean intake and throttle every 60k–100k km depending on city use, EGR/DPCV checks if you see limp modes.
      - Cooling system inspection at each service for hose integrity and replaced per any recall instructions.
  - *2.0‑litre turbo diesel (≈158–160 kW / 480–500 Nm)*
    - Failure points / owner complaints:
      - Diesel DPF and EGR fouling when used on short urban runs; owners report elevated fuel use and blocked regenerations.
      - Owner reports of high‑pressure fuel pump and injector issues on older diesel units in some markets (common to modern small‑bore diesels).
      - Cooling‑pipe recall may affect some diesel early models too.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Adhere to 10,000 km oil/service intervals in Aussie conditions if you tow or do frequent short trips; aggressive DPF maintenance (occasional highway runs to allow complete regen) and EGR cleaning at 60–100k km recommended.
  - *Earlier 2.0 variants / pre‑refresh outputs (6‑speed era)*
    - Failure points:
      - Older, early‑build engines and poor calibration caused intermittent drivability complaints in forums. Where possible buy a refreshed MY25+ car for improved ECU tuning and updated hardware. [CarExpert MY25 coverage](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2025-ldv-d90-review).

Transmission
- Hardware & known behaviours
  - Main gearbox: **ZF 8‑speed torque‑converter automatic** on modern D90s (longitudinal layout). Earlier cars used 6‑speed autos or manuals in some markets. The 8AT’s behaviour is broadly conventional but owner reports show:
    - Torque‑converter seal leaks (torque‑converter seal replacement is a known repair on D90s — AutoGuru lists this specific operation).
    - Shifting faults into reverse or limp‑mode events reported on owner sites and repair logs.
    - Heat management is critical: the earlier coolant T‑piece recall can indirectly affect transmission if the engine overheats and stalls. [SAIC Maxus D90 spec](https://en.saicmaxus.com/car/d90.shtml), [AutoGuru torque converter seal page](https://www.autoguru.com.au/repairs/torque-converter-seal-replacement/ldv/d90), [AutoGuru reverse failure guide](https://www.autoguru.com.au/repairs/transmission-wont-go-into-reverse/ldv/d90).
- Failure modes & symptoms to test during inspection
  - Slipping, hesitation on take‑off, clunking during shifts, delayed engagement to reverse, transmission fluid smell (overheating) and warning lights. If any exist in test drive, walk away or get a dealer inspection — transmission rebuilds are expensive, and some owners report short lifespans on problem boxes.

  - Median price: AUD $25,470
  - Year range: 2021 – 2025
  - Median odometer: 61,122 km
  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 10
  - Treat that median as the realistic “what you’ll pay” for an average 2021–2023 D90 in NSW right now (dealer‑listed). Use the MTA NSW snapshot to set expectations against Carsales/Drive listings. (Snapshot provided in your brief.)
- Depreciation & retained value
  - The D90 buys you a lot of space and kit for the money, but it also carries rapid depreciation versus established Japanese rivals. Analysis pieces on resale (AutoExpert) and valuation trends note LDV and similar Chinese‑made SUVs give big upfront savings but poorer three‑year retention than Toyota/Ford/Isuzu segment leaders. If you value long‑term resale you’ll lose more percentage points with the D90 than, say, a Prado or Everest. [AutoExpert resale analysis](https://autoexpert.com.au/posts/large-4wd-suv-resale-value), [CarsGuide price pages](https://www.carsguide.com.au/ldv/d90/price/2025).
- Total cost of ownership strategy (TCO)
  - How to think about costs:
    - Depreciation: buy used to avoid the worst of the new‑car drop. The NSW median price ($25.5k) shows you can get a late‑model D90 for far less than a new Toyota/Isuzu equivalent.
    - Fuel: official combined figures for petrol D90s sit around 9.1 L/100 km (ADR figures vary by trim). If you do 15,000 km/year at 9.1 L/100 km and petrol is $2.00/L, fuel ≈ $2,730/yr. At $1.80/L it’s ≈ $2,457/yr — so expect $2.5–3k/yr in fuel for typical family use. [CarsGuide fuel consumption](https://www.carsguide.com.au/ldv/d90/fuel-consumption).
    - Servicing & repairs: independent workshop labour and parts for LDV are cheaper than big brands for many items, but unpredictability of warranty work and occasional expensive mechanical failures (cooling pipe, transmission) can spike bills. Always check warranty status and dealer service history.
    - Insurance and registration: treat as standard for a large SUV — but insurance premiums might be higher if you live in a high‑theft postcode or have a history of claims.
  - Hybrid/EV premium math for D90
    - LDV doesn’t offer a mainstream hybrid/EV D90 in Australia — the current lineup is petrol (with diesels in older stock). So you’re not choosing between ICE and hybrid within the same model here. If you’re comparing an ICE D90 to a hybrid rival, run a simple payback:
      - Annual fuel expenditure differential / premium paid for hybrid = years to payback.
      - Example: if hybrid saves 3–4 L/100 km over 15,000 km/yr, that’s ~675 L saved per year. At $2.00/L = $1,350/yr saving. If hybrid costs $7,000 more, payback ~5 years (ignoring servicing/electricity cost differences). Use local fuel prices and driving profile to decide — city drivers benefit more from hybrid tech.
    - For the D90 specifically, unless you need ladder‑frame towing capacity, consider a hybrid or petrol‑efficient rival if running costs and smog‑free city driving are priorities.
- Practical buying recommendation
  - Bottom line: Yes — but with strong caveats.
    - Buy used if:
      - You want maximum space and towing for minimum capital outlay.
      - You can accept lower resale and are willing to manage preventive maintenance.
      - You pick the right year/trim and do a rigorous pre‑purchase inspection.
    - Avoid if:
      - You want a worry‑free long‑term ownership with the tightest reliability record and best dealer support (Toyota/Isuzu remain safer picks).
  - Which year/trim is the sweet spot?
    - Aim for a refreshed MY25 or a late‑model 2023–2024 Executive (4WD if you need towing/off‑road) with full dealer service history and any recall work completed — the MY25 refresh addressed interior hardware and software, so it’s the safer bet. [CarExpert 2025 review and MY25 details](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2025-ldv-d90-review), [LDV MY25 product page](https://www.ldvautomotive.com.au/vehicles/ldv-my25-d90-suv/).
  - Pre‑purchase checklist (non‑negotiable)
    - Verify any outstanding recall work (VIN check) — the coolant T‑piece recall is a show‑stopper if not done. [VehicleRecalls](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006141).
    - Full dealer/service history with evidence of cooling system work and software updates.
    - Test drive long enough to stimulate transmission shifts; watch for slip, shudder, delayed reverse, or odd noises.
    - Check infotainment/CarPlay/Android Auto operation and multiple USB ports.
    - Get a coolant pressure test and scan for stored fault codes (cooling, transmission, ADAS).
    - Inspect underbody, suspension mounts and look for evidence of serious off‑road abuse (frame checks matter on ladder‑frame vehicles).
    - Consider a pre‑purchase inspection by a specialist familiar with LDV/Maxus/T60 parts and known D90 failure points.
  - Final verdict in one line:
    - The D90 is a terrific “space for price” buy if you’re pragmatic: pick a refreshed MY25 or late‑model 2023/24 Executive with a clean recall/service history, be ready to maintain it properly (10,000 km service rhythm, check cooling pipes) and accept faster depreciation and an occasionally fiddly infotainment/ADAS experience.
