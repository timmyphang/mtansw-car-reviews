#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation and codes: This review covers the Mk3 / third‑generation Renault Trafic (2014–present in global form, with phase updates through 2019/2022 and further model-year tweaks up to 2026). The latest Australian line‑up moves the Trafic towards new powertrain choices (including the E‑Tech electric family) while retaining the same mid‑size LCV role the Trafic has occupied for decades [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Trafic), [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/renault/trafic/engine).
- Where it sits in the range: The Trafic is Renault’s mid‑size light commercial van — above small city vans (Kangoo in Europe) and below large vans (Renault Master). It is a direct competitor to mid‑size vans from Ford, VW and Mercedes in the Aussie tradie and fleet world.
- Shared platform / badge‑engineering: The Trafic shares its technical underpinnings and heavy badge‑engineering history with the Nissan NV300 / Primastar and (in previous years) the Fiat Talento and Opel/Vauxhall Vivaro — good news for parts availability and third‑party workshop knowledge [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Trafic).
- Australian market engine/transmission/drive options (market summary):
  - Diesel: 1.6 dCi variants (older phase engines) and 2.0 Blue dCi diesels (the 170 hp / 125 kW version is the commonly referenced MY26 engine producing ~380 Nm) — both front‑wheel drive layouts [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/renault/trafic/engine), [UltimateSpecs](https://www.ultimatespecs.com/car-specs/Renault/138519/Renault-Trafic-3-Phase-3-Combi-L1H1-20-Blue-dCi-170-EDC.html).
  - Auto vs manual: Historically available with manuals and the six‑speed dual‑clutch EDC automatic; from 2026 Renault has moved to a nine‑speed torque‑converter automatic (manuals were removed from the 2026 local range) [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/2026-renault-trafic-price-and-specs-confirmed-with-key-upgrades), [Carexpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/2026-renault-trafic-price-and-specs-goodbye-manual-hello-new-auto).
  - Electric: Trafic E‑Tech (battery electric) introduced as part of Renault’s Ampere LCV architecture for electric vans [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/750166/renault-flexis-trafic-estafette-goelette-electric-vans-presented/), [Fleet News/Parkers](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/van/reviews/renault-trafic-e-tech-review).
- Marketplace rivals: Ford Transit Custom, Volkswagen Transporter, Mercedes‑Benz Vito, Peugeot/Opel/Vauxhall medium vans — the usual suspects in the mid‑size LCV segment [WhatCar?](https://www.whatcar.com/news/best-medium-vans/n20726), [The Van Expert](https://www.thevanexpert.co.uk/triple-test-mercedes-benz-vito-vs-renault-trafic-vs-volkswagen-transporter/).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Renault Trafic?
This is the bit tradies and fleet managers care about. Below I split faults into Exterior, Interior, Mechanical, Engines and Transmission so you can skim to the bits you actually need.

- Exterior:
  - Panel fit and paint: Generally acceptable for a working van — but like most commercial vans the finish can be thin on base models and vulnerable to scratching on gloss‑black or dark trim pieces. Expect scuffs on bumpers, sliding doors and rear doors from everyday racking and ladder work; cheaper early‑spec vans sometimes skimp on corrosion protection compared with premium rivals.
  - Trim vulnerabilities: Higher trims have glossy trim and black plastic around mirrors — these scuff and fade in Aussie sun unless protected. The brochure/trim notes show features such as electric folding mirrors tend to become available only on mid‑to‑upper trims [Trafic eBrochure](https://cdn.group.renault.com/ren/gb/transversal-assets/brochures/van-ebrochures/TRAFIC-eBrochure.pdf.asset.pdf/82011f21e1.pdf).
  - Recalls & safety: There’s an active history of Trafic recall campaigns globally — independent trackers list multiple campaigns across build years (airbag sensor replacements and other remedies among them) so always VIN‑check for outstanding recalls before you buy [Renault recall search](https://www.renault.co.uk/recall-campaigns.html), [vehicle‑recall tracker](https://vehicle-recall.co.uk/recalls/cars/renault/trafic).
  - ADAS quirks: The Trafic’s later models (post‑Oct 2024 builds) come with upgraded ADAS and standard AEB; ANCAP reports the October‑2024‑onwards trucks achieve a higher ADAS grading with AEB detecting pedestrians and cyclists as standard [ANCAP](https://www.ancap.com.au/safety-ratings/renault/trafic/387ae4), [Euro NCAP 2025 note](https://news.euroncap.com/safertrucksandvans/renault-trafic---euro-ncap-2025-commercial-van-safety---4-stars/s/a386e83f-a988-4b8a-a6f8-2e76915e7090). However, ADAS sensors (radar/camera) are sensitive to misalignment, dirt and small bumper damage; owner threads record warning lights and false faults that may need recalibration — check for service records showing sensor calibrations [Renault ADAS page](https://business.renault.co.uk/van-range/trafic/adas.html).
  - Practical check: Inspect bumper mounts, sensor faces and look for crash repairs around the front bumper/camera housing.

- Interior:
  - Durability: The Trafic cabin is designed for hard work but lower‑spec vans use harder plastics that show wear — steering wheels and seat bolsters can look tired after heavy use (rideshare/fleet work). Higher trims have better materials and more tech [Auto Express](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/renault/trafic), [Parkers](https://www.parkers.co.uk/vans-pickups/renault/trafic/2014-review/).
  - Entry/exit / ergonomics: Door sills and sliding doors are wide and practical; the cabin is easy to climb into for tradespeople. Watch out for door check arms and hinges that can wear or rust on neglected vans.
  - Electrical & infotainment: The EasyLink 8" system appears in brochures and many late models; multiple owners report MediaNav / MediaNav Evolution faults — frozen boot screens, dead units after updates, unresponsive touch areas and blank displays. Specialist repair houses advertise Trafic unit repairs and offer symptom lists (frozen at logo, not booting) — check the unit boots cleanly and that Bluetooth/phone projection works before buying [Dealer brochure with EasyLink](https://www.thetaxishop.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/RENAULT-TRAFIC-E-BROCHURE-2024.pdf), [Revtronic infotainment repairs](https://www.revtronic.com/product/renault-media-navigation-system-repair-trafic/), [AdvancedRepair units](https://advancedrepair.co.uk/renault-trafic-radio-sat-nav-media-unit-lcd-display-touch-screen-repair-4225-p.asp).
  - 12‑volt drain and accessories: Owner‑reported electrical issues and claims data show a non‑trivial portion of problems originate in electrical systems (accessories wiring, battery drain). MotorEasy’s reliability summary flags electrical claims as a common category — check for a service history showing battery and alternator checks [MotorEasy](https://www.motoreasy.com/renault-reliability/trafic).
  - Practical check: Turn the screen on/off repeatedly, test Bluetooth/Apple CarPlay/Android Auto, leave door‑closed for 24 hrs and re‑measure battery voltage where possible; ask for any firmware updates performed by dealer.

- Mechanically:
  - General engineering reputation: The Trafic rides the balance between economy and commercial duty. The 2.0 Blue dCi (later engines) have proven reasonably robust, but the line has a history of emission‑control component grease points — EGR, DPF and turbo assemblies are the recurrent trouble spots on diesels [ClickMechanic](https://www.clickmechanic.com/blog/common-problems-with-renault-trafic/), [TransitCenter](https://www.transitcenter.ie/renault-trafic-common-problems-issues-t-100.html).
  - Security vulnerabilities: There are no widely published, specific Trafic‑only 'keyless bypass' exploits in the search results for Australian buyers. That said, modern commercial vans are attractive to thieves — recommended countermeasures are physical steering‑wheel or column locks, a visible immobiliser, and fitting a hardwired GPS tracker for fleet use.
  - Water ingress & wiring loom corrosion: Long‑running owner reports point to wiring loom corrosion and water ingress in areas around doors and rear lights — this causes mysterious drains, intermittent lighting faults and sensor errors [ClickMechanic](https://www.clickmechanic.com/blog/common-problems-with-renault-trafic/).
  - Practical check: Lift carpets in the load area and inside the cabin for water stains, inspect loom connectors for corrosion, check rear‑door seals.

- Engines:
  - (Italicised subsections for every engine variant discussed below)

  *1.6 dCi (twin‑turbo variants and earlier 1.6 series)*
  - Typical faults: EGR valve clogging, EGR cooler blockages, DPF clogging and turbocharger wear — particularly on vans used for short, urban trips where regeneration doesn’t complete. Owner threads and service guides repeatedly list EGR/DPF/turbo as the primary trouble trio on 1.6 diesels [TransitCenter](https://www.transitcenter.ie/renault-trafic-common-problems-issues-t-100.html), [Autodoc](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/renault-trafic-gearbox-egr-valve-problems-and-solutions).
  - Symptoms to watch for: Loss of power, limp‑home mode, frequent DPF regenerations, rising oil consumption and smoking.
  - Preventative maintenance: For heavy/commercial use — oil and filter every 10,000 km; EGR cleaning/check every 50–80,000 km depending on duty cycle; force‑regeneration checks and periodic long motorway drives to keep the DPF clear. If the van operates mainly in town — insist on recent DPF regeneration records or DPF replacement history.
  - Known repairs: EGR/DPF cleaning or replacement, turbo replacement if oil starvation or excessive soot. Expect mid‑to‑high bills for DPF replacement if neglected.

  *2.0 Blue dCi (commonly quoted as 170 PS / 125 kW, often **M9R**)*
  - Typical faults: Generally more robust than some early 1.6 units but still subject to turbo wear, injector issues and emissions component clogging over time. The 2.0 Blue dCi (M9R family) is broadly well regarded but workshop writeups still point to heat and soot deposit problems in stop/start urban use [UltimateSpecs](https://www.ultimatespecs.com/car-specs/Renault/138519/Renault-Trafic-3-Phase-3-Combi-L1H1-20-Blue-dCi-170-EDC.html), [motoranimal on M9R](https://motoranimal.com/renault-nissan-m9r-engine-2-0-dci-problems/).
  - Symptoms to watch for: Turbo lag, smoke on acceleration, warning lights for emission‑system faults, loss of torque.
  - Preventative maintenance: Heavy use—oil+filter every 10,000 km; light/private use—service at least every 15,000 km but do long runs to keep DPF healthy. Injector and turbo inspections from 120,000 km onwards recommended.
  - Workshop tip: Ask for a compression/injector balance check if the van has high kilometres and shaky idle.

  *Trafic E‑Tech (Electric)*
  - Typical faults: Newer item — main concerns in used market are battery state, charger health and software/thermal management. EVs remove turbo/EGR/DPF risk but introduce battery degradation risk and potential expensive repairs if the pack is out of warranty. Parkers and Fleet News coverage suggests the E‑Tech is competitive for duty cycles that allow regular charging [Parkers E-Tech review](https://www.parkers.co.uk/vans-pickups/renault/trafic/2023-e-tech-review/), [Fleet News](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/van/reviews/renault-trafic-e-tech-review).
  - Preventative maintenance: Battery inspection, state‑of‑health report, and service history showing battery cooling system checks. For TCO comparison see section 3 below and Renault’s E‑Tech materials [Renault E‑Tech brochure](https://cdn.group.renault.com/ren/gb/transversal-assets/brochures/van-ebrochures/NEW-TRAFIC-eBrochure.pdf.asset.pdf/035d904b67.pdf).

- Transmission:
  - Six‑speed EDC dual‑clutch automatic:
    - Known problems: Renault’s EDC dual‑clutch boxes have been associated with low‑speed shudder, judder and software/calibration issues as they age. Workshops warn of clutch wear and mechatronic faults; some Trafic EDC units have required software fixes and, in persistent cases, clutch pack/mechatronic overhaul [ASR Gearbox Repairs on EDC issues](https://www.asrgearboxrepairs.co.uk/edc-dual-clutch-renault/).
    - Practical checks: Test at low-speed town crawling for shudder, listen for judder under light acceleration, and ensure no limp‑home or ECU gearbox faults recorded.
  - PF6 / related manual & commercial gearboxes:
    - Typical faults: Synchro wear, dual mass flywheel failures in older models, bearing noise and gear selection issues — common in vans that have been used hard with heavy payloads [Eco-Torque PF6 guide](https://eco-torque.co.uk/blogs/news/pf6-gearbox-a-guide-to-the-renault-trafic-master-vauxhall-vivaro-movano-nissan-primastar).
    - Practical checks: With manual vehicles, test every gear for crunching and engagement quality; smell the clutch (burnt smell indicates a slipping clutch).
  - Nine‑speed torque‑converter automatic (2026 change):
    - Renault replaced the EDC with a nine‑speed torque converter on the 2026 Trafic. Early public claims from Renault say the torque‑converter box is smoother and more economical, which addresses one of the EDC complaints for low‑speed van use [CarsGuide on 2026 changes](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/2026-renault-trafic-price-and-specs-confirmed-with-key-upgrades), [Carexpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/2026-renault-trafic-price-and-specs-goodbye-manual-hello-new-auto).
    - Practical check for newer autos: Ensure smooth engagement from cold and steady shifts at highway speeds; torque converters can hide fluid overheating if oil hasn’t been serviced to schedule.

- NSW dealer market snapshot (live MTA NSW scrape):

  - Median price: AUD $13,990
  - Year range: 2014 – 2024
  - Median odometer: 96,434 km
  - Transmission mix: Automatic: 1, Manual: 2
  This is a tight local market — cheap older examples are available, as are near‑new pricier units; be realistic about km and service history when a 2014–2018 van is listed around the median price point you see above (MTA snapshot supplied by you).
- Current used marketplace pricing and depreciation:
  - Entry and ceiling: NSW listings show bargains (AUD $12,800) through to near‑new LWB / premium crew vans pushing $49,990. Median used listing of ~$13,990 implies the local market is dominated by older, high‑km units.
  - Depreciation metrics: Public used‑van analyses suggest medium vans can lose ~40–45% of their value in the first three years and retain low residuals versus passenger cars — Parkers cites a retained value example where Trafics lost a big chunk in three years [Parkers depreciation notes](https://www.parkers.co.uk/vans-pickups/best/2016/medium-van-depreciation-residual-values-compared/), and TheMoneyCalculator estimates ~42% in three years in general terms [TheMoneyCalculator](https://www.themoneycalculator.com/vehicle-finance/calculators/car-depreciation-by-make-and-model/RENAULT/TRAFIC/). In short: buy used, and you can avoid the steep early depreciation — but fleet‑spec vans will still trade on utility, condition and service history.
- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy — how to think about it:
  - Running costs to watch:
    - Fuel: Official WLTP/advertised diesel consumption for modern Trafics (2.0 Blue dCi family) sits around mid‑5 to low‑7 L/100km depending on spec and load; CarsGuide summarises the MY26 2.0 diesel at around 125 kW/380 Nm with WLTP numbers (real‑world will be higher when loaded) [CarsGuide engine page](https://www.carsguide.com.au/renault/trafic/engine), [Auto‑Data consumption figures](https://www.auto-data.net/en/renault-trafic-model-1956). Owner‑reported Fuelly entries give a reality check that many owners average more than advertised when used as a real work van [Fuelly Trafic page](https://www.fuelly.com/car/renault/trafic).
    - Maintenance and repairs: Key cost drivers are DPF/EGR/turbo work on diesels and gearbox repairs on older EDC autos. Aggregated claim data (MotorEasy/CarCostCheck) shows gearbox and engine items are a notable share of common repairs [MotorEasy](https://www.motoreasy.com/renault-reliability/trafic), [CarCostCheck](https://www.carcostcheck.co.uk/common-problems/renault/trafic).
    - Depreciation: Medium vans are high volume and fleet‑driven, so residuals are low but predictable — buying older, well‑serviced units typically gives best long‑term per‑km cost.
  - Hybrid/EV vs Diesel maths (short summary tailored for Australia):
    - If you do mainly urban stop/start runs, diesels suffer DPF/EGR issues — which translate into workshop bills and downtime. Renault claims E‑Tech hybrids can reduce consumption by ~25–40% in urban cycles, and EV versions remove the emissions hardware problem entirely [Renault E‑Tech consumption page](https://www.renault.ie/E-Tech/consumption.html), [Fleet News/Parkers E‑Tech coverage](https://www.fleetnews.co.uk/van/reviews/renault-trafic-e-tech-review).
    - Break‑even depends on annual km and charging access: use KBB’s hybrid break‑even approach — if your annual diesel spend plus expected DPF/EGR repair risk exceeds the EV/hybrid extra acquisition cost, consider EV/hybrid. If the used market premium for E‑Tech units is high and your duty cycle is mixed (country/highway), a well‑spec’d diesel may still be cheaper initially but risky on short urban duty [KBB hybrid break‑even logic](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/).
    - Practical Australian rule‑of‑thumb: For >30,000 km/year with access to charging and predictable urban stops, an E‑Tech yields TCO wins over 5–7 years. For <20,000 km/year and lots of highway work, a diesel 2.0 Blue dCi is often cheaper to buy used and simpler to maintain — but insist on solid service history.
- Final buying recommendation (clear, blunt):
  - If you want cheap, rugged, and predictable: target a late‑model 2.0 Blue dCi (post‑2018 facelift) with a full service history and a manual (if you can find one and prefer simplicity) or a 2.0 with the early EDC but only if the vendor can prove gearbox oil changes and no history of shudder. Given NSW listings skew older and high‑km, the median $13,990 buys an older work van — bargain, yes, but treat it as a rebuild candidate unless service records look good.
  - If you want fewer emissions‑system headaches and better low‑speed drivability: pay a little premium for the later 2.0 Blue dCi with the nine‑speed torque‑converter auto (2026 models) or consider the Trafic E‑Tech if you can access charging and the premium fits your TCO window [CarsGuide 2026 notes](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/2026-renault-trafic-price-and-specs-confirmed-with-key-upgrades), [Carexpert 2026 notes](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/2026-renault-trafic-price-and-specs-goodbye-manual-hello-new-auto).
  - Sweet spot pick: For NSW used market realities (median price ~AUD $14k, median ~96k km), aim for:
    - A 2017–2019 Trafic with the 2.0 Blue dCi, manual or well‑documented EDC with gearbox service records — these balance price, parts availability and avoid the very oldest 1.6 units prone to EGR/DPF grief. Verify the following before signing: full service history, VIN recall clearance, clean infotainment boot, and no persistent ADAS or gearbox fault codes.
  - Walk away or renegotiate if:
    - Owner can’t prove regular long runs or DPF regeneration history on a city‑used diesel.
    - The infotainment is dead and seller wants full price (repair bills can be several hundred to a few thousand).
    - There are outstanding recalls, major crash repairs near the AEB sensor area, or gearbox shudder on test drive.

Final word: The Trafic is a sensible mid‑size van with a lot to like for commercial buyers in Australia — cheap used examples are plentiful, parts are common thanks to badge cousins, and a careful pre‑purchase check (DPF/EGR history, EDC gearbox service, infotainment health and recall status) will save you from the usual pitfalls. Buy smart, buy serviced, and treat a bargain 100k+ km Trafic as a working tool, not a pampered runaround — and you’ll probably have decades of ladder‑carrying service ahead.
