#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation / production timeframe / internal codes  
  The GWM Cannon in Australia is the local evolution of Great Wall’s mid‑size Pao/Poer family — the Cannon nameplate appears in refreshed 2025–2026 lineups (global P71 platform derivatives). The range you see in NSW right now are 2025–2026 examples, including lifestyle (XSR) and commercial variants. For global tech context see the P71 platform notes on Wikipedia: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon).

- Where it sits in the GWM lineup  
  The Cannon sits above work‑orientated single‑cab/cab‑chassis entry grades and below the larger Cannon Alpha flagship derivatives. Trim names in Australia include Premium, Lux, Ultra, Vanta and the lifestyle‑focused XSR — official Australian specs are in the GWM brochure: [GWM Cannon AU page](https://www.gwmanz.com/au/models/ute/cannon/) and the PDF brochure: [cannon_brochure_au.pdf](https://assets.gwmanz.com/f/256395/x/2e1c1dad6e/cannon_brochure_au.pdf).

- Shared global platform & parts availability  
  The Cannon is part of the broader Pao/Poer/Cannon family built on GWM’s P71 architecture (same engineering roots as other Pao/Poer trucks). That broad family means mechanical commonality with other GWM utes — helpful for parts availability and aftermarket support in markets where GWM has a dealer footprint. See Wikipedia for platform context: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon).

- Market‑specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drivetrains (Australia)  
  Australian Cannon line‑up (as listed by GWM and the brochure):
  - 2.4‑litre turbo‑diesel — 135 kW, 480 Nm. Paired with GWM’s self‑developed 9‑speed automatic (seen on 4x4 models) — [GWM AU model page](https://www.gwmanz.com/au/models/ute/cannon/).  
  - 2.0‑litre turbo‑diesel — ~120 kW, 400 Nm. Often paired with an 8‑speed automatic depending on trim — [Cannon brochure](https://assets.gwmanz.com/f/256395/x/2e1c1dad6e/cannon_brochure_au.pdf) and CarsGuide summary: [carsguide.com.au/gwm/cannon/engine](https://www.carsguide.com.au/gwm/cannon/engine).  
  - Globally there are petrol, HEV and EV variants (not all sold in every market) — see Wikipedia for engine code lists: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon).

  Drivetrain choices in Australia include 4x2 and 4x4, with rear differential locks on 4x4s and front/rear locker capability on the XSR variant per GWM Australia: [GWM AU model page](https://www.gwmanz.com/au/models/ute/cannon/).

- Marketplace rivals  
  The Cannon targets the mainstream mid‑size ute battleground: Toyota HiLux, Ford Ranger, Isuzu D‑Max, Mitsubishi Triton, LDV T60 and SsangYong Musso. Comparative reviews and side‑by‑sides are available from CarsGuide and Drive: [carsguide.com.au/gwm/cannon/vs](https://www.carsguide.com.au/gwm/cannon/vs) and Drive’s market coverage: [drive.com.au/news](https://www.drive.com.au/news/).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used GWM Cannon?

Exterior
- Panel fit & paint
  - Panel alignment is generally decent for the class but watch doors and tailgate on demonstrator or contractor units — some owners report trim rattles and slightly loose body mouldings after heavy use (commoner on earlier Pao/Poer builds). Workshop guides and owner forums document fit‑and‑finish niggles: [mechanicvoice.com/common-gwm-cannon-x-problems](https://mechanicvoice.com/common-gwm-cannon-x-problems/).
  - Gloss black plastic and lower bumper trims are prone to scratching when used as a working ute; expect scuffs unless previous owners fitted tub liners or paint protection.

- Recalls and structural safety items
  - There’s an important Australian recall affecting some Cannon/Cannon Alpha models for an Electronic Power Steering wiring harness that may contact the driveshaft and risk power‑steering loss or engine shutdown — details on the official recall listing: [vehiclerecalls.gov.au REC‑006410](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006410).
  - A diesel fuel filter assembly recall (incorrect lubricant on filter O‑ring causing potential fuel leak) was also listed: [vehiclerecalls.gov.au REC‑006123](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006123). If you’re shopping, check the VIN against those recall lists and demand proof of rectification.

- ADAS / active safety temperament
  - Early consumer and media feedback flagged over‑zealous ADAS (lane‑keep, automatic braking, adaptive cruise) and annoying behaviours. GWM has issued software revisions starting in 2025 to tone down interventions on related models — coverage and manufacturer response summarized here: [drivencarguide.co.nz](https://www.drivencarguide.co.nz/news/how-gwm-is-making-its-tank-500-and-cannon-alpha-ute-less-annoying-active-safety-revisions/) and [drivelife.co.nz](https://www.drivelife.co.nz/2024/12/gwm-listens-and-fixes-issues-with-their-cars/).  
  - Practical check when buying: verify ADAS warnings clear, adaptive cruise behaves smoothly on long test drives, and cameras/sensors have no condensation or damage.

Interior
- Durability under hard use (rideshare / trades)
  - The Cannon’s cabin is feature‑rich for the price, but some materials (armrest seams, door pull plastics) show wear earlier under contractor/tow‑truck duty. Owner‑led guides note leather‑look seat wear and abrasion on busy utes: [gwmwiki.com/5-common-great-wall-cannon-problems](https://gwmwiki.com/5-common-great-wall-cannon-problems-and-how-to-fix-them-yourself/).
  - Entry/exit: door sills are typical ute height — not a climber’s worst friend, but repeated heavy ingress/egress may accelerate scuffs to sill plates.

- Electrics and infotainment
  - 12V battery drain patterns: intermittent reports of parasitic draws tied to infotainment and telematics modules exist in owner forums and workshop pages. Do a battery health and parasitic draw test on any used unit (bring a multimeter or ask the seller for recent service evidence). CarsGuide aggregates owner complaints: [carsguide.com.au/gwm/cannon/problems](https://www.carsguide.com.au/gwm/cannon/problems).
  - Infotainment hardware versions: screen sizes and hardware differ by trim — many XSR/Ultra models have larger central screens and wireless features; lower trims use smaller units. Owners report touchscreen freezes and software bugs; many cases are fixed by resets or dealer software updates (see the reset guides): [gwmwiki screen reset](https://gwmwiki.com/troubleshooting-unresponsive-gwm-cannon-radio-media-info-screen-and-non-functioning-options-a-simple-reset-guide/).
  - Connectivity: wired Android Auto can be flaky; owners recommend trying a variety of cables or a wireless adapter if the unit is problematic — community tips on cable fixes and adapters exist on YouTube and BitAuto guides.

Mechanically
- Engineering reputation & general notes
  - GWM’s engineering leap has narrowed the gap to Japanese rivals on features and towing capability, but the brand is still younger in Australia — that means variable dealer support coverage and mixed long‑term reliability signals. Independent writeups (ReDriven, CarsGuide) say “good so far” but warn on parts lead times: [redriven.com/reviews/gwm/cannon/](https://redriven.com/reviews/gwm/cannon/); [carsguide problems](https://www.carsguide.com.au/gwm/cannon/problems).

- Security vulnerabilities (keyless / CAN bus)
  - The industry‑wide CAN‑injection/“headlight” attack has been used to steal many modern cars and is not unique to GWM. If you worry, add physical deterrents: steering‑wheel lock, wheel clamp, or a mechanical kill switch for fuel/power, and consider hard‑wired aftermarket immobilisers. Technical background on CAN injection is well documented: [kentindell.github.io/can-injection](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/) and general coverage: [securityweek.com](https://www.securityweek.com/thieves-use-can-injection-hack-to-steal-cars/). GWM’s remote‑control app and OEM telematics can be useful for tracking — [GWM app features](https://www.gwmanz.com/au/app/).

Engines
- Note: each engine/variant name below is italicised and accompanied by specific failure/maintenance notes.

  - *GW4D24 — 2.4‑litre turbo‑diesel (135 kW / 480 Nm)*  
    - What goes wrong: DPF regeneration issues if used on short trips; some owners report torque delivery feeling flat under heavy load (towing) unless software/ECU updates are current. DPF clogging guides and owner reports: [carcarearena.com/gwm-cannon-x-dpf-problems](https://www.carcarearena.com/gwm-cannon-x-dpf-problems/).  
    - Specific component risk: turbocharger oil feed/return contamination risk if oil change intervals ignored; watch for EGR and DPF soot accumulation. Australian diesel fuel filter recall (O‑ring lubricant error) directly affects diesel engines — check recall status: [vehiclerecalls.gov.au REC‑006123](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006123).  
    - Preventative maintenance: conservative servicing every 10,000 km (or 12 months) if you do many short trips or heavy towing; insist on high‑quality diesel and routine DPF health checks (force regeneration if safe). For exact intervals consult the official manual; GWM manuals index: [en.gwm-club.com/manuals.php](https://en.gwm-club.com/manuals.php?ddlb_language=2).

  - *GW4D20M — 2.0‑litre turbo‑diesel (~120 kW / 400 Nm)*  
    - What goes wrong: similar DPF/EGR sensitivities as the 2.4; slightly less torque under load so watch towing expectations. Some paperwork and spec sheets show this pairing with an 8‑speed auto — check the spec sheet for the exact pairing on the vehicle you inspect: [3rdREV spec sheet PDF](https://zbixy8yqiusexn1c.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/Cannon/models/slux%20and%20lux/spec%20sheet/3rdREV%20GWM%20CANNON-G6RffEb6VscxCISNqi4QOGDgVUIgIm.pdf?download=1).  
    - Preventative maintenance: as per other diesel options — conservative 10,000 km checks for oil, turbo and DPF condition.

  - *GW4C20B — 2.0‑litre petrol turbo (where fitted)*  
    - What goes wrong: not widely used in AU Cannon lineups but present globally — watch for typical turbo‑petrol issues (boost leaks, high‑pressure fuel pump wear) and follow petrol service intervals and spark plug replacements as per manual: see engine code listings at ErwinSalarda: [erwinsalarda.com/great-wall-pao-transmission-and-engine-oil-specifications](https://erwinsalarda.com/great-wall-pao-transmission-and-engine-oil-specifications/).

  - *Hybrid / HEV variants (GW4N20 and 9HAT 9‑speed HEV transmissions)*  
    - What goes wrong: hybrid systems add complexity — battery pack health, high‑voltage harness corrosion or software calibration issues. The 9‑speed HEV (9HAT) has been introduced in later/HEV variants; hybrid circuitry should be scanned for codes during pre‑purchase checks. Global engine/transmission mapping: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon).

Transmission
- Mechanical behaviour and known failure modes
  - GWM uses a mix of transmissions across the Cannon family: ZF 8‑speed automatics on some variants, a self‑developed 9‑speed automatic (claimed for 2.4 diesel HEV pairings), and earlier 6‑speed manuals in some markets. Technical guides note ZF units are generally robust but owner reports indicate occasional roughness or delayed engagement in some GWM automatics — workshop writeups cover these symptoms: [gwmwiki.com/gwm-cannon-transmission/](https://gwmwiki.com/gwm-cannon-transmission/) and mechanic roundups: [sinceremechanic.com/gwm-ute-problems/](https://sinceremechanic.com/gwm-ute-problems/).  
  - Symptoms to look for on a test drive:
    - Hesitation or 'hunt' between ratios under light throttle.  
    - Shuddering or harsh engagement at take‑off (could be torque converter symptoms).  
    - Check for transmission service history — some issues trace back to neglected fluid/service intervals.  
  - Preventative care: insist on a transmission service (fluid + filter) at recommended intervals — if no evidence, budget for a fluid change soon after purchase.

  These are live‑market trade‑plate/demo/new‑vehicle dominated listings — expect many to be near‑new dealer demos or ex‑fleet units.

- Depreciation & resale signals
  - The Cannon is a value aggressive player and has shown better than expected retention in short windows — CarsGuide noted strong retained values for recent GWM utes in some analyses: [carsguide.com.au car resale coverage](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/resale-kings-the-surprising-utes-that-hold-their-value-best-exclusive-report-reveals-the). However, established rivals (Hilux, Ranger, D‑Max) historically hold value the strongest over 3–5 years. Industry analysis warns about long‑term depreciation risk for challenger brands despite impressive early retention: [aut oexpert depreciation analysis](https://autoexpert.com.au/posts/top-8-utes-the-best-sellers-and-their-used-resale-value-depreciation) and marketplace listings provide live comparatives: [carsales GWM Cannon listings](https://www.carsales.com.au/cars/gwm/cannon/).

  - Expectation: compared with a Toyota HiLux or Ford Ranger, a Cannon will likely depreciate faster over a 3–5 year horizon if brand perception and franchise network access are your priority — but you’ll start cheaper and possibly pay less per month for similar spec in the short term.

- TCO strategy and hybrid/EV premium analysis
  - TCO factors to include: purchase price, servicing, parts availability, fuel, insurance, depreciation and anticipated repair risk. With GWM:
    - Purchase price advantage is real — sample NSW median AUD $43,990 for near‑new units is lower than many comparably appointed Hilux/Ranger demos. Use that as a base.
    - Servicing: independent workshops can handle routine jobs, but genuine parts and dealer software updates (important for ADAS/DPF/transmission fixes) may carry premium and lead times — budget extra for occasional dealer visits.
    - Fuel & powertrains: diesels remain the pragmatic choice for heavy towing and regional use. Hybrid or EV Cannon variants may offer fuel savings in urban cycle, but you pay a higher premium up front. Whether the hybrid/EV premium pays off depends on your annual kilometres:
      - If you do high highway kilometres and towing, the 2.4 diesel’s torque and 3500 kg braked towing rating (advertised) make it a better fit.
      - For mostly urban commuting, hybrids/EVs can reduce running fuel costs — but check local energy costs, charging access and higher parts/repair complexity before paying a big premium. GWM hybrid/EV presence is noted on spec lists and Wikipedia: [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWM_Cannon).
  - Simple TCO tactic: run a 5‑year scenario comparing a Cannon 2.4 diesel vs a HiLux diesel and a hybrid Cannon (if available). Include projected fuel (use your daily kms * fuel consumption), servicing (annual dealer vs independent), insurance and a conservative depreciation curve (GWM slightly steeper). If your annual kms are >20,000 km and you tow frequently, diesel wins. If you’re <12,000 km and urban, a hybrid can make financial sense if the purchase premium is modest.

- Concrete price guidance (based on NSW snapshot + market context)
  - Entry market price (NSW): AUD $38,990 — ceiling: AUD $51,990 — median: AUD $43,990 (2025–2026 examples; near‑new odometers). Use this as your negotiation frame. New vs used comparators on Carsales show used 2024 Cannons around AU$29,990–$39,994 historically, so recent model re‑naming and expansion may shift values: [carsales GWM Cannon listings](https://www.carsales.com.au/cars/gwm/cannon/).

- Final buying recommendation (short, sharp & practical)
  - If you want bang for buck and are happy to accept a younger challenger brand with a smaller dealer network, a used Cannon is a very tempting buy — especially the mid‑range Lux/Ultra trims from 2025 that give the best mix of equipment and value. The median NSW price (~AUD $43,990) looks like a fair market starting point for demos.  
  - What to avoid or insist on:
    - Avoid units with unresolved recalls — require documentation showing recall work completed (see official recall pages: [REC‑006410](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006410) and [REC‑006123](https://www.vehiclerecalls.gov.au/recalls/rec-006123)).  
    - If you tow frequently, favour the 2.4‑litre diesel (135 kW / 480 Nm) and confirm transmission updates and service history. GWM’s 2.4 diesel + 9‑speed pairing is shown by GWM: [GWM AU model page](https://www.gwmanz.com/au/models/ute/cannon/).  
    - Get a pre‑purchase inspection that includes DPF health scan, transmission fluid condition and an ADAS calibration check. Ask the dealer to demonstrate adaptive cruise and camera systems on a long road test.
  - Optimal sweet spot: 2025 Lux or Ultra (2.4 diesel, 4x4) — these give the best balance of equipment, towing capability and likely residual value without the top‑end XSR lifestyle premium. Confirm software updates and recall rectifications are done before purchase.
