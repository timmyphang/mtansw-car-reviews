#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Model: Mitsubishi Triton — current (new‑generation) shown from the 2024/2025 launch cycle; historically also sold as the Mitsubishi L200. See the full model history on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Triton).
- Internal/marketing codes: the latest common diesel is listed under Mitsubishi’s modern 4N-series engines (**4N16** / **4N15** family notes appear in regional spec sheets). Older, common engines in Australia include the long‑running **4D56** 2.5 DI‑D on earlier generations (MN/MQ/MR eras).
- Product hierarchy: Triton sits as Mitsubishi’s mid‑size one‑ton ute — below full‑size pickups (RAM in some markets via partnership) and above light‑duty small‑ute/compact offerings. In the Triton line-up, trims in Australia for the new generation run GLX → GLX+ → GLS → GSR (top spec) per local reviews and launch lists ([4x4 Australia](https://www.4x4australia.com.au/reviews/mitsubishi-triton-review), [CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2025-mitsubishi-triton-review)).
- Shared platform architecture: the newest Triton uses a newly developed ladder frame and chassis architecture (Mitsubishi press releases). As a globally sold platform it’s the L200 under another badge in other markets; Nissan’s next Navara was reported to ride on Triton underpinnings for 2026 models, underlining shared parts and serviceability across marques ([Mitsubishi press release](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com/en/newsroom/newsrelease/2023/20230710_1.html), [Carscoops](https://www.carscoops.com/2025/11/new-nissan-navara-is-here-to-fight-the-ranger-and-the-hilux-with-a-mitsubishi-secret/)).
- Engine / transmission / drive options (market‑specific summary for Australia and close regional specs):
  - Diesel: 2.4‑litre MIVEC turbo‑diesel family (**4N16** / variants such as single‑turbo mid/low power and bi‑turbo high‑power). Regional spec sheets list power outputs roughly 150–204 PS and torque 330–470 Nm depending on tune ([PH PDF spec](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com.ph/content/dam/mitsubishi-motors-ph/images/brochures/24MY%20Triton%20Official%20Specifications%20List_External%20240201.pdf), [HeadlightMag](https://www.headlightmag.com/2023-07-26-technical-data-mitsubishi-triton-july-2023/)).
  - Transmission: 6‑speed manual or 6‑speed torque‑converter automatic (new models keep a 6‑speed; earlier generations also used manuals and automatics).
  - Drive: 2WD (RWD) and 4WD variants with Mitsubishi’s Super Select 4WD‑II or Easy‑Select 4WD systems depending on grade ([spec sheet](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/TRITON-Spec-Sheet-1.pdf)).
- Direct marketplace rivals in Australia: Toyota HiLux, Ford Ranger, Isuzu D‑Max, Mazda BT‑50, Volkswagen Amarok (carsguide comparisons and local utes roundups) — Triton aims for the value/capability sweet spot but competes hard on feature content and pricing ([CarsGuide vs rivals](https://www.carsguide.com.au/mitsubishi/triton/vs), [Motor Scout buyers guide](https://www.motorscout.com.au/car-advice/best-aussie-ute-ford-ranger-vs-toyota-hilux-vs-isuzu-d-max-vs-mazda-bt-50-vs-mitsubishi-triton)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Mitsubishi Triton?
Below is the practical grab‑sheet: what Triton owners, tradies and dealers in Australia actually grumble about — and what you should check on a pre‑purchase inspection.

Exterior:
- Panel fit & paint:
  - Generally acceptable factory fit, but owner threads call out clear‑coat delamination and paint flaking in certain MQ/MR examples — common locations include wheel‑arch flares and the area above the windscreen. Owners report dealer goodwill fixes sometimes but warranty denials for what manufacturers call stone‑chip or environmental damage ([newtriton forum paint thread](https://newtriton.net/phpbb/viewtopic.php?p=484023), [mitsubishiforum post](https://mitsubishiforum.com/forum/other-mitsubishi-models-54/mitsubishi-triton-mq-paint-failure-above-windshield-57362/)).
  - Rust: coastal exposure and stone‑chip damage accelerate corrosion — check chassis rails and under‑tray seams. Australian rust‑repair guidance is available for Triton owners ([AutoGuru rust page](https://www.autoguru.com.au/repairs/rust-repair/mitsubishi/triton)).
- Trim vulnerabilities:
  - Gloss black plastics and wheel‑arch mouldings can show scratching and clear‑coat failure. Plastic flares on earlier models are known to peel.
- ADAS & safety‑system quirks:
  - Driver‑attention monitoring and some driver‑assist features on new‑generation Tritons were criticised for being over‑sensitive and generating false warnings. Mitsubishi has rolled out a software update to reduce sensitivity; check service history and whether the vehicle received the ADAS firmware update ([CarExpert ADAS update story](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/mitsubishi-updates-troubled-safety-tech-for-outlander-and-triton-more-fixes-coming), [Carexpert follow-up](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/the-mitsubishi-tritons-most-annoying-feature-is-being-fixed)).
  - Post‑repair ADAS calibrations matter — I‑CAR guidance notes many ADAS faults persist until cameras/radars are correctly aligned/calibrated after body repairs; insist on evidence of calibration if panels were replaced ([I‑CAR Mitsubishi ADAS guidance](https://rts.i-car.com/oem-information/mitsubishi/mitsubishi-adas.html)).

Interior:
- Cabin durability under heavy use:
  - Triton cabins are generally practical but cloth/leather wear on seats and armrests can show early on high‑use vehicles (rideshare/taxi/tradie fitouts). Look for torn seams, depressed driver bolster, and floor mat wear.
  - Entry/egress: door sills and check angles are typical for a mid‑size dual‑cab; check rubber seals and door check bushes on high‑km tradie trucks for wear.
- Electronics and infotainment:
  - Pre‑2022 models reported touch‑screen and head‑unit flakiness; multiple owner troubleshooting posts and guides discuss resets and hardware faults. A refresh in 2022 reduced these complaints for later cars, but still check for dead pixels, microphone faults and Bluetooth pairing dropouts ([truckofmine infotainment article](https://www.truckofmine.com/mitsubishi-triton-touch-screen-not-working-causes-fixes/)).
  - 12V battery drain: multiple community reports indicate parasitic draws can appear after-market accessories or poorly terminated wiring (common in tradie-fit vehicles) — verify battery health and check for aftermarket alarm/remote starters.
  - Screen sizes and head‑unit variants vary across trims and years — confirm the model you’re inspecting matches the trim sheet for the equipment it should have (dealership spec pages and reviews list equipment per grade; see [4x4 Australia](https://www.4x4australia.com.au/reviews/mitsubishi-triton-review)).
- Practical niggles:
  - Wireless charging pads (where fitted) can be hit‑and‑miss due to alignment and phone case issues — not unique to Triton but worth testing on the demo phone.

Mechanically:
- Brand engineering reputation:
  - Mitsubishi diesels are workhorse units but the Triton has had a mixed bag of owner complaints around emissions hardware, turbo and injection systems — especially on older 2.5 DI‑D engines. Newer 2.4 MIVEC diesels are the company’s upgrade path.
- Security & theft vulnerabilities:
  - Modern Tritons with keyless systems are exposed to the same headlight/CAN‑injection theft methods reported across many makes. Owners should be pragmatic: fit an OBD‑port lock and consider an aftermarket immobiliser or steering‑wheel lock as low‑cost, effective deterrents ([RACV OBD lock advice](https://www.racv.com.au/royalauto/transport/car-safety-security/prevent-push-start-car-theft-obd-lock.html), CAN injection coverage links).
- Aftermarket anti‑theft measures (recommended):
  - Physical: steering or wheel‑to‑handbrake locks, locking wheel nuts, secure tray locks.
  - Digital/hardening: OBD port lock, hidden kill‑switch, an alarm with tilt sensor and remote engine immobiliser. Consider VIN etching and secure parking for high‑risk areas.

Engines:
- Italicised subsections for each major engine code/variant you’ll see on the used market.

  - *Bold engine code: **4D56** (2.5 DI‑D older generation)*
    - Common failure points:
      - Timing‑chain wear and tensioner issues (reported on MN era examples) — listen for rattle on cold start.
      - EGR and DPF soot build‑up, leading to loss of power and frequent regeneration events.
      - Turbocharger actuator and vane issues causing poor boost response.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Oil and filter changes at 10,000 km intervals are recommended if the vehicle does lots of short trips; inspect engine for chain rattle at cold start.
      - Regular intake/EGR cleaning if you do low‑speed urban driving, and monitor DPF regen behaviour.
    - Sources: TorqueBot, EngineFinder, Unsealed4x4 ([TorqueBot common problems](https://torquebot.io/cars/mitsubishi-triton/common-problems), [EngineFinder 2.5 DI‑D writeup](https://enginefinder.co.za/blog/problems/5-common-mitsubishi-triton-2-5-diesel-problems/), [Unsealed4x4 MQ common problems](https://unsealed4x4.com.au/mitsubishi-triton-mq-common-problems-and-solutions/)).

  - *Bold engine code: **4N16** / **4N15** family (2.4‑litre MIVEC diesel, current generation)*
    - Common failure points:
      - Injector‑seat issues and injector contamination causing misfire, fuel dilution and DPF over‑regens — aftermarket writeups show injector seating concerns in the 4N family under heavy DPF stress ([TL Ranger Diesel injector article](https://tlrangerdiesel.com/blogs/news/mitsubishi-triton-4n15-pajero-sport-outlander-4n14-common-injector-issues)).
      - EGR/intake carbon build‑up in some tunes, leading to rough idle and loss of power if not maintained; however these engines are designed to be cleaner than the old 2.5 DI‑D.
    - Preventative maintenance:
      - Fuel and oil health checks at 10,000 km if you run diesel in mixed conditions; consider fuel‑system additives only on advice of a diesel tech.
      - Inspect injector leakdown and test high‑pressure pump behaviour at major service intervals; perform intake/EGR clean every 80–120k km if city‑dominated driving.
    - Sources: PH spec sheet, HeadlightMag, TL Ranger Diesel, TorqueBot ([PH spec](https://www.mitsubishi-motors.com.ph/content/dam/mitsubishi-motors-ph/images/brochures/24MY%20Triton%20Official%20Specifications%20List_External%20240201.pdf), [HeadlightMag technical summary](https://www.headlightmag.com/2023-07-26-technical-data-mitsubishi-triton-july-2023/), [TL Ranger Diesel on injectors](https://tlrangerdiesel.com/blogs/news/mitsubishi-triton-4n15-pajero-sport-outlander-4n14-common-injector-issues)).

  - *Other petrol/diesel variants historically (note): **4N14/4N15** etc.*
    - Older petrols and small diesels had conventional failure modes — the broad takeaway is inspect the service history, turbo health and whether DPF‑related surgeries were performed.

Transmission:
- Torque‑converter 6‑speed automatic (common in modern Tritons):
  - Known behaviours:
    - Reports of harsh or delayed up/down shifts and torque‑converter lock‑up clutch slipping on some models — symptom set includes shuddering under partial load, transmission/engine light combinations and limp modes. Community threads and diagnostic articles point to torque converter electronics, valve body or controller faults as common culprits ([transmissiontroublehub summary](https://www.transmissiontroublehub.com/mitsubishi-triton-transmission-problems-facts-and-insights/), MyCarly community threads).
  - Checks on PPI:
    - Insist on a full transmission fluid condition check (colour, smell, metal particulates). Watch for jerky or delayed shifts on the road test across the full rev range and under tow.
    - Verify any transmission software updates or TSBs applied (dealers can confirm).
- Manuals:
  - Fewer widespread mechanical failures reported, but clutch wear on tradie trucks is predictable — check for slipping and pedal feel.
- DCT/CVT: Not a mainstream fitment in Aussie Triton dual‑cab market; if present in specific markets, research per variant.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Mitsubishi Triton?
- NSW dealer snapshot (live data provided): Active listings 67; Price range AUD $0 – $71,990; Median price AUD $34,990; Year range 2008 – 2026; Median odometer 62,820 km; Transmission mix Automatic: 53, Manual: 11. Use this as your market baseline when hunting in NSW (data from the MTA NSW scraper supplied in your brief).
- Current used marketplace pricing:
  - Realistic entry price: older MQ/MN era Tritons from the late 2000s/early 2010s can be cheap (watch the $0 listing — check for salvage/title). Expect solid, low‑spec dual‑cabs and tradie runouts to sit in the low‑teens to mid‑$20k. Late‑model MQ/MR and early new‑gen 2018–2022 examples typically nod at $25k–$45k depending on kms and spec. Brand new or demo 2024–2026 models climb into the $40k–$72k territory — Carsales research shows new Triton SRP ranges across trims and years ([carsales Triton research](https://www.carsales.com.au/research/mitsubishi/triton/2024/)).
  - NSW median of AUD $34,990 is a good North‑Star: expect better‑kept mid‑2015+ dual‑cabs and low‑to‑mid spec late‑models near that number.
- Depreciation metrics:
  - Triton’s three‑year retention sits broadly around 60–65% of original value in many analyses — slightly behind the top‑value king (Hilux) but competitive with Ranger and D‑Max when properly specified. Cars24 and CarsGuide data cite Triton retention around the low‑60% band for 3‑year windows ([Cars24 used ute values](https://www.cars24.com.au/car-guide/10-used-utes-australia-that-still-deliver-strong-trade-in-value/), [CarsGuide resale note](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/resale-kings-the-surprising-utes-that-hold-their-value-best-exclusive-report-reveals-the-)).
- TCO strategy and hybrid/EV premium math:
  - Fuel economy (real world): owner reports and tested figures show the Triton diesel family averages in the mid‑to‑high single digits to around 8–9 L/100 km for combined driving in many trims — actual depends on load, towing and driving style ([Fuelly owner data](https://www.fuelly.com/car/mitsubishi/triton), [CarsGuide consumption pages](https://www.carsguide.com.au/mitsubishi/triton/fuel-consumption), [Auto‑Data WLTP figures](https://www.auto-data.net/en/mitsubishi/triton-model-2688)).
  - Hybrid / PHEV: Mitsubishi has confirmed a plug‑in hybrid is under development for the new Triton, but no Australia production/spec economy figures yet; expect a significant purchase premium at launch ([Carsales PHEV confirmation](https://www.carsales.com.au/editorial/details/plug-in-hybrid-confirmed-for-new-mitsubishi-triton-143187/)).
  - How to run the break‑even:
    - Use a TCO calculator (KBB/best‑calculators guidance for hybrid break‑even) — key inputs: purchase premium, annual km, fuel price differential, maintenance costs and expected resale. If you do long highway runs and tow a lot, diesel’s high torque and real‑world fuel numbers often make sense; if you do short urban runs and can exploit electric range frequently, a PHEV may make economic sense over several years once residuals stabilise. See methodologies at [KBB hybrid break‑even advice](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/) and the calculator tools ([Best‑Calculators PHEV/EV vs gas](https://best-calculators.com/automotive/ev-vs-gas-vs-hybrid/)).
  - Maintenance and repair budget:
    - Expect routine servicing every 10,000–15,000 km depending on service schedule and driving conditions. Diesel injections, DPF/EGR cleaning and injector servicing are cost events to budget for between 100k–200k km on older engines if abused.
- Buying recommendation — clear, practical verdict:
  - If you want the safest financial sweet spot in NSW today: hunt for a late‑model MQ/MR (2018–2022) or early 2024/2025 when prices settle, with the 2.4 MIVEC diesel (**4N16** variant where possible) and the 6‑speed automatic — these offer the best balance of updated emissions/engine architecture and parts support while avoiding the oldest 2.5 **4D56** timing‑chain risk. Aim for something around the NSW median AUD $34,990 mark if the condition, service history and a clean PPI (pre‑purchase inspection) check out.
  - What to avoid or be cautious of:
    - High‑kilo tradie trucks with unknown service history (especially if they’ve had aftermarket wiring/fittings). Avoid unserviced **4D56** engines if possible unless a thorough PPI checks timing chain and turbo condition.
    - Cars without documented ADAS firmware updates if they’re new‑generation and showing false warnings.
  - Best trim pick:
    - GLS/GSR for features and off‑road hardware if you need capability. GLX and GLX+ give better value if you’re primarily after a work ute and plan to add practical accessories yourself.
  - Final nuts‑and‑bolts:
    - Get the VIN scanned for open recalls (Mitsubishi’s recall lookup), request the ADAS software update receipt, insist on transmission fluid inspection and get a diesel smoke/test for injector health. If the car’s been used for heavy towing, pay a premium for a fully documented service history.

Final practical shopping checklist for NSW buyers
- Insist on: complete service history, VIN recall/firmware check, evidence ADAS software update applied (for new‑gen cars), transmission fluid inspection, injector/high‑pressure fuel checks (diesels), full PPI including underbody and rust inspection.
- Walk away or negotiate hard if: no service history, obvious paint/body patching without calibration evidence, suspect wiring from aftermarket installs, or a car that smells of strong cleaning/air freshener (hiding issues).
- If you want the best mix of modern reliability and value: target a 2018–2022 example with the 2.4 diesel (documented service) around the NSW median price. Pay extra for low km and verified dealer history if you plan to keep for years — but don’t pay top dollar for a Triton unless it has near‑new warranty and dealer‑backed servicing.

Buy clever, get a solid PPI, and don’t be dazzled by the bull bar — check the engine first.
