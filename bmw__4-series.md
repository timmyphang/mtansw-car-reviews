#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

The BMW 4 Series is the premium coupe-focused answer to the segment's desire for style without sacrificing the chassis dynamics BMW promised in their brochures. First launched as the **F32 Coupé**, **F33 Convertible**, and **F36 Gran Coupé** spanning roughly **2013 to present day** (with a significant generational leap to the **G22/G23/G26** architecture in late 2020), the 4 Series was born when BMW ripped the two-door bodywork out of the 3 Series and gave it its own marketing label. Think of it as the 3 Series with better hair and a higher price tag—though the engineering pedigree remains deeply intertwined.

In the brand hierarchy, it sits squarely between the sporty-but-limited **2 Series** and the halo luxury of the **8 Series**. It shares its global platform architecture directly with the **G20 3 Series saloon**, meaning parts availability is bolstered by massive overlap with Australia's workhorse executive sedan. This shared DNA simplifies maintenance; many suspension arms, brake components, and electrical modules are cross-compatible.

For the Australian market, the 4 Series lineup traditionally features a roster of turbocharged petrol engines. Key variants include the efficient **420i** and the punchier **430i**, both powered by BMW's ubiquitous two-litre turbo four-cylinder, alongside the performance-oriented **M440i xDrive** packing the B58 inline-six powerhouse. Transmission duty is handled exclusively by an automatic gearbox, with rear-wheel drive (**RWD**) standard on lower trims and intelligent **xDrive** all-wheel-drive optional or standard on the M440i.

Its explicit marketplace rivals are the **Audi A5** and the **Mercedes-Benz C-Class Coupe**. According to market analysis, the 4 Series mirrors the attributes of the class-leading 3 Series, making it a formidable option for drivers prioritizing driving dynamics over the static luxury offered by the German trio's rivals [source: https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/bmw/4-series/66300/bmw-4-series-vs-rivals].

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used BMW 4 Series?

##### Exterior
* **Panel Alignment & Grilles:** Early F-series models benefit from relatively conservative styling, but the G-generation introduced polarising vertical kidney grilles that divide opinion faster than a football grand final. Ensure panel gaps are consistent; misalignment often points to prior impacts or botched bumper replacements. The large intake openings also act as fly-traps for debris, so check for bent cooling fins behind the grille.
* **Trim Vulnerabilities:** Gloss black exterior accents are a death sentence for clear coat. Scratching during washes is rampant. Chrome elements can pit over time due to Australian UV exposure. Inspect wheel arch liners; plastic clips become brittle with age, leading to rattling cowl panels.
* **Paint & Body Recalls:** Generally solid paint thickness, but check the bonnet edges for stone chips that can lead to corrosion if left unaddressed. Body recalls are infrequent but worth verifying via BMW's online registration checker.
* **ADAS Quirks:** The G-series models boast advanced driver aids like adaptive cruise control and lane keeping assist. Calibration drift can occur after windscreen replacement or sensor impact. Firmware updates may mitigate false positive warnings, so confirm the latest software loads are applied. Basic safety suites like Automatic Emergency Braking are often standalone options, meaning cheaper models may lack radar coverage in the front grille.

##### Interior
* **Cabin Durability:** The cabin quality is rated as up there with the best in class, featuring supportive bolsters and premium materials [source: https://iol.co.za/motoring/latest-launches/2020-12-07-bmw-4-series-driven-behind-that-controversial-grille-lies-a-brilliant-package/]. However, rideshare or taxi history can brutally degrade seat bolstering. Check for torn side airbag covers near the door seams—a sign of excessive ingress/egress friction.
* **Spatial Entry/Exit:** Coupé rooflines crush rear headroom. Test rear seat accessibility; the B-pillar width and door check angles require some contortionism for adults. The Gran Coupé (F36/G26) offers significantly better practicality and daily usability compared to the hardtop coupé.
* **Electrical Behaviours:** Infotainment hardware versions vary wildly. Earlier models may feature an **8.8-inch iDrive display**, while later updates jump to larger screens. Look for the 12V battery drain bug associated with the parking assistant module waking up unexpectedly. Disconnecting the battery overnight is a quick diagnostic trick. Connectivity relies on Apple CarPlay; ensure the USB port engages reliably. Wireless charging pads can overheat with thick phone cases.
* **Seat Materials:** Base models sometimes utilise imitation leather, which cracks earlier than genuine hides. Higher trims feature tan or black leather, but check for dye transfer onto light-coloured cloth inserts.

##### Mechanically & Security
* **Engineering Reputation:** BMW's engineering philosophy centres on precision and weight distribution. These cars reward good habits and punish neglect. Oil leaks are inevitable as seals dry out in older units, but catastrophic failures are rare if maintenance is kept current.
* **Security Vulnerabilities:** While no specific CAN bus injection exploits or keyless bypass vulnerabilities were flagged in the current dataset, modern BMWs with Comfort Access are susceptible to relay attacks where the key signal is amplified to unlock the vehicle. Implement a physical counter-measure: store your key fob in a Faraday pouch or metal box when parked at home. Always verify the immobiliser handshake lights flash correctly upon key insertion to prevent smart-key cloning attempts.

###### *420i / 430i (2.0L Turbo I4)*
* **Performance Profile:** The **420i** typically delivers **135kW and 300Nm of torque** via the rear wheels through an automatic transmission, offering a balanced blend of efficiency and brisk acceleration [source: https://iol.co.za/motoring/latest-launches/2020-12-07-bmw-4-series-driven-behind-that-controversial-grille-lies-a-brilliant-package/]. The **430i** bumps outputs for sharper throttle response.
* **Failure Points:** These units are generally robust, but the **coolant bypass valve** and thermostat housing assembly is a plastic weak point prone to cracking and leaking under pressure. High-pressure fuel pumps can develop internal leaks affecting cold starts. The secondary air pump relay is another known failure item causing error messages.
* **Maintenance Intervals:** BMW Australia typically enforces a **15,000 km or 12-month** service interval cap. Do not push this limit. Sludging risks increase with extended oil change cycles. Use only LL-04 approved synthetic oils to protect the particulate filtration systems.

###### *M440i xDrive (3.0L Inline-6)*
* **Performance Profile:** The **M440i xDrive** combines sleek design with a fun-to-drive personality, utilising BMW's revered twin-scroll turbo inline-six. The xDrive system distributes torque dynamically, offering confidence in wet conditions without dulling the oversteer tendencies enthusiasts crave.
* **Failure Points:** The B58 engine is a legend, but the **charge pipe** connecting the turbo outlet to the intake manifold is made of soft plastic and can detach or crack, causing boost leaks and smoke. Monitor the **oil cooler seal rings**; these harden over time, leading to external weeping. The xDrive coupling unit can develop shudders during low-speed manoeuvres if the differential fluid isn't refreshed regularly.
* **Maintenance Intervals:** Despite the increased performance, adhere strictly to the **15,000 km** schedule. The turbocharged six-cylinder runs hot and fast; degraded oil breaks down faster under spirited driving. Flush the cooling system every five years to combat galvanic corrosion in the aluminium heat exchangers.

##### Transmission
* **Mechanical Behaviour:** The 4 Series relies on the **ZF 8-speed automatic**, widely regarded as one of the finest torque-converter boxes ever built. In healthy examples, shifts are imperceptible and buttery smooth, even with high mileage.
* **Known Failures:** A documented issue in specific model years involves the **torque converter lock-up clutch**. Symptoms include **grinding noises**, **rough shifting**, **transmission engagement delays**, and activation of **emergency mode with significant power loss** [source: ]. This fault often triggers a transmission warning light and may require a remap or replacement.
* **Preventative Action:** Service the transmission fluid and filter every **80,000–100,000 km**. Don't let dealerships talk you into "lifetime fill"; the fluid degrades under thermal stress. Verify the mechatronic unit functions without pressure spikes.

#### 3. Should you buy a used BMW 4 Series?

* **Market Pricing & Supply:** Live NSW dealer data reveals a highly constrained market for the 4 Series right now. There are currently only **2 active listings** with a median odometer reading of just **19,436 km**. The price range spans from a bargain-bin **AUD $2,000** (likely a high-mileage project or overseas import needing TLC) up to a ceiling of **AUD $69,800** for pristine, low-kilometre newer models. The **median price sits at AUD $35,900** for vehicles aged between **2017 and 2022**, predominantly equipped with automatic transmissions, including sports automatic variants [source: MTA NSW Dealer Data Snapshot].
* **Depreciation Metrics:** The 4 Series is financially sensible regarding residual values. It holds its value exceptionally well compared to core segment rivals, mitigating the typical luxury coupe depreciation spike [source: https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/bmw/4-series/66300/bmw-4-series-vs-rivals]. Expect a baseline drop that is competitive; the 4 Series often commands a premium over the 3 Series Coupé equivalent due to its exclusive styling, but this retains better over a three-year ownership cycle.
* **TCO Strategy & Powertrain Math:**
 * **Petrol vs. Hybrid/EV:** The 2021 model year updates saw BMW introduce hybrid options and refine trim structures [source: https://www.thedrive.com/news/33796/2021-bmw-4-series-coupe-axes-manual-transmission-goes-hybrid-adds-that-grille]. However, from a pure TCO perspective, paying a premium for a used plug-in hybrid or EV 4 Series often fails the mathematical test. Depreciation curves for electrified variants are steeper, meaning you're absorbing the biggest hit while saving marginal amounts on fuel or charge costs. Unless you have home charging and cover massive kilometrage, the conventional **420i** or **M440i** delivers better total value retention.
 * **Fuel Reality:** Factor in real-world consumption. The **420i** will sip fuel efficiently around town, but highway cruising in a heavy coupé eats L/100km quickly. Budget for premium fuel (98 RON) is mandatory; running 91 RON on the turbo fours and the B58 six will trigger knock corrections and erode performance.
* **Buying Recommendation:** The optimal financial sweet spot is the **post-2017 facelift F32 model** or the early **G22**. The 2017 update refined the engine mappings, improved iDrive responsiveness, and addressed early build quirks. Avoid the earliest 2013–2014 units unless a full service history proves otherwise.
 * **Pick the M440i xDrive** if you can afford the purchase and service costs; the inline-six offers superior refinement and resale hardness compared to the four-cylinder variants.
 * **Steer clear** of any listing with missing service records, as BMW maintenance logs are the only currency that matters in the secondary market.
