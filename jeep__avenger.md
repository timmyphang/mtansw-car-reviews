#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation / timeframe / codes: The Jeep Avenger (first generation, launched January 2023 — often referenced as Mk1 / Avenger 2023–present) is Jeep’s B‑segment subcompact crossover built for urban and suburban markets. It’s offered in petrol, mild‑/e‑hybrid and full‑electric forms and received a MY25 range refresh and trim rationalisation in late 2024. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeep_Avenger) | Stellantis press release (MY25) [media.stellantis.com](https://www.media.stellantis.com/em-en/jeep/press/meet-the-new-jeep-avenger-my25-now-available-for-order).

- Product hierarchy: The Avenger sits at the bottom of Jeep’s global SUV ladder — below the Compass and Cherokee in size and equipment, pitched as the brand’s small, city‑friendly SUV offering.

- Shared global platform: The Avenger uses Stellantis’ Common Modular Platform family (CMP / eCMP / STLA Small). That platform is shared across many small Stellantis group cars — Citroën, Peugeot and Opel siblings — which helps parts availability for non‑EV components. See the platform notes on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeep_Avenger) and Stellantis materials [media.stellantis.com](https://www.media.stellantis.com/em-en/jeep/jeep-avenger).

- Engines / outputs / drivetrains (market specifics):
  - **Petrol**: 1.2‑litre three‑cylinder (often referenced as **1.2 GSE T3 / PureTech family**) — roughly 100–110 PS in lower tunes; FWD. (6‑speed manual offered in some markets; Australian dealer snapshot shows only automatics in used listings.) [Auto‑Data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/jeep-avenger-1.2-gse-t3-100hp-49064)
  - **e‑Hybrid / MHEV**: 1.2 e‑Hybrid (mild/full hybrid variants) pairing a petrol engine with an integrated electric drive and a 6‑speed e‑DCT; quoted WLTP combined fuel consumption ≈4.9–5.0 L/100 km for the e‑Hybrid. [auto‑data.net](https://www.auto-data.net/en/jeep-avenger-1.2-110hp-e-hybrid-edct-54799)
  - **Plug‑in / 4xe**: Stellantis lists e‑Hybrid 4x2 and 4xe variants in refreshed material — availability depends on market. [media.stellantis.com](https://www.media.stellantis.com/em-en/jeep/press/meet-the-new-jeep-avenger-my25-now-available-for-order)
  - **Full EV (BEV)**: Electric motor 115 kW (≈156 PS) with ~260 Nm; battery pack ~54 kWh nominal giving WLTP range figures often quoted in the ~386–404 km band depending on test and market. AC charging at up to 11 kW onboard; DC rapid up to ~100 kW. [Jeep UK electric page](https://www.jeep.co.uk/jeep-avenger/electric) | [Jeep Germany](https://www.jeep.de/jeep-avenger/elektro)

- Marketplace rivals: Small electric and compact B‑SUVs — e.g., Kia Niro EV, VW ID.3 / ID.2 family in EV form, Peugeot e‑2008 / Opel Mokka‑e for BEV comparisons; petrol/e‑Hybrid rivals include the Hyundai Kona, Honda HR‑V and Renault Captur sized crossovers. (Comparisons found in reviews such as Auto Express and DrivingElectric.) [Auto Express](https://www.autoexpress.co.uk/car-group-tests/362400/jeep-avenger-vs-kia-niro-ev-vs-volkswagen-id3-low-cost-compact-evs-duke-it) | [DrivingElectric](https://www.drivingelectric.com/best-small-electric-suvs).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Jeep Avenger?
This is the no‑fluff fault list: early‑life software and electrics rather than the old‑school metal creaks — but the metal bit has its villains too.

Exterior
- Panel fit and paint: generally tidy for the class (Jeep pitched the Avenger as a rugged little urban SUV), but owners report:
  - Trim vulnerability: gloss black and painted plastics scratch easily; lower cladding scuffs on kerbside parking are common.
  - Paint durability hasn’t been flagged as catastrophic, but shallow stone chips on the bonnet and lower bumper are frequent on busy urban examples.
- Recalls and fire/fuel risks:
  - There are documented recall campaigns affecting Avenger models for potential water ingress leading to electronic arcing and for a loose high‑pressure fuel hose nut that can cause fuel leaks and fire risks. If you’re buying, check VIN recall status and insist any dealer has completed the bulletined repairs. Sources: [car‑recalls.eu — electronic/fire](https://car-recalls.eu/recall/jeep-avenger-2023-2025-electronic-fire/) and [car‑recalls.eu — fuel/fire](https://car-recalls.eu/recall/jeep-avenger-2024-2025-fuel-fire/).
- ADAS quirks:
  - The Avenger’s ADAS has been subject to a recall where certain driver‑assist functions could be disabled together (brake assist + lane‑departure warning), requiring an OTA fix. Euro NCAP noted AEB behaviour as “marginal” in pedestrian/cyclist scenarios — important if you’re doing school‑run or suburban cycling routes. Check [car‑recalls.eu](https://car-recalls.eu/jeep-avenger-2024-legal-requirements-kba/) and the Euro NCAP assessment [euroncap.com](https://www.euroncap.com/assessments/jeep/avenger/1063/).

Interior
- Durability under hard use (rideshare/taxi):
  - Cabin materials are easy‑clean plastics, which is good for grubby boots, but equipment and soft‑touch surfaces at door entry points have reported scuffs after heavy usage.
  - Space is compact: rear legroom and boot are competitive for B‑SUVs, but taller adults will find long trips less comfortable than a small‑segment C‑sized SUV.
- Entry/exit ergonomics:
  - High sill and narrow door openings make getting kids or dog crates in/out straightforward, but older passengers may find the hip‑point a touch higher than hatchbacks — not a dealbreaker but worth sitting in.
- Electrical / infotainment:
  - Typical hardware: standard 10.25‑inch touchscreen on many trims, 7‑inch instrument cluster on entry cars, upgradeable on higher specs (Top Gear summary). [Top Gear](https://www.topgear.com/car-reviews/jeep/avenger/interior)
  - Common complaints: Uconnect touchscreen freezes, flicker and delamination on some units; wireless charging pads have a Stellantis technical bulletin for troubleshooting slow/non‑functioning pads across 2023–2026 models (don’t expect faultless Qi in every used car). See the Stellantis bulletin details reposted on DOT.Report: [Wireless Charging Troubleshooting](https://dot.report/bulletins/11031693) and earlier bulletin [dot.report](https://dot.report/bulletins/11025641).
  - 12‑volt parasitic drain: owners and platform analyses flag parasitic 12V drains on the eCMP platform as an ownership irritant — expect occasional flat 12V batteries if the car sits for long periods. MotorBeast and forum threads detail these as recurring complaints. [MotorBeast](https://motorbeast.org/stellantis/jeep-faults/jeep-avenger-faults/) | Owner forum threads.

Mechanically
- Brand engineering reputation: Stellantis’ small‑car engines and electrified systems are widely used and parts are available, but the Avenger has suffered teething issues typical of newly introduced platform electrifications — especially on control software and integration.
- Security vulnerabilities:
  - CAN‑bus / keyless bypass risks: the industry‑wide “CAN injection” attack (headlight wiring access to inject CAN messages) affects many modern cars. While there’s no specific Avenger CVE in the results, Jeep owners should be aware of the class risk and consider secondary physical or digital immobilisers. Read up on CAN injection mechanics and defences: Dr. Ken Tindell’s writeup / industry coverage. [Dr. Ken Tindell CAN Injection](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/) | [Autoblog summary](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update).
- Aftermarket mitigations:
  - Fit a proven CAN‑bus aware immobiliser (IGLA/Ghost/IGLA alternatives mentioned on Jeep forums) or install a visible steering‑wheel lock for low‑effort deterrence. For higher security, ask your mechanic about installing a module that interrupts the crank or fuel‑pump relay under a PIN/remote control.

Engines
- The research hits the same few engine/drive variants repeatedly. Each engine below has quoted fault patterns — I’ve italicised every variant and called out the common failure points and recommended maintenance cadence.

  - *1.2 GSE T3 / PureTech (100–110 PS petrol)*  
    - Known failure points:
      - **Wet‑belt timing arrangement vulnerability** — oil contamination or degradation can chemically attack the wet belt, risking premature failure and catastrophic engine damage. Industry analyses and owner reports flag timing belt/wet‑belt problems on the PureTech family. [PureTechLegal](https://puretechlegal.com/en/issues-with-stellantis-puretech-engines-failures-and-risks/) | [MotorBeast](https://motorbeast.org/stellantis/jeep-faults/jeep-avenger-faults/)  
      - **Valve‑cover and crankcase oil leaks** reported by workshops — watch for oil pooling in the front undertray, burning oil smell or unexplained low oil levels. [autoricambitritella.it](https://autoricambitritella.it/en/blogs/problematiche-e-guasti-delle-vetture/problemi-jeep-avenger-2023-oggi)  
      - **DPF clogging** on urban short‑trip use on dieselised cousins and on petrols forced into low‑speed driving — owners report regeneration issues under city use. (DPF complaints are common across small turbo petrols run in town.) [autoricambitritella.it](https://autoricambitritella.it/en/blogs/problematiche-e-guasti-delle-vetture/problemi-jeep-avenger-2023-oggi)
    - Preventative schedule:
      - Oil changes: err on the side of shorter intervals for PureTech wet‑belt engines — I recommend 10,000 km or 12 months max (prefer 10,000 km if you do lots of short trips or tow). This is more conservative than some 15,000 km manufacturer intervals — and critical given the wet‑belt sensitivity to oil condition. Source connection: PureTech failure analysis [puretechlegal.com](https://puretechlegal.com/en/issues-with-stellantis-puretech-engines-failures-and-risks/).
      - Check valve‑cover seals and oil leaks at every 10k service; insist the seller shows recent oil change invoices and the oil type used.

  - *1.2 e‑Hybrid (110 hp) with e‑DCT*  
    - Known failure points:
      - **Hybrid control modules / battery management quirks** — early owner reports show hybrid module faults and warning lights sometimes appearing at low km. [autodoc.co.uk](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-jeep-avenger)  
      - **e‑DCT transition jerks**: clutch engagement transitions can be jerky on low‑speed moves; some examples report hesitation or judder. MotorBeast and CarChecker cite e‑DCT and hybrid shift irregularities. [MotorBeast](https://motorbeast.org/stellantis/jeep-faults/jeep-avenger-faults/) | [carchecker.pro](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/jeep_avenger_mk1_1.2_e_hybrid.html)  
    - Preventative schedule:
      - Service the hybrid electrical connections and high‑voltage interlocks at 10,000–15,000 km intervals as per dealer; battery health checks at 20,000–30,000 km or earlier if drivability warnings appear. (Manufacturer service frequency for hybrids can vary; err conservative with 10k checks for first two years.)

  - *BEV (54 kWh, 115 kW motor)*  
    - Known failure points:
      - **Charging/climate management**: owner reports include charging faults, HVAC/climate control weirdness and occasional on‑board charger/telemetry bugs. [autoricambitritella.it](https://autoricambitritella.it/en/blogs/problematiche-e-guasti-delle-vetture/jeep-avenger-electric-2025-problemi-difetti-guasti)  
      - **Battery / range anxiety**: the pack is nominally ~54 kWh with WLTP range claims in the ~386–404 km band (real‑world urban AUS will be lower). Rapid charging at up to 100 kW is supported per Jeep pages — handy for interstate hops if you find a 100 kW DC charger. [Jeep UK](https://www.jeep.co.uk/jeep-avenger/electric) | [Jeep Germany](https://www.jeep.de/jeep-avenger/elektro)  
    - Preventative schedule:
      - Follow owner manual for battery conditioning: avoid leaving the car at 100% SOC for extended periods, and prefer AC charging for daily top‑ups; dealer battery health checks at 12–24 months recommended. Check for any outstanding OTA updates for BMS/charging software before purchase.

Transmission
- Behaviour and failure modes:
  - **6‑speed e‑DCT (used on e‑Hybrid)** — reported issues include jerky low‑speed transitions and occasional failure codes early in ownership. These are electronic/clutch control issues rather than classic torque‑converter slippage. [autodoc.co.uk](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-jeep-avenger) | [MotorBeast](https://motorbeast.org/stellantis/jeep-faults/jeep-avenger-faults/)  
  - **6‑speed manual (where fitted in other markets)** — mechanically straightforward but the engine’s torque pulses can make clutch life a little shorter in heavy city driving.
  - **BEV single‑speed gearbox** — fewer mechanical failure modes but susceptible to electronic control and inverter issues; owners report occasional e‑drive warnings and charging/regen quirks. [carchecker.pro](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/jeep_avenger_mk1_electric.html)
- Buyer checklist for transmission:
  - Test low‑speed crawl, parking lot starts, hill launches — watch for shudder, delayed engagement or hard shifts.
  - For e‑DCT cars, ask for a full service history and dealer software update records; many driveability gremlins have been addressed in software revisions elsewhere in the Stellantis range.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Jeep Avenger?
Short answer up front: If you want a compact, well‑equipped city EV with good urban range and don’t want to chase the cheapest possible price, the BEV Avenger is worth a close look — but buy with warranty and recall proof. If you want a cheap petrol runabout, pick the petrol/e‑Hybrid only if the service history is immaculate and you accept potential early‑life software niggles.

However, new‑model teething issues can blunt buyer confidence and therefore resale values if reliability reports grow. See EV Depreciation overview: [evdepreciation.com](https://evdepreciation.com/jeep-avenger-depreciation/).

- Price ceiling & buying band:
  - With retail listings clustered AUD $39k–$47k (NSW snapshot), you’re buying near new prices for demonstrators. That median $44,939 from the NSW scrape is your headline market price in early used market — and these are very low‑km 2024 cars. If you see a 2024 Avenger under $40k with reasonable options, that’s the better deal; above $47k you’re paying near new or marginally used levels and need convincing warranty/feature differentials.

- TCO and whether to pay EV premium
  - Fuel / energy baselines (published WLTP numbers used as starting points):
    - Petrol (1.2 GSE): WLTP ≈5.6 L/100 km. [auto‑data.net](https://www.auto-data.net/en/jeep-avenger-1.2-gse-t3-100hp-49064)  
    - e‑Hybrid: WLTP ≈4.9–5.0 L/100 km (combined) — the hybrid system can reduce fuel in mixed driving. [auto‑data.net](https://www.auto-data.net/en/jeep-avenger-1.2-110hp-e-hybrid-edct-54799)  
    - BEV: ~54 kWh usable and WLTP range ~386–404 km; that suggests approximate energy consumption near 13–14 kWh/100 km WLTP — real‑world Australian urban consumption will vary with climate and AC use. [Jeep Germany / UK](https://www.jeep.de/jeep-avenger/elektro) | [Jeep UK](https://www.jeep.co.uk/jeep-avenger/electric)
  - TCO strategy: compare total ownership across 3–5 years using:
    - Depreciation (largest single cost) — use the current dealer market as baseline. Avenger’s used price plateau suggests depreciation may not punish you badly relative to segment rivals (see EVDepreciation). [evdepreciation.com](https://evdepreciation.com/jeep-avenger-depreciation/)  
    - Energy cost: multiply WLTP fuel/energy by your annual km. For EVs, use local residential electricity and public charging mix. For petrol, use your local pump price. (I won’t invent specific AUD/L or kWh rates here — run the simple calc yourself or use tools like AFDC or Edmunds TCO calculators.) [AFDC calculator](https://afdc.energy.gov/calc/) | [Edmunds TCO](https://www.edmunds.com/tco.html)  
    - Servicing and repairs: EVs typically have lower scheduled maintenance (no oil or DPF servicing) but watch for potentially costly high‑voltage repairs or BMS/charger fixes if the model proves problematic. Petrol/e‑Hybrid require more routine servicing and—critically for the PureTech—more frequent oil checks to protect the wet belt. Sources: Auto‑Data fuel figures and owner‑reported faults above.
  - Quick illustrative example (method only — plug real local numbers for precision):
    - Assume 15,000 km/yr. Petrol at 5.6 L/100 km → 840 L/yr. Multiply by your local $/L to get annual fuel spend. EV at 14 kWh/100 km → 2,100 kWh/yr; multiply by your $/kWh (and public charging premium) to compare.
    - If electricity is cheap and a high proportion of charging is at home, the BEV’s running costs will usually beat petrol after a few years — but only if the price premium and potential repair risk to EV components is acceptable. Use dealer median $44,939 as purchase cost baseline and project depreciation using EVDepreciation curves.

- Practical buying checklist (what to insist on)
  - VIN recall check and proof of dealer completion for any bulletins (Mopar/Jeep recall lookup): [mopar.com recalls](https://www.mopar.com/en-us/my-vehicle/recalls/search.html)
  - Full service history with oil change invoices (PureTech owner risks demand one look at oil type and interval).
  - For e‑Hybrid / BEV: ask for BMS/charging log and any OTA update records; confirm DC fast‑charge history if possible (frequent fast charging can affect long‑term battery degradation).
  - Test drive: low‑speed e‑DCT transitions, AC charging function (for BEV) and ADAS behaviour on narrow roads to reproduce known false‑alarm scenarios.
  - 12V battery health: request a 12V test or recent service invoice; look for signs of parasitic drain if the car has been static for long.
  - Confirm warranty transferability and length — many buyers are better off paying a little more to secure remaining factory warranty.

- Final buying recommendation
  - If you want an urban BEV with good equipment and range, the Avenger BEV is a sensible shortlist candidate — but only buy with a full dealer warranty, a completed recall history and a low‑km proven example. The NSW market shows very low‑km 2024 stock trading at a median about AUD $44,939 — that’s the market reality.
  - If you prefer petrol/e‑Hybrid: lean to the e‑Hybrid if the price delta is small — it brings better urban economy and the smoother auto pairing — but insist on documented software/firmware updates and a tight service history due to known e‑DCT and hybrid‑controller teething issues.
  - Sweet spot: For NSW used stock in early market (2024 cars, <100 km): target examples priced at or a little below the listed median (~AUD $44–45k) with full recall completion and the dealer’s warranty; that’s your best value/peace‑of‑mind balance. If you find a petrol Avenger under AUD $40k with documented oil/service history and recall fixes, that’s the value buy — but remember the PureTech wet‑belt caveat and service frequently.
