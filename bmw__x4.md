#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation & codes: This review covers the BMW X4 G02 (second generation, introduced 2018) — the "coupe" take on BMW’s compact luxo‑SUV. The X4’s overall production run dates back to 2014 and BMW signalled the X4 would continue through to about 2025 in public material ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_X4)).
- Where it sits in the lineup: The X4 sits above the smaller X1/X2 family in price and sportiness but below BMW’s larger X5/X6 range. Within BMW’s own portfolio it’s essentially the swoopy, less practical sibling of the X3 — same bones, more style, less cargo. See how BMW positions the X4 versus the X3 in comparison pieces ([Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/car-comparisons/bmw-x3-vs-bmw-x4/)).
- Shared platform and sibling models: The G02 X4 shares BMW’s modular rear‑wheel‑bias CLAR architecture used across the X3/X4 pair — which is good news for parts availability and independent‑repairs knowledge. Sources that track model line relationships and timelines include BMW press and model timelines ([BMW press / Autos](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/usa/article/detail/T0176148EN_US/bmw-x4-sports-activity-coupe-and-m4-convertible-make-world-debut-at-2014-new-york-international-auto-show), [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/bmw/x4/)).
- Engines, outputs, drivetrains and transmissions (market‑specific mix relevant to used Australian examples 2018–2022):
  - Petrol 2.0L turbo: **B48** 4‑cyl turbo (badged in some markets as xDrive20i/xDrive30i depending on tune) — four‑cyl with turbo, paired to BMW’s 8‑speed automatic and standard xDrive AWD.
  - Petrol 3.0L inline‑6 (performance): **B58** (and in M / full‑M variants **S58**) — in M40i and full X4 M trims, higher power and more maintenance attention.
  - Diesel 2.0L turbo: **B47** diesel in markets that received diesels (watch for DPF issues if you buy a diesel).
  - Transmission: eight‑speed ZF torque‑converter automatic is standard across the petrol/diesel range.
  - Drive: Standard AWD (xDrive) — no FWD variants for the G02 X4 in the Australian market offering.
  - For modern lineups the headline trims are xDrive30i (2.0 turbo) and M40i (3.0 turbo / sporty) — current lineups and trims summarised by dealers and comparison sites ([Edmunds 2025 trims](https://www.edmunds.com/bmw/x4/2025/trims/), [Cars.com](https://www.cars.com/research/bmw-x4/)).
- Marketplace rivals
  - Direct competitors: Audi Q5 (and Q5 Sportback), Mercedes‑Benz GLC (and GLC Coupe), Volvo XC60, Alfa Romeo Stelvio, Porsche Macan and of course the BMW X3 if you want more cargo and less drama. See the rivals list ([The Car Connection](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/bmw_x4_choices)).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used BMW X4?

Exterior
- Panel fit & trim:
  - Expect fitment/trim niggles on some G01/G02 cars — owners report loose rear‑door moldings and brittle plastic clips which create rattles or gaps over time ([Go‑Parts: Rear Door Molding](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/rear-door-molding-bmw-x3-bmw-x4-2018-2025)). A‑pillar trim sag and fabric gaps are another recurring cosmetic gripe ([Go‑Parts A‑pillar guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/body-a-pillar-trim-panel-bmw-x3-bmw-x3-m-bmw-x4-2018-2024)).
  - Panel gap inconsistency sometimes shows after insurance repairs or factory variations — forum chatter is heavy on this; not a structural failure but irritating for luxury buyers ([Bimmerpost discussion](https://g45.bimmerpost.com/forums/showthread/2174668/panel-gaps-on-my-g20)).
- Paint & clearcoat:
  - There are owner reports of clearcoat or paint peeling on some 2019–2021 cars — not universal, but enough to check carefully on darker colours or where panels have had resprays ([XBIMMERS paint thread](https://x3.xbimmers.com/forums/showthread/1987131/paint-peeling-issues-resolved)).
- Body recalls & safety campaigns:
  - The X4 has had multiple recall campaigns and owner complaints in NHTSA/recall aggregations — search the VIN for outstanding actions is essential before purchase ([NHTSA vehicle detail](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2023/BMW/X4/SUV/AWD), [Cars.com recalls](https://www.cars.com/research/bmw-x4/recalls/), [RecallExplained summary](https://recallexplained.com/bmw/x4/)).
- ADAS / camera & radar:
  - ADAS warnings (“Driving Assistance Restricted”, camera black screens, or front radar faults) are common enough that trained buyers check the ADAS history and whether windscreen glass has been replaced (calibration after glass work is mandatory). Many forum threads and diagnostics guides document camera/radar faults that disable lane‑keep, ACC and AEB ([Go‑Parts: camera failures](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/video-camera-bmw-x3-bmw-x4-bmw-x5-2010-2025), [BangAutoGlass ADAS calibration](https://bangautoglass.com/blog/bmw-x4-m-adas-calibration-needed-now-warning-signs-before-drive)). Later firmware fixes help some symptoms but dealer calibration and module replacement remain common workshop jobs.

Interior
- Trim durability and rattles:
  - Dashboard and interior panel rattles are a recurring complaint — expect to hear them after a few years unless previous owner has had them cured at a BMW dealer or specialist ([Go‑Parts dashboard guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/dashboard-panel-bmw-x3-bmw-x4-2007-2024)).
- Infotainment & electronics:
  - iDrive screens and touch capability can go flaky — reports of black or flickering screens and welcome‑screen glitches are documented in owner threads and replacement guides ([BimmerPost screen thread](https://bmwi.bimmerpost.com/forums/showthread.php?t=2042150), [Go‑Parts infotainment guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-bmw-2-series-bmw-1-series-bmw-2-series-2014-2024)).
  - Wireless charging trays and inductive mats can stop working; troubleshooting guides indicate phone alignment, cases, or software as common causes but expect a dealer visit if hardware is dead ([GearFixes wireless charging](https://gearfixes.com/bmw-wireless-charging-not-working/)).
- Electrical & 12V battery drain:
  - “Electrical” is the most frequent complaint category across X4 model years — intermittent modules, body domain controller resets, and parasitic drains appear in complaint databases ([IsMyCarCooked](https://ismycarcooked.com/vehicles/BMW/X4/), [ProblemsByVin](https://problemsbyvin.com/vehicles/bmw/x4/)). Check service records for battery replacements and note odd audio/rear camera failure history that can hint at underlying body computer issues.
- Practicality & access:
  - The coupe‑SUV roofline makes rear headroom and cargo access tighter — entry/exit is fine for average Aussies but tall passengers or child seats can be fussier than a regular X3.

Mechanically
- BMW engineering reputation & recurring areas:
  - The mechanical engineering is generally competent — BMW used ZF 8‑speed automatics and modern BMW modular engines — but complex electronics and turbocharging introduce repair costs that are higher than mainstream makes. Aggregated guides list dozens of G02 fault points across systems ([What‑Breaks G02 guide](https://what-breaks.com/bmw/x4-g02)).
- Theft & security vulnerabilities:
  - Modern BMWs (including X4) are vulnerable to physical CAN‑bus injection attacks and other keyless bypass methods where thieves access wiring (commonly via headlamp connectors) to inject frames and defeat immobilisers. This is a documented technique and not unique to BMW ([Autoblog summary of CAN injection thefts](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update), technical background [Ken Tindell writeup](https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/)). Owners should assume a professional thief can defeat stock keyless systems unless mitigations are fitted.
- Suggested anti‑theft countermeasures:
  - Physical: wheel clamp, steering‑wheel lock, visible immobiliser decals, and mechanical kill switches on starter or fuel pump (professionally installed).
  - Electronic: Faraday pouches for keys at night, aftermarket CAN intrusion detectors or ECU locks, and subscription to BMW ConnectedDrive/remote tracking where available ([BMW ConnectedDrive info](https://www.bmwusa.com/explore/connecteddrive.html)). These won’t make it impossible to steal, but they raise the bar and reduce opportunistic theft.

Engines
- The G02 X4 was fitted with multiple engines — here’s what to expect for each code (italicised as requested):

  - *B48 (2.0L turbo, 4‑cylinder)*  
    - Common uses: xDrive30i / lower‑power petrol variants.  
    - Known weak points: oil leaks from the oil filter housing, failing turbo actuator electronics in some rare cases, and ignition coil/spark plug wear that causes misfire codes on higher‑mileage cars. Community guides show the B48 is generally improved over the old N20 but still needs attention ([B48 reliability guide](https://bimmer.ai/bmw-engines/b48/), [What‑Breaks X4 G02 list](https://what-breaks.com/bmw/x4-g02)).  
    - Preventative maintenance: oil and filter change every 10,000 km if used in hard city duty; inspect turbo hoses and PCV system around 60–80,000 km. Replace spark plugs per BMW schedule — if unknown, change them at purchase (~100,000 km max). Keep to synthetic oil meeting BMW specs.
  - *B58 (3.0L inline‑6 turbo)*  
    - Common uses: mid‑to‑high spec petrol (non‑M) and the basis of some M performance variants.  
    - Known weak points: the B58 is well‑regarded and robust compared with older BMW sixes, but turbo heat management and coolant system components (hoses, thermostat) can wear — expensive to fix if neglected ([What‑Breaks list](https://what-breaks.com/bmw/x4-g02)).  
    - Preventative maintenance: oil service every 10,000 km, coolant system inspection at 60–100,000 km, and replace drive belts/rollers per condition. Fuel and air filters per 20–40,000 km intervals.
  - *S58 (3.0L twin‑turbo, high‑performance M)*  
    - Common uses: X4 M and M40i‑adjacent high output cars.  
    - Known weak points: higher stress components — turbo and related plumbing, brakes and differential/transfer case cooling are heavier duty. Maintenance and repair costs are significantly higher; expect shorter life on brake hardware and tyres if driven enthusiastically. Service history is critical.  
    - Preventative maintenance: aggressive oil service (premium synthetic) every 7,500–10,000 km if driven hard; close attention to turbo cooling and oil supply. Expect higher running costs.
  - *B47 (2.0L diesel)*  
    - Common uses: diesel 20d/30d variants in markets where diesels were sold.  
    - Known weak points: DPF blockage on cars used for short trips or low speeds, potential EGR and DPF‑related failures if not given regular highway miles ([Autodoc common problems](https://www.autodoc.co.uk/info/problems-with-the-bmw-x4)).  
    - Preventative maintenance: regular long runs to allow DPF regeneration; follow injector and fuel‑system servicing intervals; replace timing‑related parts per schedule.

Transmission
- The X4 uses BMW’s ZF‑based 8‑speed torque‑converter automatic (commonly listed as 8A/ZF 8HP family across BMWs).
- Known failure modes and behaviour:
  - Symptoms reported by owners include jerky shifting, intermittent hesitation between gears, and low‑speed shuddering — often traced back to transfer case/drive module or D‑mechatronics issues rather than a whole‑gearbox failure ([ZF 8HP guide & common problems](https://www.fcpeuro.com/blog/zf-8-speed-transmission-guide-8hp45-specs-common-problems-diagnostics-maintenance), NHTSA TSB on driveline jerk/shudder/transfer case ([TSB PDF](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2024/MC-11006766-0001.pdf))).  
  - There are class action headlines related to transfer case jerk on BMW xDrive cars — meaning transfer case or multi‑plate clutch servicing can be costly if it fails ([CarComplaints report on transfer case lawsuits](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2025/bmw-transfer-case-problems-class-action-lawsuit.shtml)).
- Service & preventative advice:
  - Change transmission fluid and filter on a schedule if you keep the car long‑term (many independent BMW techs recommend 80–120,000 km or sooner under heavy use). Make sure any software updates in ISTA/DIAG and TSB fixes have been applied.

#### 3. Should you buy a used BMW X4?

Pricing & depreciation
- Current entry up to ceiling price: From the NSW snapshot the active listed ceiling is AUD $59,888 and median AUD $47,888 for 2019–2022 examples with ~65k km. National classifieds show a very wide range — US aggregate sites show used prices from very low to high depending on condition but Australian dealer numbers will be higher once import/tax and compliance are factored ([Autotrader US overview](https://www.autotrader.com/cars-for-sale/bmw/x4), [TrueCar / Cars.com samples](https://www.cars.com/shopping/bmw-x4/)).
- Depreciation metrics: iSeeCars and CarEdge estimate the X4 depreciates roughly 50% over five years (iSeeCars ~49.4% 5‑year, CarEdge ~51%) — that positions the X4 in line with sporty luxury compact SUVs but depreciation is slightly better than some rivals in certain trim comparisons ([iSeeCars depreciation](https://iseecars.com/car/bmw-x4/resale-value), [CarEdge depreciation](https://caredge.com/bmw/x4/depreciation)). Expect an above‑average drop in the early 3‑5 year period compared with mainstream SUVs, but better retained value than bargain luxury imports.

Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy
- How to calculate TCO for an Aussie X4: include purchase price, depreciation, fuel (L/100km), servicing/maintenance, insurance, tyres/brakes (higher wear on M variants), and expected repair bills from known failure categories (electrical, ADAS recalibration, transfer case or turbo repairs). Use 10,000–15,000 km/yr assumptions to model local ownership.
- Fuel and ownership numbers:
  - Example: the US EPA combined figure for a recent xDrive30i is ~24 mpg (US) which equates to around 9.8 L/100 km — expect real‑world Aussie suburb/highway mix to be ~10–12 L/100 km depending on spec and driving style ([FuelEconomy.gov xDrive30i data](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=48079), fuel consumption comparatives [FuelConsumptions.com](https://www.fuelconsumptions.com/bmw-x4.html)). M40i and S58‑powered cars will be significantly thirstier.
  - Edmunds’ True Cost to Own (US example) for an X4 xDrive30i shows five‑year ownership around USD $78k (including fuel, depreciation etc.) — translate that mindset to AUD and local costs: the X4 is not cheap to run compared with non‑luxury SUVs ([Edmunds TCO](https://www.edmunds.com/bmw/x4/2025/cost-to-own/)).
- Are the premium trims (M40i, plug‑in/ electrified) worth the extra?
  - If your driving is predominantly urban and you do high annual kms, the standard 2.0 turbo (B48) strikes the best TCO balance. The M40i is sexy and fast but the premium you pay for dealer servicing, brakes, tyres and potentially earlier component wear rarely pays back in fuel savings (there aren’t any) — so buy M40i only for performance desire, not economy.
  - Plug‑in hybrids (if you find an X4 xDrive30e / PHEV in some markets) can be mathematically attractive for low daily commutes with regular charging; BMW WLTP plug‑in numbers can be very low (e.g., weighted WLTP combined 2.0–2.6 L/100 km for plug‑ins in ideal conditions), but real savings vanish if you rarely charge ([BMW WLTP plug‑in note](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/global/article/attachment/T0334392EN/483093)). Always model local electricity vs petrol costs and your drive pattern before paying a premium.
- TCO rule of thumb for NSW buyers: add a 10–15% uplift to advertised price for expected dealer reconditioning/initial service, and budget $1,200–$2,500/yr for servicing & maintenance on a well‑used non‑M X4 (more for an M or poorly maintained car). Factor in tyre and brake replacement cycles if the car has 20"+ wheels.

Final verdict & buying recommendation
- Bottom line: The BMW X4 is a stylish, driver‑focused alternative to the bland crossover herd. It will reward owners who value dynamics and badge cachet more than cargo practicality or tiny running costs. However, you pay for that style in complexity, higher service/repair bills and potential electronics headaches.
- Buy it if:
  - You want a sporty compact luxury SUV with BMW handling and you accept higher running costs.
  - You find a well‑documented, dealer‑serviced 2019–2021 example with full service history and recent timing/coolant/trim repairs done.
- Don’t buy it if:
  - You need cargo practicality, cheap ownership or a car for heavy ride‑share duty (X4's coupe roofline and higher maintenance costs make it a poor ride‑share choice).
- Optimal sweet spot (specific years/trims):
  - Aim for a 2019–2021 xDrive30i (B48 2.0 turbo) with around 40,000–100,000 km that comes with a logged dealer service history and ADAS calibration receipts. This normally offers the best balance of purchase price (in the NSW median AUD $47,888 neighbourhood for the years listed), running costs and parts availability. Avoid full‑M cars unless you are prepared to accept higher consumable and repair bills.
- Inspection checklist at purchase:
  - Full BMW dealer service history and proof of ADAS recalibration after any windscreen or camera service.
  - No outstanding safety recalls (check by VIN via NHTSA/official BMW recall pages — recourse varies by market) ([BMW recall lookup](https://my.bmwusa.com/safety-and-emission-recalls), [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)).
  - Evidence that infotainment and ADAS modules are functioning (test AEB, ACC, rear camera, park assist).
  - Transfer‑case / transmission symptom check at low speeds (test for jerking/shudder).
  - Physical inspection for paint/clearcoat problems and loose external trim.
  - Consider negotiating funds aside for immediate replacement of easy‑to‑neglect wearable items (tyres/brakes/battery) and an independent pre‑purchase by a BMW‑specialist.
