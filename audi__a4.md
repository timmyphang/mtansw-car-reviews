#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

- Generation: B8/B9-era Audi A4 family (you’re looking at cars built in the mid‑2010s; the common used‑market years here are 2014–2018). The B9 (often referenced as Typ 8W) architecture covers the later facelift and models from about 2016/2017 onward; Audi’s literature and spec catalogues trace the A4 line through these updates and the B9/8W technical rollouts [Audi Spec Guide / Audi.com](https://media.audi.com/is/content/audi/nemo/audi/nemo/australia/specification_guides/a4/s4_avant/Audi_A4_S4_Specification_Guide_MY24.pdf), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_A4), [Automobile‑Catalog](https://www.automobile-catalog.com/model/audi/a4_b9.html).

- Where it sits in the Audi lineup:
  - Sits below the A6 in size and price, above the A3.
  - Performance variants (S4 / RS4) sit above the standard A4 offerings.

- Global platform architecture and parts sharing:
  - Built on Volkswagen‑Group longitudinal platforms (MLB / MLB Evo for the B8/B9 era). That means plenty of shared hardware with other Audi/VW Group models, which helps parts availability — think common sensors, brakes, suspension bits across A4, A5/S5, Q5 and some A6 components in parts bins [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_A4), [Automobile‑Catalog](https://www.automobile-catalog.com/model/audi/a4_b9.html).

- Market-specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drive configurations (common in Australia for 2014–2018 used A4s):
  - Petrol: 1.8L / 2.0L turbocharged four‑cylinders (various EA888 family members) — outputs commonly ~125–206 kW depending on tune. Transmissions: 7‑speed S tronic dual‑clutch or 8‑speed torque‑converter automatics across the range. Drive: FWD or quattro AWD depending on trim [Audi / spec catalogs](https://media.audi.com/is/content/audi/nemo/audi/nemo/australia/specification_guides/a4/s4_avant/Audi_A4_S4_Specification_Guide_MY24.pdf), [Automobile‑Catalog](https://www.automobile-catalog.com/model/audi/a4_b9.html).
  - Diesel: 2.0L TDI variants (popular for high‑km drivers), paired with S tronic or torque‑converter automatics and quattro options [Encycarpedia / Automobile‑Catalog](https://www.encycarpedia.com/us/audi/15-a4), [Automobile‑Catalog](https://www.automobile-catalog.com/model/audi/a4_b9.html).
  - Performance V6s (S4 3.0L) exist but are rare in the 2014–2018 private/dealer market and outside the typical price bracket here.

- Marketplace rivals:
  - BMW 3 Series, Mercedes‑Benz C‑Class, Genesis G70, Alfa Romeo Giulia and Volvo S60. If you’re cross‑shopping SUVs, the Q5 or BMW X3/GLC will be in the same consideration set [The Car Connection / Carbuyer](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/audi_a4_choices), [Carbuyer comparison](https://www.carbuyer.co.uk/compact-executive-cars/165459/audi-a4-vs-bmw-3-series-mercedes-c-class-and-alfa-romeo-giulia-which).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Audi A4?

Exterior
- Build and panels:
  - Fit and finish are generally tidy, but watch for panel‑gap inconsistencies and aftermarket crash repairs (rear bumper/fender gaps are an often‑reported post‑repair issue) — inspect door gaps, boot fit and front bumper alignment in daylight [AudiWorld/Audizine threads, Go‑Parts door guide](https://www.audiworld.com/forums/a4-b5-platform-discussion-1/panel-gaps-60460/), https://www.go-parts.com/garage/door-interior-trim-panel-audi-s4-audi-a4-audi-a4-allroad-2017-2024).
- Paint & clearcoat:
  - Instances of clear‑coat peeling are reported across older A4s (owner forums show localized peeling on earlier model years). If the paint looks “melted” or the sheen is patchy, factor in a professional respray or correction cost [Audizine clear coat thread](https://www.audizine.com/threads/clear-coat-peeling.506050/).
- Trim vulnerabilities:
  - Gloss black trim scuffs easily; door sills and lower bumper plastics can show stone‑chip wear. Exterior chrome is hardy, but swapped‑out grills and badges after accidents are common.
- ADAS / sensor problems:
  - Adaptive Cruise Control (ACC) radar and front camera failures are common trouble spots on 2017+ A4s — symptoms include “Adaptive Cruise Control unavailable” and deactivated Lane Assist/AEB. Replacements and calibrations are not cheap and require dealer tools/ODIS calibration [Go‑Parts ACC radar guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/video-camera-audi-a5-audi-q5-audi-s5-2017-2025), [Repairsurge/NHTSA data explorer](https://www.repairsurge.com/nhtsa-data-explorer/Audi/A4%20Quattro).
  - Audi Pre‑Sense false activations have been covered in tech bulletins — random short braking or warning jolts have been investigated by dealers [dot.report TSB on Pre‑Sense](https://dot.report/bulletins/11009380).
  - There have been class‑action style complaints about sudden/unwarranted AEB activation on various VW/Audi platforms — another reason to test every ADAS function on a test drive and check for historical TSBs/recalls [CarComplaints class action summary](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2024/vw-automatic-emergency-braking-class-action-lawsuit-settled.shtml).

Interior
- Durability:
  - Materials are premium for the class, but trims and door cards can rattle, and interior plastic edges wear if the car’s been used for rideshare/taxi duty. Door check angle and sill height are forgiving for most adults — easy in/out for city driving.
- Electronics and infotainment:
  - MMI screens (MIB2 era) can freeze, go black or exhibit stuttering UI behaviour. Common remedies start with an MMI reset but many owners have needed dealer reflashes or module swaps — replacements involve programming and healthy dealer labour bills [TalkAudi troubleshooting, DriversAdvice MMI guide, Go‑Parts MMI failure primer](https://talkaudi.com/question/how-to-troubleshoot-a-malfunctioning-touchscreen-in-an-audi-a4), https://driversadvice.com/audi-mmi-not-working/, https://www.go-parts.com/garage/audio-system-audi-e-tron-audi-q8-audi-a6-2017-2026).
  - Wireless Phone Box chargers showed failure modes and TSBs in later Audis — if the car lists wireless charging as a feature, test it with your phone and look up any relevant bulletins [CarAndProblems TSB summary](https://carandproblems.com/bulletins/audi/e-tron/10238289-audi-phone-box-wireless-charging).
- Battery drain / 12V quirks:
  - Intermittent vampire draws can surface after aftermarket audio installs or degraded modules. Verify that the 12V system holds voltage overnight and check for aftermarket alarm modules, dash cams hardwired without isolation, or known MMI sleep issues. If the car has had long periods of non‑use with telematics/luxury options, expect to replace a 12V battery within a few years.

Mechanically
- Brand engineering reputation:
  - Audi’s engineering is sophisticated, parts are high quality — but complexity means maintenance and repairs are costlier than mainstream brands. Expect European parts and dealer labour premiums when things go wrong.
- Security vulnerabilities:
  - Modern Audis, like many cars, are vulnerable to keyless/CAN‑injection style thefts if attackers gain physical access to wiring (the so‑called “headlight attack” / CAN injection). These attacks can impersonate the key‑fob on the vehicle network and defeat immobilisers — a real world problem you must consider for metro parking situations [The Register CAN attack overview, Ken Tindell CAN injection write‑up](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how-thieves-steal-cars-using-vehicle-can-bus/), https://kentindell.github.io/2023/04/03/can-injection/).
  - Practical owner mitigations: mechanical wheel‑to‑wheel lock, steering wheel lock, remove valuables and keys from keyless‑range at home (Faraday pouch), and consider an aftermarket immobiliser or CAN bus intrusion detector if you park on the street overnight.
- Aftermarket anti‑theft:
  - Physical visible deterrents still work. If you want digital assurances, ask an independent security shop about CAN‑monitor detectors (they watch for bogus messages) — they’re not cheap but are proportionally less expensive than replacing a stolen car.

Engines
(italicised subsections for specific engine codes/variants mentioned in the research)

- *EA888 Gen‑3 / 2.0 TFSI (common codes: **CPMA**, **CAEB**, **CPMB** depending on tune)*
  - What goes wrong:
    - Oil consumption / burning is a documented problem on many 2.0T EA888 variants — symptoms: blue smoke on cold throttle, rising oil consumption between services [AutoZilla / TopCarBox coverage on oil use & EA888 issues](https://autozilla.co.uk/audi/audi-2-0-tfsi-engine-problems-common-issues-explained/), https://topcargobox.com/2025/02/28/audi-b8-a4-common-problems-a/).
    - PCV / oil separator failures (oil separator/oil catch can area) leak oil to the intake, cause whistling, rough idles and increase oil consumption; Go‑Parts has a dedicated diagnosis guide for the oil separator failures on 1.8/2.0 engines [Go‑Parts PCV/oil separator guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/engine-oil-separator-unit-volkswagen-jetta-volkswagen-passat-volkswagen-golf-2014-2022).
    - Timing chain tensioner/guide warning rattle on cold starts can appear on earlier members of this family if tensioners wear — poor tensioner function can lead to catastrophic engine timing issues if ignored [Timing chain problem guides](https://carcose.com/blog/audi-a4-timing-chain-problem-complete-guide).
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Oil changes using VW/Audi‑spec synthetic oil and correct grade at 10,000 km or 12 months (many owners use 10,000 km to be conservative). Replace PCV/oil separator on preventive schedules if the car has a history of consumption or roughness. Have a compression/oil consumption check if consumption is >0.5 L/1000km.
    - Check service history for regular oil top‑ups and whether PCV work or oil‑separator replacement has been done.
  - Citations: [AutoZilla EA888 issues](https://autozilla.co.uk/audi/audi-2-0-tfsi-engine-problems-common-issues-explained/), [Go‑Parts PCV guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/engine-oil-separator-unit-volkswagen-jetta-volkswagen-passat-volkswagen-golf-2014-2022), [Timing chain writeup](https://carcose.com/blog/audi-a4-timing-chain-problem-complete-guide).

- *EA113 / earlier 1.8T and 2.0T (older B8 cars)*
  - What goes wrong:
    - Similar oil consumption and PCV vulnerabilities; earlier versions are more tolerant of high fuel mixes but still need vigilant oil checks.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Same: frequent oil/service intervals, PCV checks.

- *2.0 TDI diesel*
  - What goes wrong:
    - Diesel‑common issues: EGR and DPF clogging on low‑mileage urban use, injector or high pressure fuel pump wear on very high‑km units. Diesel A4s are good value for high‑km Australian drivers if they’ve been motorway‑used and have a DPF regen history.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Regular highway runs to allow DPF regeneration, fuel filter replacement per schedule, and injector servicing if signs of roughness exist.
  - Citations: General specs and variant range coverage [Encycarpedia / Automobile‑Catalog](https://www.encycarpedia.com/us/audi/15-a4), https://www.automobile-catalog.com/model/audi/a4_b9.html.

Transmission
- S tronic dual‑clutch boxes and torque‑converter automatics are common across the model years. Known failure modes:
  - Torque‑converter automatics: symptoms include shuddering, slipping or harsh engagement — classic torque‑converter or clutch pack wear; contamination and overdue fluid changes accelerate failure. Repair bills can be expensive due to labour and parts [700r4transmissionhq torque converter guide, WhoCanFixMyCar summary](https://www.700r4transmissionhq.com/audi-a4-bad-torque-converter-signs/), https://www.whocanfixmycar.com/advice/common-problems-with-the-audi-a4).
  - S tronic / DCT: can show judder, rough low‑speed engagement and solenoid or mechatronic pack failures; repairs often require unit replacement or reconditioning and dealer programming.
  - Multitronic (CVT) variants (older FWD A4s): known to be fragile in certain years — a different gearbox architecture altogether with documented persistent faults on earlier models [Automatika Multitronic guide](https://automatika.tech/audi-a4-cvt-multitronic-gearbox-failure-guide/).
- Preventative action:
  - Ask for transmission fluid change records (many owners defer these; DCTs or torque‑converters benefit from timely fluid and filter service every 60–90k km depending on use).
  - On a test drive, watch for smooth, crisp shifts and no vibration when accelerating at low speeds.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Audi A4?

Market pricing & depreciation
- NSW dealer snapshot (MTA NSW scraper): entry price to ceiling is AUD $14,950 – $25,950; median AUD $19,990; typical model‑years 2014–2018 and median odometer ~57,549 km (live snapshot provided).
- Depreciation metrics:
  - Audi A4s depreciate aggressively compared to mainstream cars. Independent studies show a new A4 can lose roughly 30–36% of its value in three years and near ~49% over five years in broader datasets — that’s in the ballpark of what iSeeCars and CarEdge report for A4s vs segment rivals [iSeeCars depreciation tables](https://www.iseecars.com/car/audi-a4/resale-value), [CarEdge depreciation overview](https://caredge.com/audi/a4/depreciation).
  - In practice: you’re getting a European premium interior at a mid‑market price because the big hit of depreciation has already occurred by model age 4–6 years.

Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy
- How to compute:
  - Start with purchase price + estimated 5‑year running costs = depreciation + fuel + scheduled servicing + tyres + insurance + typical repair buffer (European cars: plan a higher repair reserve).
  - Use realistic fuel numbers: factory WLTP numbers are optimistic. Example: the 40 TFSI official combined WLTP claim ~5.6 L/100km but real‑world owners report closer to 8–10 L/100km depending on driving — Fuelly and Motoreu show that gap [Motoreu 40 TFSI WLTP vs real](https://motoreu.com/audi-a4-40-tfsi-data-203242), https://www.fuelly.com/car/audi/a4.
- Practical example (simple model):
  - If you drive 15,000 km/yr, and your petrol A4 real world is ~9.0 L/100km with petrol at AUD $1.70/L:
    - Annual fuel = 15,000km * 0.09 L/km * $1.70 ≈ AUD $2,295/yr.
  - Add service (AUD $700–1,200/yr averaged), tyres/alignments (AUD $600–1,200/yr depending on mix), insurance (AUD $1,200–2,000/yr) and a repair reserve (AUD $1,000–2,500/yr depending on condition).
  - TCO can quickly outstrip a comparable mainstream car by a few thousand per year, but you get a substantially nicer cabin and AWD/quattro choices.
- Hybrids / mild‑hybrids (MHEV):
  - Audi introduced 48V MHEV systems in the later A4 lifecycle (not common in 2014–2018 used cars). MHEV saves a modest amount in urban stop‑start fuel but doesn’t radically change ownership maths unless you do lots of city driving and the model carries a small premium. Use a hybrid vs petrol calculator (e.g., CalcFuel) to model your own miles and price premium before deciding [CalcFuel hybrid calculator](https://calcfuel.com/calculators/hybrid-vs-gas-calculator).
  - For the 2014–2018 buys in the NSW market, you’re largely comparing petrol 2.0T vs diesel 2.0TDI — diesels win on fuel cost for high annual kms but have higher diesel‑specific maintenance (DPF/EGR) risk if mostly urban.

Buying advice & recommendation
- Who should buy one:
  - You want a compact executive sedan with a premium interior, strong chassis refinement and available quattro AWD for wet Australian roads.
  - You’re prepared for European‑car running costs and will stick to service schedules or get a competent independent Euro specialist for maintenance.
- Who should walk:
  - If you want cheap maintenance and absolute maximum reliability for every dollar, buy a Toyota/Honda or mainstream alternative. If you do lots of short urban trips and buy a diesel A4, expect DPF/EGR headaches.
- Which year/trim is the sweet spot (Australian used market, 2014–2018)?
  - Aim for 2016–2018 builds (later B8 / early B9 facelift years) with full service history and preferably a documented timing chain/PCV/oil‑separator refresh if it’s a 2.0T. Carxplorer and other reliability guides note that later B9‑era cars (2017+) tend to be the least troublesome among the mid‑2010s A4s [CarXplorer reliability guide](https://carxplorer.com/is-audi-a4-reliable-car/).
  - Trim: mid spec with S tronic and quattro is a pragmatic pick — you get the driving refinement without paying S4 money. If you drive long distances every week, prefer the 2.0 TDI with highway history; if you favour city driving and lower peak torque demands, pick 2.0 TFSI but verify oil use history.
- Pre‑purchase checklist (non‑negotiable):
  - Full service history (oil changes at conservative intervals, evidence of PCV/oil‑separator work where applicable).
  - Detailed mechanical inspection including compression/oil consumption test on 2.0T petrols.
  - Transmission health check (no shuddering, smooth shift under load).
  - ADAS systems tested on road (ACC/Pre‑Sense/Camera) and check for any stored TSBs/recalls.
  - VIN/history check and ensure clean title/airbag resets and no structural repair hiding in panel gaps.
  - Consider a dealer inspection for calibration and a recorded ODIS scan if ADAS warnings are present.
- Final verdict:
  - If you accept higher running costs and pick the right car, the Audi A4 is a very compelling used buy in Australia — great interior, composed chassis and quattro traction when you need it. For the NSW pricing snapshot (AUD 14,950–25,950; median $19,990), the sweet spot is an inspected 2016–2018 A4 with 2.0T engine, full service history and preferably a documented PCV/oil‑separator service. Don’t buy a cheap A4 with no history; the saving up front often costs double in repairs within 12 months.
