#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation name, timeframe, internal codes
  - Holden Trailblazer — RG generation (badge‑engineered from the Colorado7), launched 2016 and sold in Australasia through the late 2010s (MY16–MY20 examples commonly listed) — internal shorthand: RG Trailblazer. See the technical catalogue for the RG/Diesel spec listing. [Auto‑Data: RG 2.8 specs](https://www.auto-data.net/en/holden-trailblazer-rg-2.8-200hp-43568).
- Product hierarchy (what sits above/below)
  - In Holden’s local line‑up the RG Trailblazer sits above mid‑sized two‑row SUVs (Compact/Urban like the Trax) and in the large 7‑seat/pickup‑derived SUV bracket it competed with long‑legged rivals such as the Isuzu MU‑X and Ford Everest — it’s a body‑on‑frame, seven‑seat alternative to soft‑road SUVs. Compare summaries and head‑to‑head tests at [Drive](https://www.drive.com.au/reviews/2018-holden-trailblazer-ltz-v-isuzu-mu-x-ls-u-comparison/) and [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/holden/trailblazer).
- Shared platform architecture and sibling models
  - The Trailblazer is effectively the SUV cousin of the Colorado ute (the Colorado/Colorado7 family). That shared hardware means many mechanical parts are interchangeable with the Colorado range — useful for parts availability and independent‑service solutions. Drive and 4x4 Australia note the close relationship with the updated Colorado hardware. [4x4 Australia review](https://www.4x4australia.com.au/reviews/road-tests/2017-holden-trailblazer-ltz-review) | [Carsales specs](https://www.carsales.com.au/research/holden/trailblazer/2017/).
- Market‑specific engines, outputs, transmissions and drivetrains
  - Australia: the primary, widely available powerplant is the **2.8‑litre Duramax turbo‑diesel** (approx. 200 hp / 500 Nm spec in common listings) paired to a 6‑speed automatic and 4x4/AWD with dual‑range transfer options on higher trims. Official technical lists and the MY18 owner manual back this up. [Auto‑Data 2.8L spec](https://www.auto-data.net/en/holden-trailblazer-rg-2.8-200hp-43568) | [MY19 owner manual PDF](https://www.holden.com.au/content/dam/holden/oc/au/en/index/owning/my-connect/trailblazer-entertainment/02-pdfs/my19-trailblazer-owner-manual-1237047709310.pdf).
  - Petrol V6 options (GM High Feature / Alloytec) appear in the wider Colorado/previous Colorado7 family in some markets, but Australian showroom Trailblazers were marketed and most commonly found with the 2.8 diesel — check local listings for rarer petrol variants. [GM High Feature engine background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GM_High_Feature_engine).
- Key rivals in the Australian market
  - Isuzu MU‑X, Mitsubishi Pajero Sport, Ford Everest — those are the direct seven‑seat, ladder‑frame rivals. See Drive and CarsGuide comparisons for head‑to‑head positioning. [Drive comparison vs MU‑X](https://www.drive.com.au/reviews/2018-holden-trailblazer-ltz-v-isuzu-mu-x-ls-u-comparison/) | [CarsGuide vs Everest](https://www.carsguide.com.au/ford/everest/vs/holden-trailblazer).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Holden Trailblazer?

Exterior
- Panel alignment, trim and paint
  - Panel fitment is generally consistent with a locally‑assembled badge‑engineered mid‑size GM product, but owners have reported clear‑coat/paint durability problems across a swathe of GM vehicles. There are documented owner complaints and discussion of peeling/clear‑coat failure in GM vehicles; if the finish looks fragile (patchy bubbling, flaking) treat it as a real problem, not patina. [Cypaint overview of GM paint complaints](https://cypaint.com/article/has-gm-fixed-the-peelin-paint-problems) | [CarComplaints article on class action](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2024/gm-peeling-paint-class-action-recall.shtml).
  - Exterior plastic trim and cladding can suffer scuffs and finish wear; replacement trim for Chevrolet/Trailblazer families is catalogued by parts houses — expect sacrificial cladding and bumper nibs to be cheaper than full repaint jobs. [Go‑Parts trim guide (Chevy Trailblazer)](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-chevrolet-trailblazer-2021-2025).
- Body recalls / safety system mechanicals
  - Always run a VIN check through Holden’s recall lookup to validate if dealer campaigns were completed — recalls (even for trivial items like jack labelling) have been issued historically for Australian Trailblazers, and service campaigns for safety components may exist. [Holden recall lookup](https://www.holden.com.au/recall-print) | [AustraliaCar.Reviews recalls summary](https://www.australiancar.reviews/recalls-faults-holden-trailblazer-2016-20/).
- ADAS (where fitted)
  - The RG Trailblazer in Australian delivery spec had simpler driver‑assist systems compared to modern cars, but forward‑camera/radar‑type systems on later GM small SUVs have shown sensitivity to contamination/calibration; reports of AEB / adaptive cruise quirks and unintended braking exist on later Trailblazer generations. Dealer software updates have helped in many cases, but follow‑up calibrations after windscreen or front‑end work are common. [CarsafetyDB adaptive cruise complaints](https://carsafetydb.com/chevrolet/trailblazer/2025/driver-assist) | [CarProblemZoo adaptive cruise summary](https://www.carproblemzoo.com/chevrolet/trailblazer/adaptive-cruise-control-problems.php).

Interior
- Durability under heavy usage (rideshare, school runs, towing families)
  - If you want a hard‑wearing interior for taxi/rideshare use: the Trailblazer’s cabin plastics and seat coverings are durable but not indestructible. CarsGuide and owner threads list practical wear points — seat seams on cloth trims, scuffed door sills, and sun‑faded dash tops in harsh climates.
  - Entry/exit ergonomics: the ladder‑frame, tall right‑height doors make third‑row access reasonable for adults on short trips; second‑row “slide and tilt” action depends on trim pack and can be stiff on older cars — check door check angles and scuff plates for evidence of forced entry.
- Electrical and infotainment
  - Infotainment stability is a recurring theme: owners report the MyLink system freezing, going blank, or rebooting — dealership software resets or module replacements are common fixes. Specialist repair services advertise MyLink LCD/touchscreen repairs. [GetElectronic MyLink repair page](https://getelectronic.net/products/holden-lcd-touch-screen-mylink) | owner questions on ProductReview demonstrate real‑world annoyance. [ProductReview Q&A](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/holden-trailblazer-rg-ltz/q-and-a).
  - 12V battery drain: owner‑reported overnight drain events exist in the wider GM small‑SUV family; check for aftermarket accessories, retained accessory draws, and accessory module log entries during PDI at a dealer.
  - Screen sizes across trims: typical Trailblazer trims used 7–8‑inch MyLink units; higher trims add nav and surround camera functionality — confirm the exact hardware in the car you inspect.
  - Bluetooth and smartphone connectivity are fine when working, but users report intermittent pairing drops and slow boot times after cold‑start. GM issued bulletins and fixes for infotainment across model years; persistent faults often need a dealer update. [GM Authority on infotainment fixes and dealer bulletin (PIT6362)](https://gmauthority.com/blog/2025/04/gm-is-working-on-the-following-infotainment-system-issues-so-please-be-patient/).
- Practical checks (what to look for)
  - Dead pixels, touchscreen non‑response, audio dropouts, and USB port looseness.
  - Door check wear and seat bolster collapse on high‑kilometre examples.

Mechanically
- Brand engineering reputation
  - The Trailblazer’s mechanical DNA is robust: ladder‑frame chassis, Ford/Isuzu‑class rivals more about durability than refinement. However, the combination of heavy urban/aggressive towing and diesel emissions systems increases service costs and fault likelihood with age. See owner complaints and technical write‑ups for documented engine and emissions issues. [CarsGuide problems page](https://www.carsguide.com.au/holden/trailblazer/problems) | [AustraliaCar.Reviews recalls & faults](https://www.australiancar.reviews/recalls-faults-holden-trailblazer-2016-20/).
- Theft/security vulnerabilities & mitigation
  - Modern thieves use "CAN injection" and headlight‑area wiring access to spoof keyless entry and immobiliser signals. The general technique affects many makes; Trailblazer owners should assume the attack vector exists and adopt physical/digital countermeasures. Read the technical whitepaper and practical guides: [CAN Injection paper (Canis Automotive Labs)](https://www.can-cia.org/fileadmin/cia/documents/publications/cnlm/june_2023/cnlm_23-2_p20_the_can_injection_attack_ken_tindel_canis_automotive_labs.pdf) | [The Register on headlight hacking](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how_thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/).
  - Practical owner measures: OBD port locks, steering wheel clubs, aftermarket immobilisers with interrupt relays, dash‑mounted CAN intrusion detectors, and secure parking. Also ensure any dealer‑fitted alarm or vehicle tracker was retained and works.
- Physical/digital anti‑theft suggestions
  - Fit a mechanical steering lock for visible deterrence.
  - Install an aftermarket immobiliser that isolates starter/ignition circuits on a hidden relay.
  - Use OBD port block or lock; register VIN with any existing OEM recall/tracking service.

Engines
- (Every single engine variant discussed below — italicised headings)

  *2.8L Duramax LWN (turbo diesel) — bold parts and fault points: **2.8L Duramax LWN / turbo / high‑pressure fuel system***
  - What it is: the workhorse 2.8L four‑cylinder Duramax diesel used across the Colorado/Trailblazer family in Australia — robust iron block, turbocharged, common‑rail injection. [2.8L Duramax LWN guide](https://chevytrucks.org/duramax-28l-engine-guide-lwn-i4/).
  - Specific failure points reported:
    - Emissions systems: DPF regeneration issues, EGR valve fouling and associated limp‑home modes. Owner reports and guides list DEF/DPF/EGR as common diesel headaches. [SPETuner common issues summary](https://spetuner.com/blogs/news/2-8-duramax-common-problems-how-to-fix).
    - Turbocharger wear or boost leak symptoms on high‑kilometre units, especially if oil change intervals were ignored.
    - High‑pressure fuel system / injectors: injector or HPFP failures are costly; owner threads note injector‑related codes and rough running. [KBTire list of 2.8 Duramax problems](https://kbtireandautorepair.com/2-8-duramax-problems/).
    - Sensors: crankshaft position / angle sensors and related drivability codes reported in StartMyCar owner logs. [StartMyCar Trailblazer info & reports](https://www.startmycar.com/holden/trailblazer).
  - Preventative maintenance / service intervals:
    - Follow diesel‑specific maintenance: do oil changes and filter swaps at least every 10,000 km (or sooner if towing/heavy use); inspect turbo for shaft play at major service intervals; replace fuel filters per manufacturer schedule (often 20,000–30,000 km for diesel systems but confirm with the owner manual). The official owner manual is the definitive reference; head to the MY18/MY19 manual pages for factory intervals. [MY18 Owners Manual PDF](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/2408013/Holden-Trailblazer-Rg-My18.html).
  - Practical pre‑purchase checks:
    - Request evidence of DPF clean/regeneration history; scan for permanent DTCs around EGR/DPF.
    - Ask for service stamps showing regular oil and fuel filter changes; examine turbo for smoke or whine at cold start.
    - Compression and injector balance test on higher‑kilometre examples.

  *3.6L GM High Feature V6 (Alloytec / HFV6) — bold parts: **HFV6 / Alloytec V6***
  - Relevance: this engine family appears in GM’s model stable and Colorado7 history. Australian Trailblazer listings are predominantly diesel, but you may encounter petrol V6 variants in mixed market stock or import examples. [GM High Feature engine background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GM_High_Feature_engine).
  - Known issues (where fitted elsewhere in GM range):
    - Timing chain stretch and associated rattle around 140k+ km on some HFV6 variants, oil consumption and PCV system sludge risk. Just because it’s not typical in most AU Trailblazers doesn’t mean you won’t see it — if you encounter a V6 petrol Trailblazer, inspect the timing chain area and oil usage history.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - 10,000 km oil service intervals in normal Australian dealer regimes for petrol HFV6s; check for historical oil‑change receipts and PCV servicing.

Transmission
- What’s fitted and behaviour
  - The Trailblazer uses a 6‑speed automatic in the diesel variants (the exact 6L family depends on gearbox code and market). Owner reports show rough shifting, hesitation, and climb‑out over‑revs in some units. [TransmissionTroubleHub summary](https://www.transmissiontroublehub.com/holden-trailblazer-transmission-problems-key-facts-insights/).
- Known failure modes
  - Symptoms reported in Australian owner groups and national class actions point to clutch pack wear/solenoid faults and torque converter issues in the GM 6‑speed family. Multiple owners have sought transmission rebuilds or replacements; specialist rebuilders advertise Trailblazer/Colorado 6‑speed rebuild packages. [Extreme PLUS rebuild service page](https://automatictransmission.com.au/shop/extreme-plus-rebuild-of-holden-rg-2-8l-diesel-colorado-colorado-7-and-trailblazer-6-speed-auto-includes-torque-converter/).
  - There has been litigation activity around GM 6L series transmissions — a class action was publicised in Australia affecting a range of Holden models using GM 6‑speed units. [Maurice Blackburn class action overview](https://www.mauriceblackburn.com.au/class-actions/join-a-class-action/holden-transmission-class-action/).
- Practical pre‑purchase transmission checks
  - Test for clunking on cold take‑off, delayed engagement, slip at high revs, and harsh downshifts. Get a transmission fluid condition check (colour, smell) and insist on a scan for historic tranny DTCs. If the owner handbook shows 'transmission fluid change' records, that’s a confidence booster.

  - Nationally there are many used Trailblazer listings across Carsales, Autotrader and Gumtree with prices from low‑teens for high‑kilometre private sales up to mid‑$30k for low‑kilometre dealer stock — see aggregated listings on [Carsales used Trailblazer search](https://www.carsales.com.au/cars/used/holden/trailblazer/) and [Autotrader AU Trailblazer](https://www.autotrader.com.au/for-sale/holden/trailblazer). Prices vary strongly by year, kilometres, service history and region.
- Depreciation metrics
  - Expect sharper than segment‑average depreciation for a big‑volume GM product where brand rationalisation (Holden exit) and model age matter. U.S.‑based depreciation models estimate notable losses for the Trailblazer nameplate, but Australian market listings show older examples are available cheaply — Carsales listings give real‑world context for bargains and steep declines across 3–5 years. Use the Carsales used search to eyeball comparable cars by year/odometer. [Carsales Trailblazer used listings](https://www.carsales.com.au/cars/holden/trailblazer/).
  - Practical rule of thumb: buy the right year (see recommendation below) and accept that resale value will lag segment mainstays (Isuzu, Mitsubishi) because of network and perception over time.
- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy
  - Fuel & running costs: official combined diesel consumption figures are commonly quoted in the mid‑ to high‑single digits to low‑teens L/100km depending on load and towing — CarsGuide lists example combined figures around 8.6 L/100 km for certain trims. [CarsGuide fuel/ownership data](https://www.carsguide.com.au/holden/trailblazer).
  - Maintenance & repairs: diesel emissions hardware (DPF, EGR, SCR/DEF where fitted) increases service cost and risk of expensive repairs if the vehicle has been used for short‑trip urban work or heavy towing without proper service regimen. Transmission reparability (6‑speed rebuilds) is available domestically but costly if you’re the one who needs it; factor in a contingency fund of several thousand dollars by year six‑to‑eight of ownership if buying higher‑kilometre stock.
  - Warranty and dealer network: Holden’s dealer brand wound back post‑2020 complicates long‑term dealer support; independent diesel specialists and Colorado parts channels are the likely service pathway. Check for completed recall/TSB repairs on the VIN with Holden’s recall page. [Holden recall lookup](https://www.holden.com.au/recall-print).
  - Hybrid / EV premium analysis for this car: The RG Trailblazer is diesel; there wasn’t a factory hybrid/EV variant in the Australian RG lineup. That makes the “pay extra for hybrid” question moot for this generation. If you are comparing to a modern hybrid 7‑seat competitor, do the math: hybrids save fuel in urban driving but may cost more up‑front and be heavier to repair outside major dealer networks. Use tools like the AFDC/KBB cost calculators for cross‑make comparisons. [KBB cost to own (US example)](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/hybrid-vs-gas-break-even-fuel-savings/) | [AFDC vehicle cost calculator](https://afdc.energy.gov/calc/).
- Definitive buying recommendation
  - Is the Trailblazer a buy? Yes — but with caveats. The RG Trailblazer gives you a capable, seven‑seat ladder‑frame diesel SUV with genuine towing and off‑road chops and parts interchange with Colorado/ute ranges. If you find one with documented service history, regular diesel maintenance (oil, filters, DPF care), clean transmission history and a resolved infotainment/recall history, it’s a practical buy.
  - Avoid buying:
    - Any car with unknown DPF/DEF history, a flaky MyLink that reboots mid‑trip, an unserviced 2.8L with black smoke or oil leaks, or a transmission with vague history of rough shifts.
    - Examples that show paint delamination beyond small chips — GM paint issues have been serious in some cases; if the paint looks like it’s falling off, negotiate hard or walk away. [Cypaint/CarComplaints on GM paint](https://cypaint.com/article/has-gm-fixed-the-peelin-paint-problems) | [CarComplaints class action](https://www.carcomplaints.com/news/2024/gm-peeling-paint-class-action-recall.shtml).
  - Best financial sweet spot (specific year/trim)
    - For NSW market right now: that single $32,999, 2018 MY example with ~80,000 km is in the mid‑range for dealer offerings. If the car has service history and no DTCs for emissions or transmission, it’s reasonable value — but if you can find a well‑serviced 2017–2018 LTZ/LT with 60–120k km for mid‑to‑low‑$20s (private) or high‑$20s (dealer) that is likely the sweet spot: you get robust equipment and avoid paying dealer premiums on low‑volume “rare” trims.
    - Practically: aim for a 2017–2018 diesel with full service history, <120,000 km, no open recalls, and a clean transmission fault log. That usually delivers the best balance of price, remaining life on the drivetrain and parts availability.

Final tl;dr — real‑world verdict
- The Holden Trailblazer RG is an honest, hard‑wearing seven‑seat diesel SUV with great torque, genuine towing ability and Colorado parts commonality. Buy only with paperwork: DPF/DEF history, transmission service evidence, and a cleared infotainment/recall history. If the dealer‑listed NSW car at AUD $32,999 (2018/80k) has service stamps and no transmission/engine DTCs, it’s reasonable value — but don’t pay a premium for cosmetics or a tidy interior if the engine, DPF or gearbox look like ticking time bombs. And for God’s sake, fit an aftermarket immobiliser and check VIN recall status before you drive it home.
