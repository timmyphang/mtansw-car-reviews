#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Model: Maserati Levante (Tipo M161) — first launched 2016, produced through to the 2024 model cycle in various updates. The Levante used across the 2017–2024 period is the generation you’ll find on the Australian used market today. [Maserati official overview](https://www.maserati.com/us/en/models/levante) and model history summary: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_Levante).
- Hierarchy: Levante sits above the compact Maserati Grecale (smaller, newer) and below the very exclusive Maserati Purosangue (when it appears). Within Maserati’s range the Levante is the volume luxury-SUV offering — pitched against mid‑to‑large luxury performance SUVs.
- Platform / parts commonality: The Levante rides on Maserati’s rear‑wheel‑based M156/M157 architecture, a shared engineering lineage with the Quattroporte and Ghibli (double‑wishbone front, multilink rear) which helps parts availability for suspension and some drivetrain bits. See the M156 platform notes: [Wikipedia — M156 platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_M156_platform).
- Engines, transmissions and drive layouts sold in Australia (what you’ll actually see):
  - 2.0‑litre mild‑hybrid/turbo petrol — around 330 PS in later small‑engine Levantes (8‑speed ZF automatic, AWD) — marketed as a more economical Levante GT/Modena/MHEV in later years. [Auto‑Data 2.0 GDI 330 hp](https://www.auto-data.net/en/maserati-levante-2.0-gdi-330hp-mild-hybrid-awd-automatic-43286) and Maserati hybrid pages [Maserati GT Hybrid](https://www.maserati.com/global/en/models/levante/levante-gt-hybrid).
  - 3.0‑litre twin‑turbo V6 (F160 family) — widespread across early and mid runnings; typical Levante S/GTS spec (8‑speed ZF, AWD). [Wikipedia powertrain list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_Levante).
  - 3.8‑litre twin‑turbo V8 (F154 family) — GTS / Trofeo performance variants (Ferrari‑derived hardware in high‑end trims). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_Levante) and performance roundups [U.S. News](https://cars.usnews.com/cars-trucks/maserati/levante/performance).
  - Diesel 3.0L offerings exist in some markets (older/Euro markets) — less common on Australian dealer lots.
  - All commonly use an 8‑speed ZF torque‑converter automatic and AWD as standard in the Levante range. [Wikipedia / evo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_Levante).
- Marketplace rivals: Porsche Cayenne, BMW X5, Mercedes‑Benz GLE/AMG variants, Audi Q7/Q8, Range Rover Sport and Jaguar F‑PACE SVR. These are the concrete alternatives you should compare to gauge value and ownership headaches. #### 2. What goes wrong with a used Maserati Levante?

Exterior
- Panel fit & trim: Early Levantes were sporty but not German-tight. Expect plastic exterior trim and wheel‑arch mouldings to loosen, fade or chip with age — common on 2017–2020 cars. Inspect lower door mouldings, window surrounds and B‑pillar covers for peeling or rattles. See parts/trim guides and owner threads warning about fading/looseness: [Go‑Parts trim guides](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-maserati-levante-2017-2024-2) and owners’ forum chatter at [maseratilevante.org](https://www.maseratilevante.org/).
- Paint durability and chips: Owners report paint chips on leading edges (bonnet, arches) and dealer‑repaired resprays are not uncommon. CarComplaints collects body/paint issues — check any car for spot‑repairs and colour mismatches: [CarComplaints body/paint](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Maserati/Levante/body_paint/index.shtml).
- ADAS front sensors & camera: Adaptive Cruise / AEB/FCW use a forward radar and camera. Faulty radar or forward‑camera units can disable multiple ADAS features, or cause false activations. These assemblies sit behind the grille/bumper and are sensitive to impact or moisture ingress; owners see intermittent faults and dealer fixes/recall activity. Check for ADAS fault lights during startup and insist on dealer‑cleared ADAS diagnostics. See radar‑sensor and forward‑camera failure notes: [Go‑Parts radar sensor](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/radar-sensor-maserati-ghibli-maserati-levante-maserati-quattroporte-2017-2024), and RecallExplained’s compiled complaints/recalls list: [RecallExplained Levante](https://recallexplained.com/maserati/levante/).

Interior
- Infotainment & 12V drain: The Levante uses Maserati’s MTC+ infotainment (8.4‑inch on many cars) with Uconnect‑derived software. Expect touchscreen freezes, reboots, CarPlay dropouts and USB port failures — especially on older software builds. Simple soft resets sometimes help, but many owners needed dealer software updates or module replacement. Check that CarPlay/Android Auto works, all USB ports charge, and that the screen boots cleanly on cold start. Guides and failure reports: [Go‑Parts infotainment guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-maserati-levante-2017-2024) and service‑shop notes on freezes: [FastLaneEuropean touchscreen issues](https://fastlaneeuropean.com/blog/factors-leading-to-maseratis-touchscreen-freezing-problems/) (service blogs).
- Leather & dash delamination: There are technical bulletins for leather/dash problems (leather detaching/separating). On older cars expect wear on steering wheels and seat bolsters if the car was driven hard or used for rideshare. See NHTSA tech bulletin example (dashboard leather procedure): [NHTSA TSB PDF](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2025/MC-11014587-0001.pdf).
- Usability & entry/exit: Levante sits higher than sedans but lower than big SUVs. Door‑sill heights are standard for a midsize performance SUV — decent entry/exit for seniors, but check door check angles and seals for scuffs. For taxi/rideshare duty expect accelerated wear: leather creasing, scuffs on lower doors and heavier soiling of carpets.

Mechanically
- General engineering reputation: Maserati builds performance‑oriented, hand‑finished cars. That luxury and exclusivity comes with complex electronics and premium parts costs. Expect higher parts and labour bills than mainstream German rivals.
- Security vulnerabilities: Maserati ownership is not immune to modern keyless/connected theft exploits:
  - Relay attacks (classic key‑fob relay) — common to most keyless cars.
  - OBD‑port cloning/programmers and more advanced CAN‑injection attacks on newer cars: owner guides and security writeups list layered attack vectors and suggested defences. If the car has no immobiliser updates or aftermarket protections, it’s a target. See practical owner guidance: [SpareKeyFob — keyless theft prevention](https://sparekeyfob.com/blog/maserati-levante-keyless-theft-prevention-london) and technical CAN injection coverage: [SecurityWeek / CAN injection article](https://www.securityweek.com/thieves-use-can-injection-hack-to-steal-cars/).
- Recommended anti‑theft measures:
  - Fit a mechanical steering lock and keep the key‑fob in a Faraday pouch overnight.
  - Disable automatic keyless‑unlock functions where possible and ask your dealer about immobiliser/security gateway software updates.
  - If you’re in a high‑risk location, fit a professionally‑coded immobiliser or GPS tracker with a tilt/engine‑off wake alarm.
  - Request dealer proof of any published security service campaigns (VIN‑specific). Maserati recall/VIN lookup page is the authority: [Maserati recall lookup](https://www.maserati.com/us/en/shopping-tools/recall-by-vin).

Engines
(Each engine variant flagged in the research gets its own italic subsection. Bold the engine family or code where available.)

- *3.0L V6 — **F160** twin‑turbo (petrol)*
  - Common failure points: turbocharger wear (boost leaks and intercooler hoses), oil‑leaks from cam covers/gaskets, and occasional turbo oil seal issues causing smoke on cold start. Owners report more service items when cars have been driven hard or not serviced at short intervals. See reliability notes and V6 problem rundowns: [CarChecker 3.0 V6 reliability guide](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/maserati_levante_mk1_3.0_v6.html) and owner aggregations: [OwnerKeep issues](https://ownerkeep.com/models/maserati-levante/common-problems).
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Oil & filter: change every 10,000 km (or 12 months) using the specified synthetic grade. Treat 10,000 km as the practical Australian service baseline — Maserati and premium EU cars prefer more frequent oil changes vs mass‑market cars.
    - Inspect turbo boost hoses, intercooler connections and PCV system at every service (10,000 km).
    - Spark plugs and ignition coils: check/replace at 60–90,000 km depending on condition.
  - Why it matters: V6s are the common used choice for balance of performance and running costs — neglect the oil/turbo checks and you’ll face costly turbo or gasket repairs.

- *3.8L V8 — **F154** twin‑turbo (GTS/Trofeo)*
  - Failure points: extremely high performance hardware means higher wear and higher repair costs — turbo failures, exhaust‑system heat damage, and heavier brake wear. The V8 Trofeo also places more strain on drivetrain and suspension bushings. If maintenance has been skipped (particularly oil and differential/transfer case fluids), expect shock repairs.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Oil & filter every 10,000 km with high‑grade fully synthetic oil.
    - Brake fluid and high‑performance coolant checks every 20,000 km or annually.
    - More frequent brake checks (pads/rotors) if the car has been driven enthusiastically.
  - Ownership reality: Trofeo variants are glorious but thirstier and vastly more expensive to maintain. If past owner history shows track days, move on.

- *2.0L 4‑cyl Mild‑Hybrid — 2.0 GDI (330 hp)*
  - Failure points: relatively new to the Levante; common issues on mild‑hybrid implementations are electrical module glitches, battery assistance module behaviours, and potential turbocharger stress on high‑output small engines. The MHEV hardware can interplay with the infotainment/electrical systems causing software quirks.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Standard oil & filter every 10,000 km.
    - 12V battery health checks annually (mild‑hybrids can be more sensitive to 12V deterioration).
    - Follow dealer software/ECU update advice — hybrid/electrical modules often receive firmware updates.
  - Notes: If you want lower fuel use without the cost of a V8, the 2.0 MHEV is the practical compromise and is explicitly marketed (see Maserati hybrid overview): [Maserati Levante GT Hybrid](https://www.maserati.com/global/en/models/levante/levante-gt-hybrid) and specs listing: [Auto‑Data 2.0 GDI](https://www.auto-data.net/en/maserati-levante-2.0-gdi-330hp-mild-hybrid-awd-automatic-43286).

- *Diesel 3.0L (market dependent)*
  - Failure points: diesel particulate filter (DPF) clogging if the car has been used only for short runs, EGR valve carbon build‑up and associated turbo/EGR problems.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - If you buy a diesel, ensure regular motorway runs to keep DPF regeneration healthy.
    - Service intervals as above; inspect DPF and EGR at 30–50,000 km.

Transmission
- The Levante uses an 8‑speed ZF torque‑converter automatic across the range. Behaviour: smooth, rapid shifts when healthy, but several owners have reported torque‑converter shudder, harsh up/down shifts or unexplained shifts to neutral in early model years. These symptoms often tracked back to the torque converter, transmission control module (TCM) software, or in some cases mechanical wear. [Wikipedia on transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati_Levante) and owners/reliability data: [CarComplaints transmission reports](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Maserati/Levante/transmission/index.shtml), torque converter deep-dive: [Go‑Parts torque converter guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/automatic-transmission-torque-converter-maserati-quattroporte-maserati-ghibli-maserati-grancabrio-2007-2020).
- Known failure modes:
  - Torque‑converter shudder / vibration at low speeds.
  - Harsh or delayed shifts due to software calibration — many early cars improved after dealer TCM updates.
  - Mechanical leaks and fluid degradation if service intervals stretched beyond recommended.
- Buy‑check: road test for smoothness across all gears, heat soak behaviour after 20–30 min highway drive, and ensure service records include transmission fluid/service — if the TCM was updated or the transmission rebuilt, get paperwork.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Maserati Levante?

NSW market reality (use what you provided)
- Active NSW used listing snapshot (live): 7 cars, price range AUD $66,880 – $209,880, median AUD $82,990, years 2021–2022 and median odometer ≈ 46,428 km. All listings show automatic transmissions. Use that as the local price baseline you’ll be negotiating from (your scrape).

Value & depreciation
- The Levante depreciates hard vs mainstream premium rivals. Published analyses note:
  - ~50% drop in value in ≈3 years in some reports. [CarBuzz depreciation piece](https://carbuzz.com/used-maserati-levante-buyers-guide/).
  - 5‑year percentage losses well into the 60–79% range depending on source and assumptions. [iSeeCars Levante vs Cayenne](https://iseecars.com/compare/maserati-levante-vs-porsche-cayenne) and [CarEdge depreciation page](https://caredge.com/maserati/levante/depreciation).
- Practical takeaway: You’ll get huge dealer discounts vs new price, so purchase price becomes the opportunity — but accept higher running and repair risk.

Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy — how to calculate and what to watch
- The right way to assess a used Levante is to add:
  - Purchase price + immediate deferred maintenance (tyres, brakes, service) + annual servicing + fuel + insurance + potential out‑of‑warranty repairs.
- Fuel: economy is wildly variant by engine:
  - Expect the V6 petrol to be in the mid‑single‑digit to low‑double L/100km bracket in gentle driving; in real mixed driving expect ≈9–12 L/100km. The high‑power Trofeo V8 will be a heavy drinker — roughly 12–15 L/100km in normal driving and worse if driven hard. Diesels/mild‑hybrids can be significantly better in the 7–9 L/100km ballpark depending on duty. Published runs and owner logs point to these spreads ([evo running costs](https://www.evo.co.uk/maserati/levante/mpg-and-running-costs); [Fuelly real mpg logs](https://www.fuelly.com/car/maserati/levante)).
  - Use your annual km to estimate fuel spend: e.g., 15,000 km/year at 10 L/100km and petrol $2.00/L = 15,000 * 0.10 * $2.00 = $3,000/year fuel.
- Maintenance & repairs:
  - Expect higher parts/labour and variable reliability. Typical independent valuations put maintenance/repair costs well above mainstream luxury brands; CarEdge and Automonitor give illustrative annual figures and five‑year totals. [CarEdge costs](https://caredge.com/maserati/levante/costs), [Edmunds True Cost to Own](https://www.edmunds.com/maserati/levante/2024/cost-to-own/).
- Insurance: budget ~10–25% more than comparable German rivals due to parts/time costs and performance profile — local quotes vary.
- Warranty & dealer service: Maserati dealer network is small in Australia — fewer dealers means travel to specialised service centres; this can add carrying costs. Confirm remaining factory warranty or extended warranty options.

Hybrid/electric premium worth it?
- Does paying a premium for the mild‑hybrid 2.0 deliver net savings? It depends on kilometres:
  - For low annual kms (<10–12k km), fuel savings are modest and won’t offset extra purchase premium if one exists.
  - For high annual kms (>15k–20k km) and lots of urban starts/stops, the hybrid’s lower consumption likely reduces fuel spend meaningfully. Use WLTP/real‑world owner logs to estimate. Sources for hybrid consumption: [Autotijd GT Hybrid consumption](https://autotijd.be/en/consumption/maserati/levante/gt-hybrid-330-hp-awd-3486) and owner logs ([Fuelly](https://www.fuelly.com/car/maserati/levante)).
  - Software/complexity caveat: hybrids add complexity and potential electrical/ECU quirks — factor modestly higher servicing for hybrid systems and battery health checks.
- Mathematical approach: compute break‑even on purchase premium vs annual fuel saving. If the hybrid costs $5k more and saves $800/year fuel -> ~6.25 years to break even (excluding higher servicing/insurance). Do the math for your driving pattern.

Buy / don’t buy — the blunt verdict
- If you want Maserati style, V8 soundtrack and exclusivity and you accept that maintenance and depreciation are part of the ownership drama: buy, but be choosy.
- If you want a no‑drama luxury SUV with better resale and cheaper ownership: look at Porsche Cayenne, BMW X5 or Mercedes‑Benz GLE instead.

Best‑buy strategy and sweet spot
- NSW market data shows 2021–2022 cars with ~46k km at a median of AUD $82,990. At that price and odometer:
  - Target the mid‑spec V6 (S/Modena/GT) with full dealer service history and a recent transmission/ECU software stamp. It balances performance and running costs.
  - Avoid Trofeo unless you have deep pockets for fuel, tyres, brakes and mechanical sympathy. The Trofeo is a niche purchase for the enthusiast who can absorb running costs.
  - If you can find a well‑specified 2.0 MHEV (GT Hybrid) with service history and under‑warranty electrical checks, it’s the pragmatic pick for lower running costs — but hybrids are less common and you’ll want proof of battery/12V health.
- Inspection checklist (non‑negotiable):
  - Full Maserati dealer service history, including any TCM or ADAS software updates.
  - Evidence of ADAS recalibration or radar/camera replacements (no ADAS fault lights).
  - Transmission/drivetrain clear road‑test (no shudder, no unexpected neutral).
  - Infotainment and USB/CarPlay confirmed working.
  - VIN check for open recalls, and request any recall work receipts: [Maserati recall VIN tool](https://www.maserati.com/us/en/shopping-tools/recall-by-vin) and RecallExplained summary: [RecallExplained Levante](https://recallexplained.com/maserati/levante/).
  - Mechanical inspection for oil leaks, turbo oil seal smoke, and suspension bushings.
  - Security: ask if immobiliser updates were performed and consider immediate aftermarket security.

Final buying recommendation (clear)
- If you want a Levante and you’re pragmatic: aim for a 2021–2022 V6 Levante (S/GT/Modena) with ~40–60k km and a clean dealer service trail. Price around the NSW median (~AUD $82,990) is realistic — negotiate for a dealer warranty and recent software/TBA updates. Avoid Trofeo for daily use unless you have the wallet for it; the hybrid MHEV is attractive if you can verify battery/12V & software history and the premium is small.
- If depreciation and running costs terrify you: skip to a Porsche Cayenne or BMW X5 for a quieter TCO picture.
