#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

- Generation & production timeframe: Tesla Model S — first launched 2012 and continuously evolved through factory refreshes and hardware updates into the mid‑2020s (the platform remained the same family of Model S vehicles produced from 2012 onward) [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_S). Tesla does not publicise a conventional BMW‑style chassis code in owner literature — the car is referred to simply as the Model S in Tesla service and owner documentation [Tesla Owner's Manual / Specs](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-F617DA3E-11C1-4FBA-98A7-A9C3828E3536.html).

- Product hierarchy: the Model S sits at the top of Tesla’s sedan line — above the more mainstream Model 3 and Model Y and its SUV cousin, the Model X (which shares much of the same architecture in early generations) [Tesla press materials / Wikipedia](https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/tesla-motors-begin-customer-deliveries-model-s-june-22nd) / [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_S).

- Shared global platform architecture: the Model S uses Tesla’s skateboard-style floor‑mounted battery pack and drive unit architecture (battery pack in the floor, motors/inverter/gearset in compact drive‑units). The Model X originally shares this basic architecture (Model 3/Y use different, later platforms) — this matters for parts and third‑party repair options for older S/X cars [Tesla press release](https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/tesla-motors-begin-customer-deliveries-model-s-june-22nd) / [Tesla Service Manual](https://service.tesla.com/docs/ModelS/ServiceManual/en-us/index.html).

- Market‑specific powertrains and drivetrains (Australian buyer shorthand):
  - Rear‑wheel drive (early RWD variants; early single‑motor cars).
  - **Dual‑motor AWD** (most common mid‑life configurations; one motor front + one rear).
  - **Tri‑motor Plaid** (high‑performance variant introduced later).
  - Single‑speed direct drive (one reduction gear per drive unit) — no torque converter, no multi‑ratio gearbox [Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/tesla/model-s/2025/features-specs/) / [evo](https://evo.co.uk/tesla/model-s/engine-and-gearbox).

- Representative outputs (varied by year & trim): dual‑motor packages typically had roughly equivalent motor outputs (historical dual‑motor splits around ~250 kW per motor in later LR models); Plaid tri‑motor boosts system output dramatically (hundreds of kW extra). Battery usable capacities cluster around 75–100 kWh on later long‑range cars [EVSpecifications](https://www.evspecifications.com/en/model/d1e23e6) / [Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/tesla/model-s/2025/features-specs/).

- Direct rivals in Australia: Porsche Taycan, Lucid Air, Mercedes‑Benz EQS, BMW i5 and other luxury/performance electric sedans (and used luxury ICE alternatives if you’re trading dollars for certainty) [CarsCounsel](https://carscounsel.com/cars-similar-to-tesla-model-s/) / [The Car Connection](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/tesla_model-s_choices).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Tesla Model S?

Exterior
- Panel fit & trim:
  - Expect inconsistent panel gaps and fit & finish on a proportion of cars. Owner‑reported fit issues are common in public datasets and enthusiast forums; this is cosmetic but can be a persistent annoyance and show‑stopper with a high‑end badge [Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/1866703/do-new-teslas-still-have-bad-panel-gaps/) / [TrueDelta](https://www.truedelta.com/Tesla-Model-S/body-problems-1095).
  - Exterior trim (especially black/chrome window trim and gloss black plastics) is delicate — polishing aggressive cleaners can eat the finish; owners report fading/discolouration on older seals/trim pieces [Tesla Motors Club thread](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/how-to-prevent-discoloration-of-exterior-black-chrome-window-trim.307650/).
- Paint durability:
  - Reports of uneven paint and early stone chips are more frequent than on German rivals. Panels with poor gaps can also lift paint stress points.
- Body recalls & important items:
  - Tesla publishes recall service notices — Model S recalls have included steering‑assist motor bolt issues, airbag work and rear‑view camera/display issues; always check the VIN and open recalls before purchase [Tesla Recall Info](https://www.tesla.com/support/annual-and-recall-service) and NHTSA resources [NHTSA search](https://www.nhtsa.gov/search-safety-issues).
- ADAS / Autopilot safety quirks:
  - Phantom braking, AEB inconsistency and camera‑vision limitations have been widely reported and investigated by regulators. NHTSA investigations and high‑profile recalls have targeted Autosteer/traffic‑aware cruise functions — fixes are often software OTA updates but regulators have questioned whether those remedies fully fix safety issues [AutoPilotWatch](https://www.autopilotwatch.com/complaints) / [NHTSA investigation doc](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/inv/2016/INCLA-PE16007-7876.pdf) / [NBC News summary](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/tesla-issues-massive-recall-2-million-vehicles-autopilot-safety-concer-rcna129486).
  - Practical buyer check: cameras must be clean, firmware current, and expect the lane‑keep/traffic‑aware cruise features to be “driver‑assist” — not “self‑driving”. If you rely on autopilot for highway commuting, plan for imperfect behaviour and regulator scrutiny.

Interior
- Durability under hard use:
  - The Model S interior is minimalist, with large touch control surfaces. Consumer reviews cite unintuitive control placement and a reliance on the central screen for many functions — for churn‑heavy work like rideshare this can be a pain if the screen or MCU falters [Consumer Reports review](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/tesla/model-s/2026/road-test-report/).
  - Trim and soft‑touch materials can wear; door sills and entry geometry are generous for a low‑slung sedan, but repeated ingress/egress (kids, work boots) shows scuffing.
- Electrical & infotainment failures:
  - **MCU 8GB eMMC failure**: cars built before March 2018 were subject to a Tesla recall where the 8GB eMMC in the MCU could fail due to wear, causing touchscreen crashes, boot loops or a black screen — Tesla offered replacement; check VIN and service history [Tesla 8GB eMMC recall FAQ](https://www.tesla.com/support/8gb-emmc-recall-frequently-asked-questions).
  - Later Model S cars still report frozen screens and intermittent displays (2021–2024 reports of freezes/dark screens while driving), which is not a trivial issue on a car that centralises so many controls [Guardian Lemon Law summary](https://www.guardianlemonlaw.com/2021-2024-tesla-model-s-touchscreen-freezing-failure-frozen-or-unresponsive-display-system-crashes/).
  - Wireless phone pads and small electronics are known to be flaky — owners often revert to wired phone charging for reliability [Tesla Motors Club / Go‑Parts summary](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/mobile-phone-charger-tesla-3-tesla-cybertruck-tesla-s-2020-2026).
- 12V battery drain and accessories:
  - Vampire drain can occur (modules maintaining cellular/wakeup states); if a car sits for long periods you may face a dead 12V battery. Check the 12V battery age and replacement receipts — replacements are relatively cheap, but an unattended dead 12V in a modern EV can complicate diagnostics.

Mechanically
- Engineering reputation:
  - Electrically, the Model S architecture is clever and efficient — but as cars age, high‑voltage components, inverters and drive‑unit internals become the expensive items that dominate reliability chatter [Tesla Service Manual](https://service.tesla.com/docs/ModelS/ServiceManual/en-us/index.html).
- High‑priority security vulnerabilities:
  - Modern Teslas have had documented attack surfaces: physical CAN‑bus access and relay/CAN injection techniques can be used to unlock and start vehicles in some builds; mitigation often arrives as firmware updates but the research shows motivated thieves can exploit physical access points if the car is left exposed [CVE advisory / ASRG report](https://cvefeed.io/vuln/detail/CVE-2025-6785) / [ASRG security advisory](https://asrg.io/security-advisories/cve-2025-6785/).
  - Historical gateway ECU vulnerabilities were patched via OTA updates after disclosure; keep firmware current and confirm owner installed security updates [CISA advisory example](https://cisa.gov/news-events/ics-advisories/icsa-16-341-01).
- Recommended aftermarket counter‑measures:
  - Physical: disc locks, steering wheel locks, wheel‑bolt locking nuts, visible immobiliser stickers, and secure parking/garage are cheap ways to raise the theft bar.
  - Digital: ensure the car is updated with the latest firmware, disable passive/phone‑based key entry if possible when unattended, and use PIN‑to‑drive if you’re worried about unauthorised starts.

Engines (motors & powertrain variants)
Note: Tesla doesn’t use traditional engine codes. Below I’ve broken out the principal motor configurations historically available. Each subsection is italicised as requested.

- *Induction single‑motor rear drive (early RWD variants)*
  - Known failure points:
    - **Drive Unit / Inverter** bearing wear and gear/shaft damage in older high‑mileage cars; cooling seals can leak and let coolant/contaminants compromise electronics [EVReviver](https://evreviver.com/tesla-drive-unit-repair-orange-county/) / [EvSeekers drive unit guide](https://evseekers.com/tesla-drive-unit-replacement-cost/).
    - Reduced power, humming/grinding noises and vibration under load are classic symptoms.
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Follow Tesla service manual diagnostics; independent specialists recommend early inspection of lubrication seals and cooling hoses around 100,000 km if a car is used aggressively. Tesla’s official service manual is the reference for proper procedure [Tesla Service Manual](https://service.tesla.com/docs/ModelS/ServiceManual/en-us/index.html).

- *Dual‑motor AWD (mid‑life LR/Performance dual‑motor builds)*
  - Known failure points:
    - Both front and rear drive units have similar failure modes: inverter/drive‑unit seal leaks, bearing wear, and wiring/connector corrosion. In mixed mileage fleets, the rear drive unit historically tends to be the pricier item to fix [EVReviver](https://evreviver.com/tesla-drive-unit-repair-orange-county/) / [au7o.io problem list](https://au7o.io/known-issues/tesla-model-s).
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Cooling system checks for the power electronics and a watchful eye on vibration/noise at acceleration. Independent workshops commonly recommend checks every 20,000–40,000 km on drive‑unit cooling lines and connectors for ageing cars.

- *Tri‑motor Plaid (high‑performance variant)*
  - Known failure points:
    - The Plaid drive units operate at extreme RPM/power, so thermal stress and power electronics issues are theoretically more acute if the car is driven relentlessly on track or with repeated hard launches. Repair complexity is higher due to unique inverter and motor packaging [InsideEVs Plaid drive unit walk‑through](https://insideevs.com/news/513388/tesla-models-plaid-drive-unit/).
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Treat these like high‑performance cars: conservative daily driving, avoid repeated maximum‑power launches, and maintain cooling systems. Specialist inspection recommended if you plan spirited driving.

Other battery & high‑voltage notes (applies across variants)
- **Battery pack & cooling**:
  - Battery degradation is generally modest if the car has been cared for and not left at 100% SOC for long periods. Still, battery cooling issues and module problems are expensive to repair — get the pack health and module voltages checked where possible [au7o.io summary](https://au7o.io/known-issues/tesla-model-s).
  - Preventative maintenance: ensure coolant loops for battery/inverter are serviced; independent shops often recommend coolant replacement/inspection at 50,000–100,000 km on older cars.

Transmission
- The Model S uses a single‑speed reduction gear (the drive‑unit integrates motor, inverter and reduction gear). There is no torque converter or multi‑ratio gearbox — that means there’s no clutch pack slipping to worry about, but the drive‑unit itself is a complex electro‑mechanical assembly that can fail [Evo (engine & gearbox explainer)](https://evo.co.uk/tesla/model-s/engine-and-gearbox) / [Tesla Service Manual](https://service.tesla.com/docs/ModelS/ServiceManual/en-us/index.html).
- Known failure modes:
  - Bearing wear, reduction gear damage, cooling seal failure and inverter faults cause the majority of expensive repairs. Sometimes component‑level repairs are possible; at other times the entire drive unit/inverter needs replacement — cost varies enormously with age and warranty status [EvSeekers drive unit doc](https://evseekers.com/tesla-drive-unit-replacement-cost/) / [Tesla Motors Club threads](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/model-s-large-drive-unit-inverter-errors-after-seal-and-bearing-replacement.343857/).
- Behaviour on test drives:
  - Expect very smooth instant torque delivery, no shifting, and a single‑ratio feel. Any vibration, humming, or power‑loss under acceleration is a red flag for drivetrain internals.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Tesla Model S?

- NSW dealer market snapshot (live data provided):
  - Active listings: 1
  - Price range / Median: AUD $32,950
  - Year range: 2017
  - Median odometer: 2,017 km
  - Transmission mix: n/a
  - Commentary: That listing is eye‑catching — a 2017 Model S for $32,950 with only ~2,000 km is either an incredible deal or a car with paperwork quirks (insurance write‑off, battery history, or salvage). Always get a full VIN check, and insist on seeing service and recall completion records.

- Price band in the broader market:
  - Used Model S prices are all over the shop. Recent analyses show a wide used price spread (roughly US$ equivalent of $15k–$90k depending on year/trim) and steep early depreciation (iSeeCars: ~57% drop in 3 years; 5‑year declines measured in the 60–70% region depending on trim) [iSeeCars depreciation summary](https://www.iseecars.com/car/tesla-model-s/resale-value) / [Recharged used price analysis](https://recharged.com/articles/tesla-s-price-used) / [USA Today on recent price drops](https://www.usatoday.com/story/cars/shopping/evs/2026/02/10/used-tesla-model-price/88592732007/).
  - In Australia, expect earlier Model S (2013–2016) to trade lower than later 2017–2019 cars, but the spread is huge. Your NSW $32,950 2017 listing aligns with the lower end of the international used market.

- Depreciation metrics & what that means:
  - Expect far steeper depreciation than comparable German ICE luxury sedans — early owners absorbed huge depreciation. iSeeCars and industry writeups show Model S loses over half its value in the first 3 years historically [iSeeCars](https://www.iseecars.com/car/tesla-model-s/resale-value).
  - Practical implication: You're likely buying an EV discount, but you also buy exposure to possible expensive repairs (drive unit, battery modules, MCU) and changing demand for large luxury EV sedans.

- Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) strategy — how to calculate for Australia:
  - Purchase price: include any NSW on‑road costs and dealer margins.
  - Electricity: use local kWh rates. For the Model S, published consumption converts roughly to ~20–22 kWh/100 km in real‑world mixed driving for long‑range models (EPA and independent tests vary by wheel size and driving) — consult energy consumption pages and estimate home charging cost: e.g., at $0.30/kWh that’s ~$6–$6.60 per 100 km (these numbers are illustrative — adapt to your tariff) [FuelEconomy.gov / InsideEVs consumption summaries](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=49124) / [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/719347/2024-tesla-models-epa-range-energy-consumption/).
  - Maintenance & repairs: Teslas have lower scheduled mechanical maintenance (no oil changes), but high‑cost potential failures (drive unit, battery, MCU). Edmunds’ 5‑year TCO examples show the Model S can still be expensive over time, mostly because of depreciation and repair exposures [Edmunds TCO](https://www.edmunds.com/tesla/model-s/2025/cost-to-own/).
  - Insurance: luxury EVs cost more to insure in Australia — get quotes before you sign.
  - Depreciation: factor in the known steep short‑term depreciation for valuation in personal TCO.
  - Charging vs petrol math: home‑charging a Tesla is often materially cheaper per km than petrol equivalents; EnergySage and Recharged give per‑mile/kWh comparisons showing EV energy costs often half or less of petrol per‑km. But the capital cost and repair risk can swamp fuel savings if you buy a fragile bargain [EnergySage charging cost analysis](https://www.energysage.com/ev-charging/electric-vehicle-charging-cost/tesla-charging-cost-vs-gas/) / [Recharged cost comparison](https://recharged.com/articles/cost-of-tesla-vs-gas/).
  - Practical TCO verdict: If you plan >20,000 km/year, a well‑maintained long‑range EV can make electricity savings meaningful. If you do low annual km and fear expensive unexpected repairs, the lower purchase price of an old Model S might not justify the risk.

- Is paying a premium for a later hybrid/EV better?
  - If comparing a late‑model petrol hybrid (Mercedes/BMW hybrid or Toyota hybrid) vs a late‑model Model S: the hybrid will retain more predictable maintenance costs and resale stability; the Model S gives much lower running fuel cost but more uncertain repair exposure and falling market values. Use your km profile:
    - High km drivers who can home‑charge cheaply and don’t need dealer backup: an EV (Model S) can be cheaper per km.
    - Low km or no home‑charging: pay careful attention — public DC charging costs and potential waiting times reduce savings.
  - Use Edmunds / EnergySage calculator styles to input local electricity tariffs and your driving profile to determine break‑even. For many Australians, a pragmatic solution is buying a newer, low‑km Model S from a reputable vendor with parts/service history (if you can.)

- Final buying recommendation (pragmatic, blunt):
  - If you see a 2017 Model S in NSW for AUD $32,950 with 2,017 km, proceed carefully but excitedly — that price is tempting. Do these checks:
    - Full VIN PAST HISTORY CHECK (accidents, write‑offs, odometer verification).
    - Confirm all recalls are completed via Tesla recall lookup and NHTSA/official records [Tesla recall page](https://www.tesla.com/support/annual-and-recall-service) / [RecallExplained](https://recallexplained.com/tesla/model-s/).
    - Get an independent EV specialist inspection, including drive‑unit noise test, battery pack voltage/imbalance scan and MCU health test (look for eMMC replacement evidence).
    - Check firmware version and evidence of OTA security patches (important given documented CAN / gateway vulnerabilities) — if the car hasn’t been updated, that’s a red flag [ASRG/CVE discussion](https://asrg.io/security-advisories/cve-2025-6785/).
    - Insist on seeing 12V battery receipts and check for frozen screen symptoms on test drives.
  - Best sweet spot:
    - Financial sweet spot is often later‑model long‑range dual‑motor cars with documented service history and MCU replaced/upgraded (post‑2018 MCU or proof of eMMC remedy). Those cars avoid the worst of MCU failures and often have better range/efficiency.
    - For buyers on a tight budget, a low‑km 2016–2017 Model S with comprehensive professional inspection and full disclosure can be a brilliant value — but don’t buy blind. The NSW listing at $32,950 could be an absolute steal, providing the checks above come up clean.
  - If you want minimal drama and maximum support, buy through a Tesla‑certified or reputable NSW dealer that will provide a warranty or a short‑term mechanical guarantee — the aftermarket is improving, but warranty coverage is still king for peace of mind.

But you’re buying a complex electrical machine with a checkered history for body fit and software/legal drama. If you’re not prepared to pull VINs, insist on specialised inspections and live with OTA quirks or potential expensive repairs, walk away. If you are?
