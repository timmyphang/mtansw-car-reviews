#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning

- Generation / production timeframe / codes
  - First generation Palisade (US model years 2020–2025). The second-generation Palisade lands as a 2026 model with notable updates. The first-gen Palisade is often referenced by code LX2 in service literature/TSBs. See the model timeline in Hyundai's public material and Wikipedia for the generation split: [Hyundai USA specs & model years](https://www.hyundaiusa.com/us/en/vehicles/palisade/compare-specs) and [Wikipedia summary of Palisade generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Palisade).

- Where it sits in the Hyundai lineup
  - The Palisade is Hyundai’s large three-row family SUV — above Santa Fe / Santa Fe XL and below any luxury-badged models. It’s the brand’s answer to buyers who need true seven- or eight-seat practicality with near-SUV presence and near-premium interior trimmings.

- Shared global platform and sibling models
  - The Palisade shares architecture and many mechanical parts with the Kia Telluride family. That sibling relationship matters for parts availability and independent workshop knowledge — many parts and troubleshooting notes cross over between the two models (see trim/platform comparisons and timelines: [TrimAtlas / Trim comparisons](https://trimatlas.com/compare/hyundai/palisade/) and model histories: [Potamkin dealer timeline](https://www.potamkinhyundai.com/hyundai-palisade-comparison.htm)).

- Engines, outputs, transmissions and drive configurations sold into various markets (Australian-relevant groupings)
  - 3.8 L V6 (Lambda II) — naturally aspirated petrol V6 — paired to an 8‑speed automatic; FWD standard, AWD optional on many trims (mainstay for 2020–2025 Palisade in many markets). Source: Hyundai spec sheets and 2024 spec PDF: [Hyundai 2024 Palisade specs (PDF)](https://www.hyundainews.com/assets/documents/original/56241-2024PalisadeSpecs020323.pdf).
  - 3.5 L V6 (newer 2026 update) — replaces the older 3.8 L V6 in the 2026 redesign; paired with an 8‑speed auto in U.S. spec documents: [2026 Palisade specs PDF](https://www.hyundainews.com/assets/documents/original/65859-2026PalisadeSpecsandFeatures20250417.pdf) and coverage at [KBB 2026 specs](https://www.kbb.com/hyundai/palisade/2026/specs/).
  - 2.5 L turbo petrol + electric hybrid (HEV) — paired to a 6‑speed automatic in U.S. spec pages for 2026; AWD/FWD layouts vary by trim. See [Edmunds 2026 spec listing](https://www.edmunds.com/hyundai/palisade/2026/suv/features-specs/) and Hyundai press material above.
  - 2.2 L turbo‑diesel — offered in some international markets (not always Australian), listed on global spec pages and Wikipedia: [Wikipedia engine list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Palisade).

- Marketplace rivals (explicit)
  - Kia Telluride, Toyota Highlander, Honda Pilot, Mazda CX‑90, Volkswagen Atlas, Chevrolet Traverse. Comparative lists and head‑to‑heads in editorial pieces: [CarBuzz competitors list](https://carbuzz.com/10-hyundai-palisade-competitors-going-into-2026/) and [The Car Connection comparisons](https://www.thecarconnection.com/compare/hyundai_palisade_choices).

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Hyundai Palisade?

Below I’m blunt: the Palisade is a very useful family SUV, but not without character faults that bite buyers who assume “new-ish = trouble‑free.” You’ll want to check all of these items before handing over the deposit.

Exterior
- Paint, panels and trim
  - Paint durability: multiple owner reports and trackers document paint peeling, bubbling and delamination on early Palisades — enough that owner communities run dedicated trackers and class‑action discussion threads. Don’t ignore flaking or bubbling panels; these problems aren’t purely cosmetic — they can spread. See the community tracker and owner complaint aggregation: [Palisade paint issues tracker](https://www.palisadepaintissues.com/) and [CarComplaints body/paint posts](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Hyundai/Palisade/body_paint/index.shtml).
  - Panel alignment & trim fit: owners report gaps and looseness in exterior trim and some facelifted parts are not interchangeable, so check doors, boot lids and the plastic top panels for gaps / wind noise — Go‑Parts documents common exterior trim vulnerabilities and the effect of the 2023 facelift on part interchangeability: [Go‑Parts exterior trim summary](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/exterior-trim-panel-hyundai-palisade-2020-2025-2).
  - Safety recalls affecting body components: Hyundai has multiple recall campaigns across 2020–2026 Palisades; NHTSA recall files document production windows and campaigns — always check the VIN for outstanding recalls: [NHTSA recall 26V034 example (PDF)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2026/RCLRPT-26V034-7650.pdf) and Hyundai’s recall portal: [Hyundai campaigns lookup](https://autoservice.hyundaiusa.com/campaignhome).

- ADAS and sensors
  - Radar and camera warnings: the Palisade uses a front radar + windscreen camera for functions like Smart Cruise and Forward Collision Avoidance. “Radar blocked” messages are common when the radar housing gets dirty or misaligned after minor impacts; sensor replacement requires dealer calibration and special tools: [Go‑Parts ADAS sensor guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/collision-avoidance-sensor-hyundai-palisade-kia-telluride-2020-2026).
  - False or aggressive AEB / adaptive braking reports: there are numerous consumer complaints and forum threads describing unexpected or aggressive automatic emergency braking or heavy braking from adaptive cruise. These have led to dealer investigations and some regulatory attention: see aggregated complaints and advisory coverage — NHTSA listing and complaint aggregations: [NHTSA vehicle detail page (2025 Palisade)](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2025/Hyundai/Palisade/) and complaint summaries (CarProblemZoo / law‑firm summaries) such as [CarProblemZoo AEB problems](https://www.carproblemzoo.com/hyundai/palisade/automatic-emergency-braking-problems.php) and [Lemberg Law ABS braking issue page](https://lemberglaw.com/hyundai-palisade-abs-braking-problem/).

Interior
- Durability under heavy use (rideshare, school runs)
  - Leather and trim peeling: owners report coated leather delamination on seats — the kind of ageing that looks bad and reduces resale value. Check seat bolsters, driver seat outer edge, and rear seat backs for cracking or peeling: [Palisade leather peeling forum thread](https://www.palisadeforum.com/threads/leather-seats-peeling.429/).
  - Panel gaps over head unit and cheap‑feeling trim: owners have flagged gaps and rough mating edges above/around the head unit; remind yourself that even a well‑optioned Palisade isn’t up to full luxury‑level panel fit-and-finish in some early model years: [Forum gap post](https://www.palisadeforum.com/threads/gap-observed-in-plastic-panels-on-top-of-head-unit.2366/).
  - Toughness for taxi/ride‑share duty: the Palisade was built as a family hauler, not a cab — leather/coated seats and soft touch panels show wear fast under high turnover and boot scuffing can be pronounced.

- Electrical and infotainment
  - Wireless charger failures: the wireless charging pad can blink, fail or be intermittent on 2020–2022 units; diagnostics and replacement procedures are well documented on parts sites: [Go‑Parts wireless charger bulletin](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/wireless-device-charging-system-module-hyundai-palisade-2020-2022).
  - Frozen touchscreens / infotainment hangs: owners report frozen screens requiring restarts; dealers issue software updates and navigation/audio update tools exist — verify the vehicle has the latest firmware: [Hyundai navigation update page](https://update.hyundai.com/US/EN/updateNoticeList) and common owner threads on frozen screens: [forum frozen screen thread](https://palisadeforums.org/threads/frozen-touch-screen.285/).
  - 12V battery quirks: while widespread battery‑drain faults are not a documented recall in the search results, multiple forum threads and service reports show electrical niggles; check accessory operation (doors, locks, media, rear‑seat outlets) with the vehicle cold.

Mechanically
- Engineering reputation in short
  - Middle‑of‑the‑road. Consumer Reports lists the Palisade’s reliability as about average and owner reports cluster around electronics, paint and drivetrain niggles rather than chronic catastrophic faults: [Consumer Reports reliability notes](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/hyundai/palisade/2025/reliability/).
- Security vulnerabilities
  - Early production Palisades (and a number of Hyundai/Kia models) were targeted by a viral theft method that exploited missing immobilisers on some VIN windows. Hyundai ran an anti‑theft software campaign (Campaign 993) and later vehicles built after Nov 2021 included immobilisers. Check the VIN and whether the anti‑theft update or immobiliser upgrade was done. Hyundai’s anti‑theft portal and the NHTSA TSB explain the dealer campaign: [Hyundai anti‑theft page](https://hyundaiantitheft.com/) and [NHTSA TSB Campaign 993 (PDF)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10235555-0001.pdf).
  - CAN bus / headlight harness “injection” thefts: growing public analysis explains how modern CAN networks can be accessed via headlight wiring and spoofed messages can defeat locks/immobilisers. This is an industry‑wide risk, with Hyundai explicitly targeted in the viral theft wave: [Autoblog on CAN injection thefts](https://www.autoblog.com/carbuying/vehicle-headlight-can-bus-injection-theft-method-update) and technical coverage: [The Register on CAN bus method](https://www.theregister.com/security/2023/04/06/how-thieves_steal_cars_using_vehicle_can_bus/).
  - Practical aftermarket countermeasures: confirm Campaign 993 was performed (or retrofit an immobiliser/engine‑cut device), use a physical steering‑wheel lock when parking in high‑risk areas, install a professional tracking/immobiliser solution (and ensure salvage/insurance accepts it), and park in well‑lit, monitored areas.

Engines
- Notes on *every single engine variant discussed above* (each subsection italicised). Read them and check VIN years against recalls/TBS.

  - * **Lambda II 3.8‑litre V6 (3,778 cc)** *
    - What can fail: documented owner and workshop reports show increased oil consumption, valve‑cover leaks, misfires (ignition coils), carbon buildup from GDI operation and, in extreme cases, oil‑starvation routes to engine failure. Autoblog’s tear‑down and parts‑site writeups show that poor oil maintenance or unnoticed leaks can lead to catastrophic outcomes: [Autoblog engine strip / oil starvation story](https://www.autoblog.com/news/hyundai-palisade-v6-tear-down-shows-how-oil-starvation-led-to-engine-failure) and [Go‑Parts engine concerns summary](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/turbocharger-hyundai-palisade-2020-2025).
    - Recalls / safety bulletins: Hyundai issued narrow valve‑spring related recalls (example 2024 valve‑spring recall 24V‑106 affecting some build runs) — fractured valve springs can cause instant and catastrophic engine failure; check VIN for applicability and remedy history: aggregated recall lists: [RecallExplained Palisade summary](https://recallexplained.com/hyundai/palisade/) and investigative writeups: [CherishYourCar engine problems summary](https://www.cherishyourcar.com/hyundai-palisade-engine-problems/).
    - Preventative maintenance: for GDI V6s, be conservative — aim for oil changes and filter service at 10,000 km or sooner if you run short trips/tows (see "service interval note" below). Monitor oil level monthly and watch for any sudden consumption. Consider fuel‑system induction cleaning and inspect valve covers/gaskets around 80,000 km if oil seepage appears (owner forums and service threads discuss these as routine repairs).
  
  - * **3.5‑litre V6 (2026 update)** *
    - What can fail: the 3.5L is newer and initially intended to replace the 3.8L. Early press notes show similar mechanical packaging but fewer long‑term engine failure reports simply because it’s newer; keep an eye on early owner complaint databases as 2026 examples accumulate. Spec summary: [2026 Palisade specs PDF](https://www.hyundainews.com/assets/documents/original/65859-2026PalisadeSpecsandFeatures20250417.pdf).
    - Preventative maintenance: follow dealer schedules; early adopter owners should be proactive about oil monitoring and dealer software campaigns as Hyundai issues updates.

  - * **2.5‑litre turbo‑petrol hybrid (HEV)** *
    - What can fail: hybrids typically shift failure modes to 12V electrics and hybrid electric modules rather than big V6 mechanicals. The new Palisade Hybrid improves fuel economy (see TCO/fuel section) but carries hybrid complexity and possible battery/EV module service costs further down the track. EPA combined figures for the 2026 Hybrid sit around 31 mpg (US) for FWD and 29 mpg AWD — that converts roughly to 7.6–8.1 L/100km (see FuelEconomy.gov numbers below). Sources: [FuelEconomy 2026 Hybrid FWD](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=49885) and [FuelEconomy 2026 Hybrid AWD](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=49583) and spec comparisons at Edmunds / KBB.
    - Preventative maintenance: hybrid battery health checks, software updates, and regular service at dealer intervals. Hybrids may be cheaper to run on fuel but can be pricier to repair outside warranty; check KBB/Edmunds TCO guides for model year comparisons.

  - * **2.2‑litre turbo‑diesel** *
    - What can fail: diesel markets reported typical common‑rail turbo/diesel wear points (EGR/clogging, DPF regeneration issues in low‑mile city use). If you see a diesel Palisade in Australia, check for DPF history and regeneration logs.

Transmission
- Type and known failure modes
  - The Palisade uses a conventional torque‑converter automatic (8‑speed for V6 models; 6‑speed for the hybrid in some markets). It is not a CVT or eCVT in the V6 range.
  - Common gearbox complaints are torque converter shudder, TCC (torque converter clutch) solenoid circuit faults (OBD codes like P0740), and valve body/solenoid failures. Hyundai issued technical service bulletins and repair procedures to address solenoid and wiring harness replacements instead of full box replacements in many cases. TSB references and guides: [TSB 20‑AT‑005H summary (dot.report)](https://dot.report/bulletins/10170886), TSB PDF link (added Palisade to a prior bulletin) and Go‑Parts transmission diagnostic/repair guides: [Go‑Parts torque converter guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/automatic-transmission-torque-converter-kia-carnival-hyundai-palisade-kia-cadenza-2020-2024) and [P0740 explanation](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/obd-p0740-hyundai-palisade-2020-2025-3-8l-lambda).
  - Pre‑purchase checks: road test for shuddering between gears, ensure smooth shifts under load and on inclines, review service history for transmission fluid changes and any TSB/repair invoices.

So what do those numbers mean? There’s a broad price spread because the Palisade was offered from near mainstream pricing up into near‑lux levels (Calligraphy), and demand for three‑row family SUVs has kept resale values relatively firm. If you’re seeing a median of $43k with ~76k km, you’re in the sweet spot of mid‑range trims (SEL/Limited) rather than low‑spec SE or full‑lux Calligraphy machines.

Depreciation metrics (how badly do they lose money?)
- Historical and market analyses show the Palisade follows mainstream depreciation patterns for the large three‑row segment:
  - KBB data: examples of used 2022 pricing and depreciation patterns (2023 Palisade lost about 30% over three years in one KBB summary): [KBB used 2022 prices](https://www.kbb.com/hyundai/palisade/2022/) and [KBB 3‑year depreciation for 2023 example](https://www.kbb.com/hyundai/palisade/2023/depreciation/).
  - A 3‑year drop in the order of 25–35% is typical for this segment; iSeeCars and CarEdge estimate 5‑year losses between roughly 45–50% for the model overall: [iSeeCars depreciation analysis](https://www.iseecars.com/car/hyundai-palisade/resale-value) and [CarEdge 5‑year chart](https://caredge.com/hyundai/palisade/depreciation).

Total cost of ownership (TCO) strategy — what to run the numbers on
- Primary TCO levers for a Palisade in Australia are depreciation, fuel, servicing, tyres, insurance and possible repairs for known weak spots (paint, electronics, transmission solenoids, V6 oil issues).
- Fuel economy and hybrid premium
  - The newer Palisade Hybrid posts significant gains in official EPA figures: FWD ~31 mpg US (≈ 7.6 L/100km), AWD ~29 mpg US (≈ 8.1 L/100km) in 2026 US testing — those figures are useful for comparative math when you plug in Aussie fuel prices and your annual km: [FuelEconomy 2026 Hybrid FWD](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=49885) and [FuelEconomy 2026 Hybrid AWD](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbs&id=49583).
  - Edmunds/KBB TCO tools show hybrids can save on fuel but carry higher purchase premiums. Example: KBB’s 2026 Palisade Hybrid 5‑year ownership estimate and Edmunds’ True Cost to Own for a 2025 Palisade illustrate how fuel and depreciation interact: [KBB Hybrid cost to own](https://www.kbb.com/hyundai/palisade-hybrid/2026/cost-to-own/) and [Edmunds TCO (2025 example)](https://www.edmunds.com/hyundai/palisade/2025/cost-to-own/).
  - Practical Aussie take: if you do high km (40,000+ km/year) highway-biased driving, the hybrid’s lower L/100km will deliver measurable savings and can justify the premium. If you’re under ~20,000 km/year and do a mix of city/school runs with periodic towing, the petrol V6 may be simpler and cheaper up front — but watch the V6 oil‑consumption issues and service rigorously.
- Service, repairs and warranty
  - Always check for outstanding recalls/service campaigns (Hyundai recall portal and NHTSA files). For known TSBs on transmission and anti‑theft campaigns see linked vendor and NHTSA documentation: [Hyundai campaign lookup](https://autoservice.hyundaiusa.com/campaignhome) and anti‑theft Campaign 993 TSB (PDF): [Campaign 993 TSB](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10235555-0001.pdf).
  - Service interval strategy for Australian use: if you plan family duty, frequent short trips, towing or dusty/harsh conditions, err toward 10,000 km service intervals for oil changes and checks (this is conservative and protects GDI V6s). If you’re mostly long highway runs, following the manufacturer schedule is acceptable — but oil-level checks each month are mandatory with a used V6.

Concrete buying advice — should you buy one?
- Yes, with caveats.
  - If you need a genuinely usable three‑row SUV that’s roomy, comfortable and generally well‑equipped, the Palisade remains one of the best value propositions in the segment — especially if you can find a 2021–2023 example with good service history and no outstanding safety campaigns.
  - Red flags to walk away from:
    - missing anti‑theft campaign or VIN built before Nov 2021 without immobiliser upgrade (higher theft risk),
    - visible paint bubbling/peeling on several panels,
    - evidence of repeated ADAS faults or unresolved AEB complaints on the vehicle’s service record,
    - transmission solenoid or TCC repairs unaddressed (multiple transmission DTCs present),
    - high oil consumption with no documented repairs (V6 Lambda engines).
- Price guidance for NSW market snapshot:
  - You’ve got live listings between AUD $37,990 and $94,990; the median is AUD $43,435 with a median odometer ~76,635 km (your provided MTA‑NSW snapshot). That median likely reflects mid‑trim machines with reasonable options. Expect to pay:
    - Bargain territory (~$38k): older 2020–2021 high‑km units or stripped trims — ask why it’s cheap.
    - Sweet spot (~$40–$50k): 2021–2023 SEL/Limited with moderate kms and good service history — target this band.
    - Premium (~$70k+): late‑model low‑km Calligraphy or new‑generation 2024–2025 high‑spec units.
  - Recommended buy: a 2021–2023 Limited with <120,000 km and full service history, anti‑theft campaign completed, and no paint/body claims. You get good kit, decent residuals and avoid the newest model premium and the earliest 2020 teething issues.

Final checklist before signing
- Run the VIN on Hyundai’s campaign/recall lookup for outstanding recalls: [Hyundai campaign lookup](https://autoservice.hyundaiusa.com/campaignhome).
- Confirm Campaign 993 anti‑theft software/immobiliser status if the car was built before Nov 2021; if not done, insist the dealer/privates get it done before sale or discount accordingly: [Hyundai anti‑theft info](https://hyundaiantitheft.com/) and TSB: [NHTSA Campaign 993 PDF](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10235555-0001.pdf).
- Inspect for paint flaking and ask for photographs of problem areas; if there’s evidence of paint bubbling, get a dealer quote on panel repainting — it’s not cheap and hits resale.
- Road‑test specifically for transmission shudder, abrupt or aggressive AEB interventions, wind noise from trim gaps and infotainment freezes; verify any issues were fixed at the dealer.
- Check service invoices for oil consumption fixes, timing/valve‑spring recalls and gearbox solenoid work.
- If buying hybrid, check battery state and dealer history of hybrid system updates; factor hybrid premium into your TCO numbers and expected km/year.

But don’t be sentimental — check the VIN for anti‑theft updates and recalls, insist on solid service proof for oil and transmission issues, and factor in potential costs for paint or infotainment fixes. Buy smart (2021–2023 Limited with good history). Drive happy.
