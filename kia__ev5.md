#### 1. Introduction & Market Positioning
- Generation: first-production Kia EV5 (production 2023–present in China; global rollout 2025– ), internal architecture: built on Hyundai‑Kia **E‑GMP** electric platform (Electric‑Global Modular Platform / E‑GMP) for the global spec EV5. [Kia global](https://worldwide.kia.com/en/vehicle/ev5), [Hyundai Motor Group](https://www.hyundaimotorgroup.com/en/news/CONT0000000000180135)
- Hierarchy: slots between the smaller EV3/EV4‑class crossovers and the larger EV6/EV9 family SUVs — essentially Kia’s mainstream family electric SUV offering (think EV3 < EV5 < EV9 in the line-up). See the EV5 positioning on Kia’s global page. [Kia global](https://worldwide.kia.com/en/vehicle/ev5)
- Shared platform & parts availability: E‑GMP means shared high‑voltage architecture and many electrified components with Hyundai Ioniq 5/6, Kia EV6 — this helps parts availability and dealer familiarity across Hyundai‑Kia networks. [Hyundai Motor Group press](https://www.hyundaimotorgroup.com/en/news/CONT0000000000180135)
- Market-specific powertrains offered (Australia / global mix, as sold in early markets):
  - *Single‑motor FWD* with an approximate output around **160 kW / 310 Nm** and front‑wheel drive via a single‑speed reduction gear (market reporting, see CarsGuide spec summary). [CarsGuide engine page](https://www.carsguide.com.au/kia/ev5/engine)
  - *Dual‑motor AWD* variants (higher grades / GT Line and up) pushing total outputs toward the **~200–230 kW** zone depending on tune (global spec variation). [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia_EV5), [Kia press pack (UK)](https://www.kiapressoffice.com/models/ev5)
  - Battery: long‑range **81.4 kWh** pack is the headline battery for many global markets (usable figures and WLTP range vary by trim). [Kia media / brochure](https://www.kiapressoffice.com/models/ev5), [official brochure](https://www.kia.com/content/dam/kwp/kr/ko/vehicles/pdf/en_brochure/en_catalog_ev5.pdf)
- Transmission & drive configuration: electric single‑speed reduction drive for motors (no multi‑ratio gearbox), FWD on base single‑motor cars, AWD on dual‑motor cars. [CarsGuide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/kia/ev5/engine)
- Marketplace rivals: Tesla Model Y, Hyundai Ioniq 5, Volkswagen ID.4, Škoda Enyaq / Renault Scenic E‑Tech and entry‑luxury crossovers like the Genesis GV60 — essentially the mainstream compact/medium electric crossover set. [carwow alternatives](https://www.carwow.co.uk/editorial/best-cars/alternatives/new-kia-ev5-alternatives), [CarsGuide compare](https://www.carsguide.com.au/kia/ev5/vs)

#### 2. What goes wrong with a used Kia EV5?

Exterior:
- Panel gaps & alignment: early owner reports and pre‑production videos flagged variable panel gaps around doors and some boot/arch joins. Forum threads and spy photos have debated whether certain gaps are "within tolerance" or visibly sloppy — worth checking on any used example. [Kia EV Forum discussion](https://www.kiaevforums.com/threads/do-these-panel-gaps-look-excessive.14498/), [Carscoops spy coverage](https://www.carscoops.com/2023/09/check-out-the-2025-kia-ev5-on-the-road-in-new-spy-video/)
- Trim vulnerabilities: gloss black plastics and painted aero panels appear on press cars — those bits show scuffs easily in town parking and have been noted in reviews; inspect lower bumpers and rocker cladding for stone‑chips (common for taller crossovers).
- Paint durability: no broad brand‑wide failure reported specifically for EV5 paint in the public sources yet, but check for early build variance if the car has been exposed to harsh sunlight — common sense for Aussie buyers.
- Body recalls / manufacturing campaign highlights:
  - A documented safety recall for missing bolts under second‑row seats affected some early EV5 units (reported in news) — confirm VIN/recall status before purchase. [Auto123 recall report](https://www.auto123.com/en/news/kia-ev5-recalled-missing-bolts/73856/), and always run a VIN check via Kia’s owners portal or national recall registries. [Kia owners portal](https://owners.kia.com/us/en/recalls.html)
- ADAS quirks & firmware:
  - Owners report occasional ADAS sensor sensitivity and false warnings or temporary unavailability — common triggers are dirty radar/lidar housings, heavy rain or obscured cameras. Kia’s generic guidance for Smart/Adaptive Cruise limitations is that obstruction/temp conditions can affect availability. [Kia Smart Cruise doc](https://www.kia.com/content/dam/kia2/in/en/content/seltos-manual/topics/chapter6_9_6.html)
  - Community threads show that firmware updates from dealers often tidy up behaviour; always ask for a full ECU/firmware update history during your pre‑purchase check.

Interior:
- Cabin durability under heavy duty: the EV5 is pitched as a family SUV — panels, seat fabrics and switchgear are modern but some owners of contemporary Kia/HMG models reported wear on steering‑wheel leather, seat bolsters and switch finishes under high‑kilometre urban duty. Use cases like rideshare/rental are harsher than private ownership — expect higher wear.
- Entry/exit ergonomics: decent family‑car door opening angles and a sensible sill height for the class — easy for kids and grandparents. Still, check door check stops and strap joints on second row for stiffness or play after heavy use.
- Electrical behaviours:
  - Infotainment & screens: EV5s are sold with large twin 12.3‑inch displays in many markets. Across recent 2020–2025 Kia/Hyundai models, owners have seen blank screens, reboots, lag and delamination in touchscreen clusters. The wider industry is seeing intermittent touchscreen failures — soft resets and dealer software updates sometimes help, but some require part replacement. If the listing shows aftermarket re‑boots or repairs, treat that as a potential future cost item. [Go‑Parts infotainment guide](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/infotainment-display-kia-ev6-hyundai-elantra-hyundai-kona-2020-2025), [CUEscreens article](https://www.cuescreens.com/blogs/news/kia-touchscreen-problems)
  - 12V battery drain: modern EVs use the 12V for body electronics and infotainment; community reports of parasitic draw and software‑related drains exist across HMG models. If the car has been sat long, test cold‑start and scan for parasitic current draw if possible.
  - Screen sizes across trims: twin 12.3" instruments + centre screen are common on global trims; some markets list variations — confirm trim spec for the car you’re buying. [Kia press pack (UK)](https://www.kiapressoffice.com/models/ev5)
- Practical test: pair a phone with wired and wireless CarPlay/Android Auto, leave the car for a full day, and verify no unexplained battery drain or persistent warning lights on ignition.

Mechanically:
- Brand engineering reputation: Kia’s EVs share drivetrain and battery tech with Hyundai. The E‑GMP platform is proven in multiple models, but early‑production EV-specific issues (charging modules, inverter coolant) have appeared in the broader HMG family.
- High‑priority security vulnerabilities:
  - The well‑publicised CAN injection / keyless‑theft problem that hit many Kia/Hyundai vehicles carried over as a class risk — a large recall/remedy action addressed related vulnerabilities for millions of vehicles. Confirm whether an EV5 has had the security software remediation applied — if not, the car is an easier target for modern "relay/CAN injection" theft tools. [CarThreat coverage of recall](https://carthreat.com/2026/06/30/nine-million-kia-and-hyundai-vehicles-now-under-formal-recall-for-theft-vulnerability/), [Yahoo Autos summary](https://autos.yahoo.com/safety-and-recalls/articles/kia-hyundais-security-fix-now-223336920.html)
- Aftermarket theft countermeasures to consider:
  - Fit a VIN‑coded mechanical steering‑column lock (cheap and visible deterrent).
  - Consider a telematics‑connected alarm/immobiliser with CAN‑bus anomaly detection.
  - If you have a private garage, simple physical security (wheel clamps, locking) still works — thieves prefer low‑risk hauls.

Engines (powertrain variants) — italicised subsections for each powertrain:
- *81.4 kWh single‑motor FWD (approx. 160 kW, ~310 Nm)*
  - Common weak points to watch:
    - **High‑voltage charging unit / ICCU**: industry reports across Hyundai/Kia EVs have flagged ICCU/charging unit failures causing charging issues or loss of motive power — check charge history, any overnight charging failures, and ask if dealer has completed any charging control module campaigns. [Consumer Reports coverage of ICCU issues](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-recalls-defects/hyundai-ioniq-kia-iccu-failure-tesla-a3038878758/)
    - **Inverter coolant flow / contamination**: a technical service bulletin describes inverter coolant debris and the need for flushing/refill on affected HMG EVs — verify any inverter coolant service or related TSBs on your VIN. [NHTSA TSB PDF on inverter coolant](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10235407-0001.pdf)
    - Battery health: 81.4 kWh NMC pack is robust on paper and covered by Kia’s long warranties (often 7yr/150,000 km in many markets) but confirm warranty transferability and check for battery degradation logs in dealer diagnostics. [CarChecker reliability summary](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/kia_ev5_81kwh_fwd.html)
  - Preventative maintenance & intervals:
    - Adopt a 10,000 km service cadence for cabin, high‑voltage system inspection and coolant checks if you do high mileage in Australia (urban heat + towing/day usage accelerates wear). Kia dealerships can run BMS and inverter checks — ask for a printout. (Note: manufacturer intervals may vary by market; get specifics from your dealer.)
- *Dual‑motor AWD (mid‑200 kW total, higher torque)*
  - Watch for:
    - Same high‑voltage module issues as above (charging unit, inverter coolant).
    - Additional motor control software complexity — AWD torque distribution faults typically show as drive‑mode light or limp‑home behaviour in forums.
    - Greater regen braking load can stress the EPB controller and brake wear — check for EPB sticking/EPB‑refusal to release complaints in owner threads. [Kia EV Forum EPB threads](https://www.kiaevforums.com/threads/emergency-brake-behavior.13244/)
  - Preventative maintenance:
    - Same 10,000 km checks recommended: high‑voltage component scan, inverter coolant inspection, and brake system (including EPB) diagnostics.

Transmission
- Mechanical behaviour: the EV5 uses electric motor(s) with single‑speed reduction gear(s). There is no multi‑ratio automatic; behaviour is smooth, immediate torque delivery with selectable regen settings controlling one‑pedal feel. [Carwow review / drivetrain commentary](https://www.carwow.co.uk/kia/ev5)
- Known failure modes:
  - Not a traditional torque‑converter automatic failure list; instead, look for:
    - **Motor controller / inverter faults** leading to "periodically does not start" or limp‑mode reports — owners have logged intermittent start/no‑drive faults that point to CAN communication or inverter module errors. [MHHAUTO forum thread on starting intermittency](https://mhhauto.com/Thread-Fully-Electric-Kia-EV5-periodically-does-not-start)
    - Regenerative braking / EPB interactions where the EPB sometimes refuses to release or behaves oddly under certain fault states — dealer firmware and brake module updates can remedy in many cases. [Kia forum EPB thread](https://www.kiaevforums.com/threads/emergency-brake-behavior.13244/)
  - Advice: insist on a full dealer‑level diagnostic read of the drive‑motor controllers, HV battery BMS logs and any historical fault codes before purchase.

#### 3. Should you buy a used Kia EV5?

- NSW dealer snapshot (live MTA NSW scraper): Active listings: 3 — Price range: AUD $43,990 – $52,950 — Median price: AUD $48,990 — Year range: 2024–2024 — Median odometer: 35,802 km — Transmission mix: Automatic: 3. Use these as your immediate negotiation anchors.
  - Source: the NSW dealer data you supplied (live snapshot). Use this to set expectations at inspection.
- Depreciation & market context:
  - EV depreciation remains model‑dependent; the EV5 is new to the used market so long‑term resale is still settling. Industry analysis shows many mid‑tier EVs have suffered 40–60% falls in the first 3–5 years historically, though the market is stabilising post‑2024/25. Expect above‑ICE depreciation risk versus comparable petrol SUVs in the first 3 years. [recharged depreciation analysis](https://recharged.com/articles/best-used-ev-value-after-depreciation), [EV Depreciation dataset](https://evdepreciation.com/50-evs-and-associated-depreciation-from-2020-to-2025/)
  - Practical takeaway: if you buy an EV5 near the current NSW median (~$49k), accept that the first owner will absorb a lot of depreciation risk; treat it as a value play only if you can tolerate resale uncertainty or plan to keep long‑term.
- TCO strategy & math (how to think about it in Australia):
  - Real‑world efficiency: independent testing and the EV Database list efficiency around **~195 Wh/km** (approx 19.5 kWh/100 km) for the 81.4 kWh variant — use that figure to estimate running energy costs. [EV Database EV5 81.4 kWh entry](https://ev-database.org/car/3300/Kia-EV5-814-kWh)
  - How to compute: choose your average kWh price (home charging vs public DC fast), multiply 19.5 kWh/100 km by cost per kWh to get $/100 km. For example (example only — plug in your local numbers):
    - If you pay $0.30/kWh at home → 19.5 kWh × $0.30 = $5.85 per 100 km.
    - Compare that to a family petrol SUV doing 8.5 L/100 km at $1.80/L → $15.30 per 100 km.
  - Use official tools: Kia’s running‑cost calculator (local market) and the IEA TCO tool are useful to model your own mileage, charger installation and electricity rates. [Kia EV costing tool (UK example)](https://www.kia.com/uk/electric-hybrid-cars/electric-cars/ev-cost-calculator/), [IEA TCO tool](https://www.iea.org/data-and-statistics/data-tools/electric-vehicles-total-cost-of-ownership-tool)
  - Battery warranty & risk: Kia’s long warranty (typically 7 years / 150,000 km in many markets) is a big tick for TCO — it caps a major unknown (battery health) for much of typical ownership. Check Australian warranty terms with your dealer for exact transferability and exclusions. [CarChecker / warranty note](https://www.carchecker.pro/reports/kia_ev5_81kwh_fwd.html)
  - Is paying a premium for an EV (vs hybrid/petrol) sensible? It depends:
    - High urban km + home charging + access to off‑peak rates = EV5 will often be materially cheaper per km than petrol.
    - Low annual km, no home charger, or heavy rural/high‑speed duty (constant DC fast charging) reduces the savings and raises battery‑degradation & charging‑cost risk.
    - Use a three‑year ownership model: compare purchase price + 3‑yr electricity + service/maintenance + expected depreciation. If you cover high km in urban driving and can charge cheaply at home, the EV5 is a likely winner; if you don’t, the premium may not pay back.
- Depreciation metric suggestion: benchmark a 3‑year residual vs class:
  - Expect the EV5 to follow the mid‑tier EV band: steeper than mainstream petrol in years 1–3 (40–50% expected drop in worst cases) but this is normalising. Use the EV Depreciation dataset to model scenarios. [evdepreciation.com](https://evdepreciation.com/50-evs-and-associated-depreciation-from-2020-to-2025/)
- Buying recommendation — the blunt Aussie verdict:
  - If you want an EV5, the NSW market snapshot shows one‑year‑old, mid‑30k km cars listed around **AUD $44k–$53k** (median ~$48,990). At that mileage, you’re buying a still‑new vehicle in dealer network territory — get the dealer to show complete service history and recall/TSB work.
  - Recommended sweet spot: a 2024 EV5 with the **81.4 kWh single‑motor FWD** spec around the lower‑mid of your listed range (aim to negotiate toward the low $40ks if condition and warranty are clean). It gives the best balance of range, simplicity and lower repair complexity (one motor, simpler software stack).
  - Avoid if:
    - You can’t verify that charging/ICCU and inverter coolant campaigns (if applicable) were completed.
    - The car shows repeated infotainment reboots or dealer RMS logs with unresolved motor/inverter DTCs.
  - Buy if:
    - Dealer provides full VIN history including recall remedies (missing seat‑bolt recall or theft‑patch applied), BMS logs showing healthy battery SOH and no recurring HV faults, and a recent software/firmware update from Kia.
- Pre‑purchase checklist (non‑negotiables):
  - VIN‑level recall & campaign check via Kia owners portal / national recall check. [Kia recalls](https://owners.kia.com/us/en/recalls.html)
  - Dealer diagnostic printout of HV battery health, inverter coolant change history & ICCU/charging unit logs.
  - Test charge on a public DC fast charger (confirm the car accepts expected rates) and a full home‑charge cycle.
  - Full drive test verifying ADAS operation (adaptive cruise, AEB) and EPB behaviour (no sticking).
  - Confirm software remediations for CAN/keyless recall and ask for proof (given Kia/Hyundai recall actions). [recall press coverage](https://carthreat.com/2026/06/30/nine-million-kia-and-hyundai-vehicles-now-under-formal-recall-for-theft-vulnerability/)
  - Check for infotainment freeze history and ask to factory‑reset the head unit during inspection to see if it recovers.
- Final bottom line:
  - The Kia EV5 is a well‑packaged family electric SUV with modern kit and a performance/efficiency profile that stacks up against strong rivals. But it’s new enough that you should treat each used example like a late‑model new car: verify recall completion, get dealer diagnostics for the high‑voltage systems, and be prepared to use warranty and dealer support. If you can secure a low‑$40k NSW example (AUD $43,990–$48,990) with clean history and warranty transfer, it’s a strong buy for urban families who can charge at home. If you’re buying sight unseen or the car has unresolved tech/charging faults, walk away — there are plenty of EVs and dealers with better proven reliability histories.
